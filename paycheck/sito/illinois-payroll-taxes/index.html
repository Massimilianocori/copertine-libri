How to do payroll in Illinois: register, report, pay

Illinois has two agencies for payroll: the Department of Revenue (IDOR) for the income tax you withhold and the Department of Employment Security (IDES) for unemployment contributions. Both use the MyTax Illinois website.[4],[5]

“You must register with the Illinois Department of Revenue (IDOR) if you conduct business in Illinois or with Illinois customers.”

Illinois Department of Revenue, Business Registration[4]

“If necessary, you may also register with the Illinois Department of Employment Security (IDES) using MyTax Illinois.”

Illinois Department of Revenue, Business Registration[4]

For unemployment contributions, IDES says most for-profit employers are liable as soon as they have:[5]

“Paid $1,500 in wages in a single calendar quarter, or employed one or more persons for 20 weeks in a given calendar year”

Illinois Department of Employment Security (IDES), Unemployment Taxes & Reporting[5]

Reports and payments:

“With MyTax Illinois you can file monthly or quarterly UI contribution and wage reports”

Illinois Department of Employment Security (IDES), Unemployment Taxes & Reporting[5]

The return and deposit schedule for Illinois income tax withholding is not covered here: the Department of Revenue assigns it.

The federal side is the same in every state: an EIN, a Form W-4 from each employee, deposits of the tax you withhold, Form 941 every quarter and Form 940 once a year. The steps, with the IRS deadlines, are on how to do payroll yourself.[1]

What you withhold from an Illinois paycheck

Example: $2,000.00 gross every two weeks, federal filing status Single, one Line 1 allowance on Form IL-W-4, nothing else on the forms. You withhold $156.15 federal income tax, $124.00 Social Security, $29.00 Medicare and $93.43 Illinois income tax, and the employee takes home $1,597.42. Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

What payroll taxes apply in Illinois?

Two state items. The employer withholds Illinois income tax from each paycheck, at 4.95% after allowances in 2026, and pays state unemployment tax of 3.35% on the first $14,250 of each employee’s wages as a new employer. The federal payroll taxes (Social Security, Medicare, FUTA) come on top.[1],[2],[3]

What is the payroll tax rate in Illinois?

There is no single rate. In 2026 the employer pays 7.65% for Social Security and Medicare (Social Security stops at $184,500 of wages), FUTA of 0.6% on the first $7,000 and Illinois unemployment tax of 3.35% on the first $14,250 as a new employer. The employee pays 7.65% for Social Security and Medicare and has Illinois income tax withheld at 4.95%. On a $50,000 salary the employer’s taxes come to $4,344.38, about 8.7% of the wage.[1],[2]

How much are employer payroll taxes in Illinois on a $50,000 salary?

$4,344.38 in 2026 at the new-employer rate: $3,100.00 Social Security, $725.00 Medicare, $42.00 FUTA and $477.38 Illinois unemployment tax. Wages plus taxes: $54,344.38. Workers’ compensation insurance and benefits are not included.[1],[2]

Who has to pay Illinois unemployment tax?

According to the Illinois Department of Employment Security, most for-profit employers are liable as soon as they have paid $1,500 in wages in a single calendar quarter, or employed one or more persons for 20 weeks in a calendar year. New employers then pay 3.35% on the first $14,250 of each worker’s wages in 2026.[2],[5]
