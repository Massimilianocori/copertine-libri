How California withholds income tax

California employers withhold state income tax (PIT) with one of two official methods. This calculator uses Method B, the exact calculation, with the payroll-period tables for weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly and monthly pay.[1]

“Method B may be used to calculate withholding amounts either manually or by computer. This method will give an exact amount of tax to withhold.”

California Employment Development Department (EDD), California Withholding Schedules for 2026, Method B – Exact Calculation Method[1]
  1. If gross pay is not above the low income exemption for your filing status, nothing is withheld.
  2. Subtract the estimated deduction for any allowances on line 2 of Form DE 4, then the standard deduction.
  3. Apply the rate table for the pay period and filing status (1.1%, 2.2%, 4.4%, 6.6%, 8.8%, 10.23%, 11.33%, 12.43%, 13.53%, 14.63%).
  4. Subtract the exemption allowance credit for the regular allowances on line 1.

“Determine if the employee’s gross wages are less than, or equal to, the amount shown in Table 1”

California Employment Development Department (EDD), California Withholding Schedules for 2026, Method B – Exact Calculation Method[1]

“Subtract the standard deduction amount shown in Table 3 - Standard Deduction Table to arrive at the employee’s taxable income.”

California Employment Development Department (EDD), California Withholding Schedules for 2026, Method B – Exact Calculation Method[1]

The EDD’s own example, recalculated

Example B in the 2026 schedules: biweekly earnings of $1,600, married, three allowances of which one is for estimated deductions. The EDD’s answer is $2.38 withheld. Our calculator gives the same result:

  • $1,600.00 is above the low income exemption of $1,454.00
  • $1,600.00 − $38.00 estimated deduction − $439.00 standard deduction = $1,123.00 taxable
  • $9.37 + 2.2% of the amount over $852.00 = $15.33
  • $15.33 − $12.95 credit for two regular allowances = $2.38

For married employees whose spouse also works, the EDD suggests a different choice, which is why the calculator’s first filing status covers both:

“we recommend that you use one of the following options: Single filing status to compute withholding amounts for the employee and spouse; or withhold an additional flat amount of tax.”

California Employment Development Department (EDD), California Withholding Schedules for 2026, Method B – Exact Calculation Method[1]

For the “Married” column of the low income exemption and standard deduction tables (0 or 1 allowances, 2 or more) we count the regular allowances on line 1; the schedule does not spell out which line it means.

State Disability Insurance (SDI)

“The SDI withholding rate for 2026 is 1.3 percent.”

California Employment Development Department (EDD), Contribution Rates, Withholding Schedules, and Meals and Lodging Values[2]

“Senate Bill 951 removes the taxable wage limit and maximum withholdings for each employee subject to SDI contributions.”

California Employment Development Department (EDD), Contribution Rates, Withholding Schedules, and Meals and Lodging Values[2]

SDI is paid by the employee. At 1.3%, $2,000.00 of gross pay has $26.00 withheld. The EDD has already published the rate for 2027:

“The SDI withholding rate for 2027 is 1.4 percent.”

California employers pay Unemployment Insurance (UI) and the Employment Training Tax (ETT) on the first $7,000 of each employee’s wages: at the new-employer rate, $238.00 of UI and $7.00 of ETT for an employee who earns at least that much. The 2026 schedule for experienced employers runs from 1.5% to 6.2%.[2]

Higher FUTA for California in 2026

California still owes the federal government money borrowed for unemployment benefits, so its employers lose part of the usual FUTA credit. For 2026 the Department of Labor’s final list gives:[3]

“therefore employers in California are subject to a 1.5% credit reduction”

U.S. Department of Labor, Employment and Training Administration, Final 2026 Federal Unemployment Tax Act (FUTA) Credit Reductions[4]

That makes FUTA 0.6% + 1.5% = 2.1% of the first $7,000: $147.00 per employee instead of $42.00.

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

What is the California SDI tax?

State Disability Insurance (SDI) is a California payroll deduction paid by employees. The withholding rate for 2026 is 1.3% of all wages: since January 1, 2024 there is no taxable wage limit. The rate published for 2027 is 1.4%.[2]

How is California state income tax withheld from a paycheck?

Employers use the EDD’s withholding schedules. Under Method B they subtract a standard deduction, apply a rate table with rates from 1.1% to 14.63% for the pay period and filing status, and subtract a credit for each regular allowance on Form DE 4. Pay at or below the low income exemption has nothing withheld.[1]

What payroll taxes does a California employer pay?

The employer’s 6.2% Social Security and 1.45% Medicare, FUTA, California Unemployment Insurance and the 0.1% Employment Training Tax, the last two on the first $7,000 of each employee’s wages. The employer also withholds the employee’s income tax and SDI.[2],[6]

What is the California unemployment tax rate for new employers?

New employers are assigned a 3.4% UI rate for two to three years, on the first $7,000 of each employee’s wages. In 2026 experienced employers pay between 1.5% and 6.2%.[2]
