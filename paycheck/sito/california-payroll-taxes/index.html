How to do payroll in California: register, report, pay

California payroll taxes are run by the Employment Development Department (EDD). One EDD payroll tax account covers all four state taxes.[4]

“Two are paid by employers, and two are withheld from employee wages.”

California Employment Development Department (EDD), Step 5: Withhold and Pay Payroll Taxes[4]

The two paid by the employer are Unemployment Insurance (UI) and the Employment Training Tax (ETT); the two withheld from pay are State Disability Insurance (SDI) and Personal Income Tax (PIT).[4] When to register:[5]

“You must register as an employer within 15 days”

California Employment Development Department (EDD), Step 1: Register as an Employer[5]

The 15 days start, for a business with employees, once you:[5]

“Pay more than $100 in wages in a calendar quarter”

California Employment Development Department (EDD), Step 1: Register as an Employer[5]

Registration is online, through the EDD’s e-Services for Business. New employees are reported to the EDD as well:[6]

“Report all new hires within 20 days of their first day of work”

California Employment Development Department (EDD), Step 4: Report Employees and Independent Contractors[6]

Deposits and payments:

“Your SDI and PIT deposit due dates depend on your federal deposit schedule and the amount of PIT you withhold. Your UI and ETT payments are due every quarter.”

California Employment Development Department (EDD), Step 5: Withhold and Pay Payroll Taxes[4]

Every quarter you file two reports, the Quarterly Contribution Return and Report of Wages (DE 9) and its continuation (DE 9C). The EDD gives their due dates as January 1, April 1, July 1 and October 1; its “Required Filings and Due Dates” page says when a report counts as late.[7]

The federal side is the same in every state: an EIN, a Form W-4 from each employee, deposits of the tax you withhold, Form 941 every quarter and Form 940 once a year. The steps, with the IRS deadlines, are on how to do payroll yourself.[1]

What you withhold from a California paycheck

Example: $2,000.00 gross every two weeks, federal filing status Single, California Single with no allowances, nothing else on the forms. You withhold $156.15 federal income tax, $124.00 Social Security, $29.00 Medicare, $55.58 California income tax and $26.00 SDI, and the employee takes home $1,609.27. Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

What are the payroll taxes in California?

California has four state payroll taxes. The employer pays Unemployment Insurance (3.4% for new employers) and the Employment Training Tax (0.1%), both on the first $7,000 of each employee’s wages. State Disability Insurance (1.3% in 2026) and Personal Income Tax are withheld from the employee. The federal payroll taxes come on top.[2],[4]

What is the payroll tax rate in California?

There is no single rate. In 2026 the employer pays 7.65% for Social Security and Medicare (Social Security stops at $184,500 of wages), FUTA of 2.1% on the first $7,000 and California unemployment tax of 3.4% on the first $7,000 as a new employer (plus the 0.1% Employment Training Tax). The employee pays 7.65% for Social Security and Medicare, 1.3% SDI and California income tax. On a $50,000 salary the employer’s taxes come to $4,217.00, about 8.4% of the wage.[1],[2]

How much are employer payroll taxes in California on a $50,000 salary?

$4,217.00 in 2026 at the new-employer rate: $3,100.00 Social Security, $725.00 Medicare, $147.00 FUTA and $238.00 California unemployment tax, plus $7.00 Employment Training Tax. Wages plus taxes: $54,217.00. Workers’ compensation insurance and benefits are not included.[1],[2]

How do I do payroll in California?

Get an EIN from the IRS, then register with the EDD within 15 days of paying more than $100 in wages in a calendar quarter. Take a Form W-4 and a DE 4 from each employee, report new hires within 20 days, and withhold federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, California income tax and SDI. Deposit the taxes, file federal Form 941 and the EDD’s DE 9 and DE 9C every quarter, and Form 940 once a year.[1],[5],[6],[7]

Which payroll taxes does the EDD collect?

Four: Unemployment Insurance and the Employment Training Tax, paid by the employer, and State Disability Insurance and Personal Income Tax, withheld from employees’ wages. UI and ETT payments are due every quarter; SDI and PIT deposit dates depend on your federal deposit schedule and on how much PIT you withhold.[4]

What is the California DE 9?

The DE 9 is the EDD’s Quarterly Contribution Return and Report of Wages. Employers file it every quarter together with the DE 9C, the continuation that lists wages. The EDD gives the due dates as January 1, April 1, July 1 and October 1.[7]
