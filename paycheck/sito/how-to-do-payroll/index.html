How to do payroll yourself, 2026

The jobs a small employer repeats every payday, every quarter and every January: what to withhold, when to deposit it and which forms to file, from IRS Publication 15.

Payroll done by hand is the same handful of jobs repeated every payday, every quarter and every January. This page lists them in order for a small employer in 2026, with the sentence of IRS Publication 15 behind each federal deadline and the state agency to register with in Texas, Florida, California, Illinois and North Carolina.[1]

A summary of the ordinary case (an employer that files Form 941), for general information: not tax, legal or payroll advice.

The steps

1. Get an Employer Identification Number (EIN)

The IRS identifies every employer by its EIN. It goes on each deposit and return.

“If you don’t have an EIN, you may apply for one online by going to IRS.gov/EIN.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[1]

2. Register with your state

Each state has its own account for unemployment tax and, where wages are taxed, a second one for income tax withholding. The table of the five states is below.

3. Take a Form W-4 and report the new hire

Each employee fills in a federal Form W-4 (and the state form in California, Illinois and North Carolina); their entries decide how much income tax you withhold. Then the hire is reported to the state:

“You’re required to report any new employee to a designated state new hire registry.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[1]

4. Work out each paycheck

From gross pay you withhold federal income tax (Worksheet 1A of Publication 15-T), 6.2% Social Security up to $184,500 of wages in the year, 1.45% Medicare and any state income tax. As the employer you owe the same Social Security and Medicare again.[1],[2] The paycheck calculator does the employee’s side line by line; the federal withholding page shows the tables.

5. Deposit the federal taxes

The income tax you withheld and both halves of Social Security and Medicare are deposited together, electronically:

“You must use EFT to make all federal tax deposits.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[1]

How often depends on the taxes you reported in an earlier 12-month “lookback period”:

“If you reported $50,000 or less of taxes for the lookback period, you’re a monthly schedule depositor; if you reported more than $50,000, you’re a semiweekly schedule depositor.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[1]

For a new business the taxes of the lookback period count as zero, so the publication says:

“Therefore, you’re a monthly schedule depositor for the first calendar year of your business.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[1]

“Under the monthly deposit schedule, deposit employment taxes on payments made during a month by the 15th day of the following month.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[1]

For the larger employers on the other schedule:

“Under the semiweekly deposit schedule, deposit employment taxes for payments made on Wednesday, Thursday, and/or Friday by the following Wednesday. Deposit taxes for payments made on Saturday, Sunday, Monday, and/or Tuesday by the following Friday.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[1]

6. File Form 941 every quarter

Form 941 reports the wages paid and the taxes withheld and deposited in the quarter.

“Form 941 must be filed by the last day of the month that follows the end of the quarter.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[1]

So the four dates are April 30, July 31, October 31 and January 31; a date on a weekend or legal holiday moves to the next business day.

7. Pay FUTA and file Form 940 once a year

“For 2026, the FUTA tax rate is 6.0%. The tax applies to the first $7,000 you pay to each employee as wages during the year.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[1]

“The credit may be as much as 5.4% of FUTA taxable wages. If you’re entitled to the maximum 5.4% credit, the FUTA tax rate after credit is 0.6%.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[1]

“If your liability for the fourth quarter (plus any undeposited amount from any earlier quarter) is over $500, deposit the entire amount by the due date of Form 940 (January 31).”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[1]

After the full credit FUTA is 0.6% of the first $7,000: $42.00 per employee, or $147.00 in California in 2026 because of the credit reduction. Details on the employer payroll taxes page.

8. File the state report every quarter

All five states want a quarterly wage report with the unemployment tax; the three with an income tax also want the tax you withheld. See the table below.

One payday, worked through

One employee in Texas, $2,000.00 gross every two weeks, Form W-4 marked Single with nothing else filled in, 2026.

  • Withhold federal income tax: $156.15
  • Withhold Social Security 6.2%: $124.00 and Medicare 1.45%: $29.00
  • Pay the employee: $2,000.00 − $309.15 = $1,690.85
  • Add your own share of Social Security and Medicare: $153.00
  • Owed to the IRS for this payday: $156.15 + $153.00 + $153.00 = $462.15, deposited by the 15th of the following month on the monthly schedule

State unemployment tax and FUTA are worked out on the same wages but paid separately: use the calculator at the bottom of the page.

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

How do I do payroll myself?

Get an EIN, register with your state, take a Form W-4 from each employee, and on every payday withhold federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare and any state tax. Deposit the federal taxes on your schedule, file Form 941 every quarter and Form 940 once a year, file the state reports, and give each employee a Form W-2 after the year ends.[1]

How do you calculate payroll taxes?

For each paycheck in 2026: 6.2% of gross pay for Social Security (up to $184,500 of wages in the year) and 1.45% for Medicare, withheld from the employee and paid again by the employer; federal income tax from the Form W-4 and the tables in IRS Publication 15-T; then state tax. Example: on $2,000.00 every two weeks, single filer in Texas, $156.15 income tax, $124.00 Social Security and $29.00 Medicare are withheld.[1],[2]

How do I pay payroll taxes to the IRS?

By electronic funds transfer: IRS Publication 15 says you must use EFT to make all federal tax deposits, for example through the Electronic Federal Tax Payment System (EFTPS). The deposit covers the income tax withheld and both the employee and employer shares of Social Security and Medicare.[1]

When are payroll taxes due?

On the monthly schedule, federal deposits are due by the 15th day of the following month. Form 941 is due by the last day of the month after each quarter (April 30, July 31, October 31 and January 31) and Form 940 by January 31. State unemployment tax reports are quarterly in Texas, Florida, California and North Carolina, and monthly or quarterly in Illinois.[1],[3],[6],[9],[11],[13]

What is the payroll tax deposit schedule?

There are two. If you reported $50,000 or less of taxes in the lookback period you are a monthly schedule depositor and deposit by the 15th of the following month; above $50,000 you are a semiweekly schedule depositor. A new business is a monthly schedule depositor for its first calendar year.[1]

What is Form 941 and when is it due?

Form 941 is the quarterly federal return on which an employer reports wages, the income tax withheld and Social Security and Medicare taxes. It must be filed by the last day of the month that follows the end of the quarter: April 30, July 31, October 31 and January 31.[1]

What is Form 940?

Form 940 is the yearly return for FUTA, the federal unemployment tax paid only by employers: 6% on the first $7,000 of each employee’s wages, 0.6% after the full credit for state unemployment tax. Its due date is January 31.[1]

Can a small business do payroll without software?

Yes. The IRS publishes the worksheet and tables for working out withholding by hand, and the deposits and returns can be made on the IRS and state websites. What it takes is time and care with deadlines: a late deposit or return can bring penalties.[1],[2]
