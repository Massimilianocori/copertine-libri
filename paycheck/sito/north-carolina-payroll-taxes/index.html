How to do payroll in North Carolina: register, report, pay

North Carolina has two agencies for payroll: the Department of Revenue (NCDOR) for the income tax you withhold and the Division of Employment Security (DES) for unemployment tax.[3],[4]

For withholding, the NC-30 booklet says that when you begin paying wages:

“you must file Form NC-BR, Business Registration Application for Income Tax Withholding, Sales and Use Tax, and Other Taxes and Service Charge, with the Department of Revenue to obtain a North Carolina withholding identification number.”

North Carolina Department of Revenue, NC-30, 2026 Income Tax Withholding Tables and Instructions for Employers[3]

“You are required to report and pay the tax withheld on a quarterly, monthly, or semiweekly basis.”

North Carolina Department of Revenue, NC-30, 2026 Income Tax Withholding Tables and Instructions for Employers[3]

For unemployment tax, DES writes:

“Unemployment taxes are not deducted from employees’ wages.”

North Carolina Division of Employment Security, Am I Required to Pay Taxes?[4]

A general business is liable when it has:

“Paid quarterly wages of at least $1,500; OR Employed at least one worker in 20 different weeks during a calendar year.”

North Carolina Division of Employment Security, Am I Required to Pay Taxes?[4]

“You need an Employer ID Number if you must pay unemployment taxes.”

North Carolina Division of Employment Security, Am I Required to Pay Taxes?[4]

You get the number when you create an account in NCSUITS, the DES online tax system. Then, every quarter:[5]

“Employers must file the Quarterly Report for each quarter, beginning with the quarter in which employment begins for each employee.”

North Carolina Division of Employment Security, File, Adjust or Review Quarterly Tax & Wage Report[5]

“If you have ten or more employee wage items, you must file the report online.”

North Carolina Division of Employment Security, File, Adjust or Review Quarterly Tax & Wage Report[5]

The federal side is the same in every state: an EIN, a Form W-4 from each employee, deposits of the tax you withhold, Form 941 every quarter and Form 940 once a year. The steps, with the IRS deadlines, are on how to do payroll yourself.[1]

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

What payroll taxes apply in North Carolina?

Two state items. The employer withholds North Carolina income tax from each paycheck, at 4.09% after allowances in 2026, and pays state unemployment tax of 1% on the first $34,200 of each employee’s wages as a new employer. The federal payroll taxes (Social Security, Medicare, FUTA) come on top.[1],[2],[3]

What is the payroll tax rate in North Carolina?

There is no single rate. In 2026 the employer pays 7.65% for Social Security and Medicare (Social Security stops at $184,500 of wages), FUTA of 0.6% on the first $7,000 and North Carolina unemployment tax of 1% on the first $34,200 as a new employer. The employee pays 7.65% for Social Security and Medicare and has North Carolina income tax withheld at 4.09%. On a $50,000 salary the employer’s taxes come to $4,209.00, about 8.4% of the wage.[1],[2]

How much are employer payroll taxes in North Carolina on a $50,000 salary?

$4,209.00 in 2026 at the new-employer rate: $3,100.00 Social Security, $725.00 Medicare, $42.00 FUTA and $342.00 North Carolina unemployment tax. Wages plus taxes: $54,209.00. Workers’ compensation insurance and benefits are not included.[1],[2]

Who has to pay North Carolina unemployment tax?

According to the Division of Employment Security, a general business is liable when it paid quarterly wages of at least $1,500 or employed at least one worker in 20 different weeks during a calendar year. The tax is paid by the employer and is not deducted from employees’ wages. Beginning employers pay 1% on the first $34,200 in 2026.[2],[4]
