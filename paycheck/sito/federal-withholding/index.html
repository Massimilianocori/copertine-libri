Federal withholding 2026: the IRS tables and how to calculate it

The worksheet payroll software uses, the yearly tables for every filing status and a worked example.

Employers work out federal income tax withholding from IRS Publication 15-T. For payroll software the publication gives one worksheet (Worksheet 1A) and one pair of yearly tables. This page shows both for 2026 and works through an example.[1]

The worksheet, step by step

“If you’re an employer with an automated payroll system, use Worksheet 1A and the Percentage Method tables in this section to figure federal income tax withholding.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15-T (2026), Federal Income Tax Withholding Methods[1]
  1. Make it yearly. Multiply the taxable wages of the pay period by the number of pay periods (52, 26, 24 or 12).
  2. Adjust. Add the other income from Step 4(a) of Form W-4, subtract the deductions from Step 4(b), then subtract $8,600 ($12,900 if married filing jointly). If the Step 2 box is checked, subtract nothing here.
  3. Read the table. Find the row for the adjusted yearly wage: the tentative amount, plus the percentage of the amount over the start of the row.
  4. Divide by the number of pay periods.
  5. Credits. Subtract the Step 3 amount divided by the number of pay periods; the result cannot go below zero.
  6. Extra. Add any extra withholding from Step 4(c).

“If the box in Step 2 of Form W-4 is checked, enter -0-. If the box is not checked, enter $12,900 if the taxpayer is married filing jointly or $8,600 otherwise”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15-T (2026), Federal Income Tax Withholding Methods[1]

“Divide the amount on line 2g by the number of pay periods on line 1b. This is the Tentative Withholding Amount”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15-T (2026), Federal Income Tax Withholding Methods[1]

“Subtract line 3b from line 2h. If zero or less, enter -0-”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15-T (2026), Federal Income Tax Withholding Methods[1]

“Add lines 3c and 4a. This is the amount to withhold from the employee’s wages this pay period”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15-T (2026), Federal Income Tax Withholding Methods[1]

Worked example

$3,000.00 twice a month, married filing jointly, Step 2 box not checked, $4,000.00 of credits for dependents in Step 3.

  • 1c. $3,000.00 × 24 pay periods = $72,000.00
  • 1g–1i. Subtract $12,900.00: adjusted annual wage $59,100.00
  • 2b–2g. $2,480.00 + 12% of the amount over $44,100.00 = $4,280.00 a year
  • 2h. $4,280.00 ÷ 24 = $178.33 per paycheck
  • 3a–3c. Credits $4,000.00 ÷ 24 = $166.67; $178.33 − $166.67 = $11.66 withheld per paycheck

Rounding

“You may also round the tax for the pay period to the nearest dollar. If rounding is used, it must be used consistently.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15-T (2026), Federal Income Tax Withholding Methods[1]

We keep cents and round each worksheet line to the cent (lines 2h and 3b above), so a pay stub from an employer that rounds to whole dollars can differ from our figure by up to 50 cents, and payroll software that carries more decimals by a cent.

Social Security and Medicare

“For 2026, the social security tax rate is 6.2% (amount withheld) each for the employer and employee (12.4% total). The social security wage base limit is $184,500.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[2]

“The tax rate for Medicare is 1.45% (amount withheld) each for the employee and employer (2.9% total). There is no wage base limit for Medicare tax; all covered wages are subject to Medicare tax.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[2]

“0.9% Additional Medicare Tax from wages you pay to an employee in excess of $200,000 in a calendar year.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[2]

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

What is federal withholding?

It is the federal income tax your employer takes out of each paycheck and sends to the IRS on your behalf. The amount depends on your pay, how often you are paid and what you entered on Form W-4. It is a prepayment: your tax return settles the difference.[1]

How do you calculate federal withholding?

With Worksheet 1A of IRS Publication 15-T: multiply the pay by the number of pay periods, add and subtract the Form W-4 Step 4 amounts, subtract $8,600 ($12,900 if married filing jointly), read the yearly tax from the table for the filing status, divide by the number of pay periods, then subtract the Step 3 credits and add any extra withholding.[1]

Where are the federal income tax withholding tables for 2026?

In IRS Publication 15-T for 2026. Section 1 has the percentage method tables for automated payroll systems, shown on this page; sections 2 to 5 have wage bracket and percentage tables for payroll done by hand.[1]

How much is Social Security tax on a paycheck?

6.2% of gross wages, until your wages for the year reach $184,500 in 2026. On a $2,000.00 paycheck that is $124.00.[2]
