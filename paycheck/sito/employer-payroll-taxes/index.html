Federal payroll taxes paid by the employer, 2026

“For 2026, the social security tax rate is 6.2% (amount withheld) each for the employer and employee (12.4% total). The social security wage base limit is $184,500.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[1]

“The tax rate for Medicare is 1.45% (amount withheld) each for the employee and employer (2.9% total). There is no wage base limit for Medicare tax; all covered wages are subject to Medicare tax.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[1]

“Additional Medicare Tax is only imposed on the employee. There is no employer share of Additional Medicare Tax.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[1]

The employer matches the employee’s Social Security and Medicare: 7.65% of wages up to $184,500, then 1.45% above it. On a $50,000 salary that is $3,825.00 a year.

FUTA: federal unemployment tax

“For 2026, the FUTA tax rate is 6.0%. The tax applies to the first $7,000 you pay to each employee as wages during the year.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[1]

“The credit may be as much as 5.4% of FUTA taxable wages. If you’re entitled to the maximum 5.4% credit, the FUTA tax rate after credit is 0.6%.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[1]

“You’re entitled to the maximum credit if you paid your state unemployment taxes in full, on time, and on all the same wages as are subject to FUTA tax, and as long as the state isn’t determined to be a credit reduction state.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[1]

So FUTA is normally 0.6% of the first $7,000: $42.00 per employee per year. Only the employer pays it.

Credit reduction states in 2026

When a state has not repaid federal loans for unemployment benefits, its employers get a smaller credit and pay more FUTA.[2] The Department of Labor’s final list for 2026 names one of our five states:

“therefore employers in California are subject to a 1.5% credit reduction”

U.S. Department of Labor, Employment and Training Administration, Final 2026 Federal Unemployment Tax Act (FUTA) Credit Reductions[3]

For California that means 2.1% instead of 0.6%: $147.00 per employee. Texas, Florida, Illinois and North Carolina are not on the list.

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

What payroll taxes does an employer pay?

In 2026 an employer pays 6.2% Social Security on wages up to $184,500, 1.45% Medicare on all wages, federal unemployment tax (FUTA) on the first $7,000 and state unemployment tax. For a $50,000 salary in Texas at the new-employer rate these come to $4,110.00 a year.[1],[4]

What is FUTA?

FUTA is the Federal Unemployment Tax Act tax, paid only by employers. For 2026 the rate is 6% on the first $7,000 of each employee’s wages, less a credit of up to 5.4% for state unemployment taxes paid, which leaves 0.6%.[1]

What is the FUTA tax rate for 2026?

6% before the credit and 0.6% after the maximum credit, on the first $7,000 paid to each employee: $42.00 per employee. In California the 2026 credit reduction of 1.5% raises it to 2.1%.[1],[3]

What is SUTA tax?

SUTA is the common name for state unemployment tax, which each state sets. In 2026 new employers pay 2.7% on the first $9,000 in Texas, 2.7% on $7,000 in Florida, 3.4% on $7,000 in California, 3.35% on $14,250 in Illinois and 1% on $34,200 in North Carolina.[4],[5],[6],[7],[8]

How much does an employee cost on top of the wage?

Payroll taxes add roughly 8% to 9% to a $50,000 salary in these five states: $4,110.00 in Texas and $4,217.00 in California in 2026, at new-employer rates. Benefits and workers’ compensation insurance come on top and are not included here.[1],[4],[6]
