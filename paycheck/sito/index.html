How the estimate is calculated

Your take-home pay is your gross pay minus four things: federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare and, in some states, state income tax or disability insurance. Each one follows a published rule.[1],[2]

1. Federal income tax

We follow Worksheet 1A of IRS Publication 15-T, the method written for payroll software:

“If you’re an employer with an automated payroll system, use Worksheet 1A and the Percentage Method tables in this section to figure federal income tax withholding.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15-T (2026), Federal Income Tax Withholding Methods[1]

The worksheet turns your pay into a yearly figure, subtracts a fixed amount, reads the tax from the yearly table for your filing status and divides it back by the number of pay periods. Example: $2,000.00 every two weeks, Single, nothing else on the W-4.

  • 1c. $2,000.00 × 26 pay periods = $52,000.00
  • 1g–1i. Subtract $8,600.00: adjusted annual wage $43,400.00
  • 2b–2g. $1,240.00 + 12% of the amount over $19,900.00 = $4,060.00 a year
  • 2h. $4,060.00 ÷ 26 = $156.15 per paycheck

The full tables are on the federal withholding page.

2. Social Security and Medicare (FICA)

“For 2026, the social security tax rate is 6.2% (amount withheld) each for the employer and employee (12.4% total). The social security wage base limit is $184,500.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[2]

“The tax rate for Medicare is 1.45% (amount withheld) each for the employee and employer (2.9% total). There is no wage base limit for Medicare tax; all covered wages are subject to Medicare tax.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[2]

On the same $2,000.00 paycheck that is $124.00 for Social Security and $29.00 for Medicare. High earners pay a further 0.9%:

“0.9% Additional Medicare Tax from wages you pay to an employee in excess of $200,000 in a calendar year.”

Internal Revenue Service, Publication 15 (2026), (Circular E), Employer’s Tax Guide[2]

3. State income tax

Texas and Florida withhold nothing from wages. California, Illinois and North Carolina each publish their own formula, and the calculator uses it as written: California’s Method B, the Illinois automated payroll method and North Carolina’s percentage method.[3],[4],[5] California also withholds State Disability Insurance.[6]

Result for the example in Texas: $1,690.85 take-home from $2,000.00 gross.

What the estimate leaves out

  • Pre-tax benefits: 401(k) contributions, health insurance premiums, HSA and FSA amounts lower your taxable wages. Enter your pay after those amounts for a closer federal figure; Social Security and Medicare will then be slightly understated for a 401(k).
  • Local taxes and any deduction that is not a tax (garnishments, union dues, loan repayments).
  • Bonuses and other supplemental pay, which employers may tax with a separate flat-rate method.
  • Forms W-4 from 2019 or earlier (with allowances), employees who claim to be exempt, and nonresident aliens, who have their own rules in Publication 15-T.
  • Extra state withholding you asked for on the state form (an additional amount on Form DE 4, IL-W-4 or NC-4): add it to the state line yourself.
  • The deductions for tips and overtime claimed on the tax return.
  • Rounding: we keep cents. The IRS lets employers round withholding to whole dollars, so a real pay stub can differ by up to 50 cents per line.

For employers

Hiring someone costs more than the wage. On top of gross pay an employer owes its own 6.2% Social Security and 1.45% Medicare, federal unemployment tax (FUTA) on the first $7,000 of each employee’s wages and state unemployment tax. Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

How much tax is taken out of a paycheck?

It depends on your pay, your Form W-4 and your state. Every paycheck has 6.2% Social Security and 1.45% Medicare withheld, plus federal income tax worked out from IRS Publication 15-T. For example, on $2,000.00 paid every two weeks to a single filer in Texas in 2026, $156.15 federal income tax, $124.00 Social Security and $29.00 Medicare are withheld, leaving $1,690.85.[1],[2]

How do you calculate net pay?

Start from gross pay for the pay period and subtract federal income tax withholding, Social Security, Medicare, any state income tax or state disability insurance, and then any other deductions your employer takes. This calculator does the tax part; benefits and other deductions are not included.[1],[2]

What is FICA tax?

FICA is the pair of federal payroll taxes for Social Security and Medicare. In 2026 the employee pays 6.2% for Social Security on wages up to $184,500 and 1.45% for Medicare on all wages, 7.65% in total below the wage base. The employer pays the same amounts again.[2]

What is the Social Security tax rate in 2026?

6.2% for the employee and 6.2% for the employer, on wages up to the wage base limit of $184,500 for 2026. Wages above that limit are not subject to Social Security tax for the rest of the year.[2]

What is the Medicare tax rate?

1.45% for the employee and 1.45% for the employer, with no wage limit. An employer must also withhold 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax on wages above $200,000 in a calendar year; that extra part is paid only by the employee.[2]

Can I use the calculator for hourly pay?

Yes. Choose “Hourly rate”, enter the rate and the hours in the pay period (for example 80 for two weeks) and the calculator multiplies them to get gross pay. Overtime is not worked out separately: add it at its own rate or enter total pay for the period.[1]
