01Chapter one

Why a strike need not ruin your trip

Italy is one of the finest countries in the world to see by train. It is also a country where transport strikes are common. The good news is in the details: they are announced in advance, they follow rules, and they are published.

Picture two travellers with the same ticket, the same train, the same strike.

The first finds out on the morning of the trip. The departure board shows a line of cancellations, the queue at the ticket office runs back to the entrance, the coaches are full and the taxis are expensive. The whole day is spent on the problem.

The second found out eleven days earlier, sitting at home. They read who was striking and for how many hours, saw that their train was not in the guaranteed list, and moved the transfer to the day before, swapping it with a day of sightseeing. On the strike day they walked to a museum.

The difference between them is not luck. It is information that is public, free and, once you know where to look, quick to read. Italian law requires strikes in public transport to be announced at least 10 days in advance, and every announced strike is listed in a register run by the Ministry of Infrastructure and Transport. The rules about what keeps running are published too.

This guide turns those rules into decisions. It gives you three things:

  1. The rules: what keeps running during a strike, from official sources, in plain English.
  2. Your rights: refunds, re-routing and compensation for trains and flights, quoted from the official EU pages.
  3. A method: a simple way to build a trip that absorbs one strike day, with six solved situations and three ready-made itineraries.

What this guide is not

It is not a list of museums and restaurants: there are excellent guides for that. It does not predict strikes, and it never tells you that a strike "will" or "won't" happen: only the register and the transport companies can say that. And it is not legal advice: where your rights are concerned, we quote the official sources and send you to them.

A word on the numbers

When we give a time window, a deadline or an amount, it comes from an official page that we read, and the source is printed right under it. When something depends on the company, the city or the day, we say so, and we tell you where to check. If a rule has changed since this edition, the official page wins.

02Chapter two

How strikes work in Italy

Eight rules explain almost everything you will see on a departure board on a strike day.

1. At least 10 days' notice

Strikes in "essential public services", which include trains, flights, local public transport and ferries, are regulated by Law 146 of 12 June 1990. The notice period "cannot be less than ten days" (quotations from Italian texts in this guide are our translation). This is the single most useful fact for a traveller: if a strike has not been announced 10 days before your travel day, it is unlikely to happen on that day.

Unlikely, not impossible. The same law says that the notice rule does not apply to strikes "in defence of the constitutional order" or in protest at "serious events" harming workers' safety. These are rare, but they exist, and they are the reason this guide always tells you to check again close to the date.

Source: Law 146/1990, article 2, paragraphs 5 and 7 (Normattiva), checked 10 October 2026.

2. Five days before, the companies must tell you what runs

The law also obliges transport companies to inform passengers "at least five days before the start of the strike" of how and when services will run during the strike. In practice, this is when you will see notices, lists of guaranteed trains and flights, and updated timetables on the companies' websites. Five days before your trip is a good moment for a second, more detailed look.

Source: Law 146/1990, article 2, paragraph 6 (Normattiva), checked 10 October 2026.

3. Minimum services are guaranteed

A strike in these sectors never stops everything. The law requires minimum services, defined in agreements and rules assessed by an independent authority, the Commissione di garanzia (the strike guarantee commission). For a traveller this means:

  • regional trains in set time windows and a list of guaranteed long-distance trains (chapters 4 and 5);
  • flights in protected time windows and a list of guaranteed flights (chapter 7);
  • city buses, metro and trams in time windows set by each company (chapter 8).

4. Every strike is in a public register

The Ministry of Infrastructure and Transport (MIT) publishes every announced transport strike in its register at scioperi.mit.gov.it, with the date, sector, area, the staff involved, the unions and the hours. The register also has a search page with past strikes going back to 2014, each marked as carried out or called off. Chapter 3 shows you how to read it.

5. Who strikes matters more than anything

"A rail strike" can mean the staff of one company in one region for a few hours, or every railway worker in Italy for a whole day. "An air transport strike" can mean the cabin crew of one airline, the ground staff of one airport, or air traffic controllers. The register says exactly who is striking, and that tells you whether your train or flight is involved. Most strikes in the register are local and will not affect you at all.

6. Strikes can be called off

Strikes are sometimes called off (revocato), postponed (differito) or shortened, sometimes only a few days before. The register is updated when this happens. On our free site, a strike that disappears from the register before its date is flagged as "no longer in the register", and one marked as called off is shown as such.

04Chapter four

Trains: what still runs

During a rail strike, some trains run by law. Knowing which ones is the difference between a lost day and a slightly different day.

Trenitalia regional trains

Regional trains are the slow, unreserved trains that link towns and cities within a region (and some between regions). Trenitalia guarantees essential regional services in the busiest hours of the day:

Guaranteed windows
Weekdays06:00 – 09:00  and  18:00 – 21:00
Public holidays07:00 – 10:00  and  18:00 – 21:00

Source: Trenitalia, "Treni garantiti in caso di sciopero" (Italian page) and "In case of strike" (English page), checked 10 October 2026. The English page gives the weekday windows as "Mon-Sat".

Inside these windows, the regional trains listed by Trenitalia run. Outside them, a regional train may still run if enough staff are working, but nobody can promise it in advance. If you need to move by regional train on a strike day, aim for the windows, and build in a margin: a train that departs at 08:50 is inside the window, one at 09:10 is not.

Trenitalia long-distance trains

For long-distance trains (the high-speed Frecce, Intercity and night trains), Trenitalia publishes a list of trains that are guaranteed during strikes, on weekdays and holidays alike. The list for the current timetable has {{TAB_NA}} trains. It covers the whole day, not just the time windows, and it is the most useful document a train traveller can have on a strike day. Chapter 5 shows you how to read it, with real rows from it.

The one-hour rule

What about a train that is already moving when the strike begins? Trenitalia's rule: trains that are travelling when the strike starts, if they were not cancelled at their departure station, normally reach their final destination if it can be reached within one hour of the start of the strike. After that hour, a train may stop at an intermediate station.

Source: Trenitalia, "In case of strike", checked 10 October 2026.

What this means in practice

A strike starts at 21:00. Your train is due at 21:40: you will normally get there. Your train is due at 23:00: you may be left at an intermediate station late in the evening.

Rule of thumb: on the evening before a national rail strike, choose a train that arrives before the strike starts, or within the hour after.

Trenord (Lombardy, including Malpensa Express)

Trenord runs the regional trains of Lombardy, including the Malpensa Express to Milan's main airport. Its guaranteed windows are the same:

Guaranteed windows
Weekdays and Saturdays06:00 – 09:00  and  18:00 – 21:00
Public holidays07:00 – 10:00  and  18:00 – 21:00

Trenord also guarantees the EuroCity trains EC82 and EC83 between the Brenner Pass and Bologna (extended to Ancona in summer) on strike days. If a strike stopped you from travelling, Trenord says you can ask for a refund of your ticket within 30 days of the strike day.

Source: Trenord, "In case of strike action" (English page), checked 10 October 2026.

Italo

Italo is a separate, private high-speed operator. It does not have a permanent strike page: before each strike involving its staff, it publishes a notice and a list of guaranteed trains on its home page, italotreno.com. Check it a few days before you travel; by law, the notice must be out at least five days before the strike. Note that Italo's conditions treat a train it marks "not guaranteed" differently from a cancelled one (chapter 6): if yours is not guaranteed, act early.

Other rail companies

Several regions have their own railway companies (in Campania, Apulia, Sardinia and elsewhere) and some local lines are run by city transport companies. They have their own guaranteed services, often listed under local public transport in the register. The register tells you which company is striking; that company's website has the details.

Five rules for strike days

  1. Check the start and end times. National rail strikes often begin at 21:00 the evening before, and Trenitalia warns that service can change just before and after the strike hours.
  2. Use the windows. A regional train at 07:30 is far more likely to run than one at 11:00.
  3. Look up your train number in the guaranteed list (chapter 5).
  4. Do not build plans on "maybe". Trains outside the lists may run, but nobody can promise it.
  5. Keep your ticket and booking confirmation. You will need them for refunds (chapter 6).

Trenitalia's list of guaranteed long-distance trains is a plain table in Italian. Here is how to find it, how to read it, and what it looked like for the timetable running until December 2026.

Where to find it

Go to Trenitalia's Italian page "Treni garantiti in caso di sciopero" (trenitalia.com, treni garantiti). The English version of the page says "See Italian version for details on guaranteed trains", so use the Italian one; your browser can translate the text around the tables.   • Tabella B: a shorter list ({{TAB_NB}} trains) that applies only to a national general strike for the renewal of the national labour contract held on a public holiday, lasting no more than 24 hours and starting at 21:00 the day before.

The lists change with each timetable (Italian railways change timetable in June and December), so always open the current one.

06Chapter six

Your train is cancelled: what now

EU law gives rail passengers clear rights when a train is cancelled or badly delayed. A strike by the railway's own staff does not take those rights away.

The key fact

Under EU rules, "strikes by rail company staff are not considered as extraordinary circumstances".

In plain words: your right to a refund or re-routing applies whatever the cause, and if your train is cancelled or delayed because the railway's own staff are on strike, you may also be entitled to compensation. Check the company's own strike rules too (see Italo below).

Source: European Commission, Your Europe, "Rail passenger rights" (Regulation (EU) 2021/782), checked 10 October 2026.

If your train is cancelled

If a cancellation means you would reach your final destination more than 60 minutes late, you can choose one of three options:

  • a refund of your ticket within 30 days (in full, or for the part of the journey you did not make), plus, where relevant, a return journey to your starting point;
  • continuing or re-routing to your destination under comparable conditions at the earliest opportunity, at no extra cost;
  • re-routing at a later date of your choice, under comparable conditions, at no extra cost.

Help while you wait

  • Meals and refreshments, in proportion to the waiting time.
  • A hotel or other accommodation, where physically possible, if you have to stay overnight because of the cancellation, plus transport between the station and the hotel.

Making your own arrangements

On request, the railway may let you organise your own alternative transport (another rail company, a coach) and reimburse you. And if the railway does not tell you about re-routing options within 100 minutes of the scheduled departure of the cancelled train, you can make your own arrangements with other public transport (rail, coach or bus) without its agreement. The company must then reimburse "necessary, appropriate and reasonable" costs.

Collect your proof

Take a photo of the departure board showing the cancellation. Keep tickets and receipts for meals, hotels and alternative transport. Note the time the train was due and the time you were told about re-routing. This is what you attach to your claim.

Compensation

Delay at arrivalCompensation
1 to 2 hours25% of the ticket price
2 hours or more50% of the ticket price

The railway must pay within one month of your request. You will not receive compensation if:

  • you were told about the delay or cancellation before you bought your ticket;
  • the extra delay with re-routing is less than 1 hour;
  • you chose a refund of your ticket.

The trap on strike days

Strikes are announced at least 10 days ahead. If you buy a ticket for a train that has already been announced as cancelled, you lose the right to compensation for that delay. Check the register and the company's notices before you buy.

Source: European Commission, Your Europe, "Rail passenger rights", checked 10 October 2026. National rules or company conditions may be more favourable.

How to claim

  • Trenitalia, before the strike: its strike notices let you give up the trip and get a refund up to the departure of the booked train (Frecce, Intercity) or until midnight on the day before the strike (regional trains), or change to another train. Read the notice for your strike on Trenitalia's guaranteed-trains page.
  • Trenitalia, after a delay or cancellation: the "Compensation for delays and refund" section of trenitalia.com, or the call centre on 892021 from Italy.
  • Trenord: a refund within 30 days of the strike day, through its refunds page.
  • Italo: through italotreno.com or its customer service.
  • If you are not satisfied with the answer, each EU country has a national enforcement body, and the European Consumer Centre in your country can help with cross-border problems.

Getting there anyway

  • Long-distance coaches (for example FlixBus and Itabus) are not part of rail strikes. On national strike days they fill up fast: book as soon as the strike is announced.
  • Another rail company: a Trenitalia strike does not always involve Italo, and vice versa. The register says who is striking.
  • A rental car for one day can save a connection; read chapter 9 about ZTL zones before driving into a city centre.
  • One more night: if your transfer day falls on a national strike, moving it by a day is often simpler and cheaper than any alternative.
07Chapter seven

Flights and airports

Air transport strikes in Italy come in different shapes. Who is striking decides what happens to your flight, and what you can claim.

Who can strike

  • Airline staff (pilots, cabin crew) of one airline: only that airline is involved.
  • Air traffic control (ENAV and related companies): can affect many airlines at the airports or areas involved.
  • Airport staff: ground handling, security, the airport operator. Usually one airport or a group of airports.

Protected time windows

During strikes there are protected time windows, from 07:00 to 10:00 and from 18:00 to 21:00, in which flights must be operated anyway.

Source: ENAC (Italian Civil Aviation Authority), "Voli garantiti in caso di sciopero", checked 10 October 2026.

Air strikes need longer notice than other sectors: at least twelve days (chapter 2). On top of the windows, ENAC publishes for each strike a list of guaranteed flights, such as essential connections to the islands. Your airline decides which other flights to cancel and must tell you.

Your rights if your flight is cancelled

Under EU rules (Regulation 261/2004), if your flight is cancelled you can choose between:

  • reimbursement of your ticket (and, if you had a connection, a return flight to your first departure airport);
  • re-routing to your final destination at the earliest opportunity; or
  • re-routing at a later date of your choice, subject to seat availability.

You are also entitled to assistance at the airport: meals and refreshments, and a hotel if you need to stay overnight.

Compensation: it depends on who strikes

If you are told about the cancellation less than 14 days before departure, you may be entitled to compensation:

Flight distanceCompensation
1,500 km or less€250
More than 1,500 km within the EU, and other flights between 1,500 and 3,500 km€400
More than 3,500 km€600
  • Strikes by the airline's own staff are not considered extraordinary circumstances: compensation can be due.
  • Strikes outside the airline (for example air traffic control or airport staff) "may be considered as extraordinary circumstances". To avoid paying, the airline must prove the link with the strike and that the cancellation could not have been avoided with all reasonable measures.

There are exceptions when you are told 7 to 14 days, or less than 7 days, before departure and offered a re-routing close to your original times, and compensation may be halved if you are re-routed: see the official page for the details.

Source: European Commission, Your Europe, "Air passenger rights", checked 10 October 2026.

On a strike day at the airport

  1. Check your flight with your airline the day before and on the morning of the flight.
  2. A flight inside a protected window (07:00 to 10:00, 18:00 to 21:00) is much more likely to operate.
  3. Check the ground transport too. A rail or local transport strike on the same day can make getting to the airport the real problem.
  4. If your flight is cancelled, ask the airline in writing for re-routing or a refund, and keep every receipt.

Getting to the airport when the trains stop

Airport trains, such as the Leonardo Express in Rome and the Malpensa Express in Milan, are part of rail strikes. Your options: a train inside a guaranteed window, an airport coach (check whether its company is in the register), a taxi or a pre-booked transfer. Allow extra time: on national strike days the roads are busier.

Buses, metro, trams and taxis

Local transport strikes are the most frequent and the most local. They matter only if you are in that city, on that day, in those hours.

Four hours or twenty-four

Local strikes often last 4 hours or 24 hours; the register gives the exact hours. For a 24-hour strike, each company publishes the time windows in which its service is guaranteed. The windows differ from city to city, so the company's own notice is the only reliable source. For shorter strikes, read the hours carefully and plan around them.

Taxis

Taxis are not part of bus and metro strikes, but demand rises sharply on those days: book through an app or by phone early, or ask your hotel. Taxi strikes are rarer and appear in the register under "Taxi".

  • Cross the city inside the guaranteed windows, and spend the strike hours in one neighbourhood.
  • Choose sights within walking distance of where you sleep. Italian historic centres are compact: a strike day is a good day to walk.
  • For the airport or the station, book a taxi or a transfer in advance.
09Chapter nine

Driving: ZTL zones and fines

If a strike pushes you into a rental car, the biggest risk is not the traffic. It is the ZTL.

A ZTL (zona a traffico limitato, limited traffic zone) is an area, usually a historic centre, where only authorised vehicles may enter at certain times. Cameras at the entry gates record every number plate.

How much a fine costs

  • Driving into a ZTL, a pedestrian area or a bus lane without a permit costs from €83 to €332 (Italian Highway Code, article 7, paragraph 14).
  • If you pay within 60 days of the notice, you pay the minimum. If you pay within 5 days, it is reduced by 30% (article 202), plus the notification costs written on the notice.

When the fine arrives

  • For people resident abroad, the notice must be served within 360 days of the violation being recorded (article 201).
  • With a rental car, the police contact the rental company first, and the company gives them your details. Rental companies usually charge their own administration fee for this: check your contract.

Source: Italian Highway Code (Legislative Decree 285/1992), articles 7, 201 and 202, text on Normattiva checked 10 October 2026.

How to avoid it

  • Look for the round sign with a red border and the words "Zona Traffico Limitato", and for the display at the gate: "varco attivo" means the cameras are on, do not enter; "varco non attivo" means the gate is open at that moment.
  • If your hotel is inside a ZTL, contact it before you arrive: in many cities hotels can register your plate.
  • Park outside the centre and walk or take public transport. Navigation apps do not always warn you about ZTLs.
  • Hours, zones and permits are set by each city and change over time: check the city's official website.

Motorways on strike days

Strikes of motorway services (toll booths, roadside assistance) appear in the register under "Circolazione e sicurezza stradale". Check the hours if you have a long drive that day.

The strike-proof method

You cannot know today whether there will be a strike on your travel day in six months. You can build a trip that survives one.

This is our method. It is not an official rule: it is common sense built on the rules in the previous chapters. It takes about twenty minutes when you plan, and five minutes per travel day afterwards.

Step 1. Split your trip into moving days and staying days

Write your itinerary as a list of days and mark each one M (you move between cities, or to or from an airport) or S (you stay in one place). A strike can hurt you badly only on an M day; on an S day it is an inconvenience. Most trips have three to five M days, and those are the only days you need to protect.

Step 2. Protect the M days

  • Keep M days off Fridays when you can. In our snapshot of the register, {{SNAP_VEN}} of {{SNAP_N}} strikes started on a Friday. A Friday is not forbidden; it is just a little riskier.
  • Put an S day next to every long transfer. If a strike lands on the M day, you swap them.
  • Travel in the morning. Morning trains fall more often inside the guaranteed windows, and if something goes wrong you still have the day to fix it.
  • Sleep in your departure city the night before you fly home. Never put a long train journey and an international flight on the same day.

Step 3. Book flexible where it matters

For long-distance trains on M days, a fare you can change or refund costs a little more, but it turns a strike into a simple change of train. For short regional trips and S-day excursions, cheap tickets are fine.

Step 4. Check at 14, 10, 5, 3 and 1 days

  • 14 days before each M day: look at the register for that date, area and sector.
  • 10 days before: the legal notice period has begun. A strike not announced by now is unlikely, though not impossible.
  • 5 days before: by law, companies must have published how services will run. Look for your train in the guaranteed lists.
  • 3 days before: decide. Keep the plan, move the M day, or book a backup.
  • The day before: check your train or flight status, and whether the strike has been called off.

  1. Read who is striking and the hours (chapter 3). Many strikes will not touch you.
  2. If your train is in a guaranteed list or your journey fits a guaranteed window, keep it.
  3. If not, move the M day: travel a day earlier or later, swapping it with an S day.
  4. If you cannot move it, book a backup: a coach, another rail company, a transfer.
  5. Keep every document for refunds (chapter 6).

If you remember only one rule

Keep one buffer day before your flight home, in the city of your departure airport. It absorbs any strike, delay or cancellation in the last part of the trip.

11Chapter eleven

Six situations, solved

Rules are easier to use when you have seen them applied. These are typical situations, built on real types of entries in the register, solved with the rules in this guide.

Situation 1A national rail strike on your Rome to Florence day

The facts. You planned to take a mid-morning train from Rome to Florence on a Friday. Twelve days before, a national general strike appears in the register for that Friday, with trains involved from 21:00 on the day before to 20:59 on the day itself.

What to do. Check whether your train number is in Tabella A. If it is, keep it, and check its status the day before. If it is not, you have these options: travel on Thursday, before 21:00, swapping the day with your last day in Rome; only if your exact regional trains are on Trenitalia's guaranteed lists for Lazio and Tuscany, travel by regional train inside a window (a Rome to Florence trip by regional train is long and may need a change); or book a coach early. If you had a flexible fare, change it now, while there is choice.

Why it works. You acted twelve days ahead, with the whole range of trains and coaches still on sale.

Situation 2Your evening train crosses the start of a strike

The facts. A rail strike starts at 21:00. You have a ticket for a train leaving at 19:15 and arriving at 22:35.

What to do. Under Trenitalia's one-hour rule, a train already travelling when the strike begins normally reaches its destination only if it can do so within one hour of the start. 22:35 is more than an hour after 21:00, so your train may stop at an intermediate station. Change to an earlier train that arrives before 22:00, or move the journey.

Situation 3An airline crew strike on your flight day

The facts. The register shows a 24-hour strike of one airline's flight crew (personale navigante) on the day you fly that airline.

What to do. Check whether your flight is in a protected window (07:00 to 10:00 or 18:00 to 21:00) and watch for messages from the airline. If your flight is cancelled, choose a refund or re-routing, and claim assistance at the airport. Because the strike is by the airline's own staff, it is not an extraordinary circumstance: if you were told less than 14 days before departure, compensation can be due.

Situation 4An air traffic control strike instead

The facts. Same day, same flight, but the strike is by air traffic control staff (ENAV) at your departure airport.

What to do. Your rights to a refund or re-routing, and to assistance, are the same. Compensation is different: a strike outside the airline "may be considered" an extraordinary circumstance, so the airline may not owe it, if it can prove the link with the strike and that the cancellation could not have been avoided. Check ENAC's list of guaranteed flights for that strike.

Situation 5A bus and metro strike in Milan on your last day

The facts. You fly home from Malpensa at 15:00. The register shows a 24-hour strike of ATM, Milan's city transport company, that day.

What to do. Read who is striking: ATM runs Milan's buses, trams and metro, not the Malpensa Express, which is a Trenord train. Unless Trenord is also in the register, the airport train is not part of this strike; the problem is getting to the station where you board it. Check ATM's guaranteed windows, walk or take a taxi to the station, and leave early: on strike days taxis are in demand.

Situation 6You bought your ticket after the strike was announced

The facts. You booked a train for a date when a strike was already in the register, without checking. The train is cancelled.

What to do. Ask the company about a refund or another train, and check its conditions. If the train is cancelled, a refund or re-routing still applies. Compensation is another matter: under EU rules none is due if you were informed of the delay or cancellation before buying, and the railway may refuse it on that ground. The lesson costs nothing next time: check the register before you buy.

13Chapter thirteen

Strike morning: the 10-minute routine

It is the morning of a strike and you have to move. Make a coffee and do these six things, in this order.

  1. Confirm the strike is still on (1 minute). Look at the register or our daily page for today. Strikes are sometimes called off at the last moment.
  2. Re-read the hours and who is striking (1 minute). Is it your company? Does your journey fall inside the strike hours, or inside a guaranteed window?
  3. Check your train or flight by number (2 minutes). Use the company's app or website. For trains, also look for the number in the guaranteed list.
  4. Check how you get to the station or airport (2 minutes). A local transport strike can be the real obstacle. Decide now: walk, taxi or transfer.
  5. Prepare plan B (2 minutes). Have the next guaranteed train, a coach, or the extra night in mind, with prices. Do not book it yet unless your option has already gone.
  6. Charge your phone and take screenshots (2 minutes). Ticket, booking, and the company's strike notice. If your train is cancelled, photograph the departure board.

At the station

Look at the departure board first: cancelled trains show "soppresso" or "cancellato". Then ask at the ticket office or a staff desk about re-routing, and keep the answer in writing if you can (a printed ticket, an email, a note on your booking).

14Chapter fourteen

Practical Italy

A few things that are not about strikes but make every travel day easier: the codice fiscale, staying connected, insurance, regional tickets and the emergency number.

The codice fiscale

The codice fiscale is the Italian tax identification number: 16 letters and digits. The Italian Revenue Agency describes it as the means of identifying foreign citizens "in their relations with public authorities and other administrations". A short holiday does not need one. If you are moving to Italy, renting long-term or working there, you will.

How to get it

  • Living abroad: apply to the Italian consulate in your country of residence.
  • In Italy, non-EU citizens: the Single Desk for Immigration issues it to people entering for work or family reunification; the police headquarters (Questura) when you apply for or renew a residence permit.
  • Otherwise: at an office of the Agenzia delle Entrate with a valid ID. For a first codice fiscale you must book an in-person appointment.
  • EU citizens: any Agenzia delle Entrate office, with an ID card or passport.

Source: Agenzia delle Entrate, "Tax identification number for foreign citizens" (English page), checked 10 October 2026.

Our free calculator (italy-strikes-today.netlify.app/codice-fiscale-calculator) shows the code that normally matches your data, using the official country and town codes. Only the Agenzia delle Entrate can assign the official one.

Stay connected

On strike days, information changes by the hour: company websites, the register, airline messages. Mobile data is what lets you re-plan from a platform. If you come from outside the EU, a local SIM or an eSIM bought before you land gives you data from the airport onwards. Check that your phone supports eSIM before buying one.

Travel insurance

EU passenger rights cover refunds, re-routing and assistance (chapters 6 and 7), but not everything: a prepaid hotel you cannot reach, a tour you miss, a medical problem. Travel insurance can cover some of these; what it covers depends entirely on the policy. Read the terms about strikes and delays before you buy.

One option we link to on our site is SafetyWing travel medical insurance (affiliate link). Check what the plan covers, and whether it covers strikes and delays, before buying.

Regional tickets: validate before you board

Paper tickets for regional trains "must be validated before the departure of the train" in the machines at the station where your journey begins, and the journey must be completed within four hours of validation (some regional fares have exceptions). Electronic regional tickets bought online are already validated or activate automatically for the train you chose.

Source: Trenitalia, "Travelling on regional trains" (English page), checked 10 October 2026.

Emergency number

112 is the European emergency number, valid in Italy and across the EU, free of charge.

Source: European Commission, "112" policy page, checked 10 October 2026.

15Chapter fifteen

Questions travellers ask

Short answers, with the chapter where the full answer lives.

Will my train run?

Nobody can promise it in advance unless it is in a guaranteed list or runs inside a guaranteed window. Check your train number in Tabella A, and the regional windows (chapters 4 and 5).

How far ahead are strikes announced?

At least 10 days, by law. In our snapshot, strikes were announced between {{SNAP_MIN}} and {{SNAP_MAX}} days ahead. The law allows rare exceptions without notice (chapter 2).

Can a strike be called off at the last minute?

Yes. Strikes are sometimes called off, postponed or shortened, even a few days before. Check again the day before you travel (chapter 2).

Are high-speed trains affected?

It depends on who strikes. The Frecce are Trenitalia trains; Italo is a separate company. A strike of Trenitalia staff does not automatically involve Italo, and the other way round (chapters 4 and 5).

Is there a "strike season"?

{{FAQ_FRANCHIGIE}} In our snapshot, Friday was the most common starting day.

What is a general strike, and does it stop transport?

A sciopero generale involves many sectors at once. Transport is included unless the notes exclude it, and each sector may have its own hours: read the "Modalità" field (chapter 3).

Will a rail strike cancel my flight?

No, flights are a different sector. But it can make getting to the airport harder, especially by airport train (chapter 7).

Do taxis work during a bus and metro strike?

Yes, unless there is a separate taxi strike in the register. Demand is much higher, so book early (chapter 8).

Do I get my money back if my train is cancelled?

Under EU rules you can choose a refund or re-routing when a cancellation would make you more than an hour late, and you may be owed compensation. A strike by the railway's own staff does not remove these rights (chapter 6).

I have a regional ticket without a seat. What happens?

Only the regional trains on the guaranteed lists are sure to run, inside the windows. Act fast on refunds: Trenitalia's strike notices let you give up a regional trip for a refund only until midnight on the day before the strike, while Trenord accepts requests within 30 days of the strike (chapters 4 and 6).

Can I see past strikes?

Yes. The MIT register has a search page with strikes going back to 2014, each marked as carried out or called off (chapter 2).

Where can I check today's strikes in English?

On our free site, italy-strikes-today.netlify.app, updated twice a day from the official register.

Will this guide be updated?

Rules change. If we publish a new edition, buyers of this edition get it free: we send it to the email address used for the purchase. For today's strikes, the daily pages on our site are always free.
