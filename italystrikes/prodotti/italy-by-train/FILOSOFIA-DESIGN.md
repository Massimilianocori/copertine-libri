# Filosofia visiva: "Tabellone"

*(Design philosophy for the cover and the interior of the guide. Written in English as the canvas-design brief; a short Italian note is at the end.)*

**Tabellone** is the art of the departure board: a dark field where information arrives one flap at a time, and where every character is a small, physical object. The movement treats a timetable as a quiet monument. Order is the subject. A grid of rows, a column of times, a column of places, a column of verdicts: the eye reads the structure before it reads a single word, and the structure itself says *this has been checked*.

Space is organised like a station hall. Above, a calm, generous zone for a few large words set in a high-contrast serif, the way a station's name is carved over the entrance. Below, the board: a disciplined block of tiles, each glyph sitting in its own rounded cell, split by a hairline across the middle as real split-flap modules are. The rhythm comes from repetition, identical tiles, identical gutters, identical row heights, so that a single change of colour in one cell carries enormous weight. Every gutter and every baseline is measured, adjusted and measured again: the result must look meticulously crafted, the product of painstaking attention by someone at the top of the field.

Colour is limited and functional. A deep, near-black ink for the board; a warm paper white for the characters; one signal red, used sparingly, for the single word that matters; and a muted amber reserved for status. Nothing is decorative: colour appears only where it changes meaning. Texture is avoided; precision is the texture. Edges are crisp, corners consistently radiused, tones flat and calm.

Typography is plural but strict: a monospaced face for anything a machine would print (times, numbers, codes), a refined serif for the human voice (titles, the few sentences), and a clean grotesque for small labels. Text is sparse and essential. The words that appear on the board are real words from real boards, never invented filler; their authenticity is the hidden reference that a traveller will recognise without being told. Typographic decisions are master-level execution: optical kerning on large caps, tabular figures in the columns, nothing touching, nothing falling off the edge.

Inside the book, the same law continues with lower volume: chapters open like a new platform announcement, a small numbered tile and a large serif title, then calm reading pages with wide margins and a single red accent for what must not be missed. The whole object should feel labored over for countless hours, a reference you trust because every alignment has been considered.
