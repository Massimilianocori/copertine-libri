A pass rate is not a rating of a school. It shows how that school’s past students did on the exam. Schools enroll different students, and with few exams the percentage is mostly chance. We publish the official figures as they are and do not rank or recommend schools.

How to read the Hawaii figures

The Hawaii Real Estate Commission publishes pass and fail counts by prelicense school in its quarterly “School Files” bulletin. This page uses the one-year table of the October 2026 issue: October 1, 2025 to September 30, 2026.[1]

The exam has a national part and a state part, counted separately. The bulletin counts exams taken (“Tested”, “Passed”, “Failed”): it does not separate first attempts from retakes, so these rates cannot be compared with first-attempt rates in other states.

The top row is the bulletin’s own total for all salesperson exams: 721 of 1,986 national parts passed (36.3%) and 712 of 2,300 state parts (30.9%). That total also includes 29 national-part exams by candidates who are not attributed to a school (prelicense equivalency, uniform-section equivalency, license restoration, unknown school); those rows are not in the table below.[1]

The bulletin cuts percentages to one decimal instead of rounding (4 passed out of 6 is printed as 66.6%); we show them as printed. Some schools appear twice, once for classroom and once for online courses, with different school codes: the code is under each name. The bulletin prints the same name for codes 1185 and 1188. Broker exam tables and the latest quarter alone are in the bulletin.

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

What is the pass rate for the Hawaii real estate exam?

From October 1, 2025 to September 30, 2026, 36.3% of the national parts of the salesperson exam were passed (721 of 1,986) and 30.9% of the state parts (712 of 2,300). The commission's bulletin counts all exams taken, retakes included.[1]

How do I compare real estate classes in Hawaii?

The commission's School Files bulletin lists 24 prelicense school codes with exams taken and passed, classroom and online courses separately. Only 10 of them had 30 or more national-part exams in the year; for the others one or two results change the percentage a lot.[1]

Does the Hawaii commission publish first-attempt pass rates?

No. Its bulletin counts exams tested, passed and failed for each school, without separating first attempts from retakes. So the Hawaii percentages cannot be set next to first-attempt rates from other states: the two are not the same measure.[1]
