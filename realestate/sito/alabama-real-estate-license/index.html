How to get a Alabama real estate license

The steps, hours, deadlines and official fees for a Alabama real estate salesperson license, taken from the commission’s own pages and quoted word for word.

This page covers a first salesperson license for someone who is not licensed in another state. Every step and figure comes from the commission; where its pages do not give a figure, we say so instead of guessing.

The steps

  1. Check that you qualify. You must be at least 19, a U.S. citizen, permanent resident alien or legally present in the U.S., a resident of any U.S. state, and a high school graduate or the equivalent. A felony or a crime involving moral turpitude, or a real estate license rejected or revoked in any state in the past two years, stands in the way.[1]
  2. Complete the 60 clock hour prelicense course with an approved school.[1]
  3. Pass the exam within 6 months of finishing the course. The exam is scheduled with Pearson.[1],[2]
  4. Apply within 90 days of the exam date, with the fees, and have your fingerprints taken for the background check.[3]
  5. Complete the 30 hour post license course within the first twelve months to receive the original (permanent) license.[1]

What the commission says, word for word

“Prior to taking the state exam, successfully complete an approved 60 clock hour prelicense course. Applicants have 6 months to pass the state exam after completing the course. If these deadlines are not met, the course must be retaken.”

Alabama Real Estate Commission, Become a Licensed Real Estate Professional[1]

“Must complete a 30 hour post license training course within the first twelve months of licensure in order to be issued an original (permanent) license.”

Alabama Real Estate Commission, Become a Licensed Real Estate Professional[1]

“The completed applications, all additional materials and fees MUST be complete and submitted to the Alabama Real Estate Commission within 90 days of your examination date.”

Official fees

The commission’s pages that we read do not print the license fee or the exam fee. They say:

“Fees vary depending upon the license-type you are seeking. Refer to the license application for an accurate accounting of the fees owed.”

Alabama Real Estate Commission, License Application Instructions[3]

So we do not give a figure here. The fee is on the license application you receive after passing the exam; the exam fee is on the exam company’s site.

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

What are the requirements for an Alabama real estate license?

For a salesperson license: be at least 19, a U.S. citizen, permanent resident alien or legally present in the U.S., a resident of any U.S. state and a high school graduate or equivalent; complete an approved 60 clock hour prelicense course; pass the exam; and apply with a fingerprint background check.[1],[3]

How do I get a real estate license in Alabama?

Complete the 60-hour prelicense course, pass the exam within 6 months of finishing it, and send the complete application with fees within 90 days of your exam date. Then complete the 30-hour post license course within the first twelve months.[1],[3]

How much does an Alabama real estate license cost?

The commission's pages we read do not print the amount: they say fees vary with the license type and refer you to the license application. We do not publish a figure we could not read in an official source.[3]

Can I take the Alabama prelicense course online?

The commission's list of schools marks each one as classroom, online or both, and several approved schools are online. The application itself must be mailed or delivered to the commission's office, according to its instructions.[3],[4]

How long do I have to pass the Alabama exam after the course?

6 months. The commission says applicants have 6 months to pass the state exam after completing the course, and if the deadline is not met the course must be retaken.[1]
