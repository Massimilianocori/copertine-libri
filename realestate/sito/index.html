What you find here

Before the licensing exam you have to take a pre-licensing course, and the schools that sell it often advertise their own pass rates. Some state real estate commissions publish the real results: how many students of each school took the exam and how many passed. This site copies those official tables, state by state, and puts three things next to every percentage: the number of exams it is based on, the period it covers and the source.

We do not rank or recommend schools. When you sort a table by pass rate, schools with fewer than 30 exams are listed after the others, because with so few results the percentage is mostly chance.

Why the number of exams matters

A school with 4 students and 3 passes shows 75%; one more fail and it shows 60%. A school with 8,000 exams does not move like that. The Nebraska commission puts it plainly in its own report:

“The Commission would caution readers to be aware of the total number of students that are being tested from any school.”

Nebraska Real Estate Commission, License Examination Performance by School[5]

“percentages have less to tell when the number of students is quite small.”

Nebraska Real Estate Commission, License Examination Performance by School[5]

So every rate on this site has its count next to it, and rates based on fewer than 30 exams carry a “small sample” label.

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

What is the pass rate for the real estate exam?

It depends on the state and on what is counted. In Texas, 56.12% of first attempts at the sales agent exam passed (16,824 of 29,980) in the period December 1, 2023 to November 30, 2025. In Alabama, the school figures add up to 69.5% of first attempts over the previous 12 months (1,653 of 2,378), and 54.9% when retakes are counted (2,173 of 3,960).[1],[2]

How hard is the real estate exam?

Official results show that many people do not pass the first time. In Texas 13,156 of 29,980 first attempts failed in the two years covered. States let you retake the exam; in Texas three fails on either part mean more education before you can test again.[1],[6]

Is the real estate exam hard to pass on the first try?

In the states that separate first attempts, the first-try rate was 56.12% in Texas (16,824 of 29,980 exams) and 69.5% in Alabama (1,653 of 2,378, our sum of the commission's school figures). The rate differs a lot from school to school, and for small schools it is based on very few students.[1],[2]
