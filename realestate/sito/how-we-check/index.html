How we check

How we check every figure, and the sources

Official files kept on record, counts that must add up, and the number of exams next to every percentage.

People use a pass rate to choose where to spend several hundred dollars and weeks of study. So this site publishes only what a state real estate commission has published, keeps a copy of every official file, and never shows a percentage without the number of exams behind it.

How the figures get from the commission to the page

  • We download each official file (19 in all: the four data sources and the commissions’ licensing pages, fee schedules and exam information) with a program that identifies itself as a robot, and record its address, the date and a fingerprint (SHA-256).
  • Exam counts and percentages are read from the file by a program and copied as printed. The build stops if a percentage does not match its own counts (passed ÷ exams), if passed plus failed does not equal the exams taken, or if a total printed by the commission is not the sum of its rows.[1],[4]
  • For Nebraska we use only a quarterly report that the commission links from its own index page (“License Examination Performance by School”), never a file found in any other way. The report is a scan, so its numbers are typed by hand from the page images. Each row has to add up in three ways (passed + failed = graded; first-time takers + repeaters = total; the rows of a school = its total row) before the site is built, and the typed figures are tied to the fingerprint of that exact PDF.[3]
  • Every sentence in a quotation box is copied word for word and searched for in the downloaded file each time (62 sentences). If one is no longer there, the build stops.
  • A second program, written separately, reads the raw files again in its own way and compares every number on every page.

Small samples

Every rate has its count next to it. Below 30 exams a rate is labelled “small sample”, and when a table is sorted by pass rate those schools are listed after the others. 30 is our own threshold, not an official one: with 30 exams, one more pass or fail still moves the rate by more than three points.

Where a commission prints no statewide total (Alabama, Nebraska), the top row of the table is our sum of the school rows and is labelled as such; its percentage is our division, rounded to one decimal.

What we do not do

  • We do not rank, score or recommend schools, and we do not estimate figures a commission has not published.
  • We do not use pass rates advertised by schools, surveys or other websites.
  • We publish nothing about individual candidates: the sources contain only counts by school.
  • This is a four-state pilot. Other commissions publish school results too; some of their websites refuse our checking server or the files could not be found, and we do not try to get round such blocks. Those states are simply not here yet.
  • Broker exam results are in the official sources but not on these pages.

When we update

Texas publishes a two-year window, Alabama refreshes its page on the 10th of each month, Nebraska and Hawaii publish every quarter. A state page changes only when the new official file has been read and all the checks pass again; the period and the reading date are always shown at the top.

Report an error

Seen a figure that does not match the official publication, or run a school whose row is wrong? Tell us which page and, if you can, link the source: we check it and fix the page. The figures themselves come from the commission, so a correction to the official data has to be asked of the commission.

Please do not include your name, email address, exam results or other personal information: the form does not ask for them and we do not reply to messages.
