This page covers a first salesperson license for someone who is not licensed in another state. Every step and figure comes from the commission; where its pages do not give a figure, we say so instead of guessing.

The steps, from the commission’s flowchart

  1. Check the exam prerequisites. At least 18 years old on the exam date, a U.S. citizen, U.S. national or alien authorized to work in the United States, with a high school diploma or equivalent.[1]
  2. Complete the 60-hour Hawaii salesperson prelicensing course at a registered prelicense school. The completion certificate is valid for two years.[2]
  3. Book the exam with PSI, the company that gives it.[2],[3]
  4. Pass the exam. You receive a score report and information on how to apply.[2]
  5. Apply within two years of passing.[2]
  6. To work, join a brokerage. An active license requires being employed by or associated with an active Hawaii brokerage firm or sole proprietor.[2]

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

What are the requirements for a Hawaii real estate license?

To sit the exam you must have completed the 60-hour Hawaii prelicense course (or hold an equivalency), be a U.S. citizen, U.S. national or alien authorized to work in the United States, be at least 18 on the exam date and have a high school diploma or equivalent.[1],[2]

How do I get a real estate license in Hawaii?

Complete the 60-hour salesperson prelicensing course at a registered school, book and pass the exam with PSI, then submit a complete license application within two years of passing. To be active you must be associated with a Hawaii brokerage.[2],[3]

How much does a Hawaii real estate license cost?

The commission's fee page lists $282 for a salesperson license in an even-numbered year and $382 in an odd-numbered year. The exam fee and the price of the course are separate and are not on the commission's pages we read.[5]

Do I have to live in Hawaii to get a real estate license?

No. The commission's FAQ answers the question “Do I have to be a resident of Hawaii to secure a real estate license?” with “No”.[4]

What happens if I fail the Hawaii real estate exam?

The commission's FAQ says you must wait 24 hours before rescheduling an examination. The prelicense course certificate stays valid for two years from the date it was issued.[2],[4]
