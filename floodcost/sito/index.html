How this works

What the numbers are

FEMA publishes every NFIP policy, without names or addresses, in its OpenFEMA data. We take the policies in force on August 31, 2026, keep the total annual cost (premium plus the fees and surcharges in the bill) and compute medians by area, flood zone and building type. Areas or groups with fewer than 10 policies are not shown.

What they are not

They are not a quote and not an estimate for your home: they describe what other policyholders paid. Private flood insurance, which FEMA does not report, is not included. Read the full methodology.

Where these numbers come from

  • FEMA OpenFEMA – NFIP Redacted Policies (v3): policies in force on August 31, 2026 (and August 31, 2025 for the year-on-year change). FEMA updated the dataset on Sep 9, 2026.
  • FEMA OpenFEMA – NFIP Redacted Claims (v3): claims by year of loss since 1978. FEMA updated the dataset on Sep 9, 2026.

Retrieved from the OpenFEMA API on October 15, 2026. Check the raw records yourself: policies · claims (OpenFEMA API, JSON). How we count: methodology.

FloodCost

FloodCost shows what National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) policyholders actually pay, from FEMA's public policy and claims data. It is an independent site, not affiliated with FEMA, the NFIP or any insurer. These are past prices paid by other policyholders, not a quote. Your price depends on your building, its elevation and the coverage you choose. Private flood insurance is not included. Nothing here is insurance, financial or legal advice.

This product uses the FEMA OpenFEMA API, but is not endorsed by FEMA. The Federal Government or FEMA cannot vouch for the data or analyses derived from these data after the data have been retrieved from the Agency's website(s).
