California FAIR Plan policies and non-renewals by ZIP code

On September 30, 2026, the California FAIR Plan had 665,673 residential policies in force, up 7% in a year and 2.5 times as many as in 2022, according to its ZIP code report dated October 20, 2026. In 2023, the latest year with official data by ZIP code, 788,485 homeowners and dwelling policies of the voluntary market were not renewed or cancelled (the Department's "non-renewed/canceled" count, 75-80% initiated by policyholders, e.g. after a sale).

How to read these numbers

  • Non-renewals stop in 2023. The Department of Insurance file covers 2020–2023: nothing newer is published by ZIP code yet.
  • "Not renewed or cancelled" is the Department's "non-renewed/canceled" count: all policies of the voluntary market (ordinary insurers, FAIR Plan excluded) that ended without renewal or were cancelled, whoever asked. The Department writes that "Past research shows that 75-80% of non-renewals are initiated by policyholders", for instance when they buy or sell a home or change insurance company; the remaining 20-25% are initiated by insurers. So these counts are not the number of homes dropped by insurers, and the file does not name insurers.
  • Which policies. Voluntary market only. The Department collects the counts from insurers writing $5 million or more in homeowners and dwelling-fire premiums (about 98% of the market, according to its fact sheet), and the file excludes renters (HO-4) and condominium unit (HO-6) policies.
  • Non-renewal rate = policies not renewed or cancelled ÷ (new + renewed policies), the ratio the Department uses in its fact sheet ((non-renewed + canceled) ÷ (new + renewed)). We show it only when a ZIP code has at least 100 new and renewed policies.
  • FAIR Plan policies are residential policies in force on September 30 of each year (owner-occupied and rented homes, renters and condo unit owners). The FAIR Plan sells basic fire coverage; many owners add a separate Difference in Conditions (DIC) policy from another insurer, which is not in these counts.
  • Average premium = premium of the FAIR Plan policies in force on September 30, 2026 ÷ number of those policies, for owner-occupied single-family homes, as listed in the FAIR Plan report. It is an average across different homes and coverage amounts, not a price. Shown only with at least 10 policies.
  • Places. ZIP codes that cross county lines are assigned by the FAIR Plan to one county; place names come from GeoNames postal codes.

Questions people ask

How much is the California FAIR Plan?

The FAIR Plan does not publish prices. Its report for September 30, 2026 lists $1.6 billion of premium for 520,412 owner-occupied single-family policies: an average of $3,002 per policy across the state. By the report's risk groups the average is $1,431 (Low), $3,115 (Medium), $4,013 (High). What one home pays depends on its coverage amount and location; these averages are not a quote.

What does the California FAIR Plan cost by ZIP code?

Search your ZIP code on this page: for each ZIP code we divide the premium of owner-occupied single-family policies by the number of those policies, as listed in the FAIR Plan report of September 30, 2026. Among the 802 ZIP codes with at least 100 such policies, the average goes from $287 (ZIP 90222) to $12,520 (ZIP 93460).

What does the California FAIR Plan cover?

The FAIR Plan writes that its Dwelling Fire Policy "is a named peril policy, which provides coverage only for damage caused by the specific causes of loss listed in the policy": fire and lightning, internal explosion and smoke. "Optional coverages are available at an additional cost, such as coverage for vandalism and malicious mischief." Water damage, theft and liability are not included: the FAIR Plan points to Difference in Conditions (DIC) policies from other insurers for those.

What is a DIC policy in California?

Difference in Conditions (DIC) policies, sold by other insurers, add what the FAIR Plan does not cover. The FAIR Plan describes them like this: "Difference in Conditions (DIC) policies provide coverages that are not available through the California FAIR Plan, such as water damage, theft and liability coverage." It adds: "The California FAIR Plan does not offer DIC policies."

Is there a list of State Farm non-renewal ZIP codes in California?

Not from official data by company. The Department of Insurance publishes non-renewals by ZIP code for all insurers together, and its latest year is 2023. In 2023, 788,485 homeowners and dwelling policies of the voluntary market were not renewed or cancelled (the Department's "non-renewed/canceled" count, 75-80% initiated by policyholders, e.g. after a sale), statewide: 9.5% of new and renewed policies. Among ZIP codes with at least 1,000 new and renewed policies, the highest rates were in 95954 (78.6%), 96088 (37.0%), 95969 (34.0%). These totals include policies ended at the owner's request, so they do not show how many homes a given company dropped.

Which insurance companies are writing new policies in California?

This site does not track insurers. The Department of Insurance runs a Home Insurance Finder: "This online service allows you to find insurance companies and licensed agents/brokers that are appointed with them who have been identified to sell homeowners, renters, condominium, or mobile home insurance."

What to do if homeowners insurance is not renewed?

We cannot advise on a policy. The Department of Insurance answers insurance questions on 1-800-927-4357. The FAIR Plan describes itself as "an insurer of last resort, established by statute to provide basic property insurance to Californians statewide when no other option is reasonably available", and says that a broker "will perform a diligent search for comprehensive coverage in the traditional insurance market" before a FAIR Plan application.
