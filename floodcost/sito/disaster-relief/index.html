What the terms mean

Types of declaration

Major disaster (DR) and emergency (EM) declarations are made by the President at a governor's or tribal leader's request; fire management (FM) declarations help pay to fight a wildfire. A county is in a declaration when FEMA lists it as a designated area.

Programs

Individual assistance: help for households: FEMA's Individuals and Households Program. Public assistance: help for state and local governments and some nonprofits to repair public facilities. Hazard mitigation: funding to reduce the risk from future disasters. A declaration can include some programs and not others, and the programs can differ from county to county.

IRS tax relief

After a disaster declaration the IRS can postpone federal tax deadlines for people and businesses in the covered area. The IRS writes the covered counties in each news release; some recent releases follow a state's own disaster declaration, not FEMA's. In its releases the IRS writes that it "automatically identifies taxpayers located in the covered disaster area and applies filing and payment relief".

What this site is

FloodCost is an independent site about flood insurance costs, built on FEMA's open data. This section gathers the official declarations and IRS notices by county so you can find yours quickly. It is not affiliated with FEMA or the IRS and gives no tax or legal advice.

FloodCost

FloodCost shows what National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) policyholders actually pay, from FEMA's public policy and claims data. It is an independent site, not affiliated with FEMA, the NFIP or any insurer. These are past prices paid by other policyholders, not a quote.
