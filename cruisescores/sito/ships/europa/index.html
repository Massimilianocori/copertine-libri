Galleys (kitchens)18 findings

  • Item 16Deck 3 - Walk-In Refrigeration #19

    The 9 October 2025 cooling log for a fruit platter with sliced melon documented cooling began at 1230 and took five hours to reach below 41°F.

  • Item 16Deck 3 - Pastry/Bakery Station

    Reach-in refrigerator #3.2.12 was unable to maintain the internal temperatures of time/temperature control for safety (TCS) food items below 41°F. The internal temperatures of 11 ceramic containers of creme brulee measured 45°F with a calibrated thermometer. The creme brulee was prepared on 8 October 2025. Additionally, the internal temperature of mousse inside a metal container prepared on 8 October measured 45°F. An ambient thermometer placed inside the unit measured a temperature of 44°F. Crew initiated corrective action.

  • Item 17Deck 3 - Pastry/Bakery Station

  • Item 17Deck 3 - Hot Preparation Area

    The 8 October 2025 cooling log identified 'pasta rigatoni' finished cooling at 1310 with a final temperature of 5°C; however, the start time and initial cooling temperatures were not documented. Crew identified an ice bath was used to cool the pasta.

  • Item 20Deck 3 - Pastry/Bakery Station

Galleys (kitchens)21 findings

  • Item 13Warewash

    The person-in-charge of the dishwash machine did not understand the cycles of the machine. He explained to the inspector that the wash cycle occurred in the prewash compartment, that the rinse cycle occurred in the wash compartment, and that the final rinse occurred in the auxiliary rinse and final rinse compartments. This triggered a misunderstanding of the temperature required in each compartment.

  • Item 16Pastry

    The time control plan did not state what areas were on time control. Staff stated that one of the counters including a whipped cream machine were on time but they were not listed on the plan as time control units or were labeled as such. The temperature of the heavy cream inside the whip cream machine was 64°F.

  • Item 16Bakery

    A container with 17 big blocks of butter were on the counter next to the dough sheeter. The temperature of the butter was 67-71°F. The food employee explained how she needed the butter soft for her preparations and the butter was taken out of refrigeration for several hours. The inspector discussed with the food employee how this butter could be on the time control.

  • Item 16Lido Café Grill

    A pan with raw shell eggs was on time control on the food preparation counter but was not labeled with a discard time. This was corrected.

  • Item 16Room Service Menu

    The consumer advisory in the room service menu was very different from the one required in the VSP Operations Manual. Staff stated that it was a translation from a German version of a consumer advisory. It stated: US health authorities indicate that dishes containing uncooked or partially cooked animal products, as well as egg dishes, salmon fillets, cured meats and also beef fillets, must be classified as 'not entirely safe for human consumption'.

  • Item 16Lido Café Grill

    The internal temperature of 3 trays of individually packed butter inside undercounter refrigerator 8.2.1 was 48°F.

  • Item 17Hot Galley

    The Venezia cooling log did not document the cooling of the cooked artichokes that were dated 22 April and stored in the upright refrigerator. There was an entry for vegetables that the food employee stated it corresponded to the artichokes.

  • Item 20Bakery

    The dough sheeter fabric belt was difficult to clean.

  • Item 20Lido Café Grill

    The ambient temperature inside undercounter refrigerator 8.2.1 was 64°F.

  • Item 21Lido Café Grill

  • Item 23Crew Mess Warewash Area

  • Item 26Potwash

  • Item 27Bakery

    The previously cleaned dough sheeter still had some accumulations of dough and flour by the rollers.

  • Item 28Potwash

    On the clean racks, at least two pans were found stacked together and wet.

  • Item 29Crew Mess Serving Area

  • Item 33Fish Preparation Walk-in Refrigerator

Pools, hot tubs and water play areas2 findings

  • Item 10Pool/Whirlpool Spa Data Logger

    The ship had a data logger installed to monitor the free chlorine and pH values of the pool and whirlpool spa, but it did not have any security features to prevent manual manipulation of the recorded values. During the inspection, the inspector was able to change chlorine values in the data on the computer that was recorded by the data logger. Staff was aware of this problem and were manually testing the pool every 4-hours and the whirlpool spa every 1 hour and recording the values. A similar violation was written on the previous inspection report.

  • Item 10Hair and Lint Strainers and Housings

The engine's daily log book noted only that the pool filters were cleaned on 5 April, 12 April, and 20 April.

Drinking water2 findings

  • Item 06Microbiological Testing

    The ship was using an 18-hour E.coli water test kit but was not preforming it according to the manufacturer's instructions. The instructions stated that the water sample must be warmed in a 33-38°C water bath for 20 minutes until the water sample was 35°C prior to being placed in the incubator. Staff stated that they would place the water sample into the incubator without pre-warming it in a water bath, which is the procedure for the 24-hour E.coli water test kit.

  • Item 08Bunker/Production 30 Minute Test

    The chlorine and pH values were not recorded within 30 minutes of start of the bunkering and production processes to show the values were within the acceptable range.

  • Item 41Engine Changing Room

Medical center1 finding

  • Item 02Acute Gastroenteritis (AGE) Log

    The ship's AGE log was not kept in the exact format specified in Section 4.1.2.1.3 and Annex 13.2.2 of the 2011 VSP Operations Manual. For example, the meal information on the ship's AGE log was listed between the antidiarrheal medication column and the reportable case column. Also, the column between the abdominal cramps column and the headache column was left blank and the column that listed if the patient was a crew member or passenger was listed after a column labeled 'crew position/ passenger cabin number.'

Galleys (kitchens)16 findings

  • Item 17Cold Pantry

    Four different types of cut lettuce were entered in the cooling log for 8 March with a starting time between 10:25 am and 10:32 am and an ending time between 10:30 and 10:35 am. The cooling method was listed as an ice bath, and the starting temperatures were between 10°C and 12°C and all four final temperatures were at 4°C. The inspector arrived in the arrive at 10:30 am for the inspection and did not see any cut lettuce being cooled, or any cut lettuce that had the appearance of just finishing the cooling process. However, four different types of cut lettuce were stored in the walk-in refrigerator with a production date of 7 March and had a corresponding cooling log entry on that date.

Drinking water3 findings

  • Item 08Cabins 901-914 and 1001-1012

    There was some reconstruction in the above cabins that involved the bathroom facilities. In both the 900 and 1000 series cabins where changes occurred there were no backflow preventers installed on the flexible shower hoses. In addition, the hot tubs in the 1000 series cabins above were fitted with flexible spray hoses, but there was no hatch in the tub base so the water line could be inspected for leakage or changed, so we could not verify if a backflow preventer was ever installed for that flexible spray hose.

  • Item 08Potable Water Tank Painting

    In the review of the recent potable water tank maintenance records, it was noted that when a tank inspection identified areas of corrosion or other coating repairs, the drying and curing were completed according to the chemco 500 technical data sheet description of 6-12 hours. This time period for full curing of the epoxy paint conflicts with the 48 hour period specified in the certification tests made to grant this product standard 61 approval for use in potable water tanks (ANSI/NSF). Staff will need to approach the manufacturer about this difference to ensure curing is done correctly.

  • Item 08Potable Water Production/Bunkering

    The ship did not have any pH test records from either production or bunkering of potable water prior to January, 2016.

Housekeeping2 findings

  • Item 41Medical Wards and Europa Lounge

    There was no sign posted in the dialysis ward room or ward room #1 to inform users to handle the door upon exit with a cloth or paper towel to open. In the Deck 4 Europa Lounge women's public toilet there was no handwashing sign posted at the starboard side handwashing stations advising handwashing. Staff provided and installed the needed signs in all three areas during the inspection.

  • Item 41Outbreak Prevention and Response Plan (OPRP)

    Although there was repeated information identifying the non-chlorine cleaner/disinfectant chemical to use for surface treatment and the 1 to 16 dilution, there was no specification of what that meant in a part per million concentration. Recommend as well that the planners provide test strips to staff on the ship so the final mixed solution can be tested routinely to ensure the minimum concentration remains in the solution containers during use.
