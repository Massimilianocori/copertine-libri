  • Item 22Deck 2 - Warewashing

    The three-compartment sink was not being operated properly to clean and sanitize food equipment. Soiled pots and pans exceeded the capacity of the designated soiled-equipment storage areas, resulting in soiled equipment stored directly on the deck along the bulkhead adjacent to the sinks. During warewashing, the wash and rinse compartments were empty. Crew attributed this condition to a malfunctioning drain component and stated that a work order had been submitted approximately two weeks earlier. A crew member was observed cleaning soiled food equipment with a sponge over the empty rinse compartment and using the pre-rinse spray hose to remove detergent and food debris. The equipment was then submerged in the high-temperature sanitizing compartment, which was visibly soiled with food debris and soap. The inspector measured a sanitizing water temperature of 147°F using a tip-sensitive thermometer. The three-compartment sink was not equipped with a temperature-measuring device. Additionally, liquid food waste from the soiled-equipment drop-off area/drainboard was observed flowing along the back of the sinks, past the faucets, and toward the sanitizing compartment. At the time of the inspection, the liquid waste was not observed to affect the rinse or sanitizing compartments.

Food safety, general4 findings

  • Item 36Lighting Intensity

    The following food areas did not have adequate light intensity measurements: - Deck 2 - Main Galley - The light intensity behind the front-loading warewashing machine measured less than 220 lux. The deckhead light fixture was out of order. - Deck 1 - Crew Mess - The light intensity on the sides and back of the counter-mounted coffee machine measured less than 110 lux. Additionally, the light intensity at the dispensing nozzle measured less than 220 lux. - Deck 1 - Beverage Walk-In Cold Room #10 - The light intensity along the back left food storage rack measured less than 110 lux. One of the bulbs was in disrepair.

  • Item 39Integrated Pest Management (IPM) - Flies

    Flies were in the following food areas: - Deck 6 - Observatory Lounge Bar - One fly was inside the technical space beneath the handwashing station. - Deck 2 - Main Galley - One fly was by clean storage rack. - Deck 2 - Main Galley - One fly was over the pastry food preparation countertop. - Deck 1 - Crew Mess Warewashing - One fly was by the soil drop-off area. - Deck 1 - Officer Mess - Two flies were inside the undercounter refrigerator technical space.

  • Item 20Conditions of Food-Contact Surfaces

    The following food-contact surfaces were not adequately designed, constructed, or properly maintained. - Deck 2 - Main Galley - The evaporator deflector panel had four slotted fasteners on the food splash zone. Additionally, the ice scoop hook holder welded to the ice bin had crevices throughout making it difficult to clean. - Deck 2 - Main Galley - A previously cleaned and sanitized blue cutting board was excessively scored and peeling making it difficult to clean. - Deck 3 - Main Lounge Bar Pantry - The ice scoop hook holder welded to the ice machine bin had crevices throughout making it difficult to clean. - Deck 6 - Grill Restaurant Pantry - The ice scoop hook holder welded to the ice machine bin had crevices throughout making it difficult to clean. - Deck 7 - Open-Air Bar Pantry - The ice scoop hook holder welded to the ice machine bin had crevices throughout making it difficult to clean.

  • Item 33Conditions of Decks/Bulkheads/Deckheads

    The following food areas had one or more of these conditions: decks, bulkheads, or deckheads in disrepair; soiled surfaces; or missing coving: - Deck 3 - Main Lounge Bar - The open bottle shelving did not have coving throughout the bulkhead junctures. - Deck 2 - Crew Bar - Multiple beer cans and bottle caps along with more than a day's accumulation of dust were on a wood pallet underneath the refrigerator. - Deck 2 - Main Galley - Excessive accumulation of condensate above the left combination oven. Dripping onto the food equipment was not observed. - Deck 2 - Main Galley - Excessive accumulation of more than a day's grease residue and dust throughout the ventilation hood, in particular, the supply cover above the combination ovens. - Deck 2 - Main Galley Bain Marie Line - The ventilation exhaust over the food equipment had more than a day's accumulation of grease and dust. - Deck 2 - Main Galley - The deckhead light fixture adjacent to the hot production handwashing station was excessively soiled with grease and black like residue between the fixture and deckhead juncture. - Deck 2 - Main Galley Warewashing - The deck grouting along the scupper drain adjacent to the three compartment sink was recessed. - Deck 2 - Main Galley Warewashing - A large amount of soiled food equipment was stored on the deck across the three compartment sink. - Deck 1 - Crew Mess Warewashing - A large square open bulkhead penetration was underneath the handwashing station.

Housekeeping2 findings

  • Item 41Cabin Cleaning Records

    On 11 October 2026, a crew member ill with acute gastroenteritis (AGE) refused cabin cleaning. Cabin cleaning was performed on 10 and 12 October 2026.

  • Item 41Chemical Locker

    Concentration test strips were not available for the primary disinfectant solution listed in the Outbreak Prevention Response Plan (OPRP). It has been two months since switching to the current onboard disinfectant product that requires dilution. There were no alternative methods to verify disinfectant concentration when cleaning and disinfecting during normal operations, acute gastroenteritis (AGE) cabin cleaning, and during an outbreak.

Drinking water3 findings

  • Item 08Chill Lines - Ice Machines

    The following ice machine chill lines were not identified with either ISO 14726 (blue/white/blue) or by another uniquely identifiable method to prevent cross-connections: - Deck 2 - Main Galley - Deck 6 - Grill Restaurant Pantry - Deck 7 - Open-Air Bar Pantry

  • Item 06Legionella Testing Records

    There was no record of Legionella testing of any of the seven potable water storage tanks nor the hot water return line.

  • Item 44Legionella Testing

    Management was not aware of the need to include potable water storage tanks and hot water return lines in the Legionella sampling plan to ensure that the sampling plan is representative of the potable water system.

Pest control2 findings

Medical center1 finding
