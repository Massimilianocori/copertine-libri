Buffets9 findings

  • Item 16Deck 16- Windjammer Portside

    No time control labels were provided for any of the time or temperature control for safety (TCS) / potentially hazardous foods (PHF) out for service on the portside buffet lines. According to the portside time control plan, breakfast is from 0730-1130 and lunch is from 1130-1530 with no break in service. The inspector arrived in the area at 1257, after more than 4-hours elapsed from the time the venue's time control plan began at 0730 for breakfast service. The posted time control plans state 'TCS/PHFs in venues open more than 4 hours will be labeled with the 4 hour discard time' which was not followed.

  • Item 16Deck 8- Park Café Hot Service Line

    There were no time control labels on the chicken consommé, au jus, or roast beef. Staff stated they were following the 'Port Day' time control plan, which had a service period of 1130-1800.

  • Item 17Deck 8- Park Café Cold Line

    There was confusion if the three-door undercounter refrigeration unit was on time or temperature control. Two pans of cut tomatoes were labeled with set up and discard times and the unit was physically labeled as on time control. However, the unit was not listed on the time control plan. Staff stated the time control label on the unit was actually to indicate that the countertop was on time control.

  • Item 18Deck 8- Park Café Undercounter Refrigerator

    Cold-smoked salmon sandwiches were stored above ready-to-eat tuna sandwiches. This was corrected.

Pools, hot tubs and water play areas4 findings

  • Item 09Baby Splash Zone

    The pH measured 8.11, 8.02, and 7.9 from the water bowl near the entrance. During sampling, staff were alerted to the high pH. When the inspector entered the equipment room, crew had already reduced the pH level and ensured the analyzer was calibrated. During this time, the water automatically turned off due to the high pH (as the system is designed to do), but a sign was not placed out to inform bathers that the facility was closed. There was no attendant in the immediate area, as required by the variance, to inform passengers the area was closed. One bather continued to play in the RWF for a while without being informed the area was closed.

  • Item 10Baby Splash Zone Variance

    The variance provided for the baby splash zone was an older version approved in 2016 and not the most recent approved variance from 2020. When crew tried to look up the newest version within their online system, which was last updated in 2025, it still had the 2016 variance. Additionally, a pool attendant/Adventure Ocean staff or Sport's staff were not in the immediate area of the baby splash zone when the inspector arrived. While sampling, one pool attendant in the nearby Splash Away Bay was seen, but they were not able to be seen at all times and were not in the immediate area. Multiple parents asked the inspector questions about the baby splash zone because a pool attendant was not in the immediate area.

  • Item 10Pool Bather Loads

    Multiple pools had a number of bathers over the calculated bather load limit. Lifeguards were at the main pool and beach pool and did not make any effort to reduce the number of bathers. The main pool water was cloudy. 1) Solarium Pool - Bather load was 23 and 27 bathers were in the pool. 2) Main Pool - Bather load was 20 and 46 bathers were in the pool. 3) Beach Pool - Bather load was 22 and 47 bathers were in the pool.

  • Item 44Recreational Water Facilities (RWF) Managerial Controls

    Several public health issues were identified due to lack of proper managerial controls. 1) The vessel did not maintain the most recent Baby Splash Zone (BSZ) variance onboard as required. Crew presented an outdated variance from 2016 instead of the current 2020 variance. 2) A pool attendant/Adventure Ocean staff or Sport's staff were not in the immediate area of the BSZ when the inspector arrived and were not able to be seen from the BSZ at all times. As a result, the area was not continuously monitored as required by the variance. 3) The Baby Splash Zone remained open with a bather playing in the area after the pH was measured above the allowed range. The system automatically shut off, but there was no attendant in the immediate area, as required by the variance, to inform passengers the area was closed. 4) The number of bathers in multiple pools exceeded the calculated maximum bather loads. Lifeguards were stationed at the main swimming pool and the beach swimming pool and did not make any attempt to reduce the numbers of bathers inside each RWF. The water in the main swimming pool was cloudy. a. Solarium Pool: Calculated maximum bather load was 23; 27 bathers were observed in the pool. b. Main Pool: Calculated maximum bather load was 20; 46 bathers were observed in the pool. c. Beach Pool: Calculated maximum bather load was 22; 47 bathers were observed in the pool.
