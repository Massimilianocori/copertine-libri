About Cruise Health Scores

An independent site that makes the CDC’s cruise ship inspection records easy to read.

Cruise Health Scores answers one question with official data: how did my cruise ship do in its CDC health inspection?

The CDC publishes every cruise ship inspection, but in a search tool that is hard to use on a phone and that search engines cannot read. We copy the scores and reports into one simple page per ship, with the full history, a chart, and the findings written out in full, and we check the CDC every week.

Rules we follow

  • Every number comes from the CDC, with the inspection date and the date we checked the data on the page.
  • We do not rate ships ourselves and we do not say whether a ship is “safe”: a CDC score is a sanitation snapshot.
  • We do not publish names of people.
  • No advertiser or partner can change a score or a page.

Who we are

Cruise Health Scores is an independent project run by Massimiliano Cori. It is not affiliated with the CDC, any government agency or any cruise line. The site may show advertising and contains clearly marked affiliate links (such as travel medical insurance); they never change the data.

Corrections

Found a mistake? Write to us; we check the CDC record and correct the page at the next update.

Cruise Health Scores

Cruise Health Scores republishes the cruise ship inspection scores and reports of the CDC Vessel Sanitation Program, in a form that is easier to read. This site is not affiliated with or endorsed by the CDC. A CDC score shows how well a ship met sanitation standards on the day of the inspection; it is not a safety rating and does not tell you whether a ship is “safe”.

Ships fail a CDC inspection when they score 85 or lower. For the official record, always check the CDC Green Sheet.
