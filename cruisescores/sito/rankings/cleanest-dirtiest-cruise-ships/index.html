By the most recent CDC inspection, the cruise ship with the lowest score is Emerald Princess: 78 out of 100 (October 16, 2026); 3 ships currently have a failing latest score (85 or lower). At the top, 39 ships scored a perfect 100 at the latest inspection; Oceania Vista has the longest run, 5 perfect inspections in a row. Ranking of 184 ships inspected from October 18, 2025 to October 17, 2026.

What “cleanest” and “dirtiest” mean here. The CDC does not rank ships and does not call any ship clean or dirty. This ranking is ours and uses one number: the score of each ship’s most recent CDC Vessel Sanitation Program inspection. A score is a snapshot of how the ship met sanitation standards on inspection day (86 or more passes). It can change at the next inspection, and it is not a safety rating or a count of people who got sick.

How the ranking is built

  • Which ships: of the 203 ships on the CDC Green Sheet, the 184 whose most recent inspection is in the last 12 months of published inspections (October 18, 2025 to October 17, 2026). The other 19 have an older latest score and are left out.
  • Which score: only the most recent inspection of each ship, as published by the CDC. No averages and no weighting.
  • Order: lowest list by score, then by the most recent inspection date, then by name. Perfect list by the number of consecutive 100s, then date, then name. Full list by score from the highest, then date, then name.
  • Reproducible: every score is on the ship’s page with its full inspection history and on the CDC Green Sheet.

Other ways to compare

  • Cleanest cruise lines: average score of each line over 12 months
  • Lowest inspection scores of 2026 (every inspection of the year, not only the latest of each ship)

  • Norovirus and GI outbreaks by cruise line (a different CDC record: illness on a voyage, not the inspection score)

Source

  • CDC Vessel Sanitation Program, Inspection Query Tool: Green Sheet (latest score of each ship), inspection search and inspection reports.
  • Data checked on November 25, 2026. The most recent inspection the CDC has published is from October 17, 2026; inspections done after that date will appear here once the CDC posts them.
