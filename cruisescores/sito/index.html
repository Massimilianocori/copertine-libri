How to read a CDC score

CDC inspectors start from 100 and deduct points for violations of the Vessel Sanitation Program standards, in eight areas from the galleys to the pools and the medical center. A ship fails with 85 or lower.

  • 100Perfect: no points deducted
  • 95–99Passed, few problems
  • 90–94Passed
  • 86–89Passed, close to the line
  • 85 or lowerFailed (not satisfactory)

The CDC only says that 85 or lower is “not satisfactory”. The color bands above are ours, to make the list easier to scan.

Questions

What is a passing CDC score for a cruise ship?

Ships are scored out of 100. A score of 86 or higher passes; the CDC says that scores of 85 or lower are “not satisfactory” and counts them as a failed inspection (CDC FAQ).

How often are cruise ships inspected?

Cruise ships under the CDC’s jurisdiction (international voyages with a stop at a U.S. port) are subject to two unannounced inspections each year, done while the ship is in a U.S. port (CDC FAQ, CDC).

Does a low score mean a ship is unsafe?

Not on its own. The score shows how well the ship met the CDC’s sanitation standards on the day of the inspection. It is not a safety rating and it cannot tell you whether you will get sick. Read the findings to see what the inspectors actually saw.

Why can’t I find my ship?

The CDC only covers cruise ships on international voyages with a stop at a U.S. port. Ships that sail outside the United States for an extended period drop out of the CDC database and are added back when they return and are inspected (CDC FAQ).
