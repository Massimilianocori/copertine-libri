Cruise ship norovirus and GI outbreaks in 2024

The 18 gastrointestinal illness outbreaks the CDC Vessel Sanitation Program posted for 2024: ship, cruise line, sailing dates, people ill and cause.

  • Source: CDC outbreak pages

The CDC posted 18 gastrointestinal illness outbreaks on cruise ships for 2024: 15 norovirus, 1 E. coli, 1 Salmonella, 1 unknown cause. December had the most sailings with an outbreak (7). The outbreak with the most passengers ill was on Queen Mary 2 (12/21/2024-1/3/2025): 346 of 2,565 (13.5%). List read on November 27, 2026.

What this list counts, and what it leaves out. The CDC says it posts an outbreak when the ship “is under VSP jurisdiction (on voyages including both U.S. and foreign ports)” and the voyage “has 3% or more of passengers or crew reporting symptoms of GI illness to the ship’s medical staff”. It “may also post other outbreaks of public health significance” (CDC). Ships under VSP jurisdiction “carry 13 or more passengers and have a foreign itinerary with U.S. ports” (CDC, about VSP). So a sailing that never calls at a U.S. port, or one where fewer than 3% reported symptoms, is normally not here. The figures are totals for the whole voyage, not people sick at the same time. The CDC can update the counts of a recent outbreak: the figures here are the ones on the CDC pages on November 27, 2026. The number of outbreaks is not a rate: a line with more ships and more voyages that call at U.S. ports has more voyages that can appear. It is not a safety rating.

Cruise Health Scores

Cruise Health Scores republishes the cruise ship inspection scores and reports of the CDC Vessel Sanitation Program, in a form that is easier to read. This site is not affiliated with or endorsed by the CDC. A CDC score shows how well a ship met sanitation standards on the day of the inspection; it is not a safety rating and does not tell you whether a ship is “safe”.

Ships fail a CDC inspection when they score 85 or lower. For the official record, always check the CDC Green Sheet.
