Cruise ship norovirus and GI outbreaks, 2023–2026

Gastrointestinal illness outbreaks on cruise ships posted by the CDC Vessel Sanitation Program, with cause and sailing dates.

  • Source: CDC public records
  • Data checked on October 26, 2026
  • Pass mark: 86 out of 100
  • Independent site, not the CDC

The CDC posts an outbreak when a ship in its jurisdiction has 3% or more of passengers or crew reporting gastrointestinal symptoms to the ship’s medical staff, and “may also post other outbreaks of public health significance”. 64 outbreaks were posted from 2023 to 2026; the most recent on National Geographic Sea Bird (7/15/2026 – 7/20/2026).

An outbreak is not the same as a failed inspection: the inspection score measures sanitation on the day of the inspection; an outbreak counts people who got sick on one voyage. Illnesses such as norovirus “can spread quickly in closed and semienclosed environments, such as cruise ships” (CDC FAQ).

Cruise Health Scores

Cruise Health Scores republishes the cruise ship inspection scores and reports of the CDC Vessel Sanitation Program, in a form that is easier to read. This site is not affiliated with or endorsed by the CDC. A CDC score shows how well a ship met sanitation standards on the day of the inspection; it is not a safety rating and does not tell you whether a ship is “safe”.

Ships fail a CDC inspection when they score 85 or lower. For the official record, always check the CDC Green Sheet.
