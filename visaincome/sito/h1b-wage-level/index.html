H-1B wage level calculator: Level I to IV and lottery entries (2026-27)

Since 27 February 2026, the H-1B lottery enters each person 1 to 4 times by wage level: Level IV four times, III three, II two and I once (DHS final rule, 90 FR 60864). The level depends on the salary, the occupation (SOC code) and the area of the job: for software developers, Level II starts at $187,075 a year in San Jose, CA and at $116,896 in Dallas, TX (Department of Labor wage file for 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, read on 7 November 2026).

How the wage level is chosen

The wage level is the one written on the registration, for the job's SOC code and the area of the place of work. The DHS rule says:

  • The registrant must select “the highest Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS) wage level that the beneficiary’s proffered wage equals or exceeds for the relevant Standard Occupational Classification (SOC) code in the area(s) of intended employment”.
  • Several places of work: “If the beneficiary will work in multiple locations, or in multiple positions if the registrant is an agent, the registrant must select the lowest corresponding OEWS wage level that the beneficiary’s proffered wage will equal or exceed.”
  • A salary range: “If the beneficiary’s proffered wage is expressed as a range, the registrant must select the OEWS wage level that the lowest wage in the range will equal or exceed.”
  • Below Level I: “If the beneficiary’s proffered wage is lower than OEWS wage level I, because it is based on a prevailing wage from another legitimate source (other than OEWS) or an independent authoritative source, the registrant must select ‘‘wage level I.’’”
  • No OEWS wage for the job: “Where there is no current OEWS prevailing wage information for the beneficiary’s proffered position, the registrant must select the OEWS wage level that corresponds to the requirements of the beneficiary’s proffered position using the Department of Labor’s prevailing wage guidance.”
  • More than one registration for the same person: “USCIS will assign each unique beneficiary to the lowest OEWS wage level among all registrations submitted on the beneficiary’s behalf”.

“Each unique beneficiary will only be counted once toward the numerical allocation projections, regardless of how many registrations were submitted for that beneficiary or how many times the beneficiary is entered in the selection pool”. Employers must still pay at least the required wage: DHS notes that “employers remain legally obligated to pay H–1B workers at least the prevailing wage or the actual wage, whichever is higher.”

Source: Federal Register, Weighted Selection Process for Registrants and Petitioners Seeking To File Cap-Subject H-1B Petitions (final rule, 90 FR 60864, 29 December 2025). Read on 7 November 2026. U.S. government data, public domain.

Questions people ask

How do I check my H-1B wage level?

Find the SOC code of the job and the area that contains the place of work, then compare the offered salary with the four OFLC levels for that pair: the level is the highest one the salary equals or exceeds. OFLC publishes hourly wages; the yearly figure is the hourly wage × 2,080 hours. The calculator on this page does it for 299 occupations and all 530 areas.

What is the H-1B weighted lottery rule?

A DHS final rule published on 29 December 2025 (90 FR 60864), in force since 27 February 2026. If a random selection is needed, each person is entered once at Level I, twice at Level II, three times at Level III and four times at Level IV, and is still counted only once.

Are H-1B wage levels set by state?

No. They are set for each occupation and each area: a metropolitan area or a nonmetropolitan part of a state. OFLC uses statewide data only when there is no estimate for the area or for the area plus the areas next to it (Geo level 3).

Does the H-1B wage level include bonus?

Only in limited cases. DHS wrote: “While cash bonuses may, in limited circumstances, be counted towards the annual salary” (under the Department of Labor rule 20 CFR 655.731(c)(2)), while stock options, profit sharing and similar benefits cannot reliably be counted. It adds: “This rule does not change how wages are defined or measured.”

Will the H-1B wage levels increase?

OFLC publishes new wages every July, so the dollar amounts change each year. Separately, the Department of Labor proposed on 27 March 2026 to raise Level I from the 17th to the 34th percentile and Level IV from the 67th to the 88th. It is a proposal, not in force.

When is the H-1B lottery in 2027?

USCIS announces the dates on uscis.gov. The rule says the registration period lasts at least 14 calendar days and that USCIS announces its start at least 30 calendar days in advance.
