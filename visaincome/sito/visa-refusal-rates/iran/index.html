What this share means, and what it does not. A multiple-entry visa "allows several visits to the Schengen area for as long as the visa is valid"; a single-entry visa allows one entry (European Commission, checked on 30 October 2026). Every visit is still a short stay, of up to 90 days in any 180-day period. A multiple-entry visa is not a long-stay visa, a work permit or a residence permit. The Commission's file only says whether each visa allows multiple entries: it does not say how long the visas were valid for. The share describes visas issued in the past, not your chances: the consulate decides the number of entries and the validity of each visa.

UK visa refusal rate for nationals of Iran: visitor, study, work and family visas

From July 2025 to June 2026, the UK refused 47.4% of visitor visa decisions for nationals of Iran (6,251 refused out of 13,189). 15.7% of study visa decisions were refused (1,664 decisions); 11.0% of work visa decisions were refused (1,277 decisions); 6.6% of family visa decisions were refused (2,022 decisions). For partner (spouse) visas alone the rate was 6.2% (32 refused out of 520 decisions; compare all nationalities). Unlike the Schengen figures, the UK counts applicants by nationality, wherever they apply.

Canada: temporary resident applications from Iran approved (2025)

In 2025, IRCC approved 65.7% of the temporary resident applications from Iran that it processed (81,580 approved out of 124,085 people). This share mixes visitor visas, study permits and work permits, first applications and extensions; "processed" includes applications refused and withdrawn, so the other 34.3% were refused or withdrawn. In 2024: 53.5%. January to August 2026: 57.6% (42,035 processed).

No study permit or visitor visa refusal rate. For each country IRCC publishes only the number of new study permit applications processed (6,950 from Iran in 2025) and the number of visitor visas approved (18,105), never both for the same visa, so a study permit or visitor visa refusal rate cannot be calculated from the official files.

How to read these figures

  • Where you apply, not your passport. The Schengen figures count applications made in Iran, including residents of other nationalities, and leave out nationals of Iran who applied elsewhere.
  • Not-issued rate = visas not issued ÷ (uniform visas issued + visas with limited territorial validity + visas not issued), the formula in the Commission's file. Applications still pending at the end of the year are not counted.
  • If you are refused, the consulate must tell you why and how to appeal (European Commission).
  • Australia, New Zealand and Canada count applicants by citizenship (Canada: "source country"), like the UK, and each authority defines its rate differently: see the notes under each table.
