Multiple-entry Schengen visas in 2025: which countries issue them most often

In 2025, 51.7% of the 10,265,942 uniform Schengen visas issued were multiple-entry visas (5,303,749), according to the European Commission's Visa statistics for consulates 2025 (file 2025-consulates-schengen-visa-statistics.xlsx, downloaded on 15 October 2026); Germany issued the highest share (89.1%) and Finland the lowest (20.6%).

Statistics, not advice. A multiple-entry visa "allows several visits to the Schengen area for as long as the visa is valid"; a single-entry visa allows one entry (European Commission, checked on 30 October 2026). Every visit is still a short stay, of up to 90 days in any 180-day period. A multiple-entry visa is not a long-stay visa, a work permit or a residence permit. The Commission's file only says whether each visa allows multiple entries: it does not say how long the visas were valid for. The share describes visas issued in the past, not your chances: the consulate decides the number of entries and the validity of each visa.

Questions people ask

Which Schengen country gives multiple-entry visas most often?

In 2025, Germany had the highest share: 89.1% of the 1,288,597 uniform visas it issued were multiple-entry visas. Next came Croatia (77.7%), Belgium (71.8%), Slovenia (68.9%). The lowest share was Finland's (20.6%). Each country's figure mixes applicants from many places, so compare countries in the place where you apply (see the table by place of application).

What does a multiple-entry Schengen visa mean?

The European Commission says a multiple-entry visa allows several visits to the Schengen area for as long as the visa is valid, while a single-entry visa allows you to enter the Schengen area once. Each visit is a short stay of up to 90 days in any 180-day period. It is not a long-stay visa or a residence permit.

Multiple-entry vs single-entry Schengen visa: what do the statistics count?

The Commission's file gives the total number of uniform visas issued and, within it, the number of multiple-entry visas (MEVs). The rest are single-entry or double-entry visas (Commission metadata). Visas with limited territorial validity are counted separately and are not in these shares.

How long is a multiple-entry Schengen visa valid?

The statistics do not say. The Commission's file records whether a visa allows multiple entries, not its validity, so we cannot tell how many were valid for a few months, one year or several years. The validity is printed on each visa and is decided by the consulate.

Can I get a multiple-entry Schengen visa the first time I apply?

These figures cannot answer that: they do not record whether applicants had visited the Schengen area before. The consulate decides the number of entries and the validity of each visa. What the figures show is that 51.7% of all uniform visas issued in 2025 were multiple-entry visas.

Multiple-entry Schengen visa requirements: is the passport rule different?

Yes, on one point. The European Commission says the passport's expiry date should be at least 3 months after you leave the Schengen area, and for multiple-entry visas at least 3 months after your departure from the last country visited (Applying for a Schengen visa, checked on 30 October 2026). Check the full list of documents with the consulate.

Multiple-entry Schengen visa for Indians: how many were issued in India?

In 2025, Schengen consulates in India issued 556,870 multiple-entry visas out of 966,529 uniform visas: 57.6% (62.8% in 2024). Among Schengen countries with at least 500 visas issued there, Germany had the highest share (99.6%) and Latvia the lowest (8.4%). The figures count visas issued in India, whatever the applicant's nationality. Details: India page.

Multiple-entry Schengen visa for Pakistani applicants: how many were issued in Pakistan?

In 2025, Schengen consulates in Pakistan issued 18,771 multiple-entry visas out of 45,970 uniform visas: 40.8% (46.8% in 2024). Among Schengen countries with at least 500 visas issued there, Germany had the highest share (73.7%) and Austria the lowest (12.9%). The figures count visas issued in Pakistan, whatever the applicant's nationality. Details: Pakistan page.

How often do Germany and Switzerland give multiple-entry Schengen visas?

In 2025, 89.1% of the 1,288,597 uniform visas issued by Germany were multiple-entry visas, and 66.7% of the 538,341 issued by Switzerland. The share in the place where you apply can be very different: see the country pages.

What these figures do not say

  • Where the visa was issued, not your passport. The Commission counts visas by the country where the application was made: the figures for "India" are visas issued in India, including visas issued to residents of other nationalities.
  • Share of multiple-entry visas = multiple-entry uniform visas issued ÷ all uniform visas issued (multiple-entry visas included), the same as the "Share of MEVs" column in the Commission's file. Refused applications are not in the share: for those, see the refusal rates.
  • No validity length. The file does not say how long each multiple-entry visa was valid for.
  • Minimum 500 visas. On the country pages we compare Schengen countries only where they issued at least 500 visas in that place; smaller numbers swing too much from year to year.
  • Where to apply. You must apply to the country of your main destination (or, for equal stays, the first you enter): see the European Commission page on applying for a Schengen visa.
