What this share means, and what it does not. A multiple-entry visa "allows several visits to the Schengen area for as long as the visa is valid"; a single-entry visa allows one entry (European Commission, checked on 30 October 2026). Every visit is still a short stay, of up to 90 days in any 180-day period. A multiple-entry visa is not a long-stay visa, a work permit or a residence permit. The Commission's file only says whether each visa allows multiple entries: it does not say how long the visas were valid for. The share describes visas issued in the past, not your chances: the consulate decides the number of entries and the validity of each visa.

UK visa refusal rate for nationals of South Africa: visitor, study, work and family visas

From July 2025 to June 2026, the UK refused 4.4% of visitor visa decisions for nationals of South Africa (3,270 refused out of 74,412). 8.6% of study visa decisions were refused (606 decisions); 5.7% of work visa decisions were refused (3,204 decisions); 6.9% of family visa decisions were refused (696 decisions). For partner (spouse) visas alone the rate was 6.1% (33 refused out of 538 decisions; compare all nationalities). Unlike the Schengen figures, the UK counts applicants by nationality, wherever they apply.

INZ counts applications by the country that issued the passport, and its "declined" figure includes applications that were withdrawn, so these rates are not directly comparable with the UK or Australian refusal rates.

Canada: temporary resident applications from South Africa approved (2025)

In 2025, IRCC approved 66.2% of the temporary resident applications from South Africa that it processed (10,670 approved out of 16,115 people). This share mixes visitor visas, study permits and work permits, first applications and extensions; "processed" includes applications refused and withdrawn, so the other 33.8% were refused or withdrawn. In 2024: 75.8%. January to August 2026: 68.4% (9,690 processed).

No study permit or visitor visa refusal rate. For each country IRCC publishes only the number of new study permit applications processed (500 from South Africa in 2025) and the number of visitor visas approved (6,005), never both for the same visa, so a study permit or visitor visa refusal rate cannot be calculated from the official files.

How to read these figures

  • Where you apply, not your passport. The Schengen figures count applications made in South Africa, including residents of other nationalities, and leave out nationals of South Africa who applied elsewhere.
  • Not-issued rate = visas not issued ÷ (uniform visas issued + visas with limited territorial validity + visas not issued), the formula in the Commission's file. Applications still pending at the end of the year are not counted.
  • If you are refused, the consulate must tell you why and how to appeal (European Commission).
  • Australia, New Zealand and Canada count applicants by citizenship (Canada: "source country"), like the UK, and each authority defines its rate differently: see the notes under each table.
