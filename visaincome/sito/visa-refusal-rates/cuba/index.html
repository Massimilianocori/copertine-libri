UK visa refusal rate for nationals of Cuba: visitor visas

From July 2025 to June 2026, the UK refused 19.8% of visitor visa decisions for nationals of Cuba (180 refused out of 909). Unlike the Schengen figures, the UK counts applicants by nationality, wherever they apply. With only 909 visitor visa decisions, read this rate with care: where most nationals can visit the UK without a visa, the people who apply for one are a small and unusual group.

Canada: temporary resident applications from Cuba approved (2025)

In 2025, IRCC approved 48.4% of the temporary resident applications from Cuba that it processed (5,675 approved out of 11,725 people). This share mixes visitor visas, study permits and work permits, first applications and extensions; "processed" includes applications refused and withdrawn, so the other 51.6% were refused or withdrawn. In 2024: 62.4%. January to August 2026: 51.2% (6,265 processed).

No study permit or visitor visa refusal rate. For each country IRCC publishes only the number of new study permit applications processed (105 from Cuba in 2025) and the number of visitor visas approved (2,720), never both for the same visa, so a study permit or visitor visa refusal rate cannot be calculated from the official files.

A Schengen visa application must include medical insurance covering emergency medical care, hospitalisation and repatriation (European Commission, checked on 15 October 2026).

How to read these figures

  • Where you apply, not your passport. The Schengen figures count applications made in Cuba, including residents of other nationalities, and leave out nationals of Cuba who applied elsewhere.
  • Not-issued rate = visas not issued ÷ (uniform visas issued + visas with limited territorial validity + visas not issued), the formula in the Commission's file. Applications still pending at the end of the year are not counted.
  • If you are refused, the consulate must tell you why and how to appeal (European Commission).
  • Australia, New Zealand and Canada count applicants by citizenship (Canada: "source country"), like the UK, and each authority defines its rate differently: see the notes under each table.
  • Full method: how we calculate the rates.
