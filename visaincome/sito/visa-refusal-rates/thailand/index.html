UK visa refusal rate for nationals of Thailand: visitor, study and family visas

From July 2025 to June 2026, the UK refused 11.6% of visitor visa decisions for nationals of Thailand (5,733 refused out of 49,470). 0.6% of study visa decisions were refused (3,207 decisions); 6.2% of family visa decisions were refused (725 decisions). For partner (spouse) visas alone the rate was 5.0% (31 refused out of 625 decisions; compare all nationalities). Unlike the Schengen figures, the UK counts applicants by nationality, wherever they apply.

Canada: temporary resident applications from Thailand approved (2025)

In 2025, IRCC approved 72.5% of the temporary resident applications from Thailand that it processed (9,250 approved out of 12,760 people). This share mixes visitor visas, study permits and work permits, first applications and extensions; "processed" includes applications refused and withdrawn, so the other 27.5% were refused or withdrawn. In 2024: 76.2%. January to August 2026: 79.9% (9,080 processed).

No study permit or visitor visa refusal rate. For each country IRCC publishes only the number of new study permit applications processed (765 from Thailand in 2025) and the number of visitor visas approved (4,130), never both for the same visa, so a study permit or visitor visa refusal rate cannot be calculated from the official files.

Travel medical insurance

A Schengen visa application must include medical insurance covering emergency medical care, hospitalisation and repatriation (European Commission, checked on 15 October 2026).

How to read these figures

  • Where you apply, not your passport. The Schengen figures count applications made in Thailand, including residents of other nationalities, and leave out nationals of Thailand who applied elsewhere.
  • Not-issued rate = visas not issued ÷ (uniform visas issued + visas with limited territorial validity + visas not issued), the formula in the Commission's file. Applications still pending at the end of the year are not counted.
  • If you are refused, the consulate must tell you why and how to appeal (European Commission).
  • Australia, New Zealand and Canada count applicants by citizenship (Canada: "source country"), like the UK, and each authority defines its rate differently: see the notes under each table.
  • Full method: how we calculate the rates.
