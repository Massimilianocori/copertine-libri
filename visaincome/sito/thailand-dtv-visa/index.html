Thailand DTV visa by embassy: fees and who can apply where (2026)

The Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) fee depends on where you apply: 10,000 baht (THB) in cash at the embassy in Vientiane, £300 in London and US$400 in Washington, according to the official pages of the Thai embassies and consulates we read on 30 October 2026. Since 31 August 2026 you also need proof of permanent residence in the country where you apply and a criminal record certificate (Thai Ministry of Foreign Affairs).

What changed on 31 August 2026

The Department of Consular Affairs of Thailand's Ministry of Foreign Affairs announced on 28 August 2026 two changes to the documents for DTV applications, in force since 00.00 on 31 August 2026 (Thailand time):

  • Proof of permanent residence replaces the earlier "document indicating current location". The Consulate-General in Osaka words it as “Proof of permanent residence in the country/territory where the visa application is submitted”.
  • A certificate of criminal record clearance is added, “Certificate of criminal record clearance issued by the country/territory of the applicant’s nationality or permanent residence” (Osaka).
  • Applications submitted before 31 August 2026 are processed under the earlier requirements.

In practice this means you apply where you live permanently, not where you happen to be travelling. Some missions spell it out: Singapore grants the DTV only to Singapore citizens and permanent residents; Vientiane asks applicants to be Lao nationals or permanent residents of the Lao PDR; Ho Chi Minh City accepts only a Temporary or Permanent Residence Card from foreigners. Visa exemption: 30 days since 15 September 2026

For short tourist trips you may not need a DTV at all. Since 15 September 2026 the visa exemption is 30 days: the Royal Thai Embassy in London writes that British and Irish passport holders, "as well as foreign travellers from many other countries", "can enter Thailand without a visa for up to 30 days under the Visa Exemption Scheme for tourism purposes only", and the Embassy in Washington lists 60 countries and territories, the United States among them (the list is an image on its page).

  • Eligible passport holders can use the exemption "only twice within a calendar year" (London).
  • Immigration officers may ask for "proof of sufficient funds of 20,000 THB per person" and for proof of accommodation and onward travel within 30 days.
  • The London page says "they may request an extension of stay for up to 30 days", through Thailand's Immigration Bureau.
  • For Indian ordinary passport holders, the Embassy in New Delhi says the 30-day exemption "remains effective from 15 September 2026 until further announcement".

Sources: Royal Thai Embassy, London, Visa Exemption and Visa on Arrival Schemes (updated 8 September 2026); Royal Thai Embassy, Washington, DC, New Visa Exemption and Visa on Arrival (updated 17 September 2026). Read on 30 October 2026.

How to apply

  • Apply online on the Thai e-Visa website and choose the embassy or consulate for the place where you live permanently.
  • Prepare the documents on your mission's page: passport, photo, proof of permanent residence, bank statements (usually at least 500,000 baht over 3 months), a criminal record certificate and proof of the purpose (remote work, Muay Thai or cooking course, medical treatment, or being the spouse or child of a DTV holder).
  • The DTV is valid for 5 years with multiple entries and stays of up to 180 days per entry, extendable once by up to 180 days at the Immigration Bureau, as the London page and others say.
  • Processing can take a month or more (Kuala Lumpur: "approximately 1 month or more"). Fees are not refunded.

Source: Royal Thai Embassy, London, Destination Thailand Visa (updated 8 September 2026) and the pages linked in the table, read on 30 October 2026.

Questions people ask

How much does the Thailand DTV visa cost?

It depends on the embassy or consulate, and each charges in its own currency. On the pages we read on 30 October 2026: Bucharest €350; Budapest €350; Cairo 18,000 Egyptian pounds (EGP); Chicago US$400; Helsinki €350; Ho Chi Minh City US$340; Kota Bharu RM1,600; Kuala Lumpur RM1,600; London £300; Los Angeles US$400; Madrid €350; Moscow US$350; New Delhi ₹30,000; New York US$400; Qingdao 2,300 yuan (CNY); Singapore S$500; Vientiane 10,000 baht (THB); Warsaw 1,575 złoty (PLN); Washington, DC US$400; Yangon US$400.

Can I apply for the DTV in a country where I am only a tourist?

Not since 31 August 2026 at most missions: applicants must submit proof of permanent residence in the country or territory where they apply. For example, Ho Chi Minh City accepts only a Temporary or Permanent Residence Card from foreigners, and Singapore grants the DTV only to its citizens and permanent residents.

Do I need health insurance for the DTV?

None of the DTV pages we read lists health insurance among the required documents. Check the official page of your mission, because requirements can change.

How much money do I need in the bank for the DTV?

Most pages ask for financial evidence of at least 500,000 baht, usually bank statements for the last 3 months. Some write it in their own currency: £12,000 in London, US$16,000 in Washington, €15,000 in Helsinki; Hong Kong asks for 150,000 Hong Kong dollars and Cairo for 850,000 Egyptian pounds.

Where do I apply for the DTV?

Online, on the Thai e-Visa website (https://www.thaievisa.go.th/), choosing the embassy or consulate for the place where you live. Some missions add their own rules: Vientiane takes the fee in cash and needs an appointment.

Do I need a DTV for a short holiday?

Maybe not. Since 15 September 2026 many nationalities, including British, Irish and US citizens, can enter Thailand without a visa for up to 30 days for tourism.
