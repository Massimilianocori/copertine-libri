Three separate checks

FHA, VA and conventional loans each have their own project rules, written in different places (see the table), and HUD's FHA list does not cover VA or conventional loans. A project can be on HUD's FHA list and not approved by VA, or the other way around. Check the list that matches the loan you plan to use.

FHA approval

For an FHA-insured mortgage, the unit must be in a project approved by HUD or by a DELRAP lender, or qualify as a site condominium or through a single-unit approval (24 CFR 203.43b(c)). Approved projects are placed on HUD's public list, which this site copies every week with the expiration date of each approval. See how to check a project.

VA approval

The law allows a VA-guaranteed loan to buy a unit in a condominium project only if the project is approved by VA under criteria set in its regulations (38 U.S.C. 3710(a)(6); VA's condominium rules start at 38 CFR 36.4360). VA.gov lists "buy a condo in a VA-approved project" among the uses of a VA-backed purchase loan (VA.gov). VA's condo report is on its LGY Hub; your lender can also check it.

"Warrantable" condos

"Warrantable" is not a term defined in HUD's condominium rule, in the VA law quoted above or in the Fannie Mae section cited here. Lenders use it informally for a project that meets the standards of the investor that will buy a conventional loan. For Fannie Mae, for example, the lender must determine that the project meets Fannie Mae's eligibility requirements before delivering the loan (Selling Guide B4-2.1-01). If a lender calls a condo "non-warrantable", ask which standard it does not meet and whether another loan type is possible.
