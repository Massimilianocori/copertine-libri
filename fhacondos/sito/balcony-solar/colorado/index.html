Balcony solar · Colorado

Is balcony solar legal in Colorado?

Plug-in solar rules for condo owners and renters in Colorado: the law, the limits and what your association can ban. Checked on November 6, 2026.

  • Official sources linked
  • Checked November 6, 2026
  • No legal or electrical advice

Yes: as of November 6, 2026, Colorado law allows plug-in solar devices of up to 1,920 watts with no utility approval or fee, and from January 1, 2027 a condo or HOA cannot unreasonably prohibit them (HB26-1007, in force since August 12, 2026).[1][2]

What the law says

  • HB26-1007, "Improve Customer Use Distributed Energy Resources", became law: the governor signed it on May 7, 2026.[1][2]
  • It is Chapter 127 of the 2026 Session Laws, with an effective date of August 12, 2026. It adds section 40-2-140 to the Colorado Revised Statutes.[1][2]

Limits: watts, outlet and utility

  • One photovoltaic system per address, designed to supply at most 1,920 watts to the grid, labeled and listed by a nationally recognized testing laboratory.[2]
  • No more than one device on a single wall outlet per address, and a feature that stops it from energizing the grid during an outage.[2]
  • The utility may not require its approval, a fee or extra equipment. It may ask you to tell it that you use a device and its size in kilowatts.[2]
  • Devices of 391 watts or less are exempt from code or listing provisions that would require changes to the building's wiring. From January 1, 2027, devices above 391 watts that are not UL labeled and listed may not be sold, leased or rented in Colorado.[2]
  • Installation must follow fire code requirements and local building codes on health and safety.[2]

Condos and HOAs: what the association can and cannot ban

  • From January 1, 2027, a covenant or restriction that unreasonably prohibits or restricts a plug-in device is void, and the device counts as an "energy efficiency measure" under the common interest community law (CRS 38-33.3-106.7).[2]
  • An owner may install and use a device on a limited common element, such as a balcony, patio or deck that serves only the owner's unit.[2]
  • The association may require the owner to secure the device reasonably to the unit or to that balcony, patio or deck. The owner may be responsible for the costs and liability of installation, maintenance and removal, and may have to indemnify the association and other owners.[2]
  • Reasonable restrictions remain possible, for example prior notice, rules to prevent fire or electrical overload, and the location of the device.[2]

Before you buy a kit, check three things. 1. The law itself, through the links in the sources below: laws change, and this page summarizes them. 2. Your electric utility: whether it wants a notice or a form. 3. Your condo or HOA documents (declaration, bylaws, rules) and the board's written answer about balconies and railings.

Questions people ask

What does Colorado's balcony solar law say?

HB26-1007, signed on May 7, 2026 and in force since August 12, 2026, lets a customer use one plug-in solar system per address of up to 1,920 watts without utility approval or fees; the utility may ask for a notice of the device and its size. From January 1, 2027, condo and HOA rules cannot unreasonably prohibit these devices, but reasonable restrictions on notice, safety and location are allowed.
