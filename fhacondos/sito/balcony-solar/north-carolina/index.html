Balcony solar · North Carolina

Is balcony solar legal in North Carolina?

Plug-in solar rules for condo owners and renters in North Carolina: the law, the limits and what your association can ban. Checked on November 6, 2026.

  • Official sources linked
  • Checked November 6, 2026
  • No legal or electrical advice

Not yet: as of November 6, 2026, North Carolina has no plug-in solar law; House Bill 1129, "Balcony Solar", has been in the House Rules Committee since April 30, 2026.[1]

What the law says

  • HB 1129 was filed on April 29, 2026, passed first reading and was referred to the Committee on Rules, Calendar, and Operations of the House on April 30, 2026. No later action is shown.[1]

Limits: watts, outlet and utility

  • Bill: a movable device of up to 1.2 kilowatts on a standard 120-volt outlet, meeting the North Carolina Electrical Code and certified by UL or an equivalent.[2]
  • Bill: notice to the utility on a standard form at least 15 days before operating; no interconnection agreement, approval or fee; no net metering.[2]

Condos and HOAs: what the association can and cannot ban

  • The bill does not mention condominium associations or HOAs. It would stop landlords with more than four rental dwellings from prohibiting a tenant's device on the exterior of the premises, with 7 days' notice and reasonable restrictions on size and placement.[2]
