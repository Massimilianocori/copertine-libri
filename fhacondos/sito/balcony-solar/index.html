Balcony solar in condos: is it legal in your state?

Plug-in solar laws and bills state by state, with what condo and HOA rules can still ban. Checked on November 6, 2026.

  • Official texts, linked
  • Checked November 6, 2026
  • No legal or electrical advice

As of November 6, 2026, plug-in (balcony) solar is allowed by a state law we read in Colorado, Maine, Maryland, Utah and Vermont. Virginia's law starts on January 1, 2027, and California's governor has signed SB 868. Only a few of these laws limit what a condo association or HOA may ban.[1][2][3][4][5][6][7]

A plug-in or "balcony" solar kit is a small panel with its own inverter that plugs into a regular outlet[1][4]. A growing number of states now say, in their own words, how big it may be, whether you must tell your electric utility and, in a few states, whether a condominium or homeowners association may ban it. Below is each state with a law or an official bill, read on the legislature's own site.

Before you buy a kit, check three things. 1. The law itself, through the links in the sources below: laws change, and this page summarizes them. 2. Your electric utility: whether it wants a notice or a form. 3. Your condo or HOA documents (declaration, bylaws, rules) and the board's written answer about balconies and railings.

This page is general information, not legal or electrical advice. For your rights, ask a lawyer licensed in your state; for wiring, outlets and panels, ask a licensed electrician.

Questions people ask

Which states allow balcony solar?

As of November 6, 2026, we read laws in force in Utah, Colorado, Maine, Maryland and Vermont, and a law that starts on January 1, 2027 in Virginia. California's governor signed SB 868 on September 30, 2026. New Jersey, Connecticut and New Hampshire are reported as signed by a third-party tracker, but we could not confirm them on official sites. New York's bill passed both houses and awaits the governor.

Sources: [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8][9]

Can I put solar panels on my balcony?

It depends on two sets of rules: your state's law on plug-in devices and your building's rules. Even where the state law protects plug-in solar, condo and HOA rules on balconies and railings can still apply unless the law limits them, as Colorado's law does from January 1, 2027 and Vermont's law does for covenants. Ask your association in writing before you buy.

Sources: [2][5]

Can an HOA prevent solar panels on a balcony?

Among the laws we read, Colorado (from January 1, 2027) voids covenants that unreasonably prohibit plug-in devices, and Vermont's law stops covenants from prohibiting them, including on condo patio railings. New Jersey's S2368 text bars condo and HOA rules that ban them. Utah, Maine, Maryland and Virginia's plug-in laws do not mention associations, so your association's rules still matter there.

Sources: [1][2][3][4][5][6][10]

Is plug-in solar safe?

We cannot judge the safety of a product or of your wiring; ask a licensed electrician. What the laws require is clear: a device certified by a nationally recognized testing laboratory (UL 3700 is named in Maine, Vermont and New Jersey) and a feature that stops it from sending power during an outage. Maine requires a licensed electrician and a dedicated circuit above 420 watts.

Sources: [1][3][5][10]

Do you need an electrician for plug-in solar?

Among the laws we read, only Maine requires one: systems with combined output above 420 watts, up to 1,200 watts, must be installed by a Maine-licensed electrician on a dedicated circuit with a single outlet. Elsewhere the laws do not require one for a device that simply plugs in, but any change to wiring follows the electrical code. For questions about your outlet or panel, ask a licensed electrician.

Sources: [2][3][4][6]

Can renters use balcony solar?

Vermont's law lets a tenant install after giving the landlord 10 days' notice, unless the landlord answers with reasonable restrictions. New Jersey's S2368 text stops landlords from banning devices for tenants who pay their own electricity, with 14 days' written notice. North Carolina's HB 1129, still a bill, would do something similar for larger landlords.

Sources: [5][10][11]

Do I have to notify my utility about plug-in solar?

It depends on the state. Maryland and Virginia require a notice before you install; Maine requires one within 30 days for systems above 420 watts; Colorado lets the utility ask for one. Utah's law does not mention a notice. Check your utility's website for its form.

Sources: [1][2][3][4][6]

Is balcony solar legal in Texas?

Texas has no plug-in solar law that we could find. A third-party tracker lists no Texas bill as of the 2026 session, so the usual rules of your electric utility and your condo or HOA apply. Ask your utility before connecting anything that sends power into your home's wiring.

Sources: [8][12]

Is balcony solar legal in Florida?

Florida has no plug-in solar law that we could find. A third-party tracker lists no Florida bill as of the 2026 session, so the usual rules of your electric utility and your condo association apply. Ask your utility before connecting anything that sends power into your home's wiring.

Sources: [8][13]
