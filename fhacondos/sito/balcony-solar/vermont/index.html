Balcony solar · Vermont

Is balcony solar legal in Vermont?

Plug-in solar rules for condo owners and renters in Vermont: the law, the limits and what your association can ban. Checked on November 6, 2026.

  • Official sources linked
  • Checked November 6, 2026
  • No legal or electrical advice

Yes: as of November 6, 2026, Vermont law allows plug-in solar devices with up to 1,200 watts per electric meter, and condo or HOA covenants may not prohibit them (Act 149 of 2026, in force since July 1, 2026).[1][2]

What the law says

  • S.202 became Act 149, "An act relating to plug-in photovoltaic devices". The governor signed it on June 16, 2026, and it took effect on July 1, 2026.[1][2]
  • It adds 30 V.S.A. § 256 and amends the rules on zoning bylaws (24 V.S.A. § 4413) and on deed restrictions and covenants (27 V.S.A. § 544).[2]

Limits: watts, outlet and utility

  • One or more devices per electric meter with a maximum combined inverter capacity of 1,200 watts, plugged into a receptacle, certified under UL 3700 or an equivalent.[2]
  • Devices may only be connected to systems with smart meters, must be temporarily but securely attached to the ground or a structure, and must stop energizing the building during an outage.[2]
  • The utility may not require approval, a fee or extra controls, but may recover costs if a device overloads the service.[2]
  • A customer with net metering may not also install a plug-in device, and power sent to the grid is not paid for.[2]

Condos and HOAs: what the association can and cannot ban

  • Deed restrictions, covenants and similar binding agreements may not prohibit, or have the effect of prohibiting, renewable energy devices; for plug-in devices this covers devices appurtenant to the buildings.[2]
  • The exception for patio railings in condominiums, cooperatives and apartments does not apply to plug-in photovoltaic devices.[2]
  • Tenants must give the landlord at least 10 days' notice. The landlord may answer within 10 days with reasonable restrictions; without an answer, the tenant may go ahead. Electrical work needs the landlord's permission.[2]
  • Town zoning bylaws may not regulate the installation, operation and maintenance of plug-in devices.[2]

Before you buy a kit, check three things. 1. The law itself, through the links in the sources below: laws change, and this page summarizes them. 2. Your electric utility: whether it wants a notice or a form. 3. Your condo or HOA documents (declaration, bylaws, rules) and the board's written answer about balconies and railings.

This page is general information, not legal or electrical advice. For your rights, ask a lawyer licensed in your state; for wiring, outlets and panels, ask a licensed electrician.

Questions people ask

What is Vermont's balcony solar law?

It is Act 149 of 2026 (S.202), in force since July 1, 2026. You may use plug-in solar devices with up to 1,200 watts of combined inverter capacity per meter, on a smart meter, without utility approval or fees. Covenants may not prohibit them, and the condo patio-railing exception does not apply to plug-in devices.
