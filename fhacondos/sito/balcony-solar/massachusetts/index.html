Balcony solar · Massachusetts

Is balcony solar legal in Massachusetts?

Plug-in solar rules for condo owners and renters in Massachusetts: the law, the limits and what your association can ban. Checked on November 6, 2026.

  • Official sources linked
  • Checked November 6, 2026
  • No legal or electrical advice

Not yet: as of November 6, 2026, Massachusetts has no plug-in solar law; both chambers passed versions of the energy bill H.5175 that include it, and a conference committee has been working on the differences since July 16, 2026.[1]

What the law says

  • H.5175, "An Act relative to energy affordability, clean power and economic competitiveness", passed the House on February 26, 2026 (128 to 27).[1]
  • The Senate replaced its text with S.3166 and passed it on July 1, 2026 (32 to 8). On July 16, 2026 the House did not concur and a conference committee was appointed.[1]

Limits: watts, outlet and utility

  • House text: a device of up to 1,200 watts on a standard 120-volt outlet, meeting the National Electrical Code and certified by UL or an equivalent, exempt from interconnection and net metering, with no utility approval or fee.[2]
  • Senate text: certified plug-in solar and battery systems, exemption from listing provisions for systems of 391 watts or less, a notice to the utility within 30 days, and no approval, interconnection application or fee.[3]

Condos and HOAs: what the association can and cannot ban

  • The House text does not mention associations. The Senate text says no one may unreasonably prohibit these devices and that covenants or restrictions that unreasonably prohibit them are void.[2][3]

Questions people ask

Is balcony solar legal in Massachusetts?

There is no Massachusetts law on it yet. The House and the Senate passed different versions of H.5175 that include plug-in solar, and a conference committee was appointed on July 16, 2026. The final rules depend on the text that comes out of that committee.
