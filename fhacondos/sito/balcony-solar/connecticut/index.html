Is balcony solar legal in Connecticut?

Plug-in solar rules for condo owners and renters in Connecticut: the law, the limits and what your association can ban. Checked on November 6, 2026.

What the law says

  • Bright Saver's tracker lists HB 5340 as Public Act 26-127, signed on June 4, 2026, effective October 1, 2026.[1]
  • The General Assembly's site could not be opened (certificate error), so we have not read the act.[2]

Limits: watts, outlet and utility

  • According to Bright Saver (third party): up to 1,200 watts per customer, no permit and no utility approval or notice.[1][2]

Condos and HOAs: what the association can and cannot ban

  • We could not read whether the act says anything about condominium associations or HOAs. Until you can, treat your association's rules as still in force and ask the board in writing.[2]

Before you buy a kit, check three things. 1. The law itself, through the links in the sources below: laws change, and this page summarizes them. 2. Your electric utility: whether it wants a notice or a form. 3. Your condo or HOA documents (declaration, bylaws, rules) and the board's written answer about balconies and railings.

This page is general information, not legal or electrical advice. For your rights, ask a lawyer licensed in your state; for wiring, outlets and panels, ask a licensed electrician.
