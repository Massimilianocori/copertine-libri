Balcony solar · Maine

Is balcony solar legal in Maine?

Plug-in solar rules for condo owners and renters in Maine: the law, the limits and what your association can ban. Checked on November 6, 2026.

  • Official sources linked
  • Checked November 6, 2026
  • No legal or electrical advice

Yes: as of November 6, 2026, Maine law allows plug-in solar and plug-in battery systems at home, up to 420 watts with no notice and up to 1,200 watts with a licensed electrician and a notice to the utility (LD 1730, Public Law 2026, Chapter 644).[1][2][3]

What the law says

  • LD 1730 (SP 676), "An Act to Make Small Plug-in Solar Generation Devices Accessible for All Maine Residents", was enacted and signed by the governor on April 6, 2026, as Chapter 644 of the Public Laws.[1][2]
  • It adds section 3475 to Title 35-A. The law has no emergency clause, and the Legislature gives July 29, 2026 as the general effective date for non-emergency laws of that session.[2][3]

Limits: watts, outlet and utility

  • Eligible systems have an export capacity of 1,200 watts or less and are listed or certified under UL 3700 or a comparable standard, or configured under the National Electrical Code adopted in Maine.[2]
  • Up to 420 watts of combined inverter output per service address: no notice is needed.[2]
  • From 420 to 1,200 watts: each system must be installed by a Maine-licensed electrician on a dedicated circuit with a single outlet, and you must notify the utility within 30 days on the form set by the Public Utilities Commission.[2]
  • The utility may not require approval, an interconnection application, a fee or extra equipment, and may not deny a system that follows the law. The system cannot be used for net energy billing.[2]

Condos and HOAs: what the association can and cannot ban

  • Chapter 644 does not mention condominium associations or HOAs.[2]
  • If you install a system on a structure you do not own, you must make sure it does not compromise the structure or break building, fire or zoning codes, and you must restore the structure when you remove the system.[2]

Before you buy a kit, check three things. 1. The law itself, through the links in the sources below: laws change, and this page summarizes them. 2. Your electric utility: whether it wants a notice or a form. 3. Your condo or HOA documents (declaration, bylaws, rules) and the board's written answer about balconies and railings.

Questions people ask

What is Maine's balcony solar law?

It is LD 1730, Public Law 2026, Chapter 644, signed on April 6, 2026. You may use plug-in solar or battery systems of up to 420 watts per service address without notice; between 420 and 1,200 watts, a licensed electrician must install each system on a dedicated circuit and you must notify the utility within 30 days.
