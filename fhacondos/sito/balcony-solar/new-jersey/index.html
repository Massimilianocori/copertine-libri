Balcony solar · New Jersey

Is balcony solar legal in New Jersey?

Plug-in solar rules for condo owners and renters in New Jersey: the law, the limits and what your association can ban. Checked on November 6, 2026.

  • Official sources linked
  • Checked November 6, 2026
  • No legal or electrical advice

Probably yes, from 2027: as of November 6, 2026, the Garden State Balcony Solar Act (S2368) would allow plug-in devices of up to 1,200 watts and bar condo associations from banning them, and a third-party tracker reports it was signed on September 1, 2026, but we could not confirm the signature on the Legislature's site.[1][2][3][4]

What the law says

  • S2368, the "Garden State Balcony Solar Act", was reported by the Assembly Budget Committee on June 28, 2026 (third reprint), identical to Assembly Bill 4836.[1][2]
  • The bill says it takes effect six months after enactment.[1]
  • The Legislature's status page needs JavaScript and we could not read the final votes. Bright Saver's tracker lists it as signed on September 1, 2026, effective March 1, 2027.[3][4]

Limits: watts, outlet and utility

  • A device of up to 1,200 watts that plugs into a standard 120-volt outlet, is listed or certified under UL 3700 or a comparable standard, and stops exporting power during an outage.[1]
  • The combined output at one service address, or at one dwelling unit in multifamily housing, may not exceed 1,200 watts without an interconnection agreement.[1]
  • Utilities may not require approval, a fee or extra equipment. Devices of 400 watts or less are exempt from code provisions that would require changes to wiring or panels.[1]
  • Municipalities may not prohibit the devices on residential property or require a permit or fee for placing them.[1]

Condos and HOAs: what the association can and cannot ban

  • A condominium association, HOA or cooperative may not enforce a master deed, bylaw or rule that prohibits a unit owner or tenant from using a device or placing it on the exterior of their premises; such provisions are void.[1][2]
  • The association may set reasonable restrictions on size, placement and manner of placement, and may require indemnification for damage caused by negligent installation, maintenance or use.[1]
  • A tenant who pays for the unit's electricity must give written notice to the landlord and to the association at least 14 days before placing or using a device, with documentation and the proposed location.[1]

Before you buy a kit, check three things. 1. The law itself, through the links in the sources below: laws change, and this page summarizes them. 2. Your electric utility: whether it wants a notice or a form. 3. Your condo or HOA documents (declaration, bylaws, rules) and the board's written answer about balconies and railings.

Questions people ask

Is balcony solar legal in New Jersey?

The Garden State Balcony Solar Act (S2368) would allow certified plug-in devices of up to 1,200 watts per address or dwelling unit and stop condo and HOA rules from banning them, with reasonable restrictions allowed. It takes effect six months after enactment. A third-party tracker reports it was signed on September 1, 2026; we could not confirm this on the Legislature's site.

Sources: [1][4]

Can New Jersey renters use balcony solar?

Under S2368, a landlord may not prohibit a tenant who pays for the unit's electricity from using a device inside the leased premises, including a leased balcony or patio. The tenant must give written notice at least 14 days before, and the landlord may set reasonable restrictions on size and placement.

Sources: [1]
