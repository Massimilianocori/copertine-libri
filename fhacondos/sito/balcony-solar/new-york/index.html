Is balcony solar legal in New York?

Plug-in solar rules for condo owners and renters in New York: the law, the limits and what your association can ban. Checked on November 6, 2026.

What the law says

  • The "Solar Up Now NY Act" passed the Assembly and the Senate on May 28, 2026. The Assembly's action list shows no later step, such as delivery to the governor or a signature.[1]
  • If signed, it would take effect on the ninetieth day after becoming law.[1]
  • The Senate's bill page refused our request (403); we read the same bill on the Assembly's site.[2]

Limits: watts, outlet and utility

  • Bill: a system plugged into a standard outlet with at most 1,200 watts of inverter output to the grid, listed or certified as a complete plug-in system, installed under the state fire and building code and the maker's instructions.[1]
  • Bill: no interconnection or net metering, no utility approval or fee; the owner notifies the utility within 30 days of installing, on a simple form the utility must publish.[1]

Condos and HOAs: what the association can and cannot ban

  • The bill text does not mention condominium boards, co-op boards or HOAs.[1]

Questions people ask

Is balcony solar legal in New York State?

Not yet by state law. The Solar Up Now NY Act (A9111C, same as S8512C) passed both houses on May 28, 2026, and as of November 6, 2026 the Assembly's record shows no action by the governor. If signed, it would allow plug-in systems of up to 1,200 watts with a notice to the utility within 30 days, 90 days after it becomes law.
