Balcony solar · Michigan

Is balcony solar legal in Michigan?

Plug-in solar rules for condo owners and renters in Michigan: the law, the limits and what your association can ban. Checked on November 6, 2026.

  • Official sources linked
  • Checked November 6, 2026
  • No legal or electrical advice

Not yet: as of November 6, 2026, Michigan has no plug-in solar law; House Bill 5764 has been in the House Committee on Energy since March 19, 2026.[1]

What the law says

  • HB 5764 was introduced on March 19, 2026 and referred to the Committee on Energy; the Legislature's history shows no later action.[1]

Limits: watts, outlet and utility

  • Bill: a device of up to 1,200 watts on a standard 120-volt outlet, meeting the National Electrical Code and certified by UL or an equivalent, with a feature that stops it from energizing the building during an outage.[2]
  • Bill: no utility approval, fee or extra equipment; notice to the utility within 30 days of installing, on a form set by the Public Service Commission.[2]

Condos and HOAs: what the association can and cannot ban

  • The bill does not mention condominium associations or HOAs. If you install on a meter or panel you do not own, you would have to avoid damaging the structure and restore it on removal.[2]

Before you buy a kit, check three things. 1. The law itself, through the links in the sources below: laws change, and this page summarizes them. 2. Your electric utility: whether it wants a notice or a form. 3. Your condo or HOA documents (declaration, bylaws, rules) and the board's written answer about balconies and railings.

This page is general information, not legal or electrical advice. For your rights, ask a lawyer licensed in your state; for wiring, outlets and panels, ask a licensed electrician.

Questions people ask

Is plug-in solar legal in Michigan?

Not by a specific state law yet. House Bill 5764 would allow plug-in systems of up to 1,200 watts without utility approval or fees, with a notice within 30 days, but it has been in the House Committee on Energy since March 19, 2026.
