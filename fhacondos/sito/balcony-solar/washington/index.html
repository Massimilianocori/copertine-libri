Balcony solar · Washington State

Is balcony solar legal in Washington State?

Plug-in solar rules for condo owners and renters in Washington State: the law, the limits and what your association can ban. Checked on November 6, 2026.

  • Official sources linked
  • Checked November 6, 2026
  • No legal or electrical advice

Not yet: as of November 6, 2026, Washington has no plug-in solar law; SB 6050 cleared a Senate committee in January 2026 but stopped in the Senate Rules Committee and did not pass in the 2026 session.[1]

What the law says

  • SB 6050, "Expanding the use of distributed energy resources", was heard in the Senate Environment, Energy and Technology Committee, which recommended a substitute on January 30, 2026.[1]
  • It went to the Rules Committee on February 2 and was placed in the Rules "X" file on February 26, 2026; the status page shows it did not pass the Senate.[1]

Limits: watts, outlet and utility

  • Substitute bill: one device per premises of up to 1,200 watts, on a standard 120-volt outlet, certified under UL 3700, with notice to the utility before installing.[2]
  • Substitute bill: utilities with fewer than 25,000 customers could still prohibit the devices.[2]

Condos and HOAs: what the association can and cannot ban

  • The substitute bill would have barred HOA governing documents (RCW 64.38.055) from prohibiting a portable device on the owner's or resident's property, while allowing the association to require indemnification.[2]
