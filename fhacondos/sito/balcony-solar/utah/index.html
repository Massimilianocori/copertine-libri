Balcony solar · Utah

Is balcony solar legal in Utah?

Plug-in solar rules for condo owners and renters in Utah: the law, the limits and what your association can ban. Checked on November 6, 2026.

  • Official sources linked
  • Checked November 6, 2026
  • No legal or electrical advice

Yes: as of November 6, 2026, Utah law lets you plug in a solar device of up to 1,200 watts without your utility's approval or any fee, under H.B. 340, in force since May 7, 2025.[1][2][3]

What the law says

  • H.B. 340, "Solar Power Amendments", passed in the 2025 General Session; the governor signed it on March 25, 2025.[1]
  • It took effect on May 7, 2025. It added Utah Code 54-15-601 and a definition of "portable solar generation device" in 54-15-102.[2]
  • The Utah Code site lists the 2025 text of 54-15-601 as the current version.[3]

Limits: watts, outlet and utility

  • Maximum power output: 1,200 watts, through a standard 120-volt AC outlet.[2]
  • The device must meet the latest National Electrical Code and be certified by UL or an equivalent nationally recognized testing laboratory.[2]
  • It must include a feature that stops it from energizing the building's wiring during a power outage.[2]
  • The utility may not require its approval, a fee, or equipment beyond what is built into the device. The law does not mention a notice to the utility.[2]
  • The device is outside net metering, and the utility is not liable for damage or injury it causes.[2]

Condos and HOAs: what the association can and cannot ban

  • H.B. 340 does not mention condominium associations, HOAs or landlords: it limits what the utility can require, not what your association's rules say.[2]
  • Because the law is silent on associations, read your declaration, bylaws and rules, and ask the board in writing before you buy a kit.[2]

Before you buy a kit, check three things. 1. The law itself, through the links in the sources below: laws change, and this page summarizes them. 2. Your electric utility: whether it wants a notice or a form. 3. Your condo or HOA documents (declaration, bylaws, rules) and the board's written answer about balconies and railings.

Questions people ask

What is the balcony solar law in Utah?

It is H.B. 340 of 2025, now Utah Code 54-15-601. Since May 7, 2025, a certified plug-in solar device of up to 1,200 watts that plugs into a standard 120-volt outlet needs no interconnection agreement, no utility approval and no fee. The law says nothing about condominium or HOA rules.
