Balcony solar · Maryland

Is balcony solar legal in Maryland?

Plug-in solar rules for condo owners and renters in Maryland: the law, the limits and what your association can ban. Checked on November 6, 2026.

  • Official sources linked
  • Checked November 6, 2026
  • No legal or electrical advice

Yes: as of November 6, 2026, Maryland law allows one portable solar system of up to 1,200 watts per electric meter for residential use, with a notice to your utility before you install it (Public Utilities § 7-321, from HB 1532 of 2026).[1][2][3]

What the law says

  • HB 1532, the Utility RELIEF Act, was approved by the governor on May 12, 2026 as Chapter 353. Among many energy measures it adds § 7-321 to the Public Utilities Article.[1][2]
  • The Department of Legislative Services gives July 1, 2026 as the effective date of the portable solar provisions.[3]

Limits: watts, outlet and utility

  • A movable system that plugs into a standard outlet, supplies at most 1,200 watts back to the building's electric system and is certified by UL or an equivalent laboratory.[2]
  • Not more than one system per electric meter, for residential use only.[2]
  • You must notify your electric company before installation and give it a certification of the system's safety features and maximum capacity. If the system requires an automatic locking disconnect switch, you pay for its installation.[2]
  • The utility may not require approval, a fee for feeding power back, or other extra equipment. Systems of 391 watts or less are exempt from listing provisions that would require changes to wiring or panels.[2]

Condos and HOAs: what the association can and cannot ban

  • § 7-321 does not mention condominium associations, HOAs or landlords.[2]
  • Your association's declaration, bylaws and rules therefore still matter: ask the board in writing before you place a panel on a balcony or railing.[2]

Before you buy a kit, check three things. 1. The law itself, through the links in the sources below: laws change, and this page summarizes them. 2. Your electric utility: whether it wants a notice or a form. 3. Your condo or HOA documents (declaration, bylaws, rules) and the board's written answer about balconies and railings.

This page is general information, not legal or electrical advice. For your rights, ask a lawyer licensed in your state; for wiring, outlets and panels, ask a licensed electrician.

Questions people ask

What is the balcony solar law in Maryland?

It is § 7-321 of the Public Utilities Article, added by HB 1532 (Chapter 353 of 2026). You may install one portable solar system of up to 1,200 watts per meter for residential use, after notifying the electric company and certifying the system's safety features and maximum capacity.
