Is my condo FHA approved? How to check

Search the project, confirm it on HUD's lookup and read what the listing really says: expiration date, phases covered and FHA concentration.

Try: , , , . The search runs in your browser on 8,554 projects: all FHA-approved ones and those that expired or were withdrawn in the last 24 months.

Short answer: for an FHA-insured mortgage, the condo unit generally has to be in a project approved by HUD or by a lender with HUD's DELRAP authority. The other routes are a single-unit approval or, for some detached or townhouse-style projects, the "site condominium" rules (24 CFR 203.43b(c)). Here is how to check a project in a few minutes.

  1. Search the project on this site

    Type the condo name, the city or the ZIP code in the box above. HUD's lookup cuts project names at 30 characters and often abbreviates them (for example "CONDO" for "Condominium"), so one distinctive word or the ZIP code works best. Each result shows whether the approval is current and when it expires.

  2. Confirm it on HUD's official lookup

    Open the HUD FHA Condominium Lookup. Choose the state, then enter the city, the ZIP code or the start of the name, and leave the other fields empty. With "Status" set to "All" (the default), expired and withdrawn projects are shown too. Our list is a weekly copy, so HUD's page is the one that counts.

  3. Read the status and the dates

    HUD's lookup shows a status for each project (Approved, Expired, Rejected or Withdrawn), a status date and an expiration date. Unless HUD specifies otherwise in writing, a project is approved for three years from the date it is placed on the list, and HUD can rescind an approval at any time if the project fails to comply with a requirement (24 CFR 203.43b(h)(3)). In HUD's data as of October 17, 2026, 6,521 of the 6,539 current approvals run exactly three years.

  4. Check which phases or buildings are covered

    The "Composition of Project" column in HUD's lookup describes what is approved, for example "PHASES 1-8" or "BLDG C, D, E". If the unit you want is in a building or phase that is not listed, the project approval may not cover it. We do not copy that column, because it is free text; read it on HUD's page.

  5. Look at the FHA concentration

    HUD sets a maximum share of units in a project that can have FHA-insured mortgages; under the rule, the limit must be between 25% and 75%, and HUD fixes the exact figure by notice. HUD may stop issuing new FHA case numbers in a project that is over the limit (24 CFR 203.43b(d)(6)(viii)). The lookup shows an FHA concentration figure for each project; ask your lender how close the project is to the limit.

  6. Ask your lender before you make an offer

    Give the lender the HUD condo ID shown in the listing. The lender checks the project's status and the loan's other requirements. A project on the list does not mean your loan is approved.

What the statuses mean for a buyer

StatusWhat it means
ApprovedThe project is on HUD's list of approved condominium projects until the expiration date shown (203.43b(h)(2)).
Expiring soonOur label for approvals that end within 6 months (1,328 nationwide). The project can request renewal from six months before to six months after expiration, using updated information (203.43b(h)(4)).
ExpiredThe approval period ended. If the renewal request comes more than six months after expiration, a complete new application is required (203.43b(h)(4)). 1,882 approvals expired or reached their expiration date in the last 24 months.
WithdrawnHUD lists the project as withdrawn, with the date of the change (133 in the last 24 months). An FHA loan then needs a new project approval or one of the other routes in this guide.

Who approves a condo project

A project can be approved by HUD itself or by a lender with Direct Endorsement Lender Review and Approval Process (DELRAP) authority; the application goes to one or the other, and an approved project is placed on HUD's list (24 CFR 203.43b(h)(1)–(2)). HUD's lookup shows which route was used in the "Approval Method" column: of the current approvals, 6,211 went through HUD's own review (HRAP) and 328 through a DELRAP lender.

Why a condo might not be on the list

  • No one has applied: approval is not automatic. An application has to be submitted to HUD or to a DELRAP lender (203.43b(h)(1)).
  • The approval expired or was withdrawn: search with status "All" in HUD's lookup, or look at the "recently expired" section of the city or county page here.
  • The project is listed under a different or shortened name: try the ZIP code or one distinctive word.
  • It may not meet the requirements: for example, a project must be primarily residential, not intended for transient or hotel rentals, and complete (203.43b(d)).

If the project is not approved, there may still be a way: see what to do if a condo is not FHA approved.
