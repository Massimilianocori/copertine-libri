What if a condo is not FHA approved?

Single-unit approval, a project application, a renewal or another type of loan: what HUD's rules allow, in plain English.

If the condo you like is not on HUD's list, an FHA loan is not automatically ruled out. Depending on the project, there are four routes to discuss with your lender. The FHA rules below come from HUD's condominium regulation, 24 CFR 203.43b.

1. Single-unit approval

One unit in a project that is not approved can still get a "single-unit approval" (203.43b(a)(6)). Under 203.43b(i), a unit may be eligible if:

  • the project is not on the list of FHA-approved projects;
  • HUD has not identified the project as subject to an adverse determination for significant issues that affect its viability;
  • the project is complete;
  • the unit is not a manufactured home; and
  • the project has at least five dwelling units.

The lender (the "mortgagee") must make sure the project meets HUD's standards, which for single-unit approvals can be a subset of the full project rules set by HUD notice. There is also a cap on FHA loans: in projects with 10 or more units, HUD sets a maximum share of FHA-insured units between 0% and 20%; in projects with fewer than 10 units, no more than two units can have FHA-insured mortgages (203.43b(i)(2)). Ask the lender early whether it can process one.

2. The association applies for project approval

An application for project approval can be submitted to HUD or to a lender with DELRAP authority; if all the criteria are met, the project is approved and placed on HUD's list (203.43b(h)(1)–(2)). Among other things, the project must be primarily residential and not intended for transient or hotel rentals, consist only of one-family units, be complete and ready for occupancy, and have its plat or development plan approved by the local jurisdiction (203.43b(d)). Further requirements on insurance, finances, owner occupancy, commercial space and reserves are set by HUD through notices, some of them within ranges written in the rule. Of the 6,539 approvals on HUD's list as of October 17, 2026, 328 came through DELRAP lenders and 6,211 through HUD review.

3. A recently expired approval can be renewed

If the project's approval expired, an eligible party can request recertification up to six months after the expiration date by updating the information it already submitted; after six months a complete new application is needed (203.43b(h)(4)). 1,882 approvals expired or reached their expiration date in the last 24 months: each state, county and city page here lists them, so you can see whether the project you like is one of them.

4. Site condominiums

A "site condominium" is a project made up entirely of detached homes without shared garages or other attached buildings, or a project of detached or townhouse-style homes where each unit is the home and its land, with no manufactured homes (203.43b(a)(7)). It does not need to be on the approved list, but it must meet the basic project requirements, and the insurance and maintenance costs of each unit must be the sole responsibility of its owner (203.43b(c) and (j)).

Other loan types

  • VA loans (for eligible veterans and service members) can be used to buy a condo only in a VA-approved project (38 U.S.C. 3710(a)(6); VA.gov). VA approval is separate from FHA approval: check VA's own list.
  • Conventional loans sold to Fannie Mae: before delivering the loan, the lender must determine that the project meets Fannie Mae's eligibility requirements (Fannie Mae Selling Guide, B4-2.1-01). This review is separate from HUD's list.
