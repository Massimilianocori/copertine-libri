Washington statute of limitations for medical malpractice

Generally 3 years under RCW 4.16.350. Statute text, when the clock starts, exceptions and a deadline calculator. Read on 13 November 2026.

What the Washington statute says

  • Health care negligence 3 years

    based upon alleged professional negligence shall be commenced within three years of the act or omission alleged to have caused the injury or condition, or one year of the time the patient or his or her representative discovered or reasonably should have discovered that the injury or condition was caused by said act or omission, whichever period expires later,

    RCW 4.16.350 · official text (Washington State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

Washington courts have ruled on parts of this section more than once: the legislature's own note cites DeYoung v. Providence Medical Center (1998), and in 2023 the state Supreme Court ruled on the eight-year outer limit again (quoted under the exceptions below). The text here is the statute as printed. Ask a Washington attorney how it applies today.

When the clock starts

Three years from the act or omission, or one year from when the patient discovered (or should have discovered) that it caused the injury, whichever expires later.

Outer limit: 8 years

Even when the regular period has not run, the statute sets a final cut-off counted from the act or omission.

except that in no event shall an action be commenced more than eight years after said act or omission:

RCW 4.16.350 · official text (Washington State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

For example, counted from 15 January 2025, the general deadline would be Health care negligence: 15 January 2028, with the outer limit on 15 January 2033. Exceptions below can move it.

Exceptions and special rules for medical malpractice

Fraud, concealment or a foreign body

The time is tolled upon proof of fraud, intentional concealment, or a foreign body with no therapeutic or diagnostic purpose.

PROVIDED, That the time for commencement of an action is tolled upon proof of fraud, intentional concealment, or the presence of a foreign body not intended to have a therapeutic or diagnostic purpose or effect,

RCW 4.16.350 · official text (Washington State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

Minors: parents' knowledge counts

For this section, the knowledge of a custodial parent or guardian is imputed to a child, so the general minors rule in RCW 4.16.190 does not simply pause the clock.

For purposes of this section, notwithstanding RCW 4.16.190, the knowledge of a custodial parent or guardian shall be imputed to a person under the age of eighteen years,

RCW 4.16.350 · official text (Washington State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

The eight-year outer limit: held unconstitutional in 2023

In Bennett v. United States (opinion filed December 7, 2023), the Washington Supreme Court held that the eight-year statute of repose in this section violates the state constitution. The limit is still printed in the statute, so this page and the calculator show it as written. We do not summarize the rest of the opinion: ask a Washington attorney how it affects a claim.

we hold that RCW 4.16.350(3)'s statute of repose violates the independent state law protections of article I, section 12.

Bennett v. United States, No. 101300-1 (Wash. Dec. 7, 2023) · official text (Washington Supreme Court (Washington Courts)), read on 13 November 2026[2]

Rules that pause or extend deadlines in Washington

These general rules apply to many Washington deadlines, including this one, subject to their own conditions.

Minors, incompetence and imprisonment before sentencing

If the person with the claim is under 18, legally incompetent, or imprisoned on a criminal charge before sentencing when the claim accrues, that time is not counted.

the time of such disability shall not be a part of the time limited for the commencement of action.

RCW 4.16.190 · official text (Washington State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

Defendant out of state or concealed

Time while the person being sued is out of the state or concealed is not counted.

the time of his or her absence or concealment shall not be deemed or taken as any part of the time limit for the commencement of such action.

RCW 4.16.180 · official text (Washington State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

Filing versus service: 90 days

An action counts as commenced when the complaint is filed or the summons is served, but the other step must follow within 90 days or the filing does not stop the clock.

For the purpose of tolling any statute of limitations an action shall be deemed commenced when the complaint is filed or summons is served whichever occurs first.

RCW 4.16.170 · official text (Washington State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

If the claim is against a government

Claims against local governments: 60-day waiting period

A tort claim against a local government must first be presented; suit cannot start until 60 days later, and the deadline is tolled during those 60 days.

The applicable period of limitations within which an action must be commenced shall be tolled during the sixty calendar day period.

RCW 4.96.020(4) · official text (Washington State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[3]
