Washington statute of limitations for debt collection

Generally 6 or 3 years under RCW 4.16.040(1) and related sections. Statute text, when the clock starts, exceptions and a deadline calculator. Read on 13 November 2026.

What the Washington statute says

Washington sets more than one period for debt collection claims, depending on the kind of claim:

  • Written contract 6 years

    The following actions shall be commenced within six years:

    (1) An action upon a contract in writing, or liability express or implied arising out of a written agreement,

    RCW 4.16.040(1) · official text (Washington State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

  • Account receivable 6 years

    The following actions shall be commenced within six years:

    (2) An action upon an account receivable.

    RCW 4.16.040(2) · official text (Washington State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

  • Contract not in writing 3 years

    The following actions shall be commenced within three years:

    (3) Except as provided in RCW 4.16.040(2), an action upon a contract or liability, express or implied, which is not in writing, and does not arise out of any written instrument;

    RCW 4.16.080(3) · official text (Washington State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

A deadline to sue is not the end of the debt. The statutes quoted on this page limit when a lawsuit can be brought; they do not say that the debt is cancelled or erased once the period has run. The federal rule quoted on this page (Regulation F) applies to a “debt collector” as that regulation defines the term (12 CFR 1006.2(i)), so it does not cover every creditor. What appears on a credit report follows a different federal law that this site does not cover. Whether a payment or a written acknowledgment changes the deadline depends on state law; this site mentions it only where a quoted statute says so.

When the clock starts

From the day the claim accrues. For a mutual open and current account, the section says it accrues at the last item proved on either side.

For example, counted from 15 January 2025, the general deadline would be Written contract: 15 January 2031; Account receivable: 15 January 2031; Contract not in writing: 15 January 2028. Exceptions below can move it.

Exceptions and special rules for debt collection

What counts as an account receivable

An account receivable is any obligation for payment incurred in the ordinary course of the creditor's business or profession.

For purposes of this section, an account receivable is any obligation for payment incurred in the ordinary course of the claimant's business or profession, whether arising from one or more transactions and whether or not earned by performance.

RCW 4.16.040(2) · official text (Washington State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

Mutual open accounts

For a mutual open and current account, the clock starts at the last item on either side, unless more than a year passed between items.

the cause of action shall be deemed to have accrued from the time of the last item proved in the account on either side,

RCW 4.16.150 · official text (Washington State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

Federal rule on time-barred consumer debt

Under the federal Regulation F, a debt collector must not sue or threaten to sue a consumer over a debt once the applicable statute of limitations has expired.

A debt collector must not bring or threaten to bring a legal action against a consumer to collect a time-barred debt.

12 CFR 1006.26(b) · official text (eCFR, Office of the Federal Register), read on 13 November 2026[2]

Rules that pause or extend deadlines in Washington

These general rules apply to many Washington deadlines, including this one, subject to their own conditions.

Minors, incompetence and imprisonment before sentencing

If the person with the claim is under 18, legally incompetent, or imprisoned on a criminal charge before sentencing when the claim accrues, that time is not counted.

the time of such disability shall not be a part of the time limited for the commencement of action.

RCW 4.16.190 · official text (Washington State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

Defendant out of state or concealed

Time while the person being sued is out of the state or concealed is not counted.

the time of his or her absence or concealment shall not be deemed or taken as any part of the time limit for the commencement of such action.

RCW 4.16.180 · official text (Washington State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

Filing versus service: 90 days

An action counts as commenced when the complaint is filed or the summons is served, but the other step must follow within 90 days or the filing does not stop the clock.

For the purpose of tolling any statute of limitations an action shall be deemed commenced when the complaint is filed or summons is served whichever occurs first.

RCW 4.16.170 · official text (Washington State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

If the claim is against a government

Claims against local governments: 60-day waiting period

A tort claim against a local government must first be presented; suit cannot start until 60 days later, and the deadline is tolled during those 60 days.

The applicable period of limitations within which an action must be commenced shall be tolled during the sixty calendar day period.

RCW 4.96.020(4) · official text (Washington State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[3]

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the statute text. General information, not legal advice.

What is the statute of limitations for debt collection in Washington State?

Six years for a written contract and six years for an account receivable under chapter 4.16 RCW, and three years for a contract that is not in writing.[1]
