North Carolina statute of limitations for medical malpractice

What the North Carolina statute says

  • Professional malpractice 3 years

    Provided nothing herein shall be construed to reduce the statute of limitation in any such case below three years.

    N.C. Gen. Stat. § 1-15(c) · official text (North Carolina General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[1]

When the clock starts

The claim accrues at the last act of the defendant giving rise to it. If the injury is not readily apparent and is discovered two or more years later, suit must be brought within one year of discovery, but never in less than three years overall.

a cause of action for malpractice arising out of the performance of or failure to perform professional services shall be deemed to accrue at the time of the occurrence of the last act of the defendant giving rise to the cause of action:

N.C. Gen. Stat. § 1-15(c) · official text (North Carolina General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[1]

Outer limit: 4 years

Even when the regular period has not run, the statute sets a final cut-off counted from the act or omission.

Provided further, that in no event shall an action be commenced more than four years from the last act of the defendant giving rise to the cause of action:

N.C. Gen. Stat. § 1-15(c) · official text (North Carolina General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[1]

For example, counted from 15 January 2025, the general deadline would be Professional malpractice: 15 January 2028, with the outer limit on 15 January 2029. Exceptions below can move it.

Exceptions and special rules for medical malpractice

Where the three years come from

Section 1-15(c) sets when a malpractice claim accrues and says its discovery rule cannot cut the period “below three years”; the three-year period itself is in the list of three-year actions in § 1-52, which includes injuries to the person not arising on contract. Reading the two sections together this way is our interpretation of the text.

Within three years an action -

(5) For criminal conversation, or for any other injury to the person or rights of another, not arising on contract and not hereafter enumerated,

N.C. Gen. Stat. § 1-52(5) · official text (North Carolina General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[2]

Foreign object: up to 10 years

For a foreign object left in the body, one year from discovery, but no more than 10 years from the last act.

but in no event may the action be commenced more than 10 years from the last act of the defendant giving rise to the cause of action.

N.C. Gen. Stat. § 1-15(c) · official text (North Carolina General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[1]

Children: until age 10 (or 19)

If the normal time runs out before the child turns 10, the action may be brought until the 10th birthday (with further rules for abused or neglected children and children in State custody).

(1) If the time limitations specified in G.S. 1-15(c) expire before the minor attains the full age of 10 years, the action may be brought any time before the minor attains the full age of 10 years.

N.C. Gen. Stat. § 1-17(c)(1) · official text (North Carolina General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[3]

Rules that pause or extend deadlines in North Carolina

These general rules apply to many North Carolina deadlines, including this one, subject to their own conditions.

Minors and people who are insane or incompetent

A person under 18, insane or incompetent when the claim accrues may sue within the period after the disability is removed.

(a) A person entitled to commence an action who is under a disability at the time the cause of action accrued may bring his or her action within the time limited in this Subchapter, after the disability is removed,

N.C. Gen. Stat. § 1-17(a) · official text (North Carolina General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[3]

If the claim is against a government

Claims against State agencies: Industrial Commission

Tort claims against State departments and agencies go to the Industrial Commission within three years (two years after a death).

All claims against any and all State departments, institutions, and agencies shall henceforth be forever barred unless a claim be filed with the Industrial Commission within three years after the accrual of such claim,

N.C. Gen. Stat. § 143-299 · official text (North Carolina General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[4]
