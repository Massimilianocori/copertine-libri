Credit card debt

Statute of limitations on credit card debt by state

The periods to sue on a debt in eleven states, from the statute text. Among the sections quoted on this site, only Arizona's names credit cards; the others do not say which debt period covers a card account.

Among the limitations sections quoted on this site for eleven states, only Arizona’s names credit cards. The other sections set periods for debts, written or oral contracts, accounts or consumer debt, and do not say which of them covers a credit card account; that depends on the card agreement and on how courts read the statute, so it is a question for an attorney in the state. General periods from the statute text read on 13 November 2026. “Names credit cards” marks a period whose quoted text mentions credit cards.

* Florida, hospital medical debt (from referral to collection): For this kind of debt the three years run from the date the hospital referred the debt to a collector, not from the missed payment.

* Ohio, consumer debt (personal, family or household): For consumer debt the statute says the claim accrues 30 days after the later of the last charge or the last payment; the calculator adds those 30 days to the date you enter.

* Virginia, medical debt (from the final invoice due date): For medical debt the three years run from the due date of the final invoice (or the breach of a payment plan).

* Pennsylvania, note or similar written instrument: Negotiable notes also come under the Commercial Code, which gives six years (13 Pa.C.S. § 3118, quoted under the exceptions): ask a Pennsylvania attorney which period applies.

A deadline to sue is not the end of the debt. The statutes quoted on this page limit when a lawsuit can be brought; they do not say that the debt is cancelled or erased once the period has run. The federal rule quoted on this page (Regulation F) applies to a “debt collector” as that regulation defines the term (12 CFR 1006.2(i)), so it does not cover every creditor. What appears on a credit report follows a different federal law that this site does not cover. Whether a payment or a written acknowledgment changes the deadline depends on state law; this site mentions it only where a quoted statute says so.

The statute that names credit cards

Arizona: 6 years

A. An action for debt shall be commenced and prosecuted within six years after the cause of action accrues, and not afterward, if the indebtedness is evidenced by or founded on either of the following:

2. A credit card as defined in section 13-2101, paragraph 3, subdivision (a).

Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 12-548(A)(2) · official text (Arizona State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

Arizona's period wins conflicts with other states

For written-contract and credit card debts, if another state's deadline conflicts with Arizona's, the Arizona section applies.

B. If there is a conflict between another jurisdiction and this state relating to the statute of limitations for a debt action as described in subsection A of this section, this section applies.

Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 12-548(B) · official text (Arizona State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

The federal rule on time-barred debt

Federal rule on time-barred consumer debt

Under the federal Regulation F, a debt collector must not sue or threaten to sue a consumer over a debt once the applicable statute of limitations has expired.

A debt collector must not bring or threaten to bring a legal action against a consumer to collect a time-barred debt.

12 CFR 1006.26(b) · official text (eCFR, Office of the Federal Register), read on 13 November 2026[2]

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the statute text. General information, not legal advice.

What is the statute of limitations for credit card debt?

Among the limitations statutes quoted on this site, only Arizona's names credit cards: six years under Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 12-548(A)(2). The others set periods for debts, contracts, accounts or consumer debt without naming cards, for example Texas four years for a debt (Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 16.004(a)(3)), Minnesota six years for consumer debt (Minn. Stat. § 541.053) and Pennsylvania four years for an obligation founded upon a writing (42 Pa.C.S. § 5525(a)(8)). Which one covers a card account is a question for an attorney in the state.[1][3][4][5]
