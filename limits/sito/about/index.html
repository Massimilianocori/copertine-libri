About Statute of Limitations by State

What this site is, what it publishes and how it is paid for.

Statute of Limitations by State is an independent publication, part of a small network of websites that turn official sources into plain-language guides. It shows the general deadlines to start common civil lawsuits in ten states, quoting the statute itself, linking to the legislature's official website and giving the date we read it.

We are not lawyers and not a law firm, we do not give legal advice, and we do not answer questions about individual cases. Nothing on this site creates an attorney-client relationship. We do not refer readers to lawyers and are not paid by lawyers to recommend them.

What we publish

  • Only periods that are written in a statute we have read, with the text quoted word for word.
  • Where a statute does not state a deadline for a claim (Washington wrongful death, for example), we do not publish a page instead of guessing.
  • Where a state has more than one possible period, we show all of them and say that an attorney can tell which one applies.
  • Court decisions can change how a statute applies. We say so where we know a statute has been tested in court, but we do not summarize case law.

Why only ten states

We started with states whose legislatures publish their codes in a form we can read and check automatically. California and New York are not included: their official code websites block automated reading, and we do not work around that.

How the site is paid for

The site shows advertising served by Google AdSense on pages with content. Advertisers do not choose or change what we publish. We do not run lead forms and do not sell or share anything you type.

General information, not legal advice, and not a substitute for the advice of an attorney. The deadline in a real case can be shorter or longer than the general period shown here: exceptions, notice rules and court decisions can change it, and court rules decide when a case counts as filed.
