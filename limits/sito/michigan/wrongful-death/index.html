Michigan · Wrongful death

Michigan statute of limitations for wrongful death

Generally 3 years under MCL 600.5805(2). Statute text, when the clock starts, exceptions and a deadline calculator. Read on 13 November 2026.

3 yearsDeath of a person · MCL 600.5805(2)
4exceptions and tolling rules quoted on this page

Estimate, not legal advice. Deadlines can be shorter or longer than the general period. Talk to a licensed attorney in Michigan.

What the Michigan statute says

  • Death of a person 3 years

    the period of limitations is 3 years after the time of the death or injury for all actions to recover damages for the death of a person or for injury to a person or property.

    MCL 600.5805(2) · official text (Michigan Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

If the death resulted from medical care, the claim can be an action charging malpractice, which has its own two-year period and six-year outer limit (MCL 600.5805(8), 600.5838a): see the Michigan medical malpractice page.

When the clock starts

The statute counts from “the time of the death or injury” and does not say which of the two dates controls when they differ. The calculator counts from the date you enter: if the injury happened before the death, entering the date of the injury gives the earlier, more cautious date. Ask a Michigan attorney which date applies.

For example, counted from 15 January 2025, the general deadline would be Death of a person: 15 January 2028. Exceptions below can move it.

Exceptions and special rules for wrongful death

Personal representative: two years after letters of authority

If the person dies before the period runs out (or within 30 days after), the personal representative may sue within 2 years after letters of authority are issued, but never later than 3 years after the period has run.

may be commenced by the personal representative of the deceased person at any time within 2 years after letters of authority are issued although the period of limitations has run.

(4) Notwithstanding subsections (1) to (3), an action shall not be commenced under this section later than 3 years after the period of limitations has run.

MCL 600.5852 · official text (Michigan Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[2]

Rules that pause or extend deadlines in Michigan

These general rules apply to many Michigan deadlines, including this one, subject to their own conditions.

Minors and insanity: one year after it ends

If the person with the claim is under 18 or insane when it accrues, they have one year after the disability ends, even if the period has run.

shall have 1 year after the disability is removed through death or otherwise, to make the entry or bring the action although the period of limitations has run.

MCL 600.5851(1) · official text (Michigan Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[3]

Fraudulent concealment: two years from discovery

If the person liable hides the claim or their identity, the action may be brought within two years of discovery.

the action may be commenced at any time within 2 years after the person who is entitled to bring the action discovers, or should have discovered, the existence of the claim or the identity of the person who is liable for the claim,

MCL 600.5855 · official text (Michigan Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[4]

Defendant outside Michigan

Absences of more than two months at a time are not counted, unless the defendant could be served anyway.

any and all periods of absence in excess of 2 months at a time shall not be counted as any part of the time limited for the commencement of the action

MCL 600.5853 · official text (Michigan Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[5]

If the claim is against a government

Claims against the State of Michigan

A notice or claim must be filed with the court of claims within one year, and within six months for property damage or personal injuries.

(4) For a claim against this state for property damage or personal injuries, the claimant shall file the claim or notice under subsection (1) with the clerk of the court of claims within 6 months after the event that gives rise to the claim.

MCL 600.6431(4) · official text (Michigan Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[6]
