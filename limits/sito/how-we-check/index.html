How we check

How we check every deadline

Our sources, how we verify each quotation and period, and how the calculator counts.

Every period on this site comes from a statute we downloaded from the official website of the state legislature (and, for the federal debt rule, from the eCFR; one Washington court ruling comes from the state courts' website). On 13 November 2026 we saved 81 official pages and documents and recorded a fingerprint of each. Before every update a separate program re-reads the saved copies, checks that each quotation appears word for word, and reads the number of years again from the statute text to compare it with what each page shows.

What we check

  • Quotations: every quoted passage (the 133 period quotations and all the exceptions) must appear in the saved official text.
  • Periods: the number of years is read again from the statute wording (“within two years”, “WITHIN FOUR YEARS”, “3 years”) and must match every page and the calculator.
  • Calculator: the code that runs in your browser is tested against an independent calculation on hundreds of dates, including 29 February and the dates when Florida and Ohio changed their law.
  • Dates read: each quotation shows when we read the official text. We re-read all statutes at least once a year and after each legislative session.

How the calculator counts

  • It adds the number of years in the statute to the date you enter (the same day and month). If you enter 29 February and the deadline year has no 29 February, it shows 28 February, the earlier date.
  • It does not move a deadline that falls on a weekend or court holiday. Court rules sometimes do, but do not rely on it.
  • Where a statute fixes a different start (Ohio consumer debt: 30 days after the last charge or payment) or a law changed the period for older claims (Florida negligence before March 24, 2023), it applies that rule. For Ohio contract claims that accrued before June 16, 2021 it shows no date and quotes the transition rule of Senate Bill 13 instead, because the deadline depends on the period that applied before.
  • It does not compute notice deadlines for claims against a government: it lists the rules that the state page quotes.
  • It does not apply tolling or exceptions: it lists them so you can ask an attorney.

What we do not do

We do not read or work around websites that block automated access (this is why California and New York are not on the site), and we do not publish a deadline that is not written in a statute. We do not summarize court decisions, with one exception: where a state supreme court has held a limit printed on this site unconstitutional (Washington, medical malpractice), we quote the holding and link the opinion on the court's own website, and still show the statute as printed.
