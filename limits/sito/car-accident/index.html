Car accident

Car accident statute of limitations by state

A crash can lead to an injury claim and a property damage claim, each with its own deadline. For injuries, general periods in eleven states range from two years to six years. Statute text for each state.

The limitations statutes quoted on this site do not set one deadline for “car accidents”. A crash can lead to separate claims, each with its own deadline: this page shows injuries to a person under the personal injury period, damage to the car under the property damage period, and a death under the wrongful death period. Some statutes have separate wording for particular motor vehicle claims (for example Massachusetts General Laws c. 260, § 4), which this table does not use. Matching a crash to these periods is our reading of the statutes, not a quoted rule: the kind of claim, who is sued (for example a government) and the exceptions can change the deadline, so ask an attorney in the state. Insurance claims and their time limits are not covered here.

State by state

Texas: injuries 2 years

(a) Except as provided by Sections 16.010, 16.0031, and 16.0045, a person must bring suit for trespass for injury to the estate or to the property of another, conversion of personal property, taking or detaining the personal property of another, personal injury, forcible entry and detainer, and forcible detainer not later than two years after the day the cause of action accrues.

Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 16.003(a) · official text (Texas Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

Damage to the vehicle: 2 years (injury to property, Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 16.003(a)).

Florida: injuries 2 years

(5) WITHIN TWO YEARS.—(a) An action founded on negligence.

Fla. Stat. § 95.11(5)(a) · official text (Florida Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[2]

Damage to the vehicle: 2 years (damage caused by negligence (accrued after March 24, 2023), Fla. Stat. § 95.11(5)(a)).

Ohio: injuries 2 years

an action based on a product liability claim and an action for bodily injury or injuring personal property shall be brought within two years after the cause of action accrues.

Ohio Rev. Code § 2305.10(A) · official text (Ohio Legislative Service Commission), read on 13 November 2026[3]

Damage to the vehicle: 2 years (injury to personal property, Ohio Rev. Code § 2305.10(A)).

Washington: injuries 3 years

The following actions shall be commenced within three years:

(2) An action for taking, detaining, or injuring personal property, including an action for the specific recovery thereof, or for any other injury to the person or rights of another not hereinafter enumerated;

RCW 4.16.080(2) · official text (Washington State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[4]

Damage to the vehicle: 3 years (taking, detaining or injuring personal property, RCW 4.16.080(2)).

Virginia: injuries 2 years

A. Unless otherwise provided in this section or by other statute, every action for personal injuries, whatever the theory of recovery, and every action for damages resulting from fraud, shall be brought within two years after the cause of action accrues.

Va. Code § 8.01-243(A) · official text (Virginia General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[5]

Damage to the vehicle: 5 years (injury to property, Va. Code § 8.01-243(B)).

North Carolina: injuries 3 years

Within three years an action -

(16) Unless otherwise provided by law, for personal injury or physical damage to claimant's property, the cause of action, except in causes of actions referred to in G.S. 1-15(c), shall not accrue until bodily harm to the claimant or physical damage to his property becomes apparent or ought reasonably to have become apparent to the claimant, whichever event first occurs.

N.C. Gen. Stat. § 1-52(16) · official text (North Carolina General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[6]

Damage to the vehicle: 3 years (taking or injuring goods, N.C. Gen. Stat. § 1-52(4)).

Michigan: injuries 3 years

(2) Except as otherwise provided in this section, the period of limitations is 3 years after the time of the death or injury for all actions to recover damages for the death of a person or for injury to a person or property.

MCL 600.5805(2) · official text (Michigan Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[7]

Damage to the vehicle: 3 years (injury to property, MCL 600.5805(2)).

Arizona: injuries 2 years

there shall be commenced and prosecuted within two years after the cause of action accrues, and not afterward, the following actions:

1. For injuries done to the person of another including causes of action for medical malpractice as defined in section 12-561.

Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 12-542(1) · official text (Arizona State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[8]

Damage to the vehicle: 2 years (injury to the estate or property, Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 12-542(3)).

Massachusetts: injuries 3 years

Section 2A. Except as otherwise provided, actions of tort, actions of contract to recover for personal injuries, and actions of replevin, shall be commenced only within three years next after the cause of action accrues.

Mass. Gen. Laws c. 260, § 2A · official text (Massachusetts Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[9]

Damage to the vehicle: 3 years (tort (including damage to property), Mass. Gen. Laws c. 260, § 2A).

Minnesota: injuries 6 years

Except where the Uniform Commercial Code otherwise prescribes, the following actions shall be commenced within six years:

(5) for criminal conversation, or for any other injury to the person or rights of another, not arising on contract, and not hereinafter enumerated;

Minn. Stat. § 541.05, subd. 1(5) · official text (Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes), read on 13 November 2026[10]

Damage to the vehicle: 6 years (taking, detaining or injuring personal property, Minn. Stat. § 541.05, subd. 1(4)).

Pennsylvania: injuries 2 years

The following actions and proceedings must be commenced within two years:

(2) An action to recover damages for injuries to the person or for the death of an individual caused by the wrongful act or neglect or unlawful violence or negligence of another.

42 Pa.C.S. § 5524(2) · official text (Pennsylvania General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[11]

Damage to the vehicle: 2 years (taking, detaining or injuring personal property, 42 Pa.C.S. § 5524(3)).

Florida changed its negligence period in 2023

Negligence: from four years to two (HB 837)

Effective 24 March 2023

Chapter 2023-15, Laws of Florida, moved “an action founded on negligence” from the four-year list to the two-year list. It was approved by the Governor and took effect on March 24, 2023, and the change applies to causes of action accruing after that date. Negligence claims that accrued on or before March 24, 2023 kept the old four-year period.

Section 28. The amendments made by this act to s. 95.11, Florida Statutes, apply to causes of action accruing after the effective date of this act.

Section 31. This act shall take effect upon becoming a law.

Approved by the Governor March 24, 2023.

Laws of Florida ch. 2023-15, ss. 3, 28 and 31 · official text (Florida Department of State), read on 13 November 2026[12]

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the statute text. General information, not legal advice.

What is the statute of limitations for a car accident?

It depends on the state and on the claim. Matching a crash to these sections is our reading of the statutes, not a quoted rule. For injuries, the general period is two years in Texas (Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 16.003(a)), three years in North Carolina (N.C. Gen. Stat. § 1-52(16)) and two years in Florida for negligence that accrued after March 24, 2023 (Fla. Stat. § 95.11(5)(a)). For damage to the car, Virginia gives five years (Va. Code § 8.01-243(B)) and Texas two years (Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 16.003(a)). The table on this page shows every state; a claim against a government can need a written notice within months.[1][6][2][5]
