Pennsylvania statute of limitations on debt

What the Pennsylvania statute says

Pennsylvania sets more than one period for debt collection claims, depending on the kind of claim:

  • Written contract or obligation 4 years

    (a) General rule.--Except as provided for in subsection (b), the following actions and proceedings must be commenced within four years:

    (8) An action upon a contract, obligation or liability founded upon a writing not specified in paragraph (7), under seal or otherwise, except an action subject to another limitation specified in this subchapter.

    42 Pa.C.S. § 5525(a)(8) · official text (Pennsylvania General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[1]

  • Oral (express) contract 4 years

    (a) General rule.--Except as provided for in subsection (b), the following actions and proceedings must be commenced within four years:

    (3) An action upon an express contract not founded upon an instrument in writing.

    42 Pa.C.S. § 5525(a)(3) · official text (Pennsylvania General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[1]

  • Note or similar written instrument 4 years

    (a) General rule.--Except as provided for in subsection (b), the following actions and proceedings must be commenced within four years:

    (7) An action upon a negotiable or nonnegotiable bond, note or other similar instrument in writing.

    42 Pa.C.S. § 5525(a)(7) · official text (Pennsylvania General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[1]

    Negotiable notes also come under the Commercial Code, which gives six years (13 Pa.C.S. § 3118, quoted under the exceptions): ask a Pennsylvania attorney which period applies.

A deadline to sue is not the end of the debt. The statutes quoted on this page limit when a lawsuit can be brought; they do not say that the debt is cancelled or erased once the period has run. The federal rule quoted on this page (Regulation F) applies to a “debt collector” as that regulation defines the term (12 CFR 1006.2(i)), so it does not cover every creditor. What appears on a credit report follows a different federal law that this site does not cover. Whether a payment or a written acknowledgment changes the deadline depends on state law; this site mentions it only where a quoted statute says so.

When the clock starts

Four years from the time the claim accrued (§ 5502(a)). For a missed payment, when the claim accrues can depend on the contract; the calculator counts from the date you enter. The statute does not name credit cards: which paragraph covers a card account is a question for a Pennsylvania attorney.

(a) General rule.--The time within which a matter must be commenced under this chapter shall be computed, except as otherwise provided by subsection (b) or by any other provision of this chapter, from the time the cause of action accrued,

42 Pa.C.S. § 5502(a) · official text (Pennsylvania General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[2]

For example, counted from 15 January 2025, the general deadline would be Written contract or obligation: 15 January 2029; Oral (express) contract: 15 January 2029; Note or similar written instrument: 15 January 2029. Exceptions below can move it.

Exceptions and special rules for debt collection

Notes payable on demand

For a note or similar instrument payable on demand, § 5525(a)(7) counts the four years from the later of the demand or any payment of principal or interest.

Where such an instrument is payable upon demand, the time within which an action on it must be commenced shall be computed from the later of either demand or any payment of principal of or interest on the instrument.

42 Pa.C.S. § 5525(a)(7) · official text (Pennsylvania General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[1]

Negotiable notes: six years in the Commercial Code

Pennsylvania's Commercial Code has its own section for negotiable instruments: six years after the due date (or the accelerated due date) for a note payable at a definite time, and six years after a demand for a note payable on demand. Section 5525(a)(7) lists “a negotiable or nonnegotiable” note at four years; neither text says which one governs a particular note, so that is a question for a Pennsylvania attorney.

(a) Note payable at definite time.--Except as provided in subsection (e), an action to enforce the obligation of a party to pay a note payable at a definite time must be commenced within six years after the due date or dates stated in the note or, if a due date is accelerated, within six years after the accelerated due date.

(b) Note payable on demand.--Except as provided in subsection (d) or (e), if demand for payment is made to the maker of a note payable on demand, an action to enforce the obligation of a party to pay the note must be commenced within six years after the demand. If no demand for payment is made to the maker, an action to enforce the note is barred if neither principal nor interest on the note has been paid for a continuous period of ten years.

13 Pa.C.S. § 3118(a)-(b) · official text (Pennsylvania General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[3]

Instruments in writing under seal: 20 years

Section 5529 gives 20 years, instead of four, for an action on a written instrument under seal. Section 5525(a)(8) also lists writings “under seal or otherwise” in its four-year list; which section governs a particular sealed document is a question for a Pennsylvania attorney.

(1) Notwithstanding section 5525(7) (relating to four year limitation), an action upon an instrument in writing under seal must be commenced within 20 years.

42 Pa.C.S. § 5529(b)(1) · official text (Pennsylvania General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[4]

Federal rule on time-barred consumer debt

Under the federal Regulation F, a debt collector must not sue or threaten to sue a consumer over a debt once the applicable statute of limitations has expired.

A debt collector must not bring or threaten to bring a legal action against a consumer to collect a time-barred debt.

12 CFR 1006.26(b) · official text (eCFR, Office of the Federal Register), read on 13 November 2026[5]

Rules that pause or extend deadlines in Pennsylvania

These general rules apply to many Pennsylvania deadlines, including this one, subject to their own conditions.

Minors

If the person with the claim is an unemancipated minor (under 18) when the claim accrues, the years of minority are not counted: the same period starts at 18.

(i) If an individual entitled to bring a civil action is an unemancipated minor at the time the cause of action accrues, the period of minority shall not be deemed a portion of the time period within which the action must be commenced. Such person shall have the same time for commencing an action after attaining majority as is allowed to others by the provisions of this subchapter.

(ii) As used in this paragraph, the term "minor" shall mean any individual who has not yet attained 18 years of age.

42 Pa.C.S. § 5533(b)(1) · official text (Pennsylvania General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[6]

Insanity or imprisonment does not extend the deadline

Unless another statute says otherwise, insanity or imprisonment does not add time.

(a) General rule.--Except as otherwise provided by statute, insanity or imprisonment does not extend the time limited by this subchapter for the commencement of a matter.

42 Pa.C.S. § 5533(a) · official text (Pennsylvania General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[6]

Defendant out of Pennsylvania or using a false name

If the person to be sued is outside Pennsylvania when the claim accrues, the period runs from the return; a later continuous absence of four months or more, or living in Pennsylvania under a false name, is not counted. It does not apply while that person can be served without personal delivery in Pennsylvania.

(a) General rule.--If, when a cause of action accrues against a person, he is without this Commonwealth, the time within which the action or proceeding must be commenced shall be computed from the time he comes into or returns to this Commonwealth.

If, after a cause of action has accrued against a person, he departs from this Commonwealth and remains continuously absent therefrom for four months or more, or he resides within this Commonwealth under a false name which is unknown to the person entitled to commence the action or proceeding, the time of his absence or residence within this Commonwealth under such a false name is not a part of the time within which the action or proceeding must be commenced.

(3) While jurisdiction over the person of the defendant can be obtained without personal delivery of process to him within this Commonwealth.

42 Pa.C.S. § 5532 · official text (Pennsylvania General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[7]

Lawsuit stayed by a court or a statute

While starting the lawsuit is stayed by a court or by a statute, the time of the stay is not counted.

(b) Stay of matter.--Where the commencement of a civil action or proceeding has been stayed by a court or by statutory prohibition, the duration of the stay is not a part of the time within which the action or proceeding must be commenced.

42 Pa.C.S. § 5535(b) · official text (Pennsylvania General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[8]

If the claim is against a government

Claims against a government unit: written notice within six months

Before suing a Pennsylvania government unit for damages for an injury to a person or property, a signed written statement must be filed with the government unit (and with the Attorney General for a Commonwealth agency) within six months. Without it, a lawsuit started more than six months after the injury is dismissed unless the court excuses the failure for a reasonable excuse. For government units other than the Commonwealth government, the failure is not a bar if the unit had actual or constructive notice, and if the injuries result in death the time for the notice starts with the death.

(1) Within six months from the date that any injury was sustained or any cause of action accrued, any person who is about to commence any civil action or proceeding within this Commonwealth or elsewhere against a government unit for damages on account of any injury to his person or property under Chapter 85 (relating to matters affecting government units) or otherwise shall file in the office of the government unit, and if the action is against a Commonwealth agency for damages, then also file in the office of the Attorney General, a statement in writing, signed by or in his behalf,

The court shall excuse failure to comply with this requirement upon a showing of reasonable excuse for failure to file such statement.

(ii) If the injuries to an individual result in death, the time for giving notice shall commence with such death.

(iii) Failure to comply with this subsection shall not be a bar if the government unit had actual or constructive notice of the incident or condition giving rise to the claim of a person.

42 Pa.C.S. § 5522(a) · official text (Pennsylvania General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[9]

Actions against government officers: six months

An action against an officer of a government unit for anything done in the execution of the office must be started within six months, unless another period in the subchapter applies.

The following actions and proceedings must be commenced within six months:

(1) An action against any officer of any government unit for anything done in the execution of his office, except an action subject to another limitation specified in this subchapter.

42 Pa.C.S. § 5522(b)(1) · official text (Pennsylvania General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[9]

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the statute text. General information, not legal advice.

What is the statute of limitations on debt in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania gives four years for an action upon a contract, obligation or liability founded upon a writing (42 Pa.C.S. § 5525(a)(8)) and four years for an express contract not founded upon an instrument in writing (42 Pa.C.S. § 5525(a)(3)). The statute does not name credit cards. An action upon an instrument in writing under seal has 20 years under section 5529(b)(1), and the Commercial Code gives six years for negotiable notes (13 Pa.C.S. § 3118).[1][4][3]
