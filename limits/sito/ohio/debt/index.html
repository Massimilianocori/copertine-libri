Ohio · Debt collection

Ohio statute of limitations for debt collection

Generally 6 or 4 years under Ohio Rev. Code § 2305.07(C) and related sections. Statute text, when the clock starts, exceptions and a deadline calculator. Read on 13 November 2026.

What the Ohio statute says

Ohio sets more than one period for debt collection claims, depending on the kind of claim:

  • Consumer debt (personal, family or household) 6 years

    an action arising out of a consumer transaction incurred primarily for personal, family, or household purposes, based upon any contract, agreement, obligation, liability, or promise, express or implied, including an account stated, whether or not reduced to writing or signed by the party to be charged by that transaction, shall be commenced within six years after the cause of action accrued.

    Ohio Rev. Code § 2305.07(C) · official text (Ohio Legislative Service Commission), read on 13 November 2026[1]

    For consumer debt the statute says the claim accrues 30 days after the later of the last charge or the last payment; the calculator adds those 30 days to the date you enter.

  • Written contract (business debt) 6 years

    an action upon a specialty or an agreement, contract, or promise in writing shall be brought within six years after the cause of action accrued.

    Ohio Rev. Code § 2305.06 · official text (Ohio Legislative Service Commission), read on 13 November 2026[2]

  • Contract not in writing (business debt) 4 years

    an action upon a contract not in writing, express or implied, shall be brought within four years after the cause of action accrued.

    Ohio Rev. Code § 2305.07(A) · official text (Ohio Legislative Service Commission), read on 13 November 2026[1]

For claims that accrued before 16 June 2021, a transition rule in Senate Bill 13 (134th General Assembly) applies: the deadline is the earlier of the new period counted from that date or the end of the period that applied before. The rule is quoted under recent changes. The calculator shows no date for those claims; ask an Ohio attorney.

A deadline to sue is not the end of the debt. The statutes quoted on this page limit when a lawsuit can be brought; they do not say that the debt is cancelled or erased once the period has run. The federal rule quoted on this page (Regulation F) applies to a “debt collector” as that regulation defines the term (12 CFR 1006.2(i)), so it does not cover every creditor. What appears on a credit report follows a different federal law that this site does not cover. Whether a payment or a written acknowledgment changes the deadline depends on state law; this site mentions it only where a quoted statute says so.

When the clock starts

For consumer debt, the statute fixes the start date itself: thirty days after the later of the last charge or the last payment made by or for the consumer. For business debts the period runs from when the claim accrues, usually the missed payment.

For purposes of this division, a cause of action accrues thirty calendar days after the date of the last charge or payment by, or on behalf of, the consumer, whichever is later.

Ohio Rev. Code § 2305.07(C) · official text (Ohio Legislative Service Commission), read on 13 November 2026[1]

For example, counted from 15 January 2025, the general deadline would be Consumer debt (personal, family or household): 14 February 2031; Written contract (business debt): 15 January 2031; Contract not in writing (business debt): 15 January 2029. Exceptions below can move it.

Exceptions and special rules for debt collection

Federal rule on time-barred consumer debt

Under the federal Regulation F, a debt collector must not sue or threaten to sue a consumer over a debt once the applicable statute of limitations has expired.

A debt collector must not bring or threaten to bring a legal action against a consumer to collect a time-barred debt.

12 CFR 1006.26(b) · official text (eCFR, Office of the Federal Register), read on 13 November 2026[3]

Rules that pause or extend deadlines in Ohio

These general rules apply to many Ohio deadlines, including this one, subject to their own conditions.

Minors and people of unsound mind

If the person with the claim is a minor or of unsound mind when it accrues, the period starts after the disability ends.

is, at the time the cause of action accrues, within the age of minority or of unsound mind, the person may bring it within the respective times limited by those sections, after the disability is removed.

Ohio Rev. Code § 2305.16 · official text (Ohio Legislative Service Commission), read on 13 November 2026[4]

Defendant out of state, absconding or hiding

Time while the person being sued is out of Ohio, has absconded or is concealed is not counted (this does not apply to statutes of repose).

After the cause of action accrues if the person departs from the state, absconds, or conceals self, the time of the person's absence or concealment shall not be computed as any part of a period within which the action must be brought.

Ohio Rev. Code § 2305.15(A) · official text (Ohio Legislative Service Commission), read on 13 November 2026[5]

Saving statute: one year to refile

If a timely case fails otherwise than on the merits, a new action may be filed within one year or within the original period, whichever is later.

may commence a new action within one year after the date of the reversal of the judgment or the plaintiff's failure otherwise than upon the merits or within the period of the original applicable statute of limitations, whichever occurs later.

Ohio Rev. Code § 2305.19(A) · official text (Ohio Legislative Service Commission), read on 13 November 2026[6]

If the claim is against a government

Claims against cities, counties and other political subdivisions

An action against a political subdivision for injury, death or loss to person or property must be brought within two years, or any shorter period the Revised Code sets.

shall be brought within two years after the cause of action accrues, or within any applicable shorter period of time for bringing the action provided by the Revised Code.

Ohio Rev. Code § 2744.04(A) · official text (Ohio Legislative Service Commission), read on 13 November 2026[7]

Recent changes to this deadline

Contracts: written 8 → 6 years, oral 6 → 4 years (Senate Bill 13)

Effective 16 June 2021

The version of § 2305.06 in force until June 16, 2021 gave eight years for written contracts; § 2305.07 gave six years for contracts not in writing. Senate Bill 13 (134th General Assembly) cut them to six and four years and added the six-year rule for consumer transactions. A transition rule in that bill covers claims that accrued before June 16, 2021 (quoted below on the Ohio pages): our calculator does not compute those dates.

an action upon a specialty or an agreement, contract, or promise in writing shall be brought within eight years after the cause of action accrued.

Ohio Rev. Code § 2305.06 (version of September 28, 2012) · official text (Ohio Legislative Service Commission), read on 13 November 2026[8]

Claims that accrued before June 16, 2021: the transition rule

Effective 16 June 2021

For contract claims that already existed when Senate Bill 13 took effect (June 16, 2021, the effective date shown in the official code), the act sets the deadline at the earlier of two dates: the new period counted from the effective date, or the end of the period that applied before. Which earlier period applied depends on the kind of contract and on when the claim accrued; the calculator does not compute it.

For causes of action that are governed by section 2305.06 of the Revised Code and that accrued prior to the effective date of this act, the period of limitations shall be six years from the effective date of this act or the expiration of the period of limitations in effect prior to the effective date of this act, whichever occurs first.

For causes of action that are governed by division (A) of section 2305.07 of the Revised Code that accrued prior to the effective date of this act, the period of limitations shall be four years from the effective date of this act or the expiration of the period of limitations in effect prior to the effective date of this act, whichever occurs first.

For causes of action that are governed by division (C) of section 2305.07 of the Revised Code that accrued prior to the effective date of this act, the period of limitations shall be six years from the effective date of this act or the expiration of the period of limitations in effect prior to the effective date of this act, whichever occurs first.

Senate Bill 13 (134th General Assembly), Sections 4 and 5 · official text (Ohio General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[9]

§ 2305.06 amended again (Senate Bill 219)

Effective 23 September 2026

The official code shows § 2305.06 amended by Senate Bill 219 (136th General Assembly), effective September 23, 2026. The current text still says six years.

September 23, 2026 – Amended by Senate Bill 219 - 136th General Assembly

Ohio Rev. Code § 2305.06 · official text (Ohio Legislative Service Commission), read on 13 November 2026[2]
