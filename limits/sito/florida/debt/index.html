Florida · Debt collection

Florida statute of limitations for debt collection

Generally 5, 4 or 3 years under Fla. Stat. § 95.11(2)(b) and related sections. Statute text, when the clock starts, exceptions and a deadline calculator. Read on 13 November 2026.

5 yearsWritten contract or obligation · Fla. Stat. § 95.11(2)(b)
4 yearsNot in writing, including store accounts · Fla. Stat. § 95.11(3)(j)
3 yearsHospital medical debt (from referral to collection) · Fla. Stat. § 95.11(4)

What the Florida statute says

Florida sets more than one period for debt collection claims, depending on the kind of claim:

  • Written contract or obligation 5 years

    (2) WITHIN FIVE YEARS.—

    (b) A legal or equitable action on a contract, obligation, or liability founded on a written instrument,

    Fla. Stat. § 95.11(2)(b) · official text (Florida Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

  • Not in writing, including store accounts 4 years

    (3) WITHIN FOUR YEARS.—

    (j) A legal or equitable action on a contract, obligation, or liability not founded on a written instrument, including an action for the sale and delivery of goods, wares, and merchandise, and on store accounts.

    Fla. Stat. § 95.11(3)(j) · official text (Florida Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

  • Hospital medical debt (from referral to collection) 3 years

    (4) WITHIN THREE YEARS.—An action to collect medical debt for services rendered by a facility licensed under chapter 395, provided that the period of limitations shall run from the date on which the facility refers the medical debt to a third party for collection.

    Fla. Stat. § 95.11(4) · official text (Florida Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

    For this kind of debt the three years run from the date the hospital referred the debt to a collector, not from the missed payment.

A deadline to sue is not the end of the debt. The statutes quoted on this page limit when a lawsuit can be brought; they do not say that the debt is cancelled or erased once the period has run. The federal rule quoted on this page (Regulation F) applies to a “debt collector” as that regulation defines the term (12 CFR 1006.2(i)), so it does not cover every creditor. What appears on a credit report follows a different federal law that this site does not cover. Whether a payment or a written acknowledgment changes the deadline depends on state law; this site mentions it only where a quoted statute says so.

When the clock starts

From the day the claim accrues, except hospital medical debt, which runs from the referral to a third party for collection.

For example, counted from 15 January 2025, the general deadline would be Written contract or obligation: 15 January 2030; Not in writing, including store accounts: 15 January 2029; Hospital medical debt (from referral to collection): 15 January 2028. Exceptions below can move it.

Exceptions and special rules for debt collection

A payment on a written obligation pauses the clock

Paying any part of the principal or interest of an obligation founded on a written instrument tolls the deadline.

(f) The payment of any part of the principal or interest of any obligation or liability founded on a written instrument.

Fla. Stat. § 95.051(1)(f) · official text (Florida Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[2]

Promise to pay a barred debt must be in writing

An acknowledgment of, or promise to pay, a time-barred debt counts only if written and signed.

An acknowledgment of, or promise to pay, a debt barred by a statute of limitations must be in writing and signed by the person sought to be charged.

Fla. Stat. § 95.04 · official text (Florida Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[3]

Federal rule on time-barred consumer debt

Under the federal Regulation F, a debt collector must not sue or threaten to sue a consumer over a debt once the applicable statute of limitations has expired.

A debt collector must not bring or threaten to bring a legal action against a consumer to collect a time-barred debt.

12 CFR 1006.26(b) · official text (eCFR, Office of the Federal Register), read on 13 November 2026[4]

Rules that pause or extend deadlines in Florida

These general rules apply to many Florida deadlines, including this one, subject to their own conditions.

What pauses the deadline in Florida (closed list)

Florida lists the reasons that toll a deadline, and says no other disability or reason does. They include the absence of the person to be sued, use of a false name, concealment, adjudicated incapacity, payments on a written obligation and pending arbitration.

(1) The running of the time under any statute of limitations except ss. 95.281, 95.35, and 95.36 is tolled by:(a) Absence from the state of the person to be sued.

(2) A disability or other reason does not toll the running of any statute of limitations except those specified in this section, s. 95.091, the Florida Probate Code, or the Florida Guardianship Law.

Fla. Stat. § 95.051 · official text (Florida Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[2]

Minors and incapacity: seven-year outer limit

Minority tolls the deadline only while the child has no parent or guardian able to sue (or one with an adverse interest), and adjudicated incapacity before the claim tolls it; in both cases the action must be filed within 7 years of the event.

(d) The adjudicated incapacity, before the cause of action accrued, of the person entitled to sue. In any event, the action must be begun within 7 years after the act, event, or occurrence giving rise to the cause of action.

(i) The minority or previously adjudicated incapacity of the person entitled to sue during any period of time in which a parent, guardian, or guardian ad litem does not exist, has an interest adverse to the minor or incapacitated person, or is adjudicated to be incapacitated to sue;

Fla. Stat. § 95.051(1)(d), (i) · official text (Florida Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[2]

If the claim is against a government

Claims against the state, counties and cities

Before suing the state or a local government in tort, the claim must be presented in writing within 3 years (2 years for wrongful death) and denied.

(6)(a) An action may not be instituted on a claim against the state or one of its agencies or subdivisions unless the claimant presents the claim in writing to the appropriate agency

within 3 years after such claim accrues

2. Such action is for wrongful death, the claimant must present the claim in writing to the Department of Financial Services within 2 years after the claim accrues.

Fla. Stat. § 768.28(6)(a) · official text (Florida Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[5]

Deadline to sue the state or a local government

The same section sets its own deadline for the lawsuit: four years after the claim accrues for a negligent or wrongful act, while medical malpractice and wrongful death claims follow the periods in § 95.11(5). The calculator does not apply this section.

(14) Every claim against the state or one of its agencies or subdivisions for damages for a negligent or wrongful act or omission pursuant to this section shall be forever barred unless the civil action is commenced by filing a complaint in the court of appropriate jurisdiction within 4 years after such claim accrues;

Fla. Stat. § 768.28(14) · official text (Florida Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[5]

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the statute text. General information, not legal advice.

What is the statute of limitations on credit card debt in Florida?

Florida gives five years for an action on a contract or obligation founded on a written instrument and four years for one not founded on a written instrument, including store accounts. Which applies depends on the documents behind the account; ask a Florida attorney.[1]
