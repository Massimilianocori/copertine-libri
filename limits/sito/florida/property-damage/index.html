Florida · Property damage

Florida statute of limitations for property damage

Generally 2 or 4 years under Fla. Stat. § 95.11(5)(a) and related sections. Statute text, when the clock starts, exceptions and a deadline calculator. Read on 13 November 2026.

2 yearsDamage caused by negligence (accrued after March 24, 2023) · Fla. Stat. § 95.11(5)(a)
4 yearsTrespass on real property · Fla. Stat. § 95.11(3)(f)
4 yearsTaking, detaining or injuring personal property · Fla. Stat. § 95.11(3)(g)

What the Florida statute says

Florida sets more than one period for property damage claims, depending on the kind of claim:

  • Damage caused by negligence (accrued after March 24, 2023) 2 years

    (5) WITHIN TWO YEARS.—(a) An action founded on negligence.

    Fla. Stat. § 95.11(5)(a) · official text (Florida Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[2]

    Negligence that accrued on or before March 24, 2023: 4 years under the law before March 24, 2023.

    (3) WITHIN FOUR YEARS.—

    (a) An action founded on negligence.

    Laws of Florida ch. 2023-15, s. 28 (old § 95.11(3)(a)) · official text (Florida Department of State), read on 13 November 2026[1]

  • Trespass on real property 4 years

    (3) WITHIN FOUR YEARS.—

    (f) An action for trespass on real property.

    Fla. Stat. § 95.11(3)(f) · official text (Florida Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[2]

  • Taking, detaining or injuring personal property 4 years

    (3) WITHIN FOUR YEARS.—

    (g) An action for taking, detaining, or injuring personal property.

    Fla. Stat. § 95.11(3)(g) · official text (Florida Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[2]

Which period applies depends on how the claim is framed: damage caused by negligence (for example, a car crash) is in the two-year list since March 24, 2023, while trespass on land and injury to personal property are in the four-year list. Ask a Florida attorney which one fits your case.

When the clock starts

From the day the last element of the claim occurs (§ 95.031(1)), usually the damage.

For example, counted from 15 January 2025, the general deadline would be Damage caused by negligence (accrued after March 24, 2023): 15 January 2027; Trespass on real property: 15 January 2029; Taking, detaining or injuring personal property: 15 January 2029. Exceptions below can move it.

Rules that pause or extend deadlines in Florida

These general rules apply to many Florida deadlines, including this one, subject to their own conditions.

What pauses the deadline in Florida (closed list)

Florida lists the reasons that toll a deadline, and says no other disability or reason does. They include the absence of the person to be sued, use of a false name, concealment, adjudicated incapacity, payments on a written obligation and pending arbitration.

(1) The running of the time under any statute of limitations except ss. 95.281, 95.35, and 95.36 is tolled by:(a) Absence from the state of the person to be sued.

(2) A disability or other reason does not toll the running of any statute of limitations except those specified in this section, s. 95.091, the Florida Probate Code, or the Florida Guardianship Law.

Fla. Stat. § 95.051 · official text (Florida Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[3]

Minors and incapacity: seven-year outer limit

Minority tolls the deadline only while the child has no parent or guardian able to sue (or one with an adverse interest), and adjudicated incapacity before the claim tolls it; in both cases the action must be filed within 7 years of the event.

(d) The adjudicated incapacity, before the cause of action accrued, of the person entitled to sue. In any event, the action must be begun within 7 years after the act, event, or occurrence giving rise to the cause of action.

(i) The minority or previously adjudicated incapacity of the person entitled to sue during any period of time in which a parent, guardian, or guardian ad litem does not exist, has an interest adverse to the minor or incapacitated person, or is adjudicated to be incapacitated to sue;

Fla. Stat. § 95.051(1)(d), (i) · official text (Florida Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[3]

If the claim is against a government

Claims against the state, counties and cities

Before suing the state or a local government in tort, the claim must be presented in writing within 3 years (2 years for wrongful death) and denied.

(6)(a) An action may not be instituted on a claim against the state or one of its agencies or subdivisions unless the claimant presents the claim in writing to the appropriate agency

within 3 years after such claim accrues

2. Such action is for wrongful death, the claimant must present the claim in writing to the Department of Financial Services within 2 years after the claim accrues.

Fla. Stat. § 768.28(6)(a) · official text (Florida Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[4]

Deadline to sue the state or a local government

The same section sets its own deadline for the lawsuit: four years after the claim accrues for a negligent or wrongful act, while medical malpractice and wrongful death claims follow the periods in § 95.11(5). The calculator does not apply this section.

(14) Every claim against the state or one of its agencies or subdivisions for damages for a negligent or wrongful act or omission pursuant to this section shall be forever barred unless the civil action is commenced by filing a complaint in the court of appropriate jurisdiction within 4 years after such claim accrues;

Fla. Stat. § 768.28(14) · official text (Florida Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[4]

Recent changes to this deadline

Negligence: from four years to two (HB 837)

Effective 24 March 2023

Chapter 2023-15, Laws of Florida, moved “an action founded on negligence” from the four-year list to the two-year list. It was approved by the Governor and took effect on March 24, 2023, and the change applies to causes of action accruing after that date. Negligence claims that accrued on or before March 24, 2023 kept the old four-year period.

Section 28. The amendments made by this act to s. 95.11, Florida Statutes, apply to causes of action accruing after the effective date of this act.

Section 31. This act shall take effect upon becoming a law.

Approved by the Governor March 24, 2023.

Laws of Florida ch. 2023-15, ss. 3, 28 and 31 · official text (Florida Department of State), read on 13 November 2026[1]
