Wrongful death

Wrongful death statute of limitations by state

General periods in ten states range from two years (Arizona, Florida, North Carolina, Ohio, Texas, Virginia) to three years (Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota). Statute text and exceptions for each state.

A wrongful death claim is brought after a death caused by someone else's wrongful act or negligence, usually by a personal representative or family members. Several states count the period from the date of death. This page compares ten states (claims brought after a death caused by someone else's wrongful act or negligence).

State by state

Texas: 2 years

(b) A person must bring suit not later than two years after the day the cause of action accrues in an action for injury resulting in death. The cause of action accrues on the death of the injured person.

Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 16.003(b) · official text (Texas Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[1]

Florida: 2 years

(5) WITHIN TWO YEARS.—

(e) An action for wrongful death.

Fla. Stat. § 95.11(5)(e) · official text (Florida Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[2]

Ohio: 2 years

a civil action for wrongful death shall be commenced within two years after the decedent's death.

Ohio Rev. Code § 2125.02(F)(1) · official text (Ohio Legislative Service Commission), read on 13 November 2026[3]

Virginia: 2 years

B. Every action under § 8.01-50 shall be brought by the personal representative of the decedent within two years after the death of the injured person.

Va. Code § 8.01-244(B) · official text (Virginia General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[4]

North Carolina: 2 years

Within two years -

(4) Actions for damages on account of the death of a person caused by the wrongful act, neglect or fault of another under G.S. 28A-18-2; the cause of action shall not accrue until the date of death.

N.C. Gen. Stat. § 1-53(4) · official text (North Carolina General Assembly), read on 13 November 2026[5]

Michigan: 3 years

the period of limitations is 3 years after the time of the death or injury for all actions to recover damages for the death of a person or for injury to a person or property.

MCL 600.5805(2) · official text (Michigan Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[6]

Arizona: 2 years

there shall be commenced and prosecuted within two years after the cause of action accrues, and not afterward, the following actions:

2. For injuries done to the person of another when death ensues from such injuries, which action shall be considered as accruing at the death of the party injured.

Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 12-542(2) · official text (Arizona State Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[7]

Massachusetts: 3 years

An action to recover damages under this section shall be commenced within three years from the date of death, or within three years from the date when the deceased's executor or administrator knew, or in the exercise of reasonable diligence, should have known of the factual basis for a cause of action,

Mass. Gen. Laws c. 229, § 2 · official text (Massachusetts Legislature), read on 13 November 2026[8]

Minnesota: 3 years

Any other action under this section may be commenced within three years after the date of death provided that the action must be commenced within six years after the act or omission.

Minn. Stat. § 573.02, subd. 1 · official text (Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes), read on 13 November 2026[9]
