Minnesota statute of limitations for debt collection

Generally 6 years under Minn. Stat. § 541.053 and related sections. Statute text, when the clock starts, exceptions and a deadline calculator. Read on 13 November 2026.

What the Minnesota statute says

Minnesota sets more than one period for debt collection claims, depending on the kind of claim:

  • Consumer debt 6 years

    actions upon an obligation arising out of a consumer debt primarily for personal, family, or household purposes shall be commenced within six years.

    Minn. Stat. § 541.053 · official text (Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes), read on 13 November 2026[1]

  • Other contract debt 6 years

    Except where the Uniform Commercial Code otherwise prescribes, the following actions shall be commenced within six years:

    (1) upon a contract or other obligation, express or implied, as to which no other limitation is expressly prescribed;

    Minn. Stat. § 541.05, subd. 1(1) · official text (Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes), read on 13 November 2026[2]

A deadline to sue is not the end of the debt. The statutes quoted on this page limit when a lawsuit can be brought; they do not say that the debt is cancelled or erased once the period has run. The federal rule quoted on this page (Regulation F) applies to a “debt collector” as that regulation defines the term (12 CFR 1006.2(i)), so it does not cover every creditor. What appears on a credit report follows a different federal law that this site does not cover. Whether a payment or a written acknowledgment changes the deadline depends on state law; this site mentions it only where a quoted statute says so.

When the clock starts

From the day the claim accrues, usually the missed payment.

For example, counted from 15 January 2025, the general deadline would be Consumer debt: 15 January 2031; Other contract debt: 15 January 2031. Exceptions below can move it.

Exceptions and special rules for debt collection

A payment does not revive an expired consumer debt

Once the six years have run, collecting a payment, a bankruptcy discharge or a reaffirmation does not revive the deadline.

After its expiration, the statute of limitations is not revived by the collection of a payment on an account, a discharge in a bankruptcy proceeding, or an oral or written reaffirmation of the debt.

Minn. Stat. § 541.053 · official text (Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes), read on 13 November 2026[1]

Federal rule on time-barred consumer debt

Under the federal Regulation F, a debt collector must not sue or threaten to sue a consumer over a debt once the applicable statute of limitations has expired.

A debt collector must not bring or threaten to bring a legal action against a consumer to collect a time-barred debt.

12 CFR 1006.26(b) · official text (eCFR, Office of the Federal Register), read on 13 November 2026[3]

Rules that pause or extend deadlines in Minnesota

These general rules apply to many Minnesota deadlines, including this one, subject to their own conditions.

Minors and insanity (with limits)

Being under 18 or insane suspends the period until the disability is removed; except for infancy, the extension cannot exceed five years, and never more than one year after the disability ends.

provided that such period, except in the case of infancy, shall not be extended for more than five years, nor in any case for more than one year after the disability ceases:

Minn. Stat. § 541.15(a) · official text (Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes), read on 13 November 2026[4]

Defendant out of state

Time while the person being sued is out of state and cannot be served is not counted.

the time of the person's absence is not part of the time limited for the commencement of the action.

Minn. Stat. § 541.13 · official text (Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes), read on 13 November 2026[5]

If the claim is against a government

Claims against cities and other municipalities: 180-day notice

Notice must be presented to the municipality within 180 days after the loss or injury is discovered (one year for a wrongful death claim).

shall cause to be presented to the governing body of the municipality within 180 days after the alleged loss or injury is discovered a notice stating the time, place and circumstances thereof,

Minn. Stat. § 466.05 · official text (Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes), read on 13 November 2026[6]
