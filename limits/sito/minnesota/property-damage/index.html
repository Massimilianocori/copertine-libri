Minnesota statute of limitations for property damage

What the Minnesota statute says

Minnesota sets more than one period for property damage claims, depending on the kind of claim:

  • Trespass on real estate 6 years

    Except where the Uniform Commercial Code otherwise prescribes, the following actions shall be commenced within six years:

    (3) for a trespass upon real estate;

    Minn. Stat. § 541.05, subd. 1(3) · official text (Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes), read on 13 November 2026[1]

  • Taking, detaining or injuring personal property 6 years

    Except where the Uniform Commercial Code otherwise prescribes, the following actions shall be commenced within six years:

    (4) for taking, detaining, or injuring personal property, including actions for the specific recovery thereof;

    Minn. Stat. § 541.05, subd. 1(4) · official text (Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes), read on 13 November 2026[1]

When the clock starts

From the day the claim accrues; the section does not define it.

For example, counted from 15 January 2025, the general deadline would be Trespass on real estate: 15 January 2031; Taking, detaining or injuring personal property: 15 January 2031. Exceptions below can move it.

Exceptions and special rules for property damage

Pesticide application: two years

Damage to property from the application of a pesticide has two years.

(7) against the person who applies the pesticide for injury or damage to property resulting from the application, but not the manufacture or sale, of a pesticide.

Minn. Stat. § 541.07(7) · official text (Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes), read on 13 November 2026[2]

Rules that pause or extend deadlines in Minnesota

These general rules apply to many Minnesota deadlines, including this one, subject to their own conditions.

Minors and insanity (with limits)

Being under 18 or insane suspends the period until the disability is removed; except for infancy, the extension cannot exceed five years, and never more than one year after the disability ends.

provided that such period, except in the case of infancy, shall not be extended for more than five years, nor in any case for more than one year after the disability ceases:

Minn. Stat. § 541.15(a) · official text (Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes), read on 13 November 2026[3]

Defendant out of state

Time while the person being sued is out of state and cannot be served is not counted.

the time of the person's absence is not part of the time limited for the commencement of the action.

Minn. Stat. § 541.13 · official text (Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes), read on 13 November 2026[4]

If the claim is against a government

Claims against cities and other municipalities: 180-day notice

Notice must be presented to the municipality within 180 days after the loss or injury is discovered (one year for a wrongful death claim).

shall cause to be presented to the governing body of the municipality within 180 days after the alleged loss or injury is discovered a notice stating the time, place and circumstances thereof,

Minn. Stat. § 466.05 · official text (Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes), read on 13 November 2026[5]
