    How to read a NAICS code

    NAICS uses a six-digit code. The first two digits are the sector, the third the subsector, the fourth the industry group, the fifth the NAICS industry and the sixth the national industry; a zero as the sixth digit generally means the NAICS industry and the U.S. industry are the same.[2] Example with 541511 «Custom Computer Programming Services»:

    • 54: sector «Professional, Scientific, and Technical Services»
    • 541: subsector «Professional, Scientific, and Technical Services»
    • 5415: industry group «Computer Systems Design and Related Services»
    • 54151: NAICS industry «Computer Systems Design and Related Services»
    • 541511: U.S. industry, the six-digit code used on forms.

    Each industry has an official description, often with illustrative examples and cross-references to the codes where related activities are classified. The official index adds thousands of specific activities, each pointing to one six-digit code.

    Frequently asked questions

    Questions people really search on Google, answered from the official sources cited.

    What is a NAICS code?

    It is a code of the North American Industry Classification System, which groups business establishments by their primary activity so that the economic statistics of the United States, Canada and Mexico are uniform and comparable. The 2022 edition has 20 sectors, 96 subsectors, 308 industry groups, 689 NAICS industries and 1,012 six-digit U.S. industries.[1],[2]

    How many digits does a NAICS code have?

    Up to six. The first two digits are the sector, the third the subsector, the fourth the industry group, the fifth the NAICS industry and the sixth the national (U.S.) industry. A zero as the sixth digit generally means that the NAICS industry and the U.S. industry are the same, as in 238220 «Plumbing, Heating, and Air-Conditioning Contractors».[2],[3]

    How do I find the NAICS code for my business?

    Start from the primary business activity. Then search the titles from the two-digit sector down to the six-digit industry, or search the alphabetic index of activities, and read the full description of the industry, including the cross-references and the illustrative examples, to check that it fits. The search box on this page covers all 2,125 codes and the 20,373 entries of the official 2022 index.[2],[4]

    Who assigns NAICS codes, and can I change mine?

    No central government agency assigns, monitors or approves NAICS codes. Each agency keeps its own list of businesses and assigns codes with its own method: for example, the Social Security Administration assigns a code to new businesses from the application for an Employer Identification Number. A code held by an agency is changed with that agency; a business does not apply for a NAICS code.[2]

    Is a NAICS code the same as a SIC code?

    No. The Standard Industrial Classification (SIC) of 1987 was the earlier U.S. system, and NAICS replaced it in 1997. SIC codes have four digits, NAICS codes up to six. The Census Bureau publishes a concordance between 1987 SIC and 1997 NAICS: our SIC to NAICS table follows it and then the later concordances up to 2022.[2],[5]

    What is a NAICS code used for?

    NAICS was built to collect and publish statistics about establishments. Other bodies use it too: the IRS business activity codes for Schedule C are six-digit codes based on NAICS, and the NAICS manual notes that federal agencies have adopted NAICS for procurement and regulatory purposes even though its categories were not developed for those needs.[2],[6]

    Where does a NAICS-based code go on a tax return?

    On Schedule C (Form 1040) the IRS asks for a six-digit Principal Business or Professional Activity code on line B, from a list in the Schedule C instructions; the IRS says these codes are based on NAICS. For other returns, read the instructions of that form.[6]

    Does a NAICS code say whether a business is small?

    No. The NAICS manual says that NAICS categories do not distinguish between small and large business, and that the Small Business Administration (SBA) develops size standards for each NAICS category. This site does not publish the SBA size standards: they are on the SBA website.[2]

    Which NAICS edition does this site use?

    NAICS 2022, the edition the United States adopted in statistical programs for reference year 2022, as published in the Census Bureau files we read on November 13, 2026. NAICS is reviewed every five years; the 2022 manual says the next scheduled review is for a potential 2027 revision.[2],[3]
