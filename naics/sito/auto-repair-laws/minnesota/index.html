Minnesota auto repair laws: estimate, invoice and shop rules

What the Minnesota Statutes §§ 325F.56 to 325F.66 asks of a repair shop, with the official sentence next to each rule and a printable checklist.

Read in the official texts on November 19, 2026. Each rule below is followed by the sentence it comes from.

Minnesota’s repair rules are in sections 325F.56 to 325F.66 of the Minnesota Statutes. They cover repairs of motor vehicles, appliances and dwelling places used primarily for personal, family or household purposes. A shop gives a written estimate when the customer asks for one, cannot charge more than 110 percent of it without a new authorization, and gives a dated invoice when the repairs are completed.

What Minnesota requires from a repair shop, in the official words

Written estimateWritten estimate when the customer asks for one, before repairs start

In these sections “repairs” means work for a total price of more than $100. The contents of the written estimate are in the definition quoted in the checklist. Telling the customer the contents aloud before starting, and handing over the writing at the end, also satisfies the requirement.

Upon the request of a customer for a written estimate and prior to the commencement of repairs, a shop shall provide the customer with a written estimate.Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes, Minnesota Statutes § 325F.58, Estimates, read on November 19, 2026[1]
"Repairs" means work performed for a total price of more than $100, including the price of parts and materials, to restore a malfunctioning, defective, or worn motor vehicle, appliance, or dwelling place used primarily for personal, family, or household purposes and not primarily for business or agricultural purposes.Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes, Minnesota Statutes § 325F.56, Definitions, read on November 19, 2026[2]
The requirement of a written estimate in sections 325F.56 to 325F.66 is fulfilled if a shop orally communicates the contents of a required writing to the customer prior to commencing repairs and provides the writing to the customer upon completion of the repairs.Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes, Minnesota Statutes § 325F.58, Estimates, read on November 19, 2026[1]

Customer authorizationNo charge for unauthorized repairs; a revised written estimate and a new authorization to go above 110 percent of the estimate

The 110 percent limit applies when the shop has given a written estimate.

No shop shall charge for unauthorized repairs.Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes, Minnesota Statutes § 325F.59, Repairs, read on November 19, 2026[3]
If a shop provides a written estimate of the price of repairs, it shall not charge more than 110 percent of the total price stated in its estimate for the repairsMinnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes, Minnesota Statutes § 325F.58, Estimates, read on November 19, 2026[1]
… the shop may charge more than 110 percent of the estimate for the repairs if the shop immediately provides the customer a revised written estimate pursuant to this section and receives authorization to continue with the repairsMinnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes, Minnesota Statutes § 325F.58, Estimates, read on November 19, 2026[1]

InvoiceA dated invoice when the repairs are completed, for repair work of more than $50 and for any warranty, service contract or insurance work

If the customer collects the vehicle without face to face contact, the shop mails the invoice within two business days. A written estimate can serve as the invoice if the required information is written on its face.

Upon completion of repairs, a shop shall provide the customer with a copy of a dated invoice for the repairs performed.Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes, Minnesota Statutes § 325F.60, Invoice, read on November 19, 2026[4]
… any repair work performed for a total value of more than $50, including the price of parts and materialsMinnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes, Minnesota Statutes § 325F.60, Invoice, read on November 19, 2026[4]
A written estimate may be used as an invoice if the required invoice information is written on the face of the estimate.Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes, Minnesota Statutes § 325F.60, Invoice, read on November 19, 2026[4]

Replaced partsReplaced parts returned if the customer asks before repairs are commenced

Warranty and exchange parts are not returned, but the customer can examine them for five business days after the repairs.

If a customer makes a request before repairs are commenced, the shop shall return replaced parts to the customer, except parts which the shop is required to return to the manufacturer, distributor, or other person under a warranty or exchange arrangement, is required to retain pursuant to law, or is necessary for pending litigation. The customer shall be given an opportunity to examine warranty or exchange parts for a period of five business days after completion of repairs.Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes, Minnesota Statutes § 325F.62, Required shop practices, read on November 19, 2026[5]

RecordsCustomer name and address, written estimates and the repair invoice kept for at least one year

The customer can get a copy by paying reasonable reproduction costs.

When repairs are performed, a shop shall retain for at least one year the name and address of the customer, any written estimates and the repair invoice.Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes, Minnesota Statutes § 325F.62, Required shop practices, read on November 19, 2026[5]

Replaced parts

  • If a customer makes a request before repairs are commenced, the shop shall return replaced parts to the customer, except parts which the shop is required to return to the manufacturer, distributor, or other person under a warranty or exchange arrangement, is required to retain pursuant to law, or is necessary for pending litigation.§ 325F.62, subd. 1[5]
  • The customer shall be given an opportunity to examine warranty or exchange parts for a period of five business days after completion of repairs.§ 325F.62, subd. 1[5]

The shop itself

  • Each shop shall conspicuously display a sign that states the following:§ 325F.62, subd. 3[5]
  • Wording to print · § 325F.62, subd. 3[5]Upon a customer's request, this shop is required to provide a written estimate for repairs costing more than $100 if the shop agrees to perform the repairs. The shop's final price cannot exceed its written estimate by more than ten percent without the prior authorization of the customer. You must request that the estimate be in writing. An oral estimate is not subject to the above repair cost limitations.
  • If the shop charges a fee for the storage or care of repaired motor vehicles or appliances, the shop shall conspicuously display a sign that states the amount assessed for storage or care, when the charge begins to accrue, and the interval of time between assessments.§ 325F.62, subd. 3[5]
  • When repairs are performed, a shop shall retain for at least one year the name and address of the customer, any written estimates and the repair invoice.§ 325F.62, subd. 2[5]

Frequently asked questions

Questions people really search on Google, answered from the official sources cited.

What do Minnesota auto repair laws require from a repair shop?

Sections 325F.56 to 325F.66 require a shop to give a written estimate when the customer asks for one before repairs start, not to charge for unauthorized repairs, not to charge more than 110 percent of a written estimate without a revised estimate and a new authorization, to give a dated invoice on completion, to return replaced parts on a request made before the repairs, to keep records for at least one year and to display the sign quoted in the checklist.

Can a mechanic charge more than the estimate in Minnesota?

If the shop gave a written estimate, the statute says it shall not charge more than 110 percent of the total price stated in the estimate. It can charge more only if, after starting, it finds that additional work is necessary, did not unreasonably fail to disclose that possible need when it made the estimate, immediately gives the customer a revised written estimate and receives authorization to continue. The sign the statute requires adds that an oral estimate is not subject to these limits.
