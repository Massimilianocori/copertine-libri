California auto repair laws: estimate, invoice and shop rules

In California repair shops are automotive repair dealers registered with the Bureau of Automotive Repair (BAR). This page quotes BAR’s own guide for dealers, Write It Right, which summarizes the Automotive Repair Act and its regulations and gives the legal reference after each rule: an estimate before any repair, the customer’s authorization recorded on it, and an invoice when the job is completed.

What California requires from a repair shop, in the official words

Written estimateAn estimate before an automotive repair dealer performs any repair

The guide lists no dollar threshold. Certain preventative maintenance services are the exception it describes, when the service is free or its total price is displayed or acknowledged by the customer.

An estimate is required for an automotive repair dealer to perform repairs to a motor vehicle. (BPC § 9884.9)California Bureau of Automotive Repair, Write It Right: Documentation and authorization requirements for automotive repair dealers, read on November 19, 2026[1]
An estimate must contain a description of the specific job and the estimated price for all parts and labor. Each part listed in the estimate shall be new unless specifically identified as a used, rebuilt, or reconditioned part. (BPC § 9884.9, CCR § 3353(a))California Bureau of Automotive Repair, Write It Right: Documentation and authorization requirements for automotive repair dealers, read on November 19, 2026[1]

Customer authorizationCustomer authorization, written, oral or electronic, recorded on the estimate before any repair and again before any additional repair

A note left with a vehicle dropped off after hours is not an authorization, the guide says. For additional work the dealer prepares a revised work order and documents the new authorization on the work order and the invoice.

All repairs must be authorized by the customer in written, oral, or electronic form. The authorization must be obtained and recorded on the estimate before beginning any repairs.California Bureau of Automotive Repair, Write It Right: Documentation and authorization requirements for automotive repair dealers, read on November 19, 2026[1]
Authorization is required before beginning any repairs, even when a vehicle is dropped off before or after normal business hours along with a note requesting repairs to be performed. The note does not constitute authorization.California Bureau of Automotive Repair, Write It Right: Documentation and authorization requirements for automotive repair dealers, read on November 19, 2026[1]
Before beginning any additional repairs beyond the original estimate, or having any additional charges accrue, complete the following steps:California Bureau of Automotive Repair, Write It Right: Documentation and authorization requirements for automotive repair dealers, read on November 19, 2026[1]

InvoiceAn invoice when all repairs for the job are completed

Warranty repairs, repairs at no charge and preventative maintenance services are recorded on the invoice too.

The invoice is provided to the customer upon completion of all repairs for a specific job, including any preventative maintenance services defined in BPC § 9880.1(j). (BPC § 9884.8, CCR § 3356)California Bureau of Automotive Repair, Write It Right: Documentation and authorization requirements for automotive repair dealers, read on November 19, 2026[1]

Replaced partsReplaced parts returned if the customer asks when the estimate is authorized

Core parts sold on an exchange basis and warranty parts are the exceptions; the customer is offered the chance to inspect them and the invoice records what could not be returned.

Yes, if requested by the customer at the time the estimate is authorized. However, core parts (such as a battery) that are sold on an exchange basis or parts that must be returned to the supplier under warranty are not required to be returned to the customer. In these circumstances, the customer must be offered the opportunity to inspect the replaced parts. Any replaced parts that cannot be returned to the customer must be recorded on the invoice. (BPC § 9884.10, CCR §§ 3355, 3356(i))California Bureau of Automotive Repair, Write It Right: Documentation and authorization requirements for automotive repair dealers, read on November 19, 2026[1]

Shop registrationAutomotive repair dealer registration with BAR: application fee $200, annual renewal fee $200

BAR calls it a license on its pages and lists its type as a registration. It is for the business: BAR writes that a license is not required to work as a general automotive repair mechanic.

Required to diagnose, service, or repair vehicles for compensation. This includes mobile, referral, or sublet repair services.California Bureau of Automotive Repair, Apply for a license, read on November 19, 2026[2]
Application fee: $200California Bureau of Automotive Repair, Apply for a license, read on November 19, 2026[2]
Annual renewal fee: $200California Bureau of Automotive Repair, Apply for a license, read on November 19, 2026[2]
A BAR license is not required to work as a general automotive repair mechanic.California Bureau of Automotive Repair, Apply for a license, read on November 19, 2026[2]

RecordsInvoices, estimates and work orders kept for at least three years

Paper or electronic form; each record carries an identifier that links it to its transaction.

Records must be maintained for at least three years and shall be open for reasonable inspection and/or reproduction by BAR during normal business hours. (BPC § 9884.11, CCR §§ 3340.15(e), 3358(d))California Bureau of Automotive Repair, Write It Right: Documentation and authorization requirements for automotive repair dealers, read on November 19, 2026[1]

Read the rules first

On the estimate and the work order

  • An estimate must contain a description of the specific job and the estimated price for all parts and labor.BPC § 9884.9, CCR § 3353(a)[1]
  • Each part listed in the estimate shall be new unless specifically identified as a used, rebuilt, or reconditioned part.BPC § 9884.9, CCR § 3353(a)[1]
  • … any charges associated with the handling, management, and disposal costs of toxic wastes or hazardous substances directly related to the specific job must be itemized separately in both the estimate and the invoiceCCR § 3357[1]
  • The automotive repair dealer must include with the estimate a statement of any sublet repair to be performed on the vehicle.CCR § 3353(f)[1]
  • Charging for items generally noted as shop supplies or miscellaneous parts is prohibited.CCR § 3356(g)[1]
  • … sales tax is only included in the invoiceCCR § 3356(c)(5)[1]
  • A work order must contain the authorized estimate for a specific job, the repairs requested and authorized by the customer, and the vehicle’s odometer reading.CCR § 3352(b)[1]

Recording the authorization

  • Written authorization consists of the customer’s signature and the date the customer authorized the repairs.CCR § 3353.1(b)[1]
  • Oral authorization consists of the date, time, name of the person authorizing the repairs, and telephone number called, if any.CCR § 3353.1(c)[1]
  • Electronic authorization consists of the date, time, name of the person authorizing the repairs, and email address or phone number (text messaging) contacted, if any.CCR § 3353.1(d)[1]
  • The customer must be provided a copy of any document requiring their signature at the time of signing.CCR § 3353.1(a)[1]
  • Prepare a revised work order that describes all additional parts and labor, provides the cost of all additional parts and labor, and includes the total revised cost.CCR § 3354(a)[1]
  • Contact the customer by phone, email, text, or other electronic means to provide all the information listed in the revised work order and obtain the customer's authorization.CCR § 3354(a)[1]
  • Properly document the customer’s authorization on both the work order and invoice.CCR § 3354(a)[1]

On the invoice

  • The automotive repair dealer registration number and the corresponding business name and address as shown in BAR’s records.CCR § 3356(b)[1]
  • An itemized list of all services and repairs performed and the prices for each. Include any diagnosis, warranty repairs, or repairs performed at no charge.CCR § 3356(c)(1)[1]
  • An itemized list of each part supplied. List each part in common terms so that the customer can understand what they purchased and the price of the part.CCR § 3356(c)(2)[1]
  • State if each part is new, used, reconditioned, rebuilt, and whether any crash parts are OEM or non-OEM aftermarket.CCR § 3356(c)(2)[1]
  • Replaced parts that cannot be returned to the customer, if applicable.CCR § 3356(i)[1]
  • The subtotal price for all service and repair work performed.CCR § 3356(c)(3)[1]
  • The subtotal price for all parts supplied, not including sales tax.CCR § 3356(c)(4)[1]
  • The sales tax, if applicable.CCR § 3356(c)(5)[1]
  • The toxic waste fee charged, if any, as stated on the original estimate and the automotive repair dealer’s Environmental Protection Agency number.CCR § 3357[1]
  • The total cost for all service and repair work, parts supplied, and applicable sales tax.CCR § 3356(c)(6)[1]

Replaced parts

  • However, core parts (such as a battery) that are sold on an exchange basis or parts that must be returned to the supplier under warranty are not required to be returned to the customer. In these circumstances, the customer must be offered the opportunity to inspect the replaced parts.BPC § 9884.10, CCR § 3355[1]
  • Any replaced parts that cannot be returned to the customer must be recorded on the invoice.CCR § 3356(i)[1]

The shop itself

  • BAR licenses California's auto repair businesses.BAR[2]
  • Automotive repair dealers must post required signs for each license they hold.BPC § 9884.17[3]
  • Records must be maintained for at least three years and shall be open for reasonable inspection and/or reproduction by BAR during normal business hours.CCR § 3358(d)[1]
  • … all records associated with a specific transaction must have a unique identifier linking the records to that specific transactionCCR § 3358(e)[1]

Frequently asked questions

Questions people really search on Google, answered from the official sources cited.

What is BAR’s Write It Right?

Write It Right is the Bureau of Automotive Repair’s guide to the documentation and authorization requirements for automotive repair dealers: estimates, authorizations, invoices and records. BAR describes it as a quick reference that is not all-inclusive, with the statutory and regulatory references after each rule. This page quotes it and links to it.

Does an auto repair shop need a Bureau of Automotive Repair license in California?

BAR writes that the automotive repair dealer license is required to diagnose, service, or repair vehicles for compensation, including mobile, referral, or sublet repair services. Its type is a registration, with an application fee of $200 and an annual renewal fee of $200. BAR also writes that a BAR license is not required to work as a general automotive repair mechanic.

What do California auto repair laws require on an estimate?

According to BAR’s guide, an estimate is required for an automotive repair dealer to perform repairs to a motor vehicle, and it must contain a description of the specific job and the estimated price for all parts and labor. Each part listed is new unless specifically identified as used, rebuilt, or reconditioned. The customer’s authorization is recorded on the estimate before any repair begins.
