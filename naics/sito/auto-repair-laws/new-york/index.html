New York auto repair laws: estimate, invoice and shop rules

In New York repair shops are registered with the Department of Motor Vehicles, and their obligations are in the Commissioner’s Motor Vehicle Repair Shop Regulations, Part 82, which DMV publishes as form CR-82. A shop writes an estimate when the customer asks for one, works only on an authorized work order, gives an invoice and returns replaced parts on a written demand.

What New York requires from a repair shop, in the official words

Written estimateWritten estimate of parts and labor when the customer asks for one

The shop may charge a reasonable fee for making it. The estimate shows the hourly labor charge and how it is computed.

Upon the request of any customer make an estimate in writing of the parts and labor necessary for each specific repair or service offered and shall not charge for work done or parts supplied in excess of the estimate without the consent of such customer. The repair shop may charge a reasonable fee for making an estimate.New York State Department of Motor Vehicles, Motor Vehicle Repair Shop Regulations, CR-82 (PDF), Part 82, read on November 19, 2026[1]
Written estimates must indicate the hourly labor charge and how it is computed, i.e., by clock hours or flat rate. If flat rate, the manual used must be specified.New York State Department of Motor Vehicles, Motor Vehicle Repair Shop Regulations, CR-82 (PDF), Part 82, read on November 19, 2026[1]

Customer authorizationNo service without a work order from the customer; no charge above the estimate without the customer’s consent

A telephone authorization is recorded with the time, the date and the name of the person who gave it.

Not perform any services not authorized by the customer by a work order.New York State Department of Motor Vehicles, Motor Vehicle Repair Shop Regulations, CR-82 (PDF), Part 82, read on November 19, 2026[1]
The final bill cannot exceed your written estimate without the consent of the customer.New York State Department of Motor Vehicles, Open and Operate a Repair or Body Shop, read on November 19, 2026[2]
If repairs are authorized by telephone, you must record the time, the date, and the name of the person who gave authorization.New York State Department of Motor Vehicles, Open and Operate a Repair or Body Shop, read on November 19, 2026[2]

InvoiceAn invoice to the customer, also for repairs performed under warranty

The contents are in the checklist. All information on an invoice must be legible, the regulation says.

Provide the customer with an invoice.New York State Department of Motor Vehicles, Motor Vehicle Repair Shop Regulations, CR-82 (PDF), Part 82, read on November 19, 2026[1]
A repair performed under warranty requires an invoice which complies with this subdivision.New York State Department of Motor Vehicles, Motor Vehicle Repair Shop Regulations, CR-82 (PDF), Part 82, read on November 19, 2026[1]

Replaced partsReplaced parts returned on a timely written demand; presumed wanted when the work is authorized by telephone

The rule does not apply to parts normally sold on an exchange basis or under a manufacturer’s warranty.

Return replaced parts if a timely written demand is made by the customer. If work is authorized over the telephone, it shall be presumed that the customer wants his parts returned and the repair shop shall keep such parts until the customer or his agent appears to retrieve the motor vehicle at which time the replaced parts shall be given to the customer if he so directs.New York State Department of Motor Vehicles, Motor Vehicle Repair Shop Regulations, CR-82 (PDF), Part 82, read on November 19, 2026[1]
This subdivision does not apply to parts, components or equipment normally sold on an exchange basis or subject to a manufacturer's warranty;New York State Department of Motor Vehicles, Motor Vehicle Repair Shop Regulations, CR-82 (PDF), Part 82, read on November 19, 2026[1]

Shop registrationRegistration of the repair shop with the Commissioner of Motor Vehicles

DMV’s page lists what goes with the application (proof of the right to use the property, zoning, sales tax certificate, workers’ compensation, fees). The amounts of the fees are not on the pages we read.

Every motor vehicle repair shop is required to be registered with the commissioner.New York State Department of Motor Vehicles, Motor Vehicle Repair Shop Regulations, CR-82 (PDF), Part 82, read on November 19, 2026[1]
If DMV approves your application, you will receive a repair shop registration.New York State Department of Motor Vehicles, Open and Operate a Repair or Body Shop, read on November 19, 2026[2]

RecordsCopies of estimates, work orders, invoices, parts purchase orders and appraisals kept for two years

They are open to inspection by the commissioner or a designee during business hours.

Each motor vehicle repair shop shall maintain copies of estimates, work orders, invoices, parts purchase orders and appraisals prepared by that repair shop. Such copies shall be kept for two years and shall be available for inspection by the commissioner or his designee during all business hours.New York State Department of Motor Vehicles, Motor Vehicle Repair Shop Regulations, CR-82 (PDF), Part 82, read on November 19, 2026[1]

Read the rules first

On the written estimate

  • … the customer's name§ 82.5(a)[1]
  • … the name and facility number of the repair shop§ 82.5(a)[1]
  • … the date of the estimate§ 82.5(a)[1]
  • … a list of parts necessary for each specific repair together with the costs for each part, indicating any parts which are not new parts of at least original equipment quality§ 82.5(a)[1]
  • … the labor charge for each repair together with the costs of each labor charge§ 82.5(a)[1]
  • … year and make of vehicle, registration plate number or vehicle identification number§ 82.5(a)[1]
  • … a description of the problem reported by the customer§ 82.5(a)[1]
  • … a statement informing the customer of his right to receive replaced parts if the customer makes a written request for such return§ 82.5(a)[1]
  • … for body parts, the repair shop must indicate if the part is a new original equipment manufacturer part, a new after market equipment manufacturer part or a used part§ 82.5(a)[1]
  • All information on an estimate must be legible§ 82.5(a)[1]
  • Written estimates must indicate the hourly labor charge and how it is computed, i.e., by clock hours or flat rate. If flat rate, the manual used must be specified.§ 82.8(a)[1]

The work order

  • Not perform any services not authorized by the customer by a work order.§ 82.5(b)[1]
  • If a repair shop prepares a written work order, a copy shall be given to the customer.§ 82.5(b)[1]
  • If a written work order is prepared by the customer, such work order shall be attached to the invoice.§ 82.5(b)[1]
  • If the customer gives an oral work order, the oral work order shall be noted on the invoice and shall include the date, time and manner of authorization and by whom such authorization was given§ 82.5(b)[1]

On the invoice

  • … the name, address and facility number of the repair shop§ 82.5(c)[1]
  • … the date of the invoice§ 82.5(c)[1]
  • … the date the vehicle was presented to the repair shop for repair or services§ 82.5(c)[1]
  • … a list of all parts supplied and labor performed, including the cost for each such part and labor§ 82.5(c)[1]
  • … a notation indicating the status of any part used which is not new and of at least original quality (i.e. used, rebuilt, etc.)§ 82.5(c)[1]
  • … the odometer reading on the vehicle at the time it was left with the repair shop and the odometer reading at the time the invoice was prepared§ 82.5(c)[1]
  • … a promised date of delivery, if any such date was given§ 82.5(c)[1]
  • … the name of the customer, year, make, and plate number and/or vehicle identification number of the vehicle§ 82.5(c)[1]
  • … the terms and time limit of any guarantee for the repair work performed§ 82.5(c)[1]
  • … a description of the problem reported by the customer, and the repair shop registration number§ 82.5(c)[1]
  • … if body parts were used in the repair, the invoice must indicate if each such part is a new original equipment manufacturer part, a new after market equipment manufacturer part or a used part§ 82.5(c)[1]
  • All information on an invoice must be legible§ 82.5(c)[1]

Replaced parts

  • Timely written demand. A request in writing made to the repair shop operator by the customer before any work is done on the vehicle.§ 82.2(i)[1]
  • Return replaced parts if a timely written demand is made by the customer.§ 82.5(d)[1]
  • If work is authorized over the telephone, it shall be presumed that the customer wants his parts returned and the repair shop shall keep such parts until the customer or his agent appears to retrieve the motor vehicle at which time the replaced parts shall be given to the customer if he so directs.§ 82.5(d)[1]
  • Customers may not waive their rights to replaced parts over the telephone.§ 82.5(d)[1]
  • This subdivision does not apply to parts, components or equipment normally sold on an exchange basis or subject to a manufacturer's warranty§ 82.5(d)[1]

The shop itself

  • Every motor vehicle repair shop is required to be registered with the commissioner.§ 82.3(a)[1]
  • A customer may not be charged for storage unless notice in writing is given.§ 82.8(c)(1)[1]
  • Such copies shall be kept for two years and shall be available for inspection by the commissioner or his designee during all business hours.§ 82.9(a)[1]
  • Every registered repair shop, except those which are registered as repair shops solely to perform appraisal work in connection with insurance, shall post this sign indoors in a place where customers are likely to see it.§ 82.7(a)[1]
  • The repair shop shall post an official outdoor repair shop sign.§ 82.7(b)[1]
  • DMV does not provide official repair shop signs. You can purchase these signs from various sign shops and automotive supply stores.DMV[2]

Frequently asked questions

Questions people really search on Google, answered from the official sources cited.

What is a registered State of New York motor vehicle repair shop?

The DMV regulation says every motor vehicle repair shop is required to be registered with the commissioner: any person who, for compensation, is wholly or partially engaged in the business of repairing or diagnosing motor vehicle malfunctions, with the exclusions the regulation lists. DMV writes that an approved applicant receives a repair shop registration, and the registration number goes on every invoice.

What do New York auto repair laws require on a repair invoice?

The regulation lists, among other items, the name, address and facility number of the shop, the dates, every part supplied and the labor performed with their cost, a notation for parts that are not new, the odometer reading when the vehicle was left and when the invoice was prepared, the terms of any guarantee, the problem reported by the customer and the repair shop registration number. The full list is in the checklist on this page.

Can a mechanic charge more than the estimate in New York?

The regulation says a repair shop shall not charge for work done or parts supplied in excess of the estimate without the consent of the customer, and DMV’s page for shops repeats that the final bill cannot exceed the written estimate without the consent of the customer.
