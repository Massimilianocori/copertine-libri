Washington auto repair laws: estimate, invoice and shop rules

Washington’s rules for repair shops are in chapter 46.71 of the Revised Code of Washington. Before providing parts or labor a repair facility gives a written price estimate or offers the customer the three alternatives the statute prints; parts and labor are recorded on an invoice whether or not an estimate was required.

What Washington requires from a repair shop, in the official words

Written estimateWritten price estimate before providing parts or labor, or the customer’s signed choice among the three printed alternatives

Estimates above one hundred dollars are in writing and carry the listed information. No written estimate is required when the vehicle arrives without face-to-face contact, but the facility still needs the customer’s authorization first.

… a repair facility prior to providing parts or labor shall provide the customer or the customer's designee with a written price estimate of the total cost of the repair, including parts and laborWashington State Legislature, Chapter 46.71 RCW, Automotive repair (complete chapter), read on November 19, 2026[1]
… all estimates that exceed one hundred dollars shall be in writing and include the following informationWashington State Legislature, Chapter 46.71 RCW, Automotive repair (complete chapter), read on November 19, 2026[1]
A written estimate shall not be required when the customer's motor vehicle or component has been brought to an automotive repair facility's regular place of business without face-to-face contact between the customer and the repair facility.Washington State Legislature, Chapter 46.71 RCW, Automotive repair (complete chapter), read on November 19, 2026[1]

Customer authorizationOral or written authorization before charging more than one hundred ten percent of the written estimate

The limit is counted before retail sales tax. An oral authorization is noted on the estimate with the details listed in the checklist.

The repair facility may not charge the customer more than one hundred ten percent, exclusive of retail sales tax, of the total shown on the written price estimate. Neither of these limitations apply if, before providing additional parts or labor the repair facility obtains either the oral or written authorization of the customer, or the customer's designee, to exceed the written price estimate.Washington State Legislature, Chapter 46.71 RCW, Automotive repair (complete chapter), read on November 19, 2026[1]

InvoiceAn invoice for all parts and labor, given to the customer when the repairs are completed

The invoice repeats the information required on the estimate and adds the items in the checklist. Parts and labor need not be priced separately for an advertised special, a written repair menu item or a routine service package.

Whether or not a written estimate is required, parts and labor provided by an automotive repair facility shall be clearly and accurately recorded in writing on an invoiceWashington State Legislature, Chapter 46.71 RCW, Automotive repair (complete chapter), read on November 19, 2026[1]
A copy of the invoice shall be provided to the customer upon completion of the repairs.Washington State Legislature, Chapter 46.71 RCW, Automotive repair (complete chapter), read on November 19, 2026[1]

Replaced partsReplaced parts returned when the work is completed, if the customer asked when authorizing the repair

Parts under warranty or parts that go back to a distributor, remanufacturer or rebuilder are not returned; the facility offers to show them.

Except for parts covered by a manufacturer's or other warranty or parts that must be returned to a distributor, remanufacturer, or rebuilder, the repair facility shall return replaced parts to the customer at the time the work is completed if the customer requested the parts at the time of authorization of the repair.Washington State Legislature, Chapter 46.71 RCW, Automotive repair (complete chapter), read on November 19, 2026[1]

Shop registrationNo registration specific to repair shops in chapter 46.71; the chapter refers to the Department of Revenue registration certificate

The chapter does not set up a license or registration of its own for repair facilities. It mentions the registration certificate that the Department of Revenue issues to businesses, with a notice of these rules. Other state or local licenses are outside the pages we read.

When the department of revenue issues a registration certificate under RCW 82.32.030 to an automotive repair facility, it must give written notice to the person of the requirements of this chapter in a manner prescribed by the director of revenue, including by electronic means.Washington State Legislature, Chapter 46.71 RCW, Automotive repair (complete chapter), read on November 19, 2026[1]

RecordsCopies of written estimates and invoices kept for at least one year after the repairs

Electronic records are allowed if the facility can print them on request.

Every automotive repair facility shall retain and make available for inspection, upon request by the customer or the customer's authorized representative, true copies of the written price estimates and invoices required under this chapter for at least one year after the date on which the repairs were performed.Washington State Legislature, Chapter 46.71 RCW, Automotive repair (complete chapter), read on November 19, 2026[1]

Frequently asked questions

Questions people really search on Google, answered from the official sources cited.

What do Washington State auto repair laws require from a repair shop?

Chapter 46.71 RCW requires a repair facility, before providing parts or labor, to give the customer a written price estimate or to offer the three alternatives printed in the statute; to obtain authorization before charging more than one hundred ten percent of the written estimate; to record parts and labor on an invoice; to return replaced parts if the customer asked for them when authorizing the repair; to post the customer rights sign; and to keep estimates and invoices for at least one year.

Can a mechanic charge more than the estimate in Washington?

The statute says the repair facility may not charge the customer more than one hundred ten percent, exclusive of retail sales tax, of the total shown on the written price estimate, unless it obtains the oral or written authorization of the customer or the customer’s designee before providing additional parts or labor.
