LLCs and NAICS codes

A NAICS code depends on what the business does, not on its legal form. The code search and the guides by business activity give the NAICS code of the activity; before using a code on a form, check with the agency or form that asks for the code.

Frequently asked questions

Questions people really search on Google, answered from the official sources cited.

What are the LLC filing fees by state?

On the official state pages read on November 14, 2026: Texas $300; Florida $125; California $70; New York $200; North Carolina $125; Michigan $50; Colorado $50; Arizona $50. These are the fees we read on each state’s official page; other states are not covered yet.

What are the LLC annual fees by state?

On the official state pages read on November 14, 2026: Texas: none to the Secretary of State; report to the Comptroller; Florida: $138.75, January 1 to May 1; California: $20 every two years (first within 90 days) (Statement of Information); New York: $9 every two years (Biennial Statement); North Carolina: $200 ($203 online), due April 15th; Michigan: $25, due February 15 (Annual Statement); Colorado: $25 a year (Periodic Report); Arizona: none. Names and deadlines differ from state to state, and some states also have a separate state tax or fee listed on the state page.

What is a registered agent?

The Texas Secretary of State describes a registered agent as the person or entity responsible for receiving and forwarding service of process or official notices addressed to an entity. Other states use other names: resident agent in Michigan, statutory agent in Arizona, agent for service of process in California.

Is there a NAICS code for an LLC?

NAICS classifies establishments by what they do. The NAICS manual says an establishment is classified in an industry when its primary activity meets the definition for that industry. So an LLC looks up the code of its primary activity, not a code for its legal form.[1]
