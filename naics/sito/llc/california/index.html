Filing Articles of Organization for a California LLC costs $70 at the Secretary of State. A $20 Statement of Information is due within 90 days and then every two years. Separately, the Franchise Tax Board says every LLC doing business or organized in California must pay an annual tax of $800.

The NAICS code of the business

A NAICS code describes what the business does, not its legal form. The NAICS manual says an establishment is classified in an industry when its primary activity meets the definition for that industry.[4] To find it, use the code search or the guides by business activity; before using a code on a form, check with the agency or form that asks for the code.

Frequently asked questions

Questions people really search on Google, answered from the official sources cited.

How much does an LLC cost in California?

The Secretary of State charges $70 to file Articles of Organization (Form LLC-1) and $20 for the Statement of Information, due within 90 days and then every two years. The Franchise Tax Board adds an annual tax of $800 for every LLC doing business or organized in California.

What is the $800 California LLC fee?

It is the annual tax of the Franchise Tax Board: every LLC that is doing business or organized in California must pay $800 a year. The first payment is due by the 15th day of the 4th month from the date of filing with the Secretary of State. An LLC that will make more than $250,000 also has to pay a separate LLC fee.

How do you start an LLC in California?

A California LLC is formed by filing Articles of Organization (Form LLC-1) with the Secretary of State; the fee is $70 and online filings get a response in 5 days or less. A Statement of Information is due within 90 days. This page lists what the state publishes; it is not a step-by-step legal guide.

Does a California LLC need a registered agent?

California calls it an agent for service of process: an individual who resides in California, or a registered 1505 corporate agent, designated to accept court papers if the business is sued. The Secretary of State says a business entity cannot act as its own agent for service of process.
