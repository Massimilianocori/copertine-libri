Texas LLC: filing fee, annual report and registered agent

What the Texas Secretary of State and the other state offices publish, with the official sentence next to each figure.

Filing fee$300
Annual reportNone to the Secretary of State; report to the Comptroller
Franchise taxFranchise tax report due May 15
Registered agentRequired, in Texas

Read on the official pages of the state on November 14, 2026. Each figure below is followed by the sentence it comes from.

A Texas LLC is created by filing a certificate of formation with the Texas Secretary of State. The form shows a $300 filing fee. Texas LLCs do not send an annual report to the Secretary of State: they report each year to the Comptroller, with the franchise tax report due May 15.

Annual reportNo annual report to the Secretary of State; a Public Information Report goes to the Comptroller

The Secretary of State’s FAQ answers the annual report question for LLCs by pointing to the Comptroller: the Public Information Report is part of the annual franchise tax report.

For-profit and professional corporations, professional associations, LLCs, and certain limited partnerships (whether Texas or foreign) that are subject to state franchise tax laws file annually with the Comptroller of Public Accounts. As part of the annual franchise tax reports, these taxable entities file a Public Information Report (PIR).Texas Secretary of State, Formation of Texas Entities FAQs, read on November 14, 2026[3]

The Comptroller’s table lists a no tax due threshold of $2,650,000 for report years 2026 and 2027. An entity at or below the threshold still files a Public Information Report or Ownership Report.

Registered agentRequired: a registered agent and registered office in Texas

The agent is an individual Texas resident or an entity allowed to do business in Texas, and must have consented to serve.

A domestic or foreign filing entity is required to continuously maintain a registered agent and registered office in Texas.Texas Secretary of State, Registered Agents FAQs, read on November 14, 2026[5]
A registered agent is an individual Texas resident or a domestic entity, or a foreign entity that has qualified or registered to transact business in Texas who is responsible for receiving and forwarding service of process or official notices addressed to an entity.Texas Secretary of State, Formation of Texas Entities FAQs, read on November 14, 2026[3]

The NAICS code of the business

A NAICS code describes what the business does, not its legal form. The NAICS manual says an establishment is classified in an industry when its primary activity meets the definition for that industry.[6] To find it, use the code search or the guides by business activity; before using a code on a form, check with the agency or form that asks for the code.

Frequently asked questions

Questions people really search on Google, answered from the official sources cited.

How much does an LLC cost in Texas?

The state filing fee printed on Form 205, the certificate of formation for a limited liability company, is $300. After that there is no annual report fee to the Secretary of State; Texas LLCs file each year with the Comptroller as part of the franchise tax report, due May 15.

How do you start an LLC in Texas?

The Texas Secretary of State says a Texas limited liability company is created by filing a certificate of formation with its office; the form for an LLC is Form 205 and the filing fee is $300. The entity must also keep a registered agent and registered office in Texas. This page lists what the state publishes; it is not a step-by-step legal guide.

Does a Texas LLC need a registered agent?

Yes. The Secretary of State writes that a domestic or foreign filing entity is required to continuously maintain a registered agent and registered office in Texas. The agent is an individual Texas resident, or a domestic or registered foreign entity, that receives and forwards service of process and official notices.

What is the Texas franchise tax for an LLC?

The Comptroller describes it as a privilege tax on entities formed or organized in Texas or doing business in Texas. The annual franchise tax report is due May 15. For report years 2026 and 2027 the no tax due threshold in the Comptroller’s table is $2,650,000; an entity at or below it still files a Public Information Report or Ownership Report.
