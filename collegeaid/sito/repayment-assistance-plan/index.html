The official formula

The Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP) is the income-based plan created by Public Law 119-21. The payment does not depend on how much you owe. It is a percentage of your adjusted gross income, set by the band your income falls in, divided by 12, minus a fixed amount for each dependent.

monthly payment = base payment ÷ 12 − $50 × dependents never less than $10

The percentage applies to the whole income, not only to the part above the step. With no dependents, an AGI of $30,000 gives $50.00 a month; an AGI of $30,001 gives $75.00 (our calculation from the table). One dollar more of income can raise the payment by a noticeable amount at each step.

What happens to the balance

Each payment goes first to interest, then to fees, then to principal. Two rules protect borrowers whose payment is small compared with the loan, for every month in which the payment is made on time:[1]

  • Unpaid interest is not charged. If the payment does not cover the month’s interest, the rest of that interest is not added to what you owe.
  • Principal goes down by up to $50 anyway. If the payment reduces principal by less than $50, the government reduces it by the difference, up to the smaller of $50 and the amount you paid.
  • Cancellation after 360 qualifying payments. That is 30 years of monthly payments. Certain payments under other plans count too.

“the amount of interest accrued and not paid for the month shall not be charged to the borrower.”

U.S. Congress (U.S. Government Publishing Office), Public Law 119-21 (139 Stat. 72), sections 81001 and 82001, amending section 455 of the Higher Education Act of 1965[1]

“the date on which the borrower has made 360 qualifying monthly payments.”

U.S. Congress (U.S. Government Publishing Office), Public Law 119-21 (139 Stat. 72), sections 81001 and 82001, amending section 455 of the Higher Education Act of 1965[1]

Both protections need an on-time payment. The regulations count a payment as on time if it arrives on or before the due date for the current month and after the due date for the previous month. If you pay ahead, so that a payment is credited to a future month and nothing is due that month, you do not receive the matching principal payment for it.[2]

“If a borrower's payment is credited to a future monthly payment, and the payment equals or exceeds the monthly repayment amount made under (f)(5)(i) of this section, the Secretary does not provide the borrower a matching principal payment in accordance with paragraph (o)(2)(i) of this section.”

U.S. Department of Education (Federal Register), Reimagining and Improving Student Education—Federal Student Loan Program Final Regulations; Final Rule (91 FR 23768, May 1, 2026)[2]

Who can use it

  • Borrowers with a federal Direct Loan made on or after July 1, 2026 choose between two plans only: the standard plan and RAP. All of the borrower’s loans go into the same plan.[1]
  • Borrowers whose loans were all made before July 1, 2026 can also choose RAP from that date: the law adds it to the plans available for older loans.[1]
  • You can switch from the standard plan to RAP, or back, at any time.[1]
  • Parent PLUS loans, and consolidation loans that repaid them, cannot be repaid under RAP.[1]
  • Borrowers with older loans in an income-contingent plan have to pick another plan before July 1, 2028; RAP is one of the options the law lists.[1]
  • Married borrowers who file a separate federal tax return are assessed on their own income only.[1]
  • Married borrowers who file a joint return are assessed on the couple’s combined income. If the spouse also has eligible loans, each borrower’s payment is then reduced to their own share of the couple’s combined loan balance. The calculator on this page does not do that split.[2]

“in the case of a married borrower who files a separate Federal income tax return, the term does not include the adjusted gross income of the borrower's spouse.”

U.S. Congress (U.S. Government Publishing Office), Public Law 119-21 (139 Stat. 72), sections 81001 and 82001, amending section 455 of the Higher Education Act of 1965[1]

“For a married borrower filing a joint Federal income tax return, except as provided in paragraph (e)(1)(i)(A) of this section, the combined income of the borrower and spouse is used in the calculation.”

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

What is the Repayment Assistance Plan?

It is the income-based repayment plan for federal student loans that started on July 1, 2026. The monthly payment is a share of adjusted gross income (1% to 10% a year, by income band) divided by 12, minus $50 per dependent, with a $10 minimum. Any balance left after 360 qualifying payments is cancelled.[1]

How is the RAP payment calculated?

Find your income band, take that percentage of your whole adjusted gross income, divide by 12 and subtract $50 for each dependent. Example: an AGI of $50,000 is in the 4% band, so the payment is $166.67 a month with no dependents and $66.67 with two (our calculation).[1]

What is the lowest payment under RAP?

$10 a month. Borrowers with an adjusted gross income of $10,000 or less have a base payment of $120 a year, which is $10 a month, and the payment cannot go below $10 after the reduction for dependents.[1]

Does interest keep growing under RAP if my payment is small?

No, as long as you pay on time. Interest that the monthly payment does not cover is not charged to you, and if your payment reduces principal by less than $50 the government makes up the difference, up to the amount you paid.[1]

Who has to use RAP or the standard plan?

Anyone who receives a federal Direct Loan on or after July 1, 2026: those are the only two plans for such borrowers, and the choice covers all their loans. Parent PLUS loans made from that date must be repaid under the standard plan.[1]

Can I use RAP if all my loans are from before July 2026?

Yes. From July 1, 2026 the law adds the Repayment Assistance Plan to the repayment plans available to borrowers whose loans were all made before that date, except for Parent PLUS loans and consolidation loans that repaid them.[1]

Can I leave RAP later?

Yes. The law lets a borrower change from RAP to the standard plan, or from the standard plan to RAP, at any time.[1]
