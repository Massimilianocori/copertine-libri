How to read the table

  • One federal file, one date. Every figure comes from the College Scorecard institution file updated on November 2, 2026. The file runs about a year behind colleges’ own websites.
  • Net price is an average. It is the cost of attendance minus average grants and scholarships, for first-time, full-time undergraduates who received federal aid. Each college page splits it into five family income bands.
  • Public colleges: the net price counts only students paying in-state tuition. The file has no net price for out-of-state students.
  • “Left after federal loans” assumes the 2024–25 net price for four years, a dependent student borrowing the full federal limit each year and parents borrowing Parent PLUS loans up to their limit. It is arithmetic, not advice to borrow.
  • Missing values are shown as missing. We do not estimate them.

Why these 200 colleges

The Scorecard file has 6,272 institutions. This first set covers the 200 four-year colleges whose tuition and cost people in the United States search for most, by measured monthly searches. A college without a page here is not better or worse; it is just not in the first set.
