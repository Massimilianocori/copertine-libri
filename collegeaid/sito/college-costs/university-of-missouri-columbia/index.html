University of Missouri-Columbia tuition and total cost

For the 2025–26 academic year, the College Scorecard file lists tuition and required fees of $15,548 for in-state students and $37,820 for out-of-state students at University of Missouri-Columbia. The average cost of attendance for in-state students, which adds books, supplies and living expenses, was $32,790 in 2024–25, the latest year in the file.[1],[2]

Average net price by family income

Net price is the cost of attendance minus the average grant and scholarship aid. It is what families paid on average for one year, before loans. The file gives it for first-time, full-time undergraduates who paid in-state tuition and received federal student aid, in 2024–25, by the family income used to work out aid.[1],[2]

Among the 200 colleges with a page on this site, the average net price at University of Missouri-Columbia ($21,142) is the 93rd lowest, and the 76th lowest of the 103 public colleges. In Missouri it is the highest of the 2 colleges with a page here. Net prices at public colleges count in-state students only, so a public and a private figure are not measured on quite the same students.

Out-of-state students are not in these averages. For public colleges the file counts only students who pay in-state tuition, and it has no net price for out-of-state students. Out-of-state tuition and fees at University of Missouri-Columbia are $37,820, against $15,548 in-state. The cost of attendance and the gap table are also in-state only; the file has no out-of-state cost of attendance.

The gap after federal loans at University of Missouri-Columbia

For students starting now, federal loans have a ceiling. A dependent undergraduate can borrow $5,500 in the first year, $6,500 in the second and $7,500 a year after that, up to $31,000 in all.[3] Since July 1, 2026 parents can borrow at most $20,000 a year and $65,000 in total for each student with Parent PLUS loans.[4] Over four years that is at most $27,000 for the student plus $65,000 for the parents: $92,000 (our addition).

At the average net price of $21,142 a year, four years at University of Missouri-Columbia come to $84,568; the federal limits could cover all of it. The table does the same sum for each income band, holding the 2024–25 price flat for four years.

How to read this page

  • Every figure is copied from one federal file, the College Scorecard institution file the Department of Education updated on November 2, 2026. Nothing is estimated or taken from other websites.
  • The file runs behind the college’s own website. Tuition and fees are for 2025–26; cost of attendance and net price are for 2024–25. For next year’s price, check University of Missouri-Columbia directly.
  • Net price is an average, not an offer. It covers first-time, full-time undergraduates who paid in-state tuition and received federal student aid. Your own price depends on your aid package.
  • Family income is the income the college used to work out financial aid: for a dependent student, the student’s and the parents’ income together.
  • The graduation rate follows the 2019–20 entering class; the admission rate is for fall 2024.
  • The gap is our own arithmetic on the file’s net price and the official loan limits. It is not a figure published by the college or the Department.

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

How much is tuition at University of Missouri-Columbia?

Tuition and required fees at University of Missouri-Columbia are $15,548 for in-state students and $37,820 for out-of-state students for the 2025–26 academic year. The figure comes from the U.S. Department of Education’s College Scorecard file updated on November 2, 2026.[1],[2]

What does University of Missouri-Columbia cost per year after grants and scholarships?

The average net price at University of Missouri-Columbia was $21,142 a year in 2024–25, for first-time, full-time undergraduates who paid in-state tuition and received federal student aid. By family income it went from $15,038 (income of $0 to $30,000) to $27,755 (income of $110,000 or more). The average cost of attendance for in-state students before aid was $32,790. The file has no out-of-state cost of attendance.[1],[2]
