Federal student loan interest rates for 2026–27

Every federal Direct Loan first paid out between July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027 carries one of three fixed rates. Each is the yield of one Treasury auction plus a number of percentage points written in the law, up to a cap.

“The rate is equal to the high yield of the 10-year Treasury notes auctioned at the final auction held before June 1 of that 12-month period, plus a statutory add-on percentage that varies depending on the loan type and, for Direct Unsubsidized Loans, whether the loan was made to an undergraduate or graduate student.”

Federal Student Aid, U.S. Department of Education (Federal Register), Annual Notice of Interest Rates for Fixed-Rate Federal Student Loans Made Under the William D. Ford Federal Direct Loan Program (91 FR 57581, September 10, 2026)[1]

Fixed for the life of the loan

The rate is set once, when the loan is first disbursed, and does not move afterwards. A student who borrows every year ends up with several loans, each at the rate of its own year.

“The interest rate for these loans is a fixed rate that is determined annually for all loans first disbursed during any 12-month period beginning on July 1 and ending on June 30.”

Federal Student Aid, U.S. Department of Education (Federal Register), Annual Notice of Interest Rates for Fixed-Rate Federal Student Loans Made Under the William D. Ford Federal Direct Loan Program (91 FR 57581, September 10, 2026)[1]

“the rate determined for any loan is a fixed interest rate for the life of the loan”

Federal Student Aid, U.S. Department of Education (Federal Register), Annual Notice of Interest Rates for Fixed-Rate Federal Student Loans Made Under the William D. Ford Federal Direct Loan Program (91 FR 57581, September 10, 2026)[1]

“If the interest rate formula results in a rate that exceeds the statutory maximum rate, the rate is the statutory maximum rate.”

Federal Student Aid, U.S. Department of Education (Federal Register), Annual Notice of Interest Rates for Fixed-Rate Federal Student Loans Made Under the William D. Ford Federal Direct Loan Program (91 FR 57581, September 10, 2026)[1]

Student loan interest rates by year, 2013 to 2027

The same formula has applied since July 1, 2013. In these 14 years the undergraduate rate has ranged from 2.75% (2020–21) to 6.53% (2024–25); the 2026–27 rate is 6.52%. The graduate rate is always 1.55 points above the undergraduate rate, and the PLUS rate 1.00 point above the graduate rate, because the add-ons are fixed by law.

The 2027–28 rates

The rates for loans first disbursed from July 1, 2027 are not known yet. Under the formula quoted above they will come from the last 10-year Treasury note auction held before June 1, 2027. This page will change when Federal Student Aid publishes them. Loans you already have keep their rate.

Consolidation loans follow a different rule: no auction, just the average of the loans being combined.

“have a fixed interest rate that is equal to the weighted average of the interest rates on the loans consolidated, rounded to the nearest higher one-eighth of one percent”

Federal Student Aid, U.S. Department of Education (Federal Register), Annual Notice of Interest Rates for Fixed-Rate Federal Student Loans Made Under the William D. Ford Federal Direct Loan Program (91 FR 57581, September 10, 2026)[1]

PLUS loans for graduate students ended for new borrowers. The 9.07% PLUS rate still matters to parents, and to graduate and professional students who were already enrolled and borrowing before July 1, 2026. For everyone else, see the loan limits in force from July 1, 2026.

What a rate means in dollars

At 6.52%, $10,000 repaid in fixed payments over 10 years costs about $113.65 a month (our calculation). Try your own numbers in the student loan payment calculator.

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

What are the federal student loan interest rates for 2026–27?

For Direct Loans first disbursed from July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027: 6.52% for undergraduates (subsidized and unsubsidized), 8.07% for graduate and professional students (unsubsidized), and 9.07% for Direct PLUS Loans.[1]

Are these the current rates?

Yes for federal loans first disbursed between July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027. They were announced in the Federal Register on September 10, 2026. Private student loans are priced by each lender and are not covered here.[1]

What is the Parent PLUS loan interest rate for 2026–27?

9.07%, fixed for the life of the loan, for Direct PLUS Loans first disbursed from July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027. It is the Treasury yield of 4.47% plus 4.60 points; the law caps it at 10.50%.[1]

What is the Grad PLUS loan interest rate?

Direct PLUS Loans first disbursed from July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027 carry a fixed 9.07%. Since July 1, 2026 graduate and professional students cannot take new Direct PLUS Loans unless they fall under the transition rule for students already enrolled and borrowing.[1],[2]

What is the interest rate on a subsidized loan?

6.52% for Direct Subsidized Loans first disbursed from July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027. Only undergraduates can receive them.[1]

What is the interest rate on an unsubsidized loan?

For Direct Unsubsidized Loans first disbursed from July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027: 6.52% for undergraduates and 8.07% for graduate and professional students.[1]

How have federal student loan rates changed over the years?

Since the current formula began in 2013, the undergraduate rate has been as low as 2.75% (2020–21) and as high as 6.53% (2024–25). The table above lists every year with its Federal Register notice.[1]

Are federal student loan interest rates fixed?

Yes for loans first disbursed on or after July 1, 2006: the rate set for the year of first disbursement applies for the life of the loan. Older Direct Loans can have variable rates, announced in a separate notice.[1]

How are federal student loan interest rates determined?

By a formula in the Higher Education Act: the high yield of the last 10-year Treasury note auction before June 1, plus an add-on that depends on the loan type (2.05, 3.60 or 4.60 points), up to a maximum of 8.25%, 9.50% or 10.50%.[1]
