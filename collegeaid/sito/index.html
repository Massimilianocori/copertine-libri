What this site is

Paying for college in the United States runs on a handful of official numbers: the date your state wants the FAFSA form, the interest rate Congress’s formula produces each year, the most the federal government will lend, and the formula that turns a balance or an income into a monthly payment. They are public, but scattered across a 23-page form, Federal Register notices and a law.

US College Aid copies those numbers onto plain pages, quotes the sentence they come from, links the document and shows the date we read it. The calculators use only those official figures and ordinary arithmetic. Nothing here is advice about what you should borrow.

What changed for federal loans on July 1, 2026

  • Graduate students can borrow $20,500 a year and $100,000 in total; professional students $50,000 and $200,000. New Grad PLUS loans ended.[3],[5]
  • Parents can borrow $20,000 a year and $65,000 in total for each dependent student, instead of up to the full cost of attendance.[3]
  • New borrowers choose between two repayment plans: a standard plan of 10 to 25 years and the Repayment Assistance Plan.[5]
  • Undergraduate limits did not change, and students who were already enrolled and borrowing keep the old limits for up to 3 academic years.[3],[5]

“These changes include establishing new loan limits for graduate students, professional students, and parents, and phasing out the Graduate PLUS (Grad PLUS) Program.”

U.S. Department of Education (Federal Register), Reimagining and Improving Student Education—Federal Student Loan Program Final Regulations; Final Rule (91 FR 23768, May 1, 2026)[3]

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

When is the FAFSA due for 2027–28?

For federal aid the 2027–28 form must be received by June 30, 2028. State and college aid usually have earlier dates: 31 of the 59 states and territories on the official form list at least one.[1]

When did the 2027–28 FAFSA open?

On October 1, 2026: the form says to submit it as early as possible, but no earlier than that date.[1]

What does a Direct Unsubsidized Loan cost in 2026–27?

The fixed rate for loans first disbursed from July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027 is 6.52% for undergraduates and 8.07% for graduate and professional students. Graduate students can borrow up to $20,500 a year.[2],[3]
