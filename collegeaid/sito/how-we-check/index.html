How we check

How we check every figure, and the sources

Official documents kept on record, numbers that must agree between the rule and the law, and a source next to every figure.

A wrong deadline or a wrong rate costs real money. So this site publishes only what an official document says, keeps a copy of every document, and stops building its pages if a figure can no longer be found where it was.

How the figures get from the document to the page

  • We download each document with a program that identifies itself as a robot, and record its address, the date and a fingerprint (SHA-256) of the file.
  • FAFSA deadlines. The text for each of the 59 states and territories is read from page 2 of the form twice, with two different text extractions, and the two readings must agree. The dates shown next to each entry are the dates written in that text, nothing else.[1]
  • Interest rates. A program reads the tables of the notice. The build stops unless the Treasury yield plus the add-on equals the published rate on every line, and the 2026–27 line of the history table matches the first table.[2]
  • Loan limits. Each new limit is read from the final rule and from the law, and the two must give the same number. The undergraduate limits come from the official handbook and must match the totals the final rule repeats.[3],[4],[5]
  • Repayment formulas. The income bands, the $50 per dependent, the $10 minimum and the repayment periods are read from the text of the law; the bands must be consecutive.[4]
  • Every sentence in a quotation box is copied word for word and searched for in the downloaded file each time (61 sentences). If one is no longer there, the build stops.
  • A second program, written separately with the key figures typed in by hand from the documents, compares every number on every page and re-runs the calculators with its own arithmetic.

What is ours and what is official

  • Official: dates, rates, dollar limits, income bands, repayment periods and every quoted sentence.
  • Ours: monthly payments, total interest, sums of yearly limits and the “gap” figures. They are arithmetic on the official numbers and are labelled “our calculation”. The payment formula counts interest by the month; loan servicers count it by the day, so their figures differ slightly.
  • Yours: the college cost in the gap calculator. We publish no college prices.

What we do not do

  • We do not give financial advice, recommend lenders or tell anyone how much to borrow.
  • We do not use figures from other websites, lenders or news articles.
  • We do not cover private student loans, loan forgiveness programs, the exact loan fee of each year, or the deadlines of individual colleges.
  • Some official web pages cannot be read by our checking program (they need a browser, or refuse automated visits). We do not try to get round that: we use the official PDF or legal text instead, and say which document we read.
  • The 2026–27 volume of the official handbook on loan limits was not published when we read the sources, so the undergraduate table comes from the 2025–26 volume, together with the 2026 final rule stating that those limits did not change.

When we update

The FAFSA form is published once a year; states sometimes change a date afterwards, and the form is then reissued. Interest rates are announced once a year, after the May Treasury auction. Loan limits and repayment formulas change only when the law or the regulations do. A page changes only when the new official document has been read and all the checks pass again; the reading date is always shown.
