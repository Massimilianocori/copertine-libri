How the calculator works

The standard plan is a fixed payment every month until the loan is paid off. The payment is the one that brings the balance to zero in the number of months of the plan:

payment = balance × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n) r = yearly interest rate ÷ 12, n = number of monthly payments

The formula is ordinary loan arithmetic, not an official table: interest is counted month by month and each figure is rounded to the cent. Loan servicers count interest by the day, so their numbers differ slightly. The calculator does not include loan fees, interest that builds up while you are in school, or any period in which you pay less or nothing.

Minimum payment. Under the regulations a standard plan payment is at least $50 a month, in the 10-year plan and in the new plan alike. If the formula gives less than $50, the calculator uses $50 and shows the smaller number of payments and the lower total interest that result; if you owe less than $50, the payment is what you owe.[1]

“A borrower's payments under this repayment plan are at least $50 per month, except that when a borrower's balance is less than $50, the minimum payment will be equal to the outstanding amount due.”

U.S. Department of Education (Federal Register), Reimagining and Improving Student Education—Federal Student Loan Program Final Regulations; Final Rule (91 FR 23768, May 1, 2026)[1]

“A borrower's payments under this repayment plan are at least $50 per month, except that a borrower's final payment may be less than $50.”

U.S. Department of Education (Federal Register), Reimagining and Improving Student Education—Federal Student Loan Program Final Regulations; Final Rule (91 FR 23768, May 1, 2026)[1]

The standard plan: 10 years, or 10 to 25

Which standard plan you get depends on when your loans were made. If all of them were made before July 1, 2026, the repayment plans that existed before that date still apply, including the standard plan with a fixed payment over 10 years. A borrower who receives a loan on or after July 1, 2026 has two choices for all federal loans: the new standard plan, or the Repayment Assistance Plan. In the new standard plan the length depends on the total principal owed when repayment starts.[2]

Examples at 6.52%

The same balance costs less per month over a longer period, and more in total interest. $27,000 is what a dependent undergraduate can borrow in four years at the federal limits; $57,500 is the total limit for independent undergraduates. Paying more than the fixed amount is always allowed, without penalty.

If the standard payment is too high

For loans made on or after July 1, 2026 the alternative is the Repayment Assistance Plan, where the payment depends on income and family size instead of the balance. Parent PLUS loans are the exception: new ones can only be repaid under the standard plan.

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

How do I calculate a student loan payment?

Take the balance, the yearly interest rate and the number of monthly payments, and apply the fixed-payment formula on this page. For example, $10,000 at 6.52% over 10 years comes to about $113.65 a month and $3,637.92 of interest in total (our calculation).[3]

Which repayment period should I use for a federal student loan?

If all your federal loans were made before July 1, 2026, the standard plan is 10 years. If you receive a loan on or after that date, the standard plan lasts 10, 15, 20 or 25 years depending on your total principal, with steps at $25,000, $50,000 and $100,000.[2]

Can I repay a federal student loan faster than the plan?

Yes. The law says the borrower is entitled to accelerate repayment without penalty. Paying more than the fixed amount shortens the loan and lowers the total interest.[2]

How much interest will I pay on a student loan?

It depends on the balance, the rate and above all the time. On $27,000 at 6.52%, the 10-year plan costs $9,822.74 of interest and the 15-year plan $15,388.85 (our calculation).[2],[3]

What is the standard repayment plan?

A plan with a fixed monthly payment over a fixed period. For borrowers with a loan made on or after July 1, 2026 the period is 10 years below $25,000 of principal, 15 years below $50,000, 20 years below $100,000 and 25 years from $100,000. Payments are at least $50 a month. It is the plan you get if you do not choose one.[1],[2]

Does the monthly payment include fees or interest from my school years?

No. The calculator starts from the balance you type. If interest built up while you were in school and was added to the loan, type the balance including it; the figure is on your servicer’s statement.[2]

Is this an official federal calculator?

No. This is an independent site. The plan lengths and rates come from the law and the Federal Register, and the payment is our arithmetic. For the figures that apply to your loans, ask your loan servicer.[2],[3]
