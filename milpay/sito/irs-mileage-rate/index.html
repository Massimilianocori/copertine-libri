IRS Mileage Rate 2026

76¢ a mile for business from July 1, 2026 (72.5¢ from January to June), medical and moving 23.5¢, charity 14¢, plus the GSA rates for federal travel.

76¢Business, per mile, from July 1, 2026[1]
72.5¢Business, January 1 to June 30, 2026[2]
23.5¢Medical and moving from July 1 (20.5¢ before)[1],[2]
14¢Charity, all year (set by law)[1]

The IRS changed the 2026 rates in the middle of the year: Announcement 2026-11 raised business and medical/moving rates from July 1, 2026. Read on November 13, 2026.

The IRS says: “Optional standard mileage rates are used to calculate the deductible costs of operating vehicles for business, charitable, and medical purposes.”[3] “Use of the standard mileage rates is optional.” “The rates apply to fully-electric and hybrid automobiles, as well as gasoline and diesel-powered vehicles.”[3] In July the IRS said: “This modification results from recent increases in the price of fuel.”[1]

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

What is the IRS mileage rate for 2026?

72.5¢ a mile for business from January 1 to June 30, 2026 and 76¢ from July 1, 2026. Medical and moving: 20.5¢, then 23.5¢. Charity: 14¢ all year.[1],[2]

What was the IRS mileage rate in 2025?

70¢ a mile for business, 21¢ for medical or moving and 14¢ for charity.[4]

What is the GSA mileage rate for federal employees?

$0.76 a mile in your own car for official travel on or after July 1, 2026, or $0.235 if a government car was authorized and available. From January 1, 2026 the rates were $0.725 and $0.205.[5]

How is mileage reimbursement calculated?

Miles driven × the rate per mile in force on the date of travel. GSA’s example: “100 miles in your own automobile at $0.76 per mile = $76.00.”[5]

What is the charity mileage rate?

14¢ a mile. It is fixed by section 170(i) of the Internal Revenue Code and did not change in July 2026.[1]
