The formula figure, the President’s budget proposal and the final pay table are different things. Here is what each official source says, with its date.

Status as of November 13, 2026. Nothing on this page is a forecast: each line is what an official source says on that date.

Proposed, formula and enacted: three different things

Formula figure: 3.6%. 37 U.S.C. 1009 sets the default raise: the percentage by which the Employment Cost Index for private-industry wages and salaries in the July–September quarter of the year before the preceding year exceeds the same quarter a year earlier, rounded to 0.1%.[1] For January 2027 that is the 2025 Q3 figure published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics: 3.6%.[2]

Proposed: 7%, 6% or 5%. The President’s budget for fiscal year 2027 says that “the Budget funds a military pay raise of 7 percent for all military personnel ranked E-5 and below, 6 percent for E-6 to O-3, and 5 percent for O-4 and above”. A budget is a request to Congress, not a pay table.[3]

Enacted: not published yet. The 2027 rates become official when the President’s annual order with a new Schedule 8 is published in the Federal Register. On November 13, 2026 the Federal Register held no “Adjustments of Certain Rates of Pay” order published since January 1, 2026.[4] In each of the last three years the order was published between December 23 and December 30.[5],[6],[7]

What we will not tell you

We do not publish a “2027 pay chart” before the official one exists: any such table would be a guess built on one of the figures above. Until the new order is published, the official rates are the 2026 chart.

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

When will the 2027 military pay chart come out?

When the President's annual pay order with Schedule 8 is published in the Federal Register. As of November 13, 2026 it had not been published; the last three were published between December 23 and December 30.[4],[5]

Will military pay go up in 2027?

The law's formula gives 3.6% for January 1, 2027, and the FY2027 budget proposed 7% for E-5 and below, 6% for E-6 to O-3 and 5% for O-4 and above. The final figure is the one in the 2027 pay table, not yet published as of November 13, 2026.[1],[2],[3]
