How we check

How we check every figure

Three independent readings of each official cell, arithmetic checks, and the sources we could and could not read.

The 2026 tables in the Federal Register are printed as images, not text.[1] So every one of the 616 cells (542 amounts and 74 dashes) was read three separate ways, and the site is built only if they agree.

How each cell is read

  1. We download the original images of Schedule 8 from the Federal Register (6 images) and record a fingerprint (SHA-256) of each.[1]
  2. A text-recognition program reads each cell on its own, cut out along the table’s grid lines, at two different magnifications. Both readings matched on 616 of 616 cells.
  3. The official PDF from the Government Publishing Office carries its own text layer for the same images. Where that layer is readable it matched our reading on 515 cells and disagreed on none; on 101 cells it was garbled and could not be compared.
  4. Any disagreement stops the build and the cell is checked by eye on the image.

Checks on the numbers

  • Count: 28 rows × 22 columns = 616 cells, with dashes exactly where the official table prints them (for example W-5 before 20 years, E-9 before 10).
  • Order: in every row pay never falls as years of service rise, and at the same years a higher grade never pays less (O-1E pays at least O-1, and so on).
  • Caps: no rate for O-6 and below is above the level V cap of $15,408.30.
  • Last year: 374 cells compared with the January 2025 table (Executive Order 14132) are within 15 cents of the 2025 figure × 1.038. Rows E-1 to E-5 and capped cells are left out of this check because they changed by a different amount.[1],[2]
  • The calculator in your browser is checked against our own reference calculation on every grade and every column.

What we could not read

The pay pages of the Defense Finance and Accounting Service and of the Department of Defense answer our checking server with an error (403). We do not try to get round these blocks, so this site uses the Federal Register and govinfo.gov, where the official order is published. The 2020 table could not be read reliably enough for the raise history, which shows only the formula figure for that year.

When we update

The pay order for the next year is normally published in the Federal Register in late December and takes effect on January 1. The site moves to the new table only after the same three readings and checks pass.

Contact and corrections

Seen a figure that does not match the official table, or have a question about the site? Tell us the page and, if you can, the official source. Your email is optional. We cannot answer questions about individual pay: your finance office can.
