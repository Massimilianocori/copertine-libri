How per diem works

GSA: “Per diem is an allowance for lodging, meals, and incidental expenses.”[3] It sets “the per diem rates that federal agencies use to reimburse their employees for lodging and meals and incidental expenses incurred while on official travel within the continental United States.” “A standard rate applies to most of CONUS.” “Individual rates apply to about 300 non-standard areas.”[4]

  • Lodging is a ceiling, not a payment: “The lodging per diem rates are a maximum amount; the traveler only receives actual lodging costs up to that maximum rate.” “Lodging taxes are not included in the CONUS per diem rate.”[3] In many areas the ceiling changes with the season, so the tables show it month by month.
  • M&IE (meals and incidental expenses) is a daily rate; it “does include taxes and tips”.[3]
  • First and last day: “On the first and last travel day, Federal employees are only eligible for 75 percent of the total M&IE rate for their temporary duty travel location (not the official duty station location).”[3]
  • Which rate applies: “If the city is not listed, but the county is, then the per diem rate is the rate for that entire county.” “If neither the city nor county you are looking for is listed on the GSA per diem rate page, then the standard CONUS rate applies.”[3]
  • Hotels: “Hotels are not required to honor the federal per diem rates.”[3]

Example (our arithmetic): four travel days, December 7 to 10, 2026, in a standard-rate area: lodging up to 3 nights × $113 = $339.00; M&IE $51.00 + $68 + $68 + $51.00 = $238.00; at most $577.00 in all.

GSA publishes the breakdown so that travelers know “how much to deduct from your meals and incidental expenses allowance, or M&IE, when a meal is provided by the government or included in a registration fee.” “Meals provided by a common carrier or complimentary meals provided by a hotel or motel do not affect your per diem allowance.”[2]

What this covers: the GSA rates for official travel within the continental United States: the 48 contiguous states and the District of Columbia.[3] “The U.S. Department of Defense (DOD) establishes rates for travel in non-foreign areas outside of CONUS, which includes Alaska, Hawaii, and U.S. territories and possessions.” and “The U.S. Department of State establishes rates for travel in foreign areas.”[3] Those rates are not on this site.

When the rates change

Per diem rates follow the federal fiscal year, October 1 to September 30. GSA says “Rates for the coming federal government fiscal year (October 1st to September 30th) are typically announced in mid-August.”[5] FY 2027 took effect on October 1, 2026.[1] Compared with FY 2026, 259 areas have a higher top lodging rate, 18 a lower one, and Allentown / Easton / Bethlehem, PA is no longer listed in FY 2027 (the standard rate applies there unless the county is in another area). GSA renamed Oak Brook Terrace as Oakbrook Terrace.

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

What is per diem?

GSA defines per diem as “an allowance for lodging, meals, and incidental expenses”. For official travel in the continental U.S. it has two parts: a maximum lodging rate that depends on the place and the month, and a daily rate for meals and incidental expenses (M&IE).[3],[5]

What is the standard CONUS per diem rate for FY 2027?

$113 a night for lodging and $68 a day for M&IE, from October 1, 2026 to September 30, 2027. In FY 2026 it was $110 and $68. It applies wherever neither the city nor its county is listed by GSA.[1],[3],[6]

What are the M&IE rates?

In FY 2027 GSA uses five M&IE rates in the continental U.S.: $68, $74, $80, $86 and $92 a day, the same as in FY 2026. Each area has one of them all year; the standard rate is $68.[2],[5]

How much M&IE do you get on the first and last day of travel?

75 percent of the M&IE rate of the place you travel to. GSA’s table gives $51.00 for the $68 rate and $69.00 for the $92 rate.[2],[3]

How is per diem calculated for a trip?

Lodging: what you actually pay each night, up to the GSA maximum for that place and month (taxes are claimed separately). M&IE: the full daily rate for each day between the first and the last day, and 75 percent on the first and on the last day.[3]

Does this cover OCONUS, Alaska, Hawaii or foreign per diem rates?

No. GSA sets rates for the 48 contiguous states and the District of Columbia. The Department of Defense sets rates for Alaska, Hawaii and U.S. territories, and the Department of State for foreign areas; they are not on this site.[3]

Are lodging taxes included in the GSA lodging rate?

No. GSA: “Lodging taxes are not included in the CONUS per diem rate.” The M&IE rate, on the other hand, includes taxes and tips on meals.[3]
