Formula: lodging = the GSA maximum for the month of each night (one night fewer than travel days); M&IE = 75% of the daily rate on the first and on the last day, 100% on every day in between; total = lodging + M&IE. It runs in your browser.

How the calculator works

  1. It finds the GSA area you choose and, for each date, the fiscal year it falls in (FY 2026 up to September 30, 2026, FY 2027 from October 1, 2026).[1],[2]
  2. Lodging: one night for every travel day except the last, each at the GSA maximum for the month of that night. “The lodging per diem rates are a maximum amount; the traveler only receives actual lodging costs up to that maximum rate.”[3]
  3. M&IE: the full daily rate for each day between the first and the last; on the first and on the last day, 75 percent of the rate of the place you travel to.[3]
  4. Total = lodging + M&IE. A trip of one day, with no overnight stay, shows 75 percent of the M&IE rate: GSA says travelers are entitled to it “for one day travel away from your official station if it is longer than 12 hours”.[3]

What it does not include: lodging taxes (“Lodging taxes are not included in the CONUS per diem rate”[3]), deductions for meals provided to you (see the M&IE breakdown), actual-expense authorizations, any reduced rate your agency sets for long stays, and travel to more than one place. Your travel authorization and your agency’s rules decide the final amount.

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

How does this per diem calculator work?

Choose the GSA area and the first and last day of travel. It adds the GSA lodging maximum for each night (by month) and the M&IE for each day, with 75 percent on the first and last day, using the FY 2026 and FY 2027 rates.[1],[2],[3]

Is this the official GSA per diem calculator?

No. It is an independent calculator that uses the rates published by GSA for FY 2026 and FY 2027. GSA publishes the rates and its own lookup on gsa.gov.[6]
