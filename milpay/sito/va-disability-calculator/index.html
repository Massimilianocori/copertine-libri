How the calculator works

  1. Rating. Pick your combined rating, the one in your VA decision. If you only know the single ratings, type them and the calculator combines them with VA’s table (below).
  2. Basic rate. It reads the row of VA’s table for your dependent status: “Your basic monthly rate is where your dependent status and disability rating meet.”[2]
  3. Added amounts. It adds VA’s amounts for each child after the first and for a spouse receiving Aid and Attendance.[2]

At 10% and 20% dependents change nothing: “If you have a 10% to 20% disability rating, you won’t receive a higher rate even if you have a dependent spouse, child, or parent.”[2] The calculator does not work out a household whose only children are over 18 and in school: VA’s table lists that amount only as an addition for a further child.

How VA combines ratings

VA: “Calculating your combined disability rating involves more than adding up your individual ratings.” “That’s why your combined rating may be different from the sum of your individual ratings.”[3] The rule is in the regulation: “the disabilities will first be arranged in the exact order of their severity, beginning with the greatest disability and then combined with use of table I”. “This combined value will then be converted to the nearest number divisible by 10, and combined values ending in 5 will be adjusted upward.”[4]

  • 50% and 30%: the table gives 65, converted to 70%. The regulation’s own example.[4]
  • 60%, 40% and 20%: 60 and 40 give 76; 76 and 20 give 81, converted to 80%. Also the regulation’s example.[4]
  • The rounding happens once, at the end: “The conversion to the nearest degree divisible by 10 will be done only once per rating decision”.[4]

Not included: the bilateral factor. For disabilities of both arms, both legs or paired skeletal muscles, “the ratings for the disabilities of the right and left sides will be combined as usual, and 10 percent of this value will be added” before the other ratings are combined.[5] The calculator does not apply it, so with bilateral disabilities your VA combined rating can be higher than the one shown here.

Common questions

Questions people in the United States search for, answered from the official sources.

How is the monthly VA disability payment calculated?

VA's tables give a basic monthly rate for your disability rating and dependent status. If you have more than one child or your spouse receives Aid and Attendance, VA's added amounts are added to it.[2]

How does VA combine more than one rating?

Not by adding them. The ratings are put in order of severity and combined with Table I of 38 CFR 4.25; the result is converted to the nearest number divisible by 10, and values ending in 5 go up.[3],[4]

Why do 50% and 30% make 70%, not 80%?

Table I combines 50 and 30 into 65, and the regulation says 65 must be converted to 70. The table measures the second disability against the efficiency left after the first.[4]

Does this calculator apply the bilateral factor?

No. Under 38 CFR 4.26, ratings for both arms, both legs or paired skeletal muscles are combined and 10 percent of that value is added before the other combinations. The calculator leaves that step out.[5]

Which rates does the calculator use?

VA's rates effective December 1, 2025, the ones VA calls 2026 rates. As of November 15, 2026 VA had not published the table for December 1, 2026.[2]
