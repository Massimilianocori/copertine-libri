How we check

How we check every index value

Our sources and licences, the checks the data must pass and what we could not read.

Every index value on this site comes from an official file that we download from the statistics office and keep with its address, the date we read it and a fingerprint (SHA-256). The current files were read on 13 November 2026.

Sources and licences

United States: BLS CPI-U

Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers, U.S. city average, all items, not seasonally adjusted (series CUUR0000SA0, 1982-84=100), monthly from January 1913 with the annual averages, read from the BLS data viewer. A second copy from the BLS Public Data API is kept to check it month by month. BLS data are in the public domain as works of the U.S. federal government.

United Kingdom: ONS CPI and RPI

CPI index (D7BT, 2015=100) and CPI annual rate (D7G7); Retail Prices Index long-run series (CDKO, Jan 1974=100), RPI (CHAW, Jan 1987=100) and its 12-month rate (CZBH), all from dataset MM23. Source: Office for National Statistics licensed under the Open Government Licence v.3.0.

UK results for dates before 1988 use the RPI long-run series and are higher than those of the Bank of England calculator, which uses modelled CPI estimates for those years; the UK page shows the difference with an example.

Retirement and HSA limits: IRS

The 401(k), IRA, Roth IRA, SIMPLE IRA and HSA limits (2015–2027) come from IRS documents: the yearly notice on cost-of-living adjustments for retirement items, the yearly news release (2015), the revenue procedure with the HSA amounts and Publication 969 for the age-55 HSA contribution (for the years whose edition is not out yet, section 223(b)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, read in the U.S. Code on govinfo.gov, which fixes it at $1,000); the Roth IRA calculator follows Worksheet 2-2 of Publication 590-A. We keep the text of each document with its address, reading date and SHA-256 fingerprint, and store next to every figure the IRS sentence it comes from (in six sentences from HSA revenue procedures a PDF page number falls in the middle of the sentence: the quote on the page leaves that number out and changes nothing else). The checks confirm that each sentence is still in the document, that it contains the figure, that the pages print that figure, and that the calculator in your browser gives the same result as our reference code on thousands of incomes, including the edges of each range. IRS documents are works of the U.S. federal government and in the public domain.

What the checks do

  • Continuity: no year or month may be missing between the first and the last value of a series. The only gap allowed is a month the office itself marks as not collected (October 2025 in the US), and it is shown on the pages.
  • Annual averages: the average of the twelve monthly values must match the published annual average within the rounding of the index.
  • Published rates: the rates we calculate from the indexes must match the rates the offices publish (ONS CPI and RPI 12-month rates) within the rounding of the published indexes: 0.1 to 0.2 percentage points, and up to 0.3 for the RPI long-run series, which is published to one decimal place. Where an office publishes a rate (ONS CPI and RPI annual rates) the tables show the published rate, not our own calculation.
  • Official calculator: 10 results are compared with the BLS CPI Inflation Calculator, to the cent.
  • Two files, one series: where two official files overlap (BLS viewer and API; RPI long-run and RPI 1987=100) they must agree.
  • Browser = reference: the calculator in your browser is run against our reference implementation on thousands of random cases.

What we could not read

  • download.bls.gov: HTTP 403 (Access Denied) to our server; we use the BLS data viewer instead.
  • www.bls.gov: HTTP 403 to our server (www.bls.gov); public-domain status of U.S. federal works per 17 U.S.C. § 105.
  • www.irs.gov: HTTP 404; the same notice is read in Internal Revenue Bulletin 2014-48.
  • www.irs.gov: HTTP 404; the same revenue procedure is read in Internal Revenue Bulletin 2019-22.
  • www.irs.gov: HTTP 404; Rev. Proc. 2018-27 (read) restores the 2018 family HSA limit and cites it.

We never try to get round a block.

When we update

The BLS and the ONS publish a new month in the middle of the following month. We download the files again each month, run the checks and update the pages only if all of them pass.
