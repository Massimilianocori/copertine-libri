How we check

How we check every index value

Our sources and licences, the checks the data must pass and what we could not read.

Every index value on this site comes from an official file that we download from the statistics office and keep with its address, the date we read it and a fingerprint (SHA-256). The current files were read on 13 November 2026.

Sources and licences

United States: BLS CPI-U

Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers, U.S. city average, all items, not seasonally adjusted (series CUUR0000SA0, 1982-84=100), monthly from January 1913 with the annual averages, read from the BLS data viewer. A second copy from the BLS Public Data API is kept to check it month by month. BLS data are in the public domain as works of the U.S. federal government.

United Kingdom: ONS CPI and RPI

CPI index (D7BT, 2015=100) and CPI annual rate (D7G7); Retail Prices Index long-run series (CDKO, Jan 1974=100), RPI (CHAW, Jan 1987=100) and its 12-month rate (CZBH), all from dataset MM23. Source: Office for National Statistics licensed under the Open Government Licence v.3.0.

UK results for dates before 1988 use the RPI long-run series and are higher than those of the Bank of England calculator, which uses modelled CPI estimates for those years; the UK page shows the difference with an example.

Retirement and HSA limits: IRS

The 401(k), IRA, Roth IRA, SIMPLE IRA and HSA limits (2015–2027) come from IRS documents: the yearly notice on cost-of-living adjustments for retirement items, the yearly news release (2015), the revenue procedure with the HSA amounts and Publication 969 for the age-55 HSA contribution (for the years whose edition is not out yet, section 223(b)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, read in the U.S. Code on govinfo.gov, which fixes it at $1,000); the Roth IRA calculator follows Worksheet 2-2 of Publication 590-A. We keep the text of each document with its address, reading date and SHA-256 fingerprint, and store next to every figure the IRS sentence it comes from (in six sentences from HSA revenue procedures a PDF page number falls in the middle of the sentence: the quote on the page leaves that number out and changes nothing else). The checks confirm that each sentence is still in the document, that it contains the figure, that the pages print that figure, and that the calculator in your browser gives the same result as our reference code on thousands of incomes, including the edges of each range. IRS documents are works of the U.S. federal government and in the public domain.

Heating oil and propane prices: EIA

The heating oil and propane pages show the Weekly Heating Oil and Propane Prices of the U.S. Energy Information Administration: average residential prices in dollars per gallon, excluding taxes, one per Monday from October through March, since 1990. We download the two spreadsheets with the full history, the two EIA tables with the last six weeks and the EIA update page, and keep each file with its address, reading date and SHA-256 fingerprint. A new download replaces the old one only if the spreadsheet still has the expected series and units, every date is a Monday, every value has three decimals, no earlier week has changed (the EIA may revise the last two) and the six weeks of the table match the spreadsheet. Every price printed on the 43 pages carries the series, state and week it belongs to, and the checks read it again from the spreadsheet; week-to-week and year-to-year changes are compared with those on the EIA update page; averages per winter and the cost of a delivery are recalculated, and the calculator in your browser must give the same result as our reference code. Averages per winter and amounts adjusted with the CPI-U are our own calculations and are labelled as such. EIA publications are in the public domain.

UK rates from April 2027: GOV.UK

The UK rates pages copy the minimum wage rates from the GOV.UK rates page and the Low Pay Commission announcement of 28 October 2026, and the State Pension, benefit and statutory pay rates from the Department for Work and Pensions document “Proposed benefit and pension rates 2027 to 2028” of 11 November 2026. Each figure is stored with the row of the official table it comes from. The checks find every quoted row and sentence in the text we downloaded, read the tables again from the saved GOV.UK page with a separate reader, compare every amount, change and percentage printed on the pages, and recalculate the weekly, monthly and yearly pay shown by the minimum wage calculator. The rates in force that GOV.UK gives in its guides must equal the 2026 to 2027 column. The benefit and pension rates are labelled as proposed until the legislation is made. GOV.UK content is used under the Open Government Licence v3.0.

Rent increase caps: state, provincial and city pages

The rent increase cap pages copy each percentage from the official page that publishes it: the California Attorney General, the Oregon Department of Administrative Services, the Washington State Department of Commerce, the New York City Rent Guidelines Board, the governments of Ontario and British Columbia and the City of Saint Paul. Each percentage is stored with the sentence it comes from and the period it covers, and the checks find the sentence in the downloaded text. For California we also recalculate the five regional caps from the Bureau of Labor Statistics indexes for the four metropolitan areas and the California CPI of the Department of Industrial Relations: they must equal the Attorney General’s chart. For Washington the two index values on the Commerce page must equal the BLS Seattle series. The list of local California caps is read again from the saved Attorney General page. The calculator multiplies the rent by the published percentage and rounds the increase down to the cent; the result in your browser must equal our reference code. We do not say whether a home is covered by a cap. 25 files are kept for these two sections, each with its address, reading date and SHA-256 fingerprint.

What the checks do

  • Continuity: no year or month may be missing between the first and the last value of a series. The only gap allowed is a month the office itself marks as not collected (October 2025 in the US), and it is shown on the pages.
  • Annual averages: the average of the twelve monthly values must match the published annual average within the rounding of the index.
  • Published rates: the rates we calculate from the indexes must match the rates the offices publish (ONS CPI and RPI 12-month rates) within the rounding of the published indexes: 0.1 to 0.2 percentage points, and up to 0.3 for the RPI long-run series, which is published to one decimal place. Where an office publishes a rate (ONS CPI and RPI annual rates) the tables show the published rate, not our own calculation.
  • Official calculator: 10 results are compared with the BLS CPI Inflation Calculator, to the cent.
  • Two files, one series: where two official files overlap (BLS viewer and API; RPI long-run and RPI 1987=100) they must agree.
  • Browser = reference: the calculator in your browser is run against our reference implementation on thousands of random cases.

What we could not read

  • download.bls.gov: HTTP 403 (Access Denied) to our server; we use the BLS data viewer instead.
  • www.bls.gov: HTTP 403 to our server (www.bls.gov); public-domain status of U.S. federal works per 17 U.S.C. § 105.
  • www.irs.gov: HTTP 404; the same notice is read in Internal Revenue Bulletin 2014-48.
  • www.irs.gov: HTTP 404; the same revenue procedure is read in Internal Revenue Bulletin 2019-22.
  • www.irs.gov: HTTP 404; Rev. Proc. 2018-27 (read) restores the 2018 family HSA limit and cites it.
  • questions-statements.parliament.uk: HTTP 403 (browser check) to our server; the DWP publication that lists the rates set out in this statement is read instead.
  • leginfo.legislature.ca.gov: HTTP 403 to our server; the rule and the regional figures are read on the California Attorney General's page.
  • www.sanjoseca.gov: HTTP 403 to our server; the San Jose figure shown is the one in the Attorney General's chart.
  • housing.lacity.gov: page read (HTTP 200) but the yearly percentage is only in a linked PDF we did not find; the Los Angeles figure shown is the one in the Attorney General's chart.
  • www.sf.gov: HTTP 404 on the address tried; the San Francisco figure shown is the one in the Attorney General's chart.

We never try to get round a block.

When we update

The BLS and the ONS publish a new month in the middle of the following month. We download the files again each month, run the checks and update the pages only if all of them pass. The EIA heating oil and propane prices are released weekly from October through March; those pages are updated after each release. The UK rates and the rent caps change on fixed dates (1 January, 1 April, 1 August, 1 October, depending on the place): we read the official pages again when a new figure is due.
