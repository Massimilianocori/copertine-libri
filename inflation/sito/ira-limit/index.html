Traditional and Roth IRA

IRA contribution limits for 2026 and 2027

One limit for all your traditional and Roth IRAs, the catch-up from age 50, and the income ranges for deducting traditional IRA contributions.

The IRA contribution limit is$7,500for 2026, and $8,600 if you are 50 or older. For 2027 it stays at $7,500 ($8,600 from age 50). It is one limit for all your traditional and Roth IRAs together.

Traditional IRA deduction: income phase-out ranges

Anyone with taxable compensation can contribute to a traditional IRA. Whether the contribution is deductible depends on income when you or your spouse are covered by a retirement plan at work. Inside each range the deduction is reduced; above it there is no deduction. The IRS explains:

“If neither the taxpayer nor the spouse is covered by a retirement plan at work, the phase-outs of the deduction do not apply.”IR-2026-136

Common questions

What is the maximum IRA contribution?

$7,500 for 2026 and 2027, or $8,600 for 2026 and 2027 if you are 50 or older, across all your traditional and Roth IRAs, and never more than your taxable compensation.

What are the traditional IRA income limits?

There is no income limit to contribute to a traditional IRA. Income limits apply to the deduction when you or your spouse are covered by a workplace plan: for 2026, $81,000–$91,000 for single filers and $129,000–$149,000 for joint filers who are covered; $242,000–$252,000 when only the spouse is covered.

What is the IRA contribution deadline?

Contributions for a year can be made during that year or by the due date of the tax return for that year, not including extensions. The IRS notes that for most people the 2025 contributions had to be made by April 15, 2026.
