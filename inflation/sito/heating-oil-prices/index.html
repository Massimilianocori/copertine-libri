What heating oil cost in past winters, in today’s dollars

The table converts the U.S. average of each winter into October 2026 dollars with the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U, all items), the same index as our inflation calculator. Formula: winter average × CPI-U of October 2026 (337.511) ÷ CPI-U of the January of that winter. Example: $1.220 in the winter of 1990–91 × 337.511 ÷ 134.600 = $3.06 (open this example in the calculator).

What this does and does not show. The CPI-U measures the prices of everything households buy, not of heating oil. The adjusted column therefore says how much general purchasing power a gallon cost in that winter; it is not an official “real” heating oil price and not a forecast. The winter in progress is not in the table because the CPI-U for its January is not published yet.

What these prices are

The figures come from the Weekly Heating Oil and Propane Prices of the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), collected with the state energy offices (State Heating Oil and Propane Program). The EIA defines the residential heating oil price as:

“The price charged for home delivery of No.2 heating oil, exclusive of any discounts such as those for prompt cash payment.”EIA, definitions, sources and explanatory notes
  • Taxes are not included. The EIA table is headed “Dollars per Gallon Excluding Taxes”.
  • It is an average for a whole state, for one day (Monday) of each week. It is not the price of any supplier, town or contract.
  • Revisions. The EIA notes: “Values shown for the previous week may be revised to account for late submissions and corrections.” When that happens the figures here change with the next update.
  • Averages per winter on this page are our own calculation: the simple average of the weekly prices published for the heating season (October to March). The EIA does not publish them.

Updates. The EIA collects these prices every Monday from October through March and publishes them the same week. This page shows the release of November 18, 2026; the next release is scheduled for November 25, 2026. We download the new file after each release and update the page once our checks pass. From April through September the EIA collects one price a month, on the second Monday, instead of one a week; those monthly prices are not part of the winter averages or of the weekly tables on this page.

Common questions

How much is heating oil per gallon?

The U.S. average residential heating oil price was $5.717 per gallon on November 16, 2026, excluding taxes, in the weekly survey of the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA). Among the 21 states surveyed that week, the state averages ranged from $5.110 in Nebraska to $6.066 in Maryland.

What are heating oil prices today?

The EIA publishes one average price per week, for Monday; it does not publish a daily price. The latest is for November 16, 2026: $5.717 per gallon for the U.S., released on November 18, 2026. The next release is scheduled for November 25, 2026. The table on this page has the latest price for each state.

How much does it cost to fill an oil tank?

Multiply the gallons delivered by the price per gallon. At the latest U.S. average of $5.717 per gallon, 200 gallons cost $1,143.40 and 250 gallons cost $1,429.25 before taxes. The gallons delivered are those on the supplier’s quote or ticket, not the size of the tank. The calculator on this page does the same sum with the average price of each state.

What is the average heating oil price?

Over the 7 weeks of the winter of 2026–27 published so far, the U.S. residential heating oil price averaged $6.056 per gallon, against $3.620 in the same weeks a year earlier. For the whole winter of 2025–26 (October to March) the average of the weekly prices was $4.011 per gallon. These averages are our own calculation from the weekly EIA prices, which exclude taxes.
