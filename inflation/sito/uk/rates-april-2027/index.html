UK rates from April 2027

Minimum wage, State Pension, benefits and Statutory Sick Pay: the rate now, the announced or proposed rate for 2027 to 2028 and the change, each copied from its GOV.UK page.

  • Figures quoted from GOV.UK
  • 2026 to 2027 and 2027 to 2028
  • No advice, no products
£13.21National Living Wage an hour, from 1 April 2027
£250.70new State Pension a week (2027 to 2028, proposed)
£443.21Universal Credit, single 25 or over, a month (proposed)
£127.55Statutory Sick Pay a week (proposed)

What is official, and since when. The minimum wage rates for April 2027 were announced by the Government on 28 October 2026. The benefit and pension rates were published by the Department for Work and Pensions on 11 November 2026 under the title “Proposed benefit and pension rates 2027 to 2028”: they are the rates set out in the ministerial statement of 28 October 2026 and stay proposed until the legislation that brings them into force is made. If a rate changes, this page changes with the source. Sources read on 18 November 2026.

What is not announced, or not shown here

  • Final benefit and pension rates. As of 18 November 2026 the DWP document is titled “proposed”; the legislation that fixes the rates had not been read on GOV.UK by us, so the benefit and pension tables say proposed.
  • Rates that are not in the two documents read. Payments made by HMRC or by the Scottish Government, tax allowances and National Insurance thresholds are not listed in the DWP rates document or on the minimum wage page, so they are not shown.
  • The day each benefit rate starts. The DWP document gives the year (“2027 to 2028”) and not a start day for each payment; the minimum wage page gives 1 April.

Compared with inflation

The National Living Wage rises by 3.9% from £12.71 to £13.21 an hour. The latest CPI 12-month rate published by the Office for National Statistics is 3.6% (October 2026). The two figures measure different things over different periods: the rate is fixed once for the year, the CPI changes every month. To see what an amount from another year is worth today, use the UK inflation calculator.
