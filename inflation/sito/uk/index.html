CPI or RPI?

The CPI (Consumer Prices Index, series D7BT, 2015=100) is the UK's headline measure of inflation. The ONS publishes it monthly from January 1988; the latest value is 144.1 for September 2026.

For earlier years the calculator uses the ONS Retail Prices Index long-run series (series CDKO, January 1974=100). It has an annual value for every year from 1800 and monthly values from June 1947, so it can answer questions such as the value of £1 in 1950.

The two indexes measure prices differently and the RPI has usually risen faster, so the calculator never mixes them in one calculation: it uses the CPI when both dates are from 1988 on (unless you choose the RPI) and the RPI long-run series for both dates otherwise. No CPI value is linked to an RPI value.

Choose a year on its own to use that year's annual average index, or pick a month to use the index of that month.

Why results before 1988 differ from the Bank of England calculator

The Bank of England's inflation calculator also uses the ONS CPI from 1988, but for earlier years it uses CPI estimates that, in its words, “are modelled based on data collected for the Retail Price Index (RPI)”. This site uses the published RPI long-run series itself for those years, and the RPI rose faster. Example from the Bank's page: £23 in 1975 becomes £57.68 in 1985 (its price indexes: 17.78 and 44.6). With the RPI long-run series (134.8 and 373.2) the same £23 becomes £63.68. The two answers use different indexes, so for dates before 1988 expect a higher figure here than at the Bank of England.

Common questions

Can I use the inflation calculator for a salary?

Yes: enter the pay and the year it was paid. The result is the pay needed in September 2026 to buy the same as before, measured with the CPI (or, when a date is before 1988, with the RPI long-run series). For example £25,000 in 2015 corresponds to £36,025 with the CPI. It does not show what pay rises were actually agreed or what any employer must pay.

What is the current UK CPI?

The ONS CPI index (2015=100) was 144.1 in September 2026, 3.5% higher than a year earlier. The next figures are due on 18 November 2026.
