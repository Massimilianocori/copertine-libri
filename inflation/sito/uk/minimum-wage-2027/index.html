Compared with inflation

The National Living Wage rises by 3.9% from £12.71 to £13.21 an hour. The latest CPI 12-month rate published by the Office for National Statistics is 3.5% (September 2026). The two figures measure different things over different periods: the rate is fixed once for the year, the CPI changes every month. Common questions

What is the minimum wage for 18 year olds?

From 1 April 2027 the minimum wage for workers aged 18 to 20 is £11.27 an hour, up from £10.85 (April 2026). At 37.5 hours a week that is £422.63 a week before tax. Source: GOV.UK, National Minimum Wage and National Living Wage rates.

What is the minimum wage for 16 year olds?

The rate for workers under 18 is £8.31 an hour from 1 April 2027, up from £8.00; the Low Pay Commission calls it the 16-17 year old rate. GOV.UK says a worker must be at least school leaving age to get the National Minimum Wage.

What is the apprentice minimum wage?

The apprentice rate is £8.31 an hour from 1 April 2027 (£8.00 from April 2026). GOV.UK says it applies to apprentices aged under 19, or aged 19 or over and in the first year of their apprenticeship; other apprentices get the minimum wage for their age.

What is the National Living Wage?

It is the name of the minimum wage for workers aged 21 and over: £13.21 an hour from 1 April 2027, up 50p from £12.71. The Government announced the rate on 28 October 2026, accepting the recommendation of the Low Pay Commission.

What is the minimum wage as an annual salary?

There is no official yearly figure: the minimum wage is an hourly rate. At £13.21 an hour, 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks comes to £25,759.50 a year before tax (£27,476.80 at 40 hours). The calculator above works it out for other hours and ages.

How much is the minimum wage increase in April 2027?

All four rates rise by 3.9%: the National Living Wage by 50p to £13.21, the 18 to 20 rate by 42p to £11.27, and the under-18 and apprentice rates by 31p to £8.31.
