New or basic State Pension: who gets which

GOV.UK draws the line by date of birth:

“You’ll be able to claim the new State Pension when you reach State Pension age if you’re: a man born on or after 6 April 1951 a woman born on or after 6 April 1953”GOV.UK, “The new State Pension: Eligibility” · read on 18 November 2026
“If you were born on or after these dates, you’ll claim the new State Pension instead.”GOV.UK, “The basic State Pension” · read on 18 November 2026

The table above gives the full rates. GOV.UK explains why a person’s own amount can be lower or higher:

“The full rate of new State Pension is £241.30 a week. Your amount could be different depending on: if you were contracted out before 2016 the number of National Insurance qualifying years you have if you paid into the Additional State Pension before 2016”GOV.UK, “The new State Pension: What you'll get” · read on 18 November 2026
“If your National Insurance record started after April 2016 you will need 35 qualifying years to get the full rate of new State Pension.”GOV.UK, “The new State Pension: What you'll get” · read on 18 November 2026
“If you paid into the Additional State Pension before 2016 and would have got more State Pension under the old rules, you’ll get a ‘protected payment’. This is paid on top of the full rate of new State Pension.”GOV.UK, “The new State Pension: What you'll get” · read on 18 November 2026

Common questions

How much is the State Pension?

The full new State Pension is £241.30 a week in 2026 to 2027 and £250.70 a week in 2027 to 2028. The basic State Pension (men born before 6 April 1951, women born before 6 April 1953) is £184.90 and then £192.10 a week. These are full rates: GOV.UK says a person’s amount depends on their National Insurance record. The 2027 to 2028 figures are the proposed rates the DWP published on 11 November 2026.

How much is the State Pension increase?

For 2027 to 2028 the full new State Pension rises by £9.40 a week (3.9%), from £241.30 to £250.70. The basic State Pension rises by £7.20 a week, from £184.90 to £192.10. The Department for Work and Pensions published these as proposed rates on 11 November 2026.

What is the new State Pension?

It is the State Pension for men born on or after 6 April 1951 and women born on or after 6 April 1953. Its full rate is £250.70 a week in 2027 to 2028 (£13,036.40 over 52 weeks), up from £241.30. The 2027 to 2028 figures are the proposed rates the DWP published on 11 November 2026.

What is the full State Pension?

“Full” means the highest standard weekly rate: £250.70 for the new State Pension and £192.10 for the basic State Pension in 2027 to 2028. GOV.UK says the amount can differ with the number of National Insurance qualifying years, contracting out before 2016 and the Additional State Pension; with a record that started after April 2016, 35 qualifying years are needed for the full rate. The 2027 to 2028 figures are the proposed rates the DWP published on 11 November 2026.
