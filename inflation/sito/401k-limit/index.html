401(k) contribution limits for 2026 and 2027

Employee deferral limit, catch-up contributions and the total limit, as published by the IRS each year from 2015 to 2027.

  • Figures quoted from IRS documents
  • 2015–2027, including 2027
  • No advice, no products

In 2026 employees can defer up to$24,500into a 401(k), 403(b), governmental 457(b) plan or the Thrift Savings Plan. For 2027 the IRS has raised the limit to $25,500. From age 50 the catch-up adds $8,000 in 2026 and $8,500 in 2027; at ages 60 to 63 it is $11,250 and $11,750.

“All contributions” is the section 415(c) limit for defined contribution plans. The 60–63 catch-up applies instead of the age-50 catch-up for those ages; the IRS first published it for 2025.

What the limits cover

Employee limit. One limit per person per calendar year for elective deferrals, pre-tax and Roth together, however many plans you are in; a 457(b) plan has its own separate limit. The IRS explains:

“The amount of salary deferrals you can contribute to retirement plans is your individual limit each calendar year no matter how many plans you're in. This limit must be aggregated for these plan types: • 401(k) • 403(b) • SIMPLE plans (SIMPLE IRA and SIMPLE 401(k) plans) • SARSEP.”IRS, “How much salary can you defer if you're eligible for more than one retirement plan?”
“You have a separate deferral limit if you're also eligible to participate in a 457(b) plan.”IRS, “How much salary can you defer if you're eligible for more than one retirement plan?”
“Your plan's terms may impose a lower limit on elective deferrals.”IRS, “Retirement topics: 401(k) and profit-sharing plan contribution limits”

Catch-up 50+. An extra amount for participants aged 50 or over at the end of the calendar year. In the words of the IRS:

“If permitted by the 401(k) plan, participants age 50 or over at the end of the calendar year can also make catch-up contributions.”IRS, “Retirement topics: 401(k) and profit-sharing plan contribution limits”

Catch-up 60–63. A higher catch-up limit for employees who attain age 60, 61, 62 or 63 during the year (a change made in SECURE 2.0); the IRS sentences are under “The IRS wording” below.

All contributions (415(c)). The overall limit on what goes into your accounts in the plans of one employer. Catch-up contributions are not part of the total, as the IRS lists it:

“Total annual contributions (annual additions) to all of your accounts in plans maintained by one employer (and any related employer) are limited. The limit applies to the total of: • elective deferrals (but not catch-up contributions) • employer matching contributions • employer nonelective contributions • allocations of forfeitures.”IRS, “Retirement topics: 401(k) and profit-sharing plan contribution limits”

Roth catch-up wage threshold. The IRS notices set a wage threshold that decides whether catch-up contributions must be designated as Roth contributions: $150,000 of 2025 wages for 2026 catch-up contributions and $155,000 of 2026 wages for 2027 catch-up contributions. The wages are those from the employer that sponsors the plan.

“Beginning in 2026, participants of plans with Roth features offering catch-up contributions must make catch-up contributions on a Roth basis if prior-year wages with the plan sponsor exceeded $150,000 (for 2026).”IRS, “Retirement topics: catch-up contributions”

Common questions

How much can I put in my 401(k)?

Up to $24,500 of your own deferrals in 2026 and $25,500 in 2027, plus the catch-up if you are 50 or older and your plan permits it. The IRS notes that a plan's terms may impose a lower limit.

What is the 401(k) catch-up contribution?

An extra amount for employees aged 50 or over: $8,000 in 2026 and $8,500 in 2027, so up to $32,500 and $34,000 in total deferrals.

What is the super catch-up for ages 60 to 63?

Under SECURE 2.0, employees who turn 60, 61, 62 or 63 during the year have a higher catch-up limit: $11,250 in 2025, $11,250 in 2026 and $11,750 in 2027, instead of the age-50 catch-up.

What are the 403(b) contribution limits?

The same as for a 401(k): $24,500 in 2026 and $25,500 in 2027, with the same catch-up amounts. The IRS announces one limit for 401(k), 403(b), governmental 457 plans and the Thrift Savings Plan.
