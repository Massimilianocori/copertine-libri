Retirement and HSA limits

Retirement contribution limits for 2026 and 2027

401(k), IRA, Roth IRA, SIMPLE IRA and HSA limits in one place, from the IRS documents for 2015–2027, and how they compare with inflation.

  • Figures quoted from IRS documents
  • 2015–2027, including 2027
  • No advice, no products

For 2027 the IRS has raised the 401(k) limit to$25,500(from $24,500 in 2026); the IRA limit stays at $7,500 and the HSA limits rise to $4,500 self-only and $9,000 family.

Limits and inflation, 2015 to 2025

The IRS adjusts most of these limits each year for the cost of living and rounds them (the IRS notices speak of “the applicable rounding rules”), so they move in steps. Measured with the CPI-U annual averages, prices rose 35.8% from 2015 (237.017) to 2025 (321.943). The table puts each 2015 limit in 2025 dollars next to the actual 2025 limit.

Where the figures come from

Every amount is copied from the IRS document for that year: the annual notice on cost-of-living adjustments for retirement items (and the news release for 2015), the revenue procedure with the HSA amounts, and Publication 969 for the age-55 HSA contribution (for 2026 and 2027, the law that fixes it: section 223(b)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code). We keep the text of each document with its address, the date we read it and a SHA-256 fingerprint, and our checks confirm that every number on these pages appears in the quoted IRS sentence. Common questions

How have 401(k) contribution limits changed over time?

The employee limit was $18,000 in 2015, $19,500 in 2020, $24,500 in 2026 and is $25,500 for 2027. It stayed at $18,000 from 2015 to 2017 and at $19,500 in 2020 and 2021; it has risen every year since 2022.
