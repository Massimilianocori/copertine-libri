Health savings account

HSA contribution limits for 2026 and 2027

Self-only and family limits, the extra $1,000 from age 55 and the high deductible health plan amounts, from IRS revenue procedures since 2015.

  • Figures quoted from IRS documents
  • 2015–2027, including 2027
  • No advice, no products

For 2026 you can put up to$4,400into a health savings account with self-only HDHP coverage, or $8,750 with family coverage. For 2027 the limits are $4,500 and $9,000. From age 55 the limit is $1,000 higher in every year.

$9,000family coverage, 2027

$8,700HDHP out-of-pocket maximum, self-only, 2027

Extra at 55+: the additional contribution is fixed by law at $1,000 for 2009 and every later year (Internal Revenue Code, section 223(b)(3)(B)) and is not adjusted for inflation: the cost-of-living adjustment of section 223(g) covers only the self-only and family limits and the HDHP amounts. High deductible health plan (HDHP) amounts, 2015–2027

To contribute to an HSA you need coverage under a high deductible health plan: a deductible at least as high as the minimum, and out-of-pocket expenses (not premiums) no higher than the maximum. From 2026 there is one exception to these amounts, for bronze and catastrophic plans offered as individual coverage through an Exchange; IRS Notice 2026-5 says:

“for months beginning after December 31, 2025, a bronze or catastrophic plan will be treated as an HDHP if the plan is available as individual coverage through an Exchange established under section 1311 or 1321 of the ACA even if the plan does not satisfy the minimum annual deductible requirement or maximum out-of-pocket expenses requirement for an HDHP under section 223(c)(2)(A)(i) and (ii) of the Code.”IRS, “Notice 2026-5: expanded availability of health savings accounts under the One, Big, Beautiful Bill Act”

“For calendar year 2027, the annual limitation on deductions under § 223(b)(2)(B) for an individual with family coverage under a high deductible health plan is $9,000.”Rev. Proc. 2026-24 · read on 13 November 2026

Common questions

Is there a catch-up contribution for an HSA?

Yes. If you are an eligible individual aged 55 or older at the end of your tax year, your HSA contribution limit is $1,000 higher (Publication 969, editions 2015–2025). The amount is fixed by section 223(b)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code for 2009 and later years and is not adjusted for inflation, so it is also $1,000 for 2026 and 2027.

How much can I contribute to my HSA?

For 2026: $4,400 with self-only HDHP coverage or $8,750 with family coverage. For 2027: $4,500 and $9,000, plus $1,000 from age 55.

What is the HSA contribution deadline?

Publication 969 (2025) says that contributions to an HSA for 2025 can be made through April 15, 2026.
