House Bill 1217 (2025) limits rent increases for most residential tenancies and tells the Department of Commerce to publish the percentage for each calendar year:

“After the first 12 months, a landlord may increase the rent during any 12-month period of the tenancy up to 7% plus the Consumer Price Index, or 10 percent, whichever is less.”Washington State Department of Commerce, “HB 1217 Landlord Resource Center” · read on 18 November 2026
“RCW 59.18.700 specifies that a landlord may not increase the rent for any type of tenancy during the first 12 months after the tenancy begins.”Washington State Department of Commerce, “HB 1217 Landlord Resource Center” · read on 18 November 2026
“The maximum annual rent increase percentage is calculated using the June 12-month percent change in the Consumer Price Index for all urban consumers, all items, for the Seattle area as published by the United States Bureau of Labor Statistics”Washington State Department of Commerce, “HB 1217 Landlord Resource Center” · read on 18 November 2026
“The maximum annual rent increase percentage allowed for each calendar year will be published shortly after release of the June data by the federal U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, which is typically in early July of each year.”Washington State Department of Commerce, “HB 1217 Landlord Resource Center” · read on 18 November 2026
“Certain properties and situations are exempted, as defined in RCW 59.18.710”Washington State Department of Commerce, “HB 1217 Landlord Resource Center” · read on 18 November 2026

How the 2027 figure was worked out

Commerce shows its calculation. For 2027 the Seattle-area index rose enough that 7% plus the change is above 10%, so the 10% ceiling applies:

“Calculation for 1/1/27 - 12/31/27 June 2025 index value: 364.344 June 2026 index value: 380.849 Increase calculation: =(380.849-364.344)+0.07 = 11.53%”Washington State Department of Commerce, “HB 1217 Landlord Resource Center” · read on 18 November 2026
“11.5% > 10% (maximum allowable rent increase)”Washington State Department of Commerce, “HB 1217 Landlord Resource Center” · read on 18 November 2026

The two index values are the ones the Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes for Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue (series CUURS49DSA0): 364.344 for June 2025 and 380.849 for June 2026, a rise of 4.53% by our own calculation; plus 7 points that is 11.53%, the figure Commerce shows before applying the ceiling (the formula as printed on its page leaves out the division by the first index; the result is the same). For 2026 the same sum was below 10%:

“Increase calculation: =((364.344-354.824)/354.824)+0.07 = 9.683%”Washington State Department of Commerce, “HB 1217 Landlord Resource Center” · read on 18 November 2026

Common questions

What is HB 1217 in Washington State?

HB 1217 is the 2025 law that caps residential rent increases in Washington. The Department of Commerce summarizes it: no increase in the first 12 months of a tenancy, then up to 7% plus the Consumer Price Index or 10%, whichever is less, in any 12-month period. The published maximum is 9.683% for 2026 and 10% for 2027.

Does Washington State have rent control?

Since 2025 Washington has a statewide limit on yearly rent increases for tenancies under the Residential Landlord-Tenant Act, unless exempt: 10% for 2027 and 9.683% for 2026. The percentage is published each year by the Department of Commerce.

What is the maximum rent increase in Washington State?

10% for increases between 1 January and 31 December 2027, and 9.683% between 1 January and 31 December 2026, for properties subject to the Residential Landlord-Tenant Act that are not exempt.
