Saint Paul rent stabilization: the 3% limit

PeriodAny 12-month period
Saint Paul3%

Saint Paul’s limit is a fixed percentage, so it does not change with the year. The city’s summary:

“The Ordinance limits residential rent increases to no more than 3% in a 12-month period, however, there are exceptions.”City of Saint Paul, Minnesota, “Rent Stabilization” · read on 18 November 2026
“The Ordinance also directs the City to create a process for landlords to request an exception to the 3% limit based on the right to a Reasonable Return on Investment.”City of Saint Paul, Minnesota, “Rent Stabilization” · read on 18 November 2026

The exceptions the city lists, and the exemption added by the amended ordinance:

“Self-Certification Increase Exception: Available for 3%-8% (evaluated annually) Just Cause Vacancy Increase Exception: CPI + 8% Staff Determination Increase Exception: No limit with appropriate justification”City of Saint Paul, Minnesota, “Rent Stabilization” · read on 18 November 2026
“The amended Ordinance creates an exemption for newly-constructed residential rental properties, among other changes detailed below.”City of Saint Paul, Minnesota, “Rent Stabilization” · read on 18 November 2026
“May 7, 2025: The City Council adopted an amendment to the ordinance which went into effect on June 13, 2025.”City of Saint Paul, Minnesota, “Rent Stabilization” · read on 18 November 2026

Common questions

What is the rent control limit in St. Paul?

The City of Saint Paul’s Rent Stabilization Ordinance limits residential rent increases to no more than 3% in a 12-month period. The city lists exceptions: self-certification for 3% to 8%, CPI plus 8% after a just-cause vacancy, and staff determinations; newly constructed rental properties are exempt under the amended ordinance.
