Each percentage is the one published on the official page of the state, province or city, linked from the page of each place. In California, Oregon and Washington the cap follows a formula with a consumer price index; New York City, Ontario and British Columbia publish a figure each year; Saint Paul’s is fixed.

What these caps are, and are not

A rent increase cap limits how much the rent of a sitting tenant can go up in a year. Each one covers only some homes: the official pages list exemptions (newer buildings, for example), and California and Ontario say the landlord sets the rent when a new tenant moves in. A city can also have a lower limit than its state: the California page lists the local caps published by the Attorney General.

For scale: the national CPI-U, the index behind this site’s inflation calculator, rose 3.7% in the 12 months to September 2026 (BLS). The caps in California, Oregon and Washington use regional indexes and fixed months, so they do not move with the national figure month by month.

Places not covered. Only places whose official page we have read are listed; a place that is missing is not a statement that it has no cap. For cities such as Los Angeles, San José and San Francisco we could not read the yearly figure on the city’s own page, so they appear only through the California Attorney General’s chart.

Common questions

How much can a landlord raise rent?

It depends on where the home is and whether a cap covers it. Among the places with a published limit: 8.7% in the Los Angeles Area of California (increases from 1 August 2026), 10% in Oregon and 10% in Washington State for 2027, 1.9% in Ontario and 2.2% in British Columbia for 2027. Where no cap applies, this page gives no figure.

How do I calculate a rent increase?

Multiply the current rent by the percentage and add the result: $2,000 × 8.7% = $174.00, so the highest new rent under that cap is $2,174.00. The calculator above does this with the official percentage for the place and period you choose.

How much can rent increase in a year?

Under the caps listed here, between 0% (New York City rent-stabilized leases starting from 1 October 2026) and 10% (Oregon and Washington State in 2027), depending on the place, the period and the type of home.
