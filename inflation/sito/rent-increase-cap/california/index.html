California · AB 1482

California rent increase cap by area

The statewide limit is 5% plus inflation, up to 10%. From 1 August 2026 to 31 July 2027 that is 8.7% in the Los Angeles Area and 8.6% in most other counties.

The statewide cap comes from the Tenant Protection Act (AB 1482). The California Attorney General states the rule and publishes the percentage for each area; the cap changes every 1 August:

“Annual rent increases are capped at “5% plus the percentage change in the cost of living,” up to a maximum of 10%.”California Department of Justice, Office of the Attorney General, “Limits on Rent Increases” · read on 18 November 2026
“For convenience, this chart shows the maximum rent increase currently allowed under the Tenant Protection Act. This chart is informational only”California Department of Justice, Office of the Attorney General, “Limits on Rent Increases” · read on 18 November 2026
“The percentage change in the cost of living for most areas is found by looking at the April national consumer price index data published by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. The Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes separate data for the Los Angeles Area, Riverside Area, San Francisco Area, and San Diego Area. In areas where April data is not published, March data is used. For all other counties, the change in the cost of living is measured using the April California consumer price index data published by the California Department of Industrial Relations.”California Department of Justice, Office of the Attorney General, “Limits on Rent Increases” · read on 18 November 2026

Which homes the statewide cap covers

“These rent increase caps apply to most rental housing in California that is more than 15 years old, including single-family homes and condos owned by corporations, mobilehomes rented from mobilehome park management, and housing rented by Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher recipients.”California Department of Justice, Office of the Attorney General, “Limits on Rent Increases” · read on 18 November 2026
“If the tenants of a unit move out and new tenants move in, the landlord may establish the initial rent to charge.”California Department of Justice, Office of the Attorney General, “Limits on Rent Increases” · read on 18 November 2026

The notice a landlord must give is set separately:

“If the rent increase is 10% or less, landlords must provide notice 30 days before the increase can take effect.”California Department of Justice, Office of the Attorney General, “Landlord-Tenant Issues” · read on 18 November 2026

Common questions

How much can a landlord raise rent in California?

Under the statewide cap, by 5% plus the change in the cost of living, up to 10% in total, over 12 months. For increases that start between 1 August 2026 and 31 July 2027 the Attorney General’s chart gives 8.7% in the Los Angeles Area, 8.1% in the Riverside Area, 8.2% in the San Diego Area, 8.8% in the San Francisco Area and 8.6% in all other counties. A city can set a lower limit, and some homes are exempt.

What is AB 1482?

AB 1482 is the California Tenant Protection Act of 2019. The Attorney General describes its rent rule this way: annual rent increases are capped at 5% plus the percentage change in the cost of living, up to a maximum of 10%. It applies to most rental housing that is more than 15 years old.

Does California have rent control?

California has a statewide cap on yearly rent increases (the Tenant Protection Act) and, in addition, local rent stabilization laws in a number of cities and counties: the Attorney General’s chart lists 35. Where the local cap gives a lower increase than the state cap, the local rule applies.

What is the rent increase limit under Los Angeles rent control?

The Attorney General’s chart gives 3% for the City of Los Angeles for 7/1/26 – 6/30/27, for the units covered by the city’s Rent Stabilization Ordinance. Other homes in Los Angeles County fall under the statewide cap for the Los Angeles Area, 8.7%, unless another local rule or an exemption applies.

What is the San Francisco rent control increase?

The Attorney General’s chart gives 1.6% for the City and County of San Francisco for 3/1/26 – 2/28/27, for the units covered by the city’s ordinance. The statewide cap for the San Francisco Area is 8.8%.

What is the San Jose rent control limit?

The Attorney General’s chart gives 5% (every 12 months) for the buildings covered by San Jose’s ordinance. San Jose is in Santa Clara County, which is under “all other counties” for the statewide cap: 8.6%.
