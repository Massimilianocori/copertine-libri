How the calculator works

The calculator multiplies your amount by the ratio of two values of the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U), U.S. city average, all items, published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Example: $100 in 1970, when the annual average CPI-U was 38.8, is worth 100 × 337.511 ÷ 38.8 = $869.87 in October 2026.

Choose a year on its own to use that year's annual average, or pick a month to use the index for that month. The index is not seasonally adjusted (series CUUR0000SA0, 1982-84=100), which is the version the BLS uses for its own CPI Inflation Calculator and for annual averages. Monthly values start in January 1913.

One month is missing: October 2025. The BLS footnote reads “Data unavailable due to the 2025 lapse in appropriations”. The calculator does not invent a value for that month; choose September or November 2025 instead. The 2025 annual average published by the BLS (321.943) is based on the eleven published months.

Common questions

What is the normal inflation rate?

There is no single normal rate. The Federal Reserve judges that inflation of 2 percent over the longer run, measured by the price index for personal consumption expenditures (PCE), is most consistent with its mandate. The CPI-U used here is a different index: from 2000 to 2025 it rose on average 2.5% a year.

What is the average inflation rate in the US?

Measured with the annual average CPI-U, prices rose on average 3.2% a year from 1913 to 2025, 2.5% a year over the 30 years from 1995 to 2025 and 3.1% a year over the 10 years from 2015 to 2025.

How do you adjust an amount for inflation?

Multiply the amount by the price index of the later date and divide by the index of the earlier date. With the CPI-U, $50 in 2000 (index 172.2) becomes 50 × 321.943 ÷ 172.2 = $93.48 in 2025 dollars. The calculator above does the same with any month or year.
