Warm Homes: Local Grant: who can get it and what it pays for

Free energy upgrades through councils for homes on a low income. 6,803 homes upgraded to the end of October 2026.

Warm Homes: Local Grant statistics for England, reported by councils to the end of October 2026; provisional. DESNZ, published 26 November 2026.

GOV.UK says you could get free energy saving improvements made to your home if you are on a low income, getting certain benefits or living in a certain postcode area. The Warm Homes: Local Grant is only available in England, and it is run by local councils.[1] In DESNZ's words: “The Government has published its Warm Homes Plan to upgrade up to 5 million homes by 2030. The Warm Homes: Local Grant (WH:LG) is part of this plan.”[2]

Who is eligible

GOV.UK lists these conditions for the home:[1]

  • it is in England;
  • it is privately owned, either by you or by your landlord;
  • it has an Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) rating of D, E, F or G (you can find it out when you apply).

Your household income must usually be £36,000 a year or less. Above that you might still be eligible if you live in a certain postcode area or someone in your household is getting certain benefits.[1] Social housing is not on the list: the home must be privately owned.

What it pays for

If you are eligible and your council has funding available, the council arranges a home survey and might suggest wall, loft and underfloor insulation, air source heat pumps, smart controls or solar panels. The council then organises and pays for the work it agrees with you, and GOV.UK says you will not need to pay for it; if you have a landlord, they may need to pay for some of the improvements.[1]

Solar panels are by far the most common upgrade so far: 5,417 solar PV systems, often with a battery (2,739), against 1,018 air source heat pumps. Councils reported an average total cost of £7,400 per solar PV system and £14,300 per air source heat pump.[2]

How to apply

You check your eligibility and apply through the GOV.UK service. Your local council will usually contact you within 10 working days to get more information and arrange a home survey.[1]

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme has no income test: it takes £7,500 off a heat pump for owners in England and Wales who replace fossil fuel or electric heating.[3] Boiler Upgrade Scheme rules.

Common questions

Questions people in the UK search for, answered from the official sources.

What is the Warm Homes: Local Grant?

A grant in England, run by local councils, for free energy saving improvements in homes on a low income, getting certain benefits or in certain postcode areas. The council arranges a survey and organises and pays for the work it agrees with you, such as insulation, an air source heat pump, smart controls or solar panels. Councils had reported 6,803 homes upgraded with 13,972 measures by the end of October 2026.[1],[2]

Is the Warm Homes: Local Grant part of the Warm Homes Plan?

Yes. DESNZ writes: “The Government has published its Warm Homes Plan to upgrade up to 5 million homes by 2030. The Warm Homes: Local Grant (WH:LG) is part of this plan.”[2]

Who is eligible for the Warm Homes: Local Grant?

GOV.UK says the home must be in England, be privately owned (by you or your landlord) and have an EPC rating of D, E, F or G. Household income must usually be £36,000 a year or less; above that you might still be eligible if you live in a certain postcode area or someone in your household gets certain benefits.[1]

Is there a Warm Homes grant in Scotland or Wales?

The Warm Homes: Local Grant is only available in England. In Scotland, Home Energy Scotland offers grant funding to homeowners for heat pumps and insulation; in Wales, the Welsh Government's Nest scheme provides free advice and home improvements.[1],[4],[5]
