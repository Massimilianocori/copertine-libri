Heat pump grants paid in Rossendale, year by year

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme had paid grants for 84 heat pumps in Rossendale by the end of September 2026: 1.1% of all those paid in the North West and 0.09% of England and Wales.[1] In the 2025/26 financial year 22 were paid. The 2025/26 total was the same as in 2024/25. From April to September 2026 another 15 were paid (our calculation: the total to date minus the four full years).

What a heat pump costs after the grant

DESNZ publishes installation costs only for England and Wales as a whole, not for Rossendale. Nationally, the median air source heat pump with a grant paid in July to September 2026 cost £12,826 including the grant, so about £5,326 after the £7,500 grant; half of all installations cost between £10,717 and £15,461 before the grant.[1] Prices in Rossendale can be higher or lower: only a quote from an MCS certified installer tells you yours. Who can get the grant in Rossendale

The rules are the same across England and Wales. The scheme takes £7,500 off an air source or ground source heat pump, or £9,000 until 31 March 2027 where the property is heated by oil or LPG and has no mains gas connection, for owners replacing fossil fuel or electric heating; an MCS certified installer applies for you, and Ofgem checks each application.[3],[4] All the rules, with the official wording.

Solar panels in Rossendale

At the end of 2025 Rossendale had 1,888 solar PV installations with a combined capacity of 9.2 MW, up from 1,099 installations at the end of 2020. That is 62 installations per 1,000 households.[2] The count is mostly rooftop systems on homes; the capacity also includes business roofs and any solar farms in the area. DESNZ warns that many 2025 installations could not yet be placed in a local authority, so the latest figures will be revised upwards.[2]

In the North West, small solar systems up to 4 kW installed in 2025/26 cost on average £1,800 per kW and systems of 4 to 10 kW £1,627 per kW, including installation and VAT where applicable but not batteries.[5] That puts a 3 kW system at about £5,399 and a 6 kW system at about £9,761 (our arithmetic on the regional average).
