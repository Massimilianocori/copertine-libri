Heat pump grants paid in Bracknell Forest, year by year

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme had paid grants for 170 heat pumps in Bracknell Forest by the end of September 2026: 0.9% of all those paid in the South East and 0.18% of England and Wales.[1] In the 2025/26 financial year 68 were paid. That is up 51% on 2024/25 (45). From April to September 2026 another 24 were paid (our calculation: the total to date minus the four full years).

What a heat pump costs after the grant

DESNZ publishes installation costs only for England and Wales as a whole, not for Bracknell Forest. Nationally, the median air source heat pump with a grant paid in July to September 2026 cost £12,826 including the grant, so about £5,326 after the £7,500 grant; half of all installations cost between £10,717 and £15,461 before the grant.[1] Prices in Bracknell Forest can be higher or lower: only a quote from an MCS certified installer tells you yours. Who can get the grant in Bracknell Forest

The rules are the same across England and Wales. The scheme takes £7,500 off an air source or ground source heat pump, or £9,000 until 31 March 2027 where the property is heated by oil or LPG and has no mains gas connection, for owners replacing fossil fuel or electric heating; an MCS certified installer applies for you, and Ofgem checks each application.[3],[4] All the rules, with the official wording.

Solar panels in Bracknell Forest

At the end of 2025 Bracknell Forest had 3,853 solar PV installations with a combined capacity of 17.0 MW, up from 1,616 installations at the end of 2020. That is 77 installations per 1,000 households.[2] The count is mostly rooftop systems on homes; the capacity also includes business roofs and any solar farms in the area. DESNZ warns that many 2025 installations could not yet be placed in a local authority, so the latest figures will be revised upwards.[2]

In the South East, small solar systems up to 4 kW installed in 2025/26 cost on average £1,859 per kW and systems of 4 to 10 kW £1,721 per kW, including installation and VAT where applicable but not batteries.[5] That puts a 3 kW system at about £5,577 and a 6 kW system at about £10,329 (our arithmetic on the regional average).
