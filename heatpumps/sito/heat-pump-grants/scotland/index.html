Scotland · Scottish Government schemes

Heat pump grants in Scotland: Home Energy Scotland grant and Warmer Homes Scotland

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme stops at the border. What the Scottish Government's own schemes offer for heat pumps and insulation, and who they are for.

Scheme pages read on 26 November 2026; solar figures from DESNZ.

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme does not cover Scotland: GOV.UK says it is open to people in England and Wales.[1] Scotland has its own schemes, funded by the Scottish Government and reached through Home Energy Scotland, a free advice service.[2] ECO4, the energy suppliers' scheme for households on certain benefits, covers Scotland too until 31 December 2026.[3],[4] ECO4 eligibility.

Home Energy Scotland grant

Home Energy Scotland says homeowners can get grant funding of up to £7,500 for a heating system such as a heat pump or a heat network connection. For insulation and other energy efficiency measures, the grant covers up to 75% of the combined cost, up to £7,500. Rural and island homes can get an extra £1,500 on both, so a rural household can claim up to £18,000 in grants.[2]

  • Who can apply: the homeowner of an existing property in Scotland that is their only or main home. You do not need to be getting benefits.[2]
  • Heat pumps: only heat pumps that provide all the heating and hot water are eligible. Hybrid, air-to-air and exhaust air heat pumps are not (in England and Wales, the Boiler Upgrade Scheme gives £2,500 for air-to-air).[2],[5]
  • Installer: renewable systems need a Microgeneration Certification Scheme (MCS) installer.[2]
  • Funding: first come, first served and subject to availability; funding agreements are valid for nine months.[2]
  • EPC: a valid Energy Performance Certificate is needed to apply for energy efficiency improvements.[2]

To apply, the first step is to contact Home Energy Scotland (freephone 0808 808 2282); an advisor checks which funding fits the home.[2]

Warmer Homes Scotland

The Scottish Government's Warmer Homes Scotland programme offers support worth £10,000 or more for energy-saving improvements such as heating and insulation, and in most cases the Scottish Government meets all the costs. The work is assessed and installed by Warmworks.[6] Home Energy Scotland lists these conditions:[6]

  • you own the home or rent it from a private landlord, and it is your main residence;
  • you have lived there for at least six months (with some exceptions);
  • the home has a poor energy rating, a floor area of 230 m² or less (typically up to five bedrooms) and is in council tax band A to G;
  • someone in the household is 75 or over with no working heating, has a DS1500 or BASRiS certificate, or receives at least one of the qualifying (“passport”) benefits on Home Energy Scotland's list.

Improvements can include wall and loft insulation, draught-proofing, central heating (an electric storage heater, a new gas boiler or an air source heat pump) and a home renewables system. Work already completed cannot be funded, and private tenants need their landlord's permission.[6]

Common questions

Questions people in the UK search for, answered from the official sources.

Is there a heat pump grant in Scotland?

Not the Boiler Upgrade Scheme, which is open to people in England and Wales only. In Scotland, Home Energy Scotland offers homeowners grant funding of up to £7,500 for heating systems such as heat pumps, with an extra £1,500 for rural and island homes. Only heat pumps that provide all the heating and hot water are eligible: hybrid, air-to-air and exhaust air heat pumps are not.[1],[2]

What is the Home Energy Scotland grant?

It is Scottish Government grant funding for homeowners, reached through Home Energy Scotland, a free advice service. It pays up to £7,500 for heating systems such as heat pumps and, for insulation and other energy efficiency measures, up to 75% of the combined cost with a maximum of £7,500. Rural and island homes get £1,500 more on each, up to £18,000 in grants. The home must be in Scotland and be your only or main home; you do not need to be getting benefits. Funding is first come, first served.[2]

Who is eligible for Warmer Homes Scotland?

Home Energy Scotland lists these conditions: you own the home or rent it from a private landlord and live there as your main residence, usually for at least six months; the home has a poor energy rating, a floor area of 230 m² or less and is in council tax band A to G; and someone in the household is 75 or over with no working heating, has a DS1500 or BASRiS certificate, or gets one of the qualifying benefits. The programme offers support worth £10,000 or more.[6]
