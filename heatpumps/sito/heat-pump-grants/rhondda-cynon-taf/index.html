Heat pump grants paid in Rhondda Cynon Taf, year by year

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme had paid grants for 121 heat pumps in Rhondda Cynon Taf by the end of September 2026: 2.4% of all those paid in Wales and 0.13% of England and Wales.[1] In the 2025/26 financial year 51 were paid. That is up 155% on 2024/25 (20). From April to September 2026 another 29 were paid (our calculation: the total to date minus the four full years).

What a heat pump costs after the grant

DESNZ publishes installation costs only for England and Wales as a whole, not for Rhondda Cynon Taf. Nationally, the median air source heat pump with a grant paid in July to September 2026 cost £12,826 including the grant, so about £5,326 after the £7,500 grant; half of all installations cost between £10,717 and £15,461 before the grant.[1] Prices in Rhondda Cynon Taf can be higher or lower: only a quote from an MCS certified installer tells you yours. Who can get the grant in Rhondda Cynon Taf

The rules are the same across England and Wales. The scheme takes £7,500 off an air source or ground source heat pump, or £9,000 until 31 March 2027 where the property is heated by oil or LPG and has no mains gas connection, for owners replacing fossil fuel or electric heating; an MCS certified installer applies for you, and Ofgem checks each application.[3],[4] All the rules, with the official wording.

Solar panels in Rhondda Cynon Taf

At the end of 2025 Rhondda Cynon Taf had 7,233 solar PV installations with a combined capacity of 70.3 MW, up from 4,074 installations at the end of 2020. That is 67 installations per 1,000 households.[2] The count is mostly rooftop systems on homes; the capacity also includes business roofs and any solar farms in the area. DESNZ warns that many 2025 installations could not yet be placed in a local authority, so the latest figures will be revised upwards.[2]

In Wales, small solar systems up to 4 kW installed in 2025/26 cost on average £1,838 per kW and systems of 4 to 10 kW £1,424 per kW, including installation and VAT where applicable but not batteries.[5] That puts a 3 kW system at about £5,514 and a 6 kW system at about £8,545 (our arithmetic on the regional average).
