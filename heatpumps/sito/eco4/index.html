Energy Company Obligation · Great Britain

ECO4 grant scheme: who is eligible and when it ends

Help from energy suppliers with insulation and heating for households on certain benefits. The scheme closes on 31 December 2026.

The Energy Company Obligation (ECO) is “a government energy efficiency scheme in Great Britain designed to tackle fuel poverty and help reduce carbon emissions”, in Ofgem's words. Medium and large energy suppliers have to fund measures that help low-income, fuel-poor and vulnerable households heat their homes, such as insulation or a heating upgrade. ECO4 is the current phase: it covers measures installed from 1 April 2022 and runs until 31 December 2026, after a nine-month extension. Ofgem runs it for the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero.[1] GOV.UK confirms that the scheme is closing on 31 December 2026.[2]

Who ECO4 is for

GOV.UK says you might be able to get help if you claim certain benefits and live in private housing (you own your home or rent from a private landlord), or if you live in social housing.[2]

  • Benefits listed by GOV.UK: Universal Credit, Pension Guarantee Credit, Pension Savings Credit, Income Support, income-based Jobseeker's Allowance, income-related Employment and Support Allowance, Child Benefit and Housing Benefit.[2]
  • If you own your home, it must have an energy efficiency rating of D, E, F or G.[2]
  • If you rent from a private landlord, the home must be rated E, F or G, and you need the owner's permission for the work.[2]
  • Social housing rated E, F or G may get help with insulation or a first heating system.[2]

The rating is the one on the home's Energy Performance Certificate: GOV.UK points to the energy performance certificate register to look it up. Councils can also decide which homes are eligible through ECO4's “flexible eligibility” route.[2],[3]

What ECO4 has paid for

GOV.UK says the scheme can help with the cost of insulation work, for example to your loft or cavity walls, and with replacing or repairing your boiler, or other upgrades to your heating.[2] The DESNZ statistics show what has actually been fitted: 143,305 loft insulation jobs, 103,339 boiler measures (of which 19,928 first-time central heating systems), and 45,870 air source heat pumps.[3]

How to get help

  • Contact your local council to find out if it is taking part, or contact an energy supplier directly. Ofgem publishes the contact details of the suppliers taking part.[2]
  • Installers or agents might contact you directly. If you are not sure an offer is genuine, GOV.UK says to check with your local council or the energy supplier they represent.[2]
  • GOV.UK warns of quality issues with some solid wall insulation fitted through the scheme; it explains what to do about poor quality wall insulation.[2]

ECO4 and the heat pump grant

ECO4 is for households on certain benefits (or chosen by their council) in homes with a low energy rating. The Boiler Upgrade Scheme, in England and Wales, has no such test: it asks that you own the property and replace fossil fuel or electric heating, and it takes £7,500 off a heat pump. A property that has already had government funding or support for a heat pump cannot get the Boiler Upgrade Scheme grant as well.[4] The Boiler Upgrade Scheme rules.

After ECO4

The Great British Insulation Scheme, which ran alongside ECO4, ended on 31 March 2026.[5] In England, the Warm Homes: Local Grant pays for insulation, heat pumps and solar panels for households on a low income, run by councils.[6]

Common questions

Questions people in the UK search for, answered from the official sources.

What is the ECO4 scheme?

ECO4 is the current phase of the Energy Company Obligation, “a government energy efficiency scheme in Great Britain designed to tackle fuel poverty and help reduce carbon emissions”, in Ofgem's words. Medium and large energy suppliers must fund measures, such as insulation or a heating upgrade, for low-income, fuel-poor and vulnerable households. It covers measures installed from 1 April 2022 and runs until 31 December 2026. By the end of April 2026 DESNZ had counted 1,043,846 measures in 306,624 homes, including ECO3 interim delivery and surplus actions carried over to ECO4.[1],[2],[3]

Who is eligible for ECO4?

GOV.UK says you might be eligible if you live in private housing and get one of these benefits: Universal Credit, Pension Guarantee Credit, Pension Savings Credit, Income Support, income-based Jobseeker's Allowance, income-related Employment and Support Allowance, Child Benefit or Housing Benefit. If you own the home it must be rated D, E, F or G; if you rent privately, E, F or G, with the owner's permission. Social housing rated E, F or G may get help with insulation or a first heating system.[2]

When does ECO4 end?

On 31 December 2026. Ofgem says ECO4 will run until 31 December 2026, following a nine-month extension, and GOV.UK says the Energy Company Obligation scheme is closing on 31 December 2026.[1],[2]

Is there a government grant for a new boiler?

The Energy Company Obligation can help with the cost of replacing or repairing your boiler, or other upgrades to your heating, if you live in private housing and get certain benefits; DESNZ counted 103,339 boiler measures under ECO4 by the end of April 2026. ECO4 closes on 31 December 2026. The Boiler Upgrade Scheme does not pay for a new gas or oil boiler: its grants are for replacing fossil fuel heating with a heat pump or a biomass boiler.[2],[3],[4]

Does ECO4 cover solar panels?

Solar panels are among the measures fitted under ECO4: DESNZ counted 65,484 solar PV systems by the end of April 2026, along with 45,870 air source heat pumps.[3]
