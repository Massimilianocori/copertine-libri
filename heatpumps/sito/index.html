What the Boiler Upgrade Scheme pays

  • £7,500air source (air-to-water) heat pump
  • £7,500ground source or water source heat pump
  • £9,000air or ground source, oil or LPG home with no mains gas, until 31 March 2027
  • £2,500air-to-air heat pump, homes only, since 28 April 2026
  • £5,000biomass boiler, rural and off the gas grid only

Grant amounts from GOV.UK and Ofgem.[1],[2],[3] One grant per property, in England and Wales only. The installer takes the grant off your quote.

What you pay after the grant

DESNZ publishes the cost reported for every installation with a grant paid. In July to September 2026 the median air source heat pump cost £12,826 including the grant, so a typical owner paid about £5,326 after £7,500, or £3,826 with the £9,000 grant.[4]

Applications are at a record

Ofgem received 9,738 applications in September 2026, 138% more than in September 2025. From July to September 2026, 8,107 of the 25,374 applications (32%) were for the new £9,000 grant. 3,332 grants were paid in September 2026.[4] The 2026/27 budget is £400 million, and vouchers are issued first come, first served until the year's budget is used.[4]

Who can get it

  • £7,500 off an air source (air-to-water) or ground source heat pump; £9,000 if the property is heated by oil or LPG and has no mains gas, until 31 March 2027; £2,500 off an air-to-air heat pump (homes only); £5,000 off a biomass boiler (rural, off the gas grid).[1],[2]
  • You must own the property (your home, a second home, a property you let or a business building) and replace fossil fuel or electric heating. Hybrid systems, most new builds and social housing are excluded.[1],[3]
  • An MCS certified installer applies for you and takes the grant off the quote; Ofgem asks you to confirm within 14 days.[3]

Common questions

Questions people in the UK search for, answered from the official sources.

How much is the heat pump grant?

Under the Boiler Upgrade Scheme you get £7,500 off an air source (air-to-water) or ground source heat pump, £2,500 off an air-to-air heat pump and £5,000 off a biomass boiler. Homes off the gas grid that are heated by oil or LPG can get £9,000 towards an air source or ground source heat pump until 31 March 2027. There is one grant per property, and the installer takes it off your quote.[1],[2]

Who can get the £9,000 heat pump grant?

Ofgem lists £9,000 off an air-to-water or ground source heat pump for eligible off-gas-grid properties, available from 21 July 2026 until 31 March 2027. GOV.UK describes it as an extra £1,500 on top of the £7,500 when your property is heated by oil or LPG and has no mains gas connection. DESNZ counted 8,107 applications for the £9,000 grant from July to the end of September 2026, 32% of all applications in those months.[1],[3],[4]

When does the Boiler Upgrade Scheme end?

DESNZ says the government confirmed funding for the scheme up to 2029/30, with a budget of £400 million for 2026/27. Vouchers are issued first come, first served until the budget for the financial year runs out. The higher £9,000 rate for oil and LPG homes is available until 31 March 2027.[2],[4]

Can I get a grant for a heat pump?

GOV.UK says you are eligible if you own the property in England or Wales (a home, a second home, a property you let or a business building) and the heat pump replaces fossil fuel or electric heating such as gas, oil, LPG or electric heaters. It must be installed by an MCS certified installer, who applies for you. Most new builds, social housing and properties that already had government funding for a heat pump are excluded. Your installer and Ofgem check each property before a voucher is issued.[1],[3]

Is the Boiler Upgrade Scheme available in Scotland?

No. The scheme is open in England and Wales only. GOV.UK points people in Scotland to the Scottish Government's own home energy schemes, and says the Boiler Replacement Scheme in Northern Ireland is now closed.[1]

How many heat pumps has the grant paid for?

From May 2022 to the end of September 2026, the scheme paid grants for 96,185 heat pumps in England and Wales (96,611 installations including biomass boilers). This counts only installations paid through the Boiler Upgrade Scheme, not every heat pump fitted in the UK.[4]
