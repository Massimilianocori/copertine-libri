Boiler Upgrade Scheme · rules

Who can get the heat pump grant

Eligibility for the Boiler Upgrade Scheme in England and Wales, with the official wording from GOV.UK and Ofgem, checked on 12 November 2026.

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme gives a grant towards replacing fossil fuel or electric heating with a heat pump (or, in limited cases, a biomass boiler) in England and Wales. It is run by Ofgem for the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero, and it is installer-led: your MCS certified installer applies for you and takes the grant off your quote.[1],[2]

How much you can get

TechnologyGrantConditions
Air source (air-to-water) heat pump£7,500Replaces fossil fuel or electric heating
Ground source heat pump (including water source and shared ground loops)£7,500As above
Air source or ground source heat pump, off the gas grid£9,000Property heated by oil or LPG with no mains gas connection; from 21 July 2026 until 31 March 2027
Air-to-air heat pump£2,500Residential properties only; since 28 April 2026
Biomass boiler£5,000Rural property with no gas grid connection; not self-builds
One grant per property.[1],[2],[3]

GOV.UK describes the £9,000 rate as an additional £1,500 “until March 2027” for a property heated by oil or LPG that does not have a mains gas connection; Ofgem lists it as £9,000 “available from 21 July 2026 until 31 March 2027”. Both describe the same grant.[2],[3]

Who is eligible

  • You own the property: your home, a second home, a property you rent out to tenants or a business building.[2]
  • You replace fossil fuel or electric heating, such as gas, oil, LPG or electric heaters. You cannot get a grant to replace an existing low carbon system.[2],[3]
  • You are still eligible if you have already had funding for insulation or other energy efficiency work.[2]
  • The property is in England or Wales. Scotland and Northern Ireland are not covered.[1],[2]

Properties that cannot get the grant

  • Most new builds, and any new build the developer is still building. If you move into a finished new build that has a fossil fuel boiler, you may be able to get the grant.[2]
  • Social housing: Ofgem means accommodation provided at lower than market value to people who could not otherwise afford it, including shared ownership.[3]
  • A property that has already had government funding (or Energy Company Obligation funding) for a heat pump or biomass boiler.[2],[3]
  • Non-residential buildings, if the system is an air-to-air heat pump.[3]

Self-builds can qualify if you or the original owner built the property (or paid a builder to) and it has never been owned by a business or organisation.[2]

What the system must be

  • Not a hybrid: a heat pump combined with a gas or oil boiler is not eligible.[2],[3]
  • Able to meet the full space heating and hot water needs of the property, and sized for the full space heating need.[3]
  • Up to 45 kWth for a single system (70 kWth with more than one heat pump, 300 kWth for shared ground loops).[2]
  • Installed and commissioned by an MCS certified installer; since 28 April 2026 MCS is the only certification scheme accepted.[2],[4]
  • Biomass boilers only in rural properties with no gas grid connection, with an emissions certificate.[2],[3]

How the application works

  1. Get quotes from MCS certified installers. Ofgem recommends getting more than one.[2],[3],[5]
  2. Your installer confirms you are eligible, you agree a quote, and the installer applies to Ofgem for you.[2]
  3. Ofgem contacts you to confirm the installer is acting for you. If you do not confirm within 14 calendar days, the application may be rejected.[3]
  4. Once the voucher is approved the installer has three months to finish the work (six months for a ground source heat pump), then claims the grant from Ofgem.[3],[4]
  5. The grant appears as a discount on your quote and invoice. Since 28 April 2026 installers must take it off the upfront cost.[2],[4]

Ofgem or MCS may contact you, or visit, to check the installation, including after the grant has been paid.[2],[3]

VAT

From 1 April 2022 to 31 March 2027, the installation of heat pumps and biomass boilers is charged VAT at the zero rate, according to Ofgem.[3]

This grant was available for off-gas grid properties heated by oil or liquefied petroleum gas (LPG).
From the “Scheme background” sheet of the DESNZ statistics.[4]

The scheme is in its fifth year, with a budget of £400 million for 2026/27. The Government confirmed funding for further years up to 2029/30 through the Warm Homes Plan in January 2026. Vouchers are issued on a first come, first served basis, to applicants who meet the eligibility requirements until the budget cap for the financial year is reached.[4]

Common questions

Questions people in the UK search for, answered from the official sources.

What are the Boiler Upgrade Scheme eligibility criteria?

GOV.UK lists two conditions for you: you must own the property you are applying for (including a business, a second home or a property you rent out) and you must be replacing fossil fuel heating such as oil, gas, electric or LPG. The installer must be MCS certified and must commission the heat pump within 120 days of applying. You are still eligible if you have already had funding for insulation.[2]

Can landlords get the heat pump grant?

Yes. GOV.UK says you can apply for a property you rent out to tenants. Social housing is excluded: Ofgem defines it as accommodation provided at lower than market value to people who could not otherwise afford it, including shared ownership.[2],[3]

Can you get a heat pump grant on a new build?

Usually not: most new build properties are excluded, and so are new builds the developer is still building. If you move into a finished new build that has a fossil fuel boiler, GOV.UK says you may be able to get the grant. Self-builds are eligible if you or the original owner built them (or paid a builder to) and they have never been owned by a business or organisation.[2],[3]

Do I need an EPC for the Boiler Upgrade Scheme?

The rules on Energy Performance Certificates have been loosened twice. From 8 May 2024 you no longer needed to have no outstanding loft and cavity wall insulation recommendations, and the changes of 28 April 2026 include “relaxing the requirement for a valid EPC”, in the words of DESNZ. Ofgem still suggests reading the improvements listed on your EPC if you have one. Your installer confirms what your application needs.[3],[4]

Can I get £2,500 for an air-to-air heat pump?

Yes, since 28 April 2026 the scheme gives £2,500 off an air-to-air heat pump, for residential properties only. By the end of September 2026 DESNZ had counted 157 applications for air-to-air heat pumps and no grants paid yet; it will add air-to-air figures once the first grants are paid.[1],[4]

How do I apply for the Boiler Upgrade Scheme?

You do not apply yourself. You get quotes from MCS certified installers, agree one, and that installer applies to Ofgem for you. Ofgem then asks you to confirm, within 14 calendar days, that the installer acts for you. After approval the installer has three months to fit the system (six months for a ground source heat pump) and claims the grant at the end; the grant is taken off your quote.[2],[3]

Is there VAT on heat pumps in the UK?

Not until 31 March 2027. Ofgem says that from 1 April 2022 to 31 March 2027 the installation of energy-saving materials, including heat pumps and biomass boilers, is charged VAT at the zero rate.[3]

Is the Boiler Upgrade Scheme available in Wales?

Yes. The scheme covers England and Wales with the same rules and grant amounts. By the end of September 2026 it had paid grants for 5,148 installations in Wales.[2],[4]
