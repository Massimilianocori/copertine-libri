Solar panel grants in the UK: which official schemes pay for panels

Where solar panels are paid for by a government scheme, who those schemes are for, and the VAT and export payments that apply to everyone.

There is no single UK-wide grant for solar panels. Panels are paid for through schemes for eligible households: in England the Warm Homes: Local Grant run by councils, in Wales the Nest scheme, and across Great Britain the Energy Company Obligation (ECO4) until 31 December 2026.[1],[2],[3] The Boiler Upgrade Scheme does not cover solar panels: its grants are for heat pumps and biomass boilers.[4]

England: Warm Homes: Local Grant

Solar panels are on the GOV.UK list of improvements the council might suggest, together with insulation, air source heat pumps and smart controls. The council organises and pays for the work it agrees with you, and GOV.UK says you will not need to pay for it. The home must be privately owned, rated D to G, and household income must usually be £36,000 a year or less (or you live in a certain postcode area, or someone in the household gets certain benefits).[1]

Solar PV is the most common measure so far: councils reported 4,297 systems by the end of July 2026, at an average total cost of £7,200 each, and 2,000 batteries.[5] Warm Homes: Local Grant in detail.

Great Britain: ECO4, until 31 December 2026

The Energy Company Obligation helps households on certain benefits with insulation and heating; DESNZ counts 65,484 solar PV systems among the measures fitted under ECO4 to the end of April 2026.[3],[6] ECO4 eligibility.

Wales: Nest

Nest is a Welsh Government scheme that provides free advice and home improvements. Nest can confirm whether a household is eligible for a package of free home energy efficiency improvements, such as a heat pump, insulation or solar panels.[2],[7] Nest and heat pump grants in Wales.

Scotland

Home Energy Scotland's funding table for homeowners marks grant funding as “Not available” for solar thermal and for hybrid solar PV and water heating systems, the solar technologies it lists; its grants are for heat pumps, insulation and other measures.[8] Warmer Homes Scotland, for households on certain benefits, lists installing a home renewables system among its possible improvements.[9] Grants in Scotland.

Northern Ireland

nidirect's energy saving grants page lists the Affordable Warmth Scheme, run by the Northern Ireland Housing Executive, and the Northern Ireland Sustainable Energy Programme, and points to NI Energy Advice for referrals to energy grants.[10]

VAT on solar panels

HMRC says a zero rate applies to the installation of certain energy-saving materials, solar panels among them, from 1 May 2023 to 31 March 2027, when installed in residential accommodation. Batteries for storing energy were added from 1 February 2024. From 1 April 2027 the reduced rate of 5% applies again.[11]

Selling your spare electricity: the Smart Export Guarantee

The Smart Export Guarantee is not a grant. Since 1 January 2020 it has required some electricity suppliers to pay small-scale generators for the low-carbon electricity they export to the grid. Suppliers set the rate, contract length and other terms; the rate must always be above zero, payments are based on export meter readings, and installations must be in Great Britain. Ofgem advises shopping around for the tariff that suits you.[12]

What solar panels cost

DESNZ puts the median cost of systems up to 4 kW installed in 2025/26 at £1,595 per kW of panel capacity, so a 3 kW system comes to about £4,785 (our arithmetic).[13] Solar panel costs and installations by region.

Common questions

Questions people in the UK search for, answered from the official sources.

Are there government grants for solar panels?

There is no UK-wide solar panel grant. In England, solar panels are among the improvements the Warm Homes: Local Grant can pay for, for households on a low income, on certain benefits or in certain postcode areas; councils had reported 4,297 solar PV systems by the end of July 2026, at an average total cost of £7,200. In Wales, Nest's free improvements can include solar panels. The Boiler Upgrade Scheme pays towards heat pumps and biomass boilers, not solar panels.[1],[2],[4],[5]

Can I get free solar panels?

Only through schemes for eligible households. Under the Warm Homes: Local Grant in England, the council organises and pays for the work it agrees with you and GOV.UK says you will not need to pay for it; if you rent, your landlord may need to pay for some of it. In Wales, Nest offers a package of free improvements that can include solar panels, and ECO4 has fitted 65,484 solar PV systems. GOV.UK warns that installers or agents might contact you directly: if you are not sure an offer is genuine, check with your council.[1],[2],[3],[6]

Is there a solar panel grant in Scotland?

Home Energy Scotland's funding table for homeowners marks grant funding as “Not available” for solar thermal and for hybrid solar PV and water heating systems; its grants are for heat pumps, insulation and other measures. Warmer Homes Scotland, for households on certain benefits, lists installing a home renewables system among its possible improvements.[8],[9]

Is there a solar panel grant in Wales?

Through Nest, a Welsh Government scheme. Nest can confirm whether a household is eligible for a package of free home energy efficiency improvements, such as a heat pump, insulation or solar panels. Nest is free on 0808 808 2244.[2],[7]

What is the Smart Export Guarantee?

A government-backed scheme, launched on 1 January 2020, that requires some electricity suppliers to pay small-scale generators (installations up to 5 MW, solar panels among them) for the low-carbon electricity they export to the grid. Suppliers set the rate and terms, the rate must always be above zero, and payments are based on export meter readings. It applies in Great Britain; Ofgem advises shopping around for the tariff that suits you.[12]

Is there VAT on solar panels?

Not on installations in homes at the moment: HMRC says a zero rate applies to the installation of certain energy-saving materials, including solar panels and, from 1 February 2024, batteries, from 1 May 2023 to 31 March 2027. From 1 April 2027 the reduced rate of 5% applies again.[11]
