About Before You Fly and our sources

Before You Fly is an independent project. It collects official travel rules and shows them word for word, with the link and the date we checked them. We are not affiliated with any government, airport or airline.

Rules we follow

  • Only official sources: the UK government (GOV.UK, FCDO), the European Commission and EU portals, airport operators, and national aviation security authorities (TSA, CATSA, Hong Kong Civil Aviation Department).
  • Every rule is quoted, not rewritten. Our short summaries are labelled as such and the official text is always on the same page.
  • If we cannot read an official page, or it is unclear, we leave it out and say so.
  • When two official texts disagree, we show both and do not choose.
  • This is information, not legal advice. Airlines, airports and border officials decide.

Passport validity (FCDO)

Source: the “Passport validity requirements” section of the FCDO foreign travel advice for each destination, read through the GOV.UK Content API (api/content/foreign-travel-advice), last checked on 10 October 2026. The FCDO advice is written for British citizens.

Licence: contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0. We reproduce the FCDO text unchanged; summaries, the short labels and the date checker are ours.

How the checker works: it applies the FCDO sentence literally. “At least 6 months after the date you arrive” means the expiry date must be on or after the same day of the month 6 months after arrival (if that day does not exist, the last day of that month). “More than” means at least one day later. “Valid for the duration of your stay” means not expiring before the day you leave. The Schengen “date of issue less than 10 years before the date you arrive” means issued after the same day 10 years before arrival.

Destinations where we do not apply the rule automatically (12), because the official sentence has conditions we do not turn into a calculation:

  • Afghanistan: No sentence states a validity period we can apply.
  • Azerbaijan: The official sentence has conditions we do not turn into a calculation.
  • Bangladesh: The official sentence does not match a rule pattern we can apply with confidence.
  • Colombia: The official sentence has conditions we do not turn into a calculation.
  • Cook Islands, Tokelau and Niue: The official page gives different rules for different territories.
  • Dominican Republic: The official sentence has conditions we do not turn into a calculation.
  • Ireland: The official page has no 'Passport validity requirements' section; it has a 'Passport and ID requirements' section instead.
  • Pakistan: The official sentence has conditions we do not turn into a calculation.
  • Russia: The official sentence has conditions we do not turn into a calculation.
  • Sierra Leone: The official sentence has conditions we do not turn into a calculation.
  • South Georgia and the South Sandwich Islands: The official sentence has conditions we do not turn into a calculation.
  • St Helena, Ascension and Tristan da Cunha: The official page gives different rules for different territories.

No page published (the official section is empty):

  • Antarctica/British Antarctic Territory: The official page has no text under this heading.

Liquids rules

Each airport's official security page, last read by us on 10 October 2026 (the date is shown on each page). Some airport sites block simple automated requests or load their text with JavaScript: we read those in a browser and say so on the page. Spanish airports use the general hand-luggage page of Aena, which runs Spain's public airports (it lists no airport- or lane-specific exceptions); Hong Kong uses the Civil Aviation Department page; the US and Canada each have one national rule (TSA, CATSA). See the full list and the airports we left out on the liquids page.
