Driving abroad: do I need an international driving permit? UK, Canadian and Australian licences (2026)

Whether you need an international driving permit (IDP) depends on the country you visit and on the licence you hold. For each country we quote what the UK government (GOV.UK and FCDO), the Government of Canada (travel.gc.ca) and the Australian Government (Smartraveller) say about driving there with their licence, with the date we checked it (last check 28 October 2026).

Rules change, and car hire companies can ask for more than the law. We quote the official travel advice word for word, with its date; this is information, not legal advice. If you are unsure, ask the embassy of the country you are visiting, your car hire company or your travel insurer before you travel.

How we check

For UK licences we read two GOV.UK pages: the international driving permit table (“Country or territory / Type of IDP / More information”) and the driving paragraphs of the FCDO travel advice for each country. A table row with nothing under “More information” only tells you the type of IDP: the answer then comes from the FCDO advice. When the two pages disagree we give the more cautious answer and quote both. For Canadian licences we read the “Driving” part of the “Laws and culture” section of the Government of Canada travel advice for each destination, through the official open data files. “Must carry an international driving permit” is “IDP needed”; “should carry” is “Recommended”. For Australian licences we read the driving paragraphs (“Driving permit” and similar) of the “Local travel” part of the Smartraveller advice for each destination. Smartraveller blocks simple automated requests, so its pages are read in a browser. We only use official government sources and never fill a gap with our own reading: where the advice is silent, we say so. More on the About page.
