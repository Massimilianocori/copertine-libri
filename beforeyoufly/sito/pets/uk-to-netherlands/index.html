Taking your dog or cat from the UK to Netherlands (2026)

Pets · UK to Netherlands2026

According to GOV.UK and the European Commission, checked on 31 October 2026, to take a dog, cat or ferret from Great Britain to Netherlands you need a microchip, a rabies vaccination given at least 21 full days before you travel and an animal health certificate, valid for entering the EU for 10 days; Netherlands is not on GOV.UK's tapeworm list, so dogs need no tapeworm treatment to enter, but they need one before coming back to Great Britain.

Changes expected in 2027

GOV.UK says that pet owners will be able to get a multiuse pet passport for EU travel when the UK–EU SPS agreement takes effect: “It is our intention that the agreement will take effect in mid-2027.” Until then, the animal health certificate rules on this page apply. What GOV.UK says so far about the 2027 pet passport.

This is not veterinary advice. A missing step can mean that your pet is refused entry or put into quarantine. We quote GOV.UK and the European Commission word for word, with the date we checked them. Check with your vet and the official page before you travel.

What GOV.UK says

You must get your pet microchipped before, or at the same time as, their rabies vaccination. If you do not, they’ll need to be vaccinated again.

You will have to wait at least 21 full days after the first rabies vaccination (or the last of the first course of rabies vaccinations) is given. Day 1 is the day after the rabies vaccination.

You must get an animal health certificate before you enter the EU. The certificate is valid for travel any time within 10 days after it’s issued by your vet, so you’ll need to travel during this time. Day 1 is the date the animal health certificate is issued.

You must take your pet to your vet to get an animal health certificate. The certificate needs to be signed by an ‘official veterinarian’ (OV).

If you live in England, Scotland or Wales, you cannot use a pet passport to enter the EU - even if it was issued in the EU. If you use a pet passport instead of an animal health certificate, your pet may be refused entry.

Source: GOV.UK: Taking your pet dog, cat or ferret abroad (GOV.UK (Animal and Plant Health Agency)), checked on 31 October 2026. Quoted word for word.

Tapeworm treatment for dogs

Netherlands is not on the GOV.UK list, so there is no tapeworm rule to enter:

A vet must treat your dog for tapeworm and record it in their pet travel document if you’re travelling to:

  • Finland
  • Ireland
  • Malta
  • Northern Ireland (unless you have a Northern Ireland pet travel document)
  • Norway

Source: GOV.UK: Taking your pet dog, cat or ferret abroad (GOV.UK (Animal and Plant Health Agency)), checked on 31 October 2026. Quoted word for word.

Arriving in Netherlands

You’ll need to go through a travellers’ point of entry when you arrive in an EU country.

Check what you need to do before you can travel with your pet dog, cat or ferret to an EU country. You’ll also need to check for any additional rules or requirements for the country you’re travelling to. These rules also apply to assistance dogs.

Source: GOV.UK: Taking your pet dog, cat or ferret abroad (GOV.UK (Animal and Plant Health Agency)), checked on 31 October 2026. Quoted word for word.

Coming back to Great Britain from Netherlands

Your pet can enter Great Britain (England, Wales and Scotland) with an animal health certificate (AHC) if all the following apply:

  • the AHC was issued in Great Britain
  • you’re travelling from the EU or another country where you can use an AHC
  • it’s less than 6 months since the date you entered the EU or a country that accepts AHCs

If you stay abroad for more than 6 months, to bring your pet back to Great Britain you’ll need a Great Britain pet health certificate.

If you have a dog, you may need a vet to give them tapeworm treatment. Your dog must be treated no less than 24 hours before (and no more than 5 days before) you arrive in Great Britain.

Source: GOV.UK: Bringing your pet dog, cat or ferret to Great Britain (GOV.UK (Animal and Plant Health Agency)), checked on 31 October 2026. Quoted word for word.

Questions

What do I need to take my dog from the UK to Netherlands?

GOV.UK says: “You must get your pet microchipped before, or at the same time as, their rabies vaccination. If you do not, they’ll need to be vaccinated again.” “You will have to wait at least 21 full days after the first rabies vaccination (or the last of the first course of rabies vaccinations) is given. Day 1 is the day after the rabies vaccination.” “You must get an animal health certificate before you enter the EU. The certificate is valid for travel any time within 10 days after it’s issued by your vet, so you’ll need to travel during this time. Day 1 is the date the animal health certificate is issued.” Netherlands is not on GOV.UK’s tapeworm list, so no tapeworm treatment is needed to enter.

Are the rules the same for a cat?

Yes for the microchip, the rabies vaccination and the animal health certificate; the tapeworm treatment is for dogs only. GOV.UK and the European Commission say: “Check what you need to do before you can travel with your pet dog, cat or ferret to an EU country. You’ll also need to check for any additional rules or requirements for the country you’re travelling to. These rules also apply to assistance dogs.” “Pet dogs (not pet cats or pet ferrets) must be treated against the parasite Echinococcus multilocularis prior to entering: any EU country listed in Annex XIX to Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/620, (Finland, Ireland, Malta and United Kingdom (Northern Ireland)). Norway.”
