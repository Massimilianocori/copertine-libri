Payday Super · from 1 July 2026

Payday Super: super due within 7 business days of payday

What the Payday Superannuation Act 2025 requires of employers from 1 July 2026, read from the Act itself.

Payday Super is a federal change, separate from state payroll tax. The Treasury Laws Amendment (Payday Superannuation) Act 2025 (assented to on 6 November 2025) commenced on 1 July 2026.[1] Instead of paying super guarantee contributions quarterly, employers must make sure contributions reach each employee's fund shortly after every payday.

The new deadline: 7 business days after payday

Every day on which you pay an employee qualifying earnings is a QE day. The contribution for that payday has to be received by the fund within the usual period, which starts on the QE day and ends on the seventh business day after it.[1] Business days exclude weekends and public holidays that apply in a whole state or territory.[1]

A longer period, ending on the 20th business day after the QE day, applies in some cases, for example to the first contribution to a fund for a new employee or after you stop paying into another fund.[1]

The super guarantee is worked out on qualifying earnings: ordinary time earnings, commissions, directors' fees and some other payments, plus amounts the employee salary-sacrifices into super. The charge percentage is 12.[1]

If a contribution is late or short

You owe the superannuation guarantee charge for that QE day. It is made up of the shortfall, a notional earnings component, an administrative uplift equal to 60% of the shortfall and notional earnings (which regulations can reduce, for example if you disclose voluntarily), and a choice loading if you did not follow the employee's choice of fund.[1]

Clearing houses

The Act repeals the definition of “approved clearing house” in the Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act 1992.[1] For what this means for the ATO's Small Business Superannuation Clearing House and for your payroll process, check the ATO's Payday Super page: ato.gov.au does not answer our checking server, so we do not summarise it here.[2]

Payday Super and payroll tax

The two are separate, but connected: employer super contributions are taxable wages for payroll tax in every state.[3] A payroll that grows past a state threshold has to manage both: monthly payroll tax returns and super paid within days of each payday. See what to do when you go over the threshold.

Common questions

Questions people in Australia search for, answered from the official sources.

When is super due under Payday Super?

From 1 July 2026, contributions must be received by the employee's fund within 7 business days after the day you pay their qualifying earnings (the QE day). The first contribution for a new employee, or to a new fund, can take up to 20 business days.[1]

What are the penalties under Payday Super?

If contributions arrive late or short you owe the superannuation guarantee charge for that payday, which includes the shortfall, a notional earnings component and an administrative uplift of up to 60% of the shortfall (which regulations can reduce), plus a choice loading if you ignored the employee's choice of fund.[1]
