The main changes, year by year

  • 2022-23: Queensland widened its deduction range so that the deduction reaches zero at $10.4 million instead of $6.5 million, from 1 January 2023.[7]
  • 2023-24: Western Australia's extra 6% and 6.5% rates for payrolls above $100 million ended on 30 June 2023.[9] Victoria's COVID-19 debt surcharge began on 1 July 2023 (the mental health and wellbeing surcharge started on 1 January 2022).[4]
  • 2024-25: Victoria raised its threshold from $700,000 to $900,000 and started phasing it out between $3m and $5m (45%); the ACT added surcharges for employers above $50 million.[2],[3],[13]
  • 2025-26: Victoria went to $1,000,000 and a 50% phase-out; the ACT raised its surcharges and, from 1 January 2026, charged 8.75% on employers above $150 million.[4],[14],[15]
  • 2026-27: the ACT threshold fell to $1,750,000 with five rates from 6.75% to 8.75%; the NT added 6.5% for payrolls of $100 million or more.[16],[17]

Common questions

Questions people in Australia search for, answered from the official sources.

How has the Victorian payroll tax threshold changed?

$700,000 from 1 July 2021 to 30 June 2024, $900,000 in 2024-25 and $1,000,000 from 1 July 2025. The phase-out between $3 million and $5 million started on 1 July 2024 (45%, then 50% from 2025-26).[2],[3]

What was the NSW payroll tax threshold in 2022?

$1,200,000, at a rate of 5.45%, for 2022-23. It has stayed at $1,200,000 and 5.45% every year since.[1]
