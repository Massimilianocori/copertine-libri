How NSW payroll tax is worked out

NSW charges 5.45% on the wages you pay in NSW above your tax-free threshold of $1,200,000.[1] You get the whole threshold only if you pay wages only in NSW, for the whole year, and are not part of a group.

If you also pay wages in another state or territory, your threshold is $1,200,000 × NSW wages ÷ total Australian wages. If you paid wages for only part of the year, it is reduced again by the days you paid wages ÷ the days in the financial year (days you paid wages anywhere in Australia: an employer that keeps paying wages interstate keeps the full year).[1],[2]

In a group, only one member claims the threshold (the designated group employer, or the group single lodger), worked out on the group's NSW and Australian wages.[1]

threshold = $1,200,000 × (NSW wages ÷ Australian wages) × (days paid ÷ 365)
tax       = (NSW wages − threshold) × 5.45%

Formulas used
  1. New South Wales: threshold = $1,200,000 × share × days; tax = (NSW wages − threshold) × 5.45%.

Where W is total Australian wages (yours or your group's), the share is the state's wages ÷ W and days is the days you paid wages ÷ 365. Method and sources.

Monthly threshold and registration

The monthly threshold for 2026-27 is $92,055 for a 28-day month, $98,630 for a 30-day month and $101,918 for a 31-day month.[1] Register with Revenue NSW within 7 days after the month in which your total Australian wages (or your group's) go above it.[3]

Due dates

Monthly returns are due on the 7th of the following month; the December 2026 return is due on 14 January 2027. There is no June monthly return: June goes into the annual return, due on 28 July 2027.[4]

All states: see payroll tax due dates.

What changed in 2026-27

Nothing changed on 1 July 2026: the threshold ($1,200,000) and the rate (5.45%) have been the same in every year since 2022-23.[1]

NSW payroll tax: common questions

Questions people in Australia search for, answered from the official sources.

What is the NSW payroll tax threshold for 2026-27?

$1,200,000 a year, and the rate is 5.45% of wages above it. The threshold is reduced if you also pay wages interstate (by the NSW share of your Australian wages) or if you did not pay wages for the whole year (by days).[1]

What is the NSW monthly payroll tax threshold?

For 2026-27 it is $92,055 for a 28-day month, $98,630 for a 30-day month and $101,918 for a 31-day month. Register within 7 days after the month in which your total Australian wages go above it.[1],[3]

When is NSW payroll tax due?

Monthly returns are due on the 7th of the following month (the December 2026 return is due on 14 January 2027). There is no June monthly return: June is included in the annual return, due on 28 July 2027 for 2026-27.[4]
