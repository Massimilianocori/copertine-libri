Formulas used
  1. New South Wales: threshold = $1,200,000 × share × days; tax = (NSW wages − threshold) × 5.45%.
  2. Victoria: threshold = $1,000,000 × share × days, cut by 50% of (Victorian wages − $3m × share × days) when W is between $3m and $5m, nil above $5m; tax at 4.85% (1.2125% regional); surcharges 1% above $10m and another 1% above $100m (shared thresholds).
  3. Queensland: deduction = share × ($1.3m × days − (W − $1.3m × days) ÷ 7), not below nil; tax at 4.75% (4.95% if W is above $6.5m; 1% less for regional employers); mental health levy 0.25% above $10m and 0.5% above $100m (shared thresholds).
  4. Western Australia: nil if W ≤ $1m × days; 5.5% of all WA wages if W ≥ $7.5m × days; otherwise deductable amount = ($1m × days − (W − $1m × days) × 2/13) × share, tax = (WA wages − deductable amount) × 5.5%.
  5. South Australia: nil if W ≤ $1.5m × days; deduction = $600,000 × share × days; rate 4.95%, or (annualised W − $1.5m) ÷ $200,000 × 4.95% when annualised W is $1.7m or less.
  6. Tasmania: nil if W ≤ $1.25m × days; 4% on wages above the lower threshold ($1.25m × share × days); above $2m × days, 4% on the band between the two thresholds and 6.1% above the upper one.
  7. Australian Capital Territory: nil if W ≤ $1.75m × days; tax = (ACT wages − $1.75m × share × days) × rate, where the rate is 6.75%, 6.85%, 7.35%, 7.85% or 8.75% according to W.
  8. Northern Territory: nil if W ≤ $2.5m × days; deduction = $2.5m × days − half of the excess, not below nil; tax = (NT wages − deduction × share) × 5.5% (6.5% if W is $100m or more).

Where W is total Australian wages (yours or your group's), the share is the state's wages ÷ W and days is the days you paid wages ÷ 365. Method and sources.

How the calculator works

The calculator adds up the wages you enter to get your total Australian wages (W). For each state it works out your share of that state's threshold, then applies the state's own formula, taken from the revenue office pages and the payroll tax Acts:[1]

  1. New South Wales: threshold = $1,200,000 × share × days; tax = (NSW wages − threshold) × 5.45%.[2]
  2. Victoria: threshold = $1,000,000 × share × days, cut by 50% of (Victorian wages − $3m × share × days) when W is between $3m and $5m, nil above $5m; tax at 4.85% (1.2125% regional); surcharges 1% above $10m and another 1% above $100m (shared thresholds).[3],[4]
  3. Queensland: deduction = share × ($1.3m × days − (W − $1.3m × days) ÷ 7), not below nil; tax at 4.75% (4.95% if W is above $6.5m; 1% less for regional employers); mental health levy 0.25% above $10m and 0.5% above $100m (shared thresholds).[5],[6]
  4. Western Australia: nil if W ≤ $1m × days; 5.5% of all WA wages if W ≥ $7.5m × days; otherwise deductable amount = ($1m × days − (W − $1m × days) × 2/13) × share, tax = (WA wages − deductable amount) × 5.5%.[7],[8]
  5. South Australia: nil if W ≤ $1.5m × days; deduction = $600,000 × share × days; rate 4.95%, or (annualised W − $1.5m) ÷ $200,000 × 4.95% when annualised W is $1.7m or less.[9]
  6. Tasmania: nil if W ≤ $1.25m × days; 4% on wages above the lower threshold ($1.25m × share × days); above $2m × days, 4% on the band between the two thresholds and 6.1% above the upper one.[10]
  7. Australian Capital Territory: nil if W ≤ $1.75m × days; tax = (ACT wages − $1.75m × share × days) × rate, where the rate is 6.75%, 6.85%, 7.35%, 7.85% or 8.75% according to W.[11],[12]
  8. Northern Territory: nil if W ≤ $2.5m × days; deduction = $2.5m × days − half of the excess, not below nil; tax = (NT wages − deduction × share) × 5.5% (6.5% if W is $100m or more).[13]

Days are the days in the financial year in which you (or a member of your group) paid taxable or interstate wages, which is how the Acts define the part-year adjustment.[9],[11],[13] The calculator is checked against every official worked example we could find (see how we check).

What the calculator does not do

  • It does not decide what counts as taxable wages, exemptions (charities, apprentices and trainees, some health and education employers), contractor rules or allowances: enter wages already adjusted.
  • It does not split a group's tax between members: enter the group's totals; the result is the group total.
  • It does not produce monthly return figures: monthly returns use monthly thresholds and estimates, and the annual return reconciles them. The monthly figure shown is the annual estimate divided by 12.
  • For the ACT, a part year with total wages of $1.75 million or less has no rate in the 2026-27 determination: the calculator says so instead of guessing.

Common questions

Questions people in Australia search for, answered from the official sources.

How is payroll tax calculated in Australia?

Each state taxes the wages paid in that state above its share of the threshold. Your share is the threshold multiplied by the state's wages divided by your total Australian wages, and by the days you paid wages divided by the days in the financial year. Some states also shrink the threshold as wages grow (Victoria, Queensland, Western Australia, Northern Territory) or set the rate by your total wages (Queensland, South Australia, ACT, Northern Territory).[2],[14]

How do I claim the payroll tax threshold in multiple states?

You do not get a full threshold in every state. Each state gives you its threshold multiplied by the share of your Australian wages paid there: for example, with $900,000 of NSW wages out of $3,000,000 in total, your NSW threshold is $1,200,000 × 900,000 ÷ 3,000,000 = $360,000.[2],[15]

What are the monthly payroll tax thresholds?

For 2026-27: NSW $92,055, $98,630 or $101,918 for a 28, 30 or 31-day month; Victoria $83,333; Queensland $108,333; Western Australia $83,333; South Australia $125,000; Tasmania days in the month ÷ days in the year × $1.25 million; ACT $145,833.33; Northern Territory $208,333. Monthly thresholds decide when you must register; your tax for the year is settled in the annual return.[1],[2],[16]
