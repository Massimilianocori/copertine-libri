How NT payroll tax is worked out

The Northern Territory has the highest threshold in Australia: $2,500,000 a year ($208,333 a month).[1] The rate is 5.5%; from 1 July 2026 it is 6.5% for employers and groups whose Australian wages for the year are $100 million or more.[1],[2]

Schedule 1 of the Payroll Tax Act 2009 sets the annual deduction as the threshold (apportioned by days) minus half of the wages above it, never below zero. It reaches zero at three times the threshold, $7.5 million for a full year, and is then shared by the NT share of Australian wages.[2]

The NT Treasury website does not answer our checking server (Cloudflare), so the figures here come from the Act on the NT legislation website and from the joint portal of the revenue offices.[3]

ETA       = $2,500,000 × days ÷ 365
no tax if W ≤ ETA
deduction = ETA − (W − ETA) ÷ 2, not below 0
tax       = (NT wages − deduction × NT wages ÷ W) × 5.5%   (6.5% if W ≥ $100m)

Formulas used
  1. Northern Territory: nil if W ≤ $2.5m × days; deduction = $2.5m × days − half of the excess, not below nil; tax = (NT wages − deduction × share) × 5.5% (6.5% if W is $100m or more).

Where W is total Australian wages (yours or your group's), the share is the state's wages ÷ W and days is the days you paid wages ÷ 365. Method and sources.

Monthly threshold and registration

The monthly threshold is $208,333 (weekly $48,077).[1]

Due dates

Under the Payroll Tax Act 2009, returns are due within 21 days after the end of each month, and the June return with the annual adjustment within 21 days after the end of June.[2]

All states: see payroll tax due dates.

What changed in 2026-27

From 1 July 2026 the Treasury Legislation Amendment Act 2026 added a 6.5% rate for employers and groups with Australian wages of $100 million or more. The $2.5m threshold, the deduction and the 5.5% rate are unchanged.[1],[2]

NT payroll tax: common questions

Questions people in Australia search for, answered from the official sources.

What is the NT payroll tax threshold for 2026-27?

$2,500,000 a year ($208,333 a month). Above it, the annual deduction falls by $1 for every $2 of wages and is zero at $7.5 million.[1],[2]

What is the payroll tax rate in the Northern Territory?

5.5%. From 1 July 2026 it is 6.5% for employers and groups with Australian wages of $100 million or more.[1],[2]

When is NT payroll tax due?

The Payroll Tax Act 2009 requires a return within 21 days after the end of each month, and the June return with the annual adjustment within 21 days after the end of June.[2]
