Regional rates

Regional payroll tax rates: Victoria 1.2125% and the Queensland 1% discount

Who qualifies, which areas count and what the lower rate is worth, from the State Revenue Office Victoria and the Queensland Revenue Office.

Two states charge regional employers less: Victoria taxes them at 1.2125% instead of 4.85%,[1] and Queensland takes 1% off its rate until 30 June 2030.[2] No other state or territory has a regional rate for 2026-27.[3]

Regional Victoria

Regional Victoria means 48 regional councils, from Alpine Shire to Yarriambiack Shire (including Ballarat, Greater Bendigo, Greater Geelong, Latrobe, Mildura, Warrnambool and Wodonga), plus the six alpine resorts of Mt Baw Baw, Mt Buller, Mt Hotham, Mt Stirling, Falls Creek and Lake Mountain. Only work done in Victoria counts: an employee who spends 60% of a month interstate, 10% in Melbourne and 30% in regional Victoria spent 75% of their Victorian time in regional Victoria, so is a regional employee.[1]

If you meet the 85% rule for the year but not every month, you pay 4.85% in those months and get a refund in the annual reconciliation.[1]

Regional Queensland

Regional Queensland is defined by seven ABS statistical areas (SA4): Cairns, Central Queensland, Darling Downs – Maranoa, Mackay – Isaac – Whitsunday, Queensland – Outback, Townsville and Wide Bay. Short-term residents such as working holiday makers and PALM scheme workers generally do not have their principal place of residence there.[2]

On 11 November 2026 QRO announced a temporary amnesty, until 31 December 2026, for certain employers who incorrectly claimed the discount.[4]

Victoria: monthly returns versus the annual test

The SRO's own example: in 2024-25 RedCar paid $100,000 of Victorian taxable wages every month ($1,200,000 for the year). Its regional employees received $90,000 a month from July to October and from March to June, but only $80,000 a month from November to February. In those four months it was below 85%, so its monthly returns used 4.85%; in the other eight months it used 1.2125%. Over the year it paid $1,040,000 to regional employees, 86.67% of its wages, so the annual reconciliation applied 1.2125% to the whole year and refunded the overpaid tax for November to February.[1]

Groups that lodge a joint (schedule) return split the taxable amount: the share of wages paid by regional members is taxed at 1.2125% and the rest at 4.85%. In the SRO example, three grouped businesses paid $90,000 in July 2024 against a $75,000 monthly threshold; one third of the wages came from a regional member, so $5,000 of the $15,000 taxable amount was taxed at 1.2125% and $10,000 at 4.85%.[1] For a joint return, enter only the group totals in our calculator and work the split out by hand.

Queensland: residence, ABN address and groups

An employee's principal place of residence is where they usually live, judged on facts such as whether they live there daily, where their belongings and immediate family are, how long they have lived there, their mail and electoral roll address, the address on their driver licence and whose name the utility accounts are in.[2]

Your principal place of employment is your registered ABN business address on the Australian Business Register; without an ABN, your principal place of business.[2]

In a group, each member is tested on its own: in QRO's example, two companies grouped with $7 million of Australian wages pay 3.95% (the regional member) and 4.95% (the member that is not regional).[2]

The discount stops above $350 million of wages. QRO's page describes the cap as Australian taxable wages; the Payroll Tax Act 1971, s 10A(4), refers to the taxable wages paid by the employer during the return period. Our calculator applies the cap to total Australian wages and says so; if your Queensland wages alone are under $350 million but your Australian wages are above it, ask QRO.[2],[5]

Common questions

Questions people in Australia search for, answered from the official sources.

Who counts as a regional employer in Victoria?

An employer that pays at least 85% of its Victorian taxable wages to regional employees, who must do more than half of their work for it in regional Victoria (the listed regional councils and six alpine resorts). For the annual return the 85% rule is tested over the whole year.[1]

Who gets the Queensland regional payroll tax discount?

An employer whose principal place of employment (its registered ABN address) is in regional Queensland and that pays at least 85% of its Queensland taxable wages to employees who live in regional Queensland. The discount is 1% until 30 June 2030 and does not apply above $350 million of wages.[2]
