Registration

Payroll tax registration: when you must register, state by state

Registering in each state, the monthly and weekly thresholds, grouping, and what happens in the annual return.

Payroll tax is self-assessed: you must register with each state and territory where you pay taxable wages once your Australia-wide wages (or your group's) exceed the threshold.[1] It is collected monthly but it is an annual tax, based on the wages paid in the whole financial year,[2] so the annual return settles the year.

Step by step

  1. Watch the monthly figure. Most offices test a monthly (or weekly) threshold against total Australian wages: see the table below.
  2. Register on time. NSW and Western Australia want you registered within 7 days after the month in which you go over; Queensland within 7 days of the first month in which wages exceed $25,000 a week.[3],[4],[5] Registration takes your wage history: NSW asks for wages for the current year and the 4 previous ones.[3]
  3. Check grouping. If related businesses are grouped, the group's wages decide whether you register, and one member claims the threshold.[1],[6] See grouping.
  4. Lodge monthly. Returns are generally due on the 7th of the next month.[6] See due dates.
  5. Reconcile in July. The annual return (21 July, or 28 July in NSW, SA and the ACT) works out the tax for the whole year with your real threshold share.[7]

If you registered late

The revenue offices audit employers and match data with the ATO. Penalties may apply for failing to register on time, for not registering in every state where you pay wages, and for not registering a related entity under the grouping rules. Employers who voluntarily declare their liabilities may receive significantly lower penalties than those who do not.[1]

Common questions

Questions people in Australia search for, answered from the official sources.

Do I need to register for payroll tax?

You must register in each state or territory where you pay taxable wages once your Australia-wide wages (or your group's) are above the threshold. Most states test this monthly: for example NSW and WA require registration within 7 days after the month in which you go over.[1],[3],[4]

What wage level triggers payroll tax registration?

The monthly or weekly threshold of each state: NSW $92,055 to $101,918 a month; Victoria $83,333; Queensland $25,000 a week; WA $83,333; SA $125,000 a month ($28,846 a week); Tasmania $24,038 a week during a month; ACT $145,833.33; NT $208,333 a month.[4],[5],[7],[8],[9],[10],[11]
