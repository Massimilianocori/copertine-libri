Formulas used
  1. New South Wales: threshold = $1,200,000 × share × days; tax = (NSW wages − threshold) × 5.45%.
  2. Victoria: threshold = the lesser of $1,000,000 × share × days and that amount minus 50% of (Victorian wages − $3m × share × days), never below nil (so it shrinks from $3m × days and is nil from $5m × days); tax at 4.85% (1.2125% regional); surcharges 1% above $10m and another 1% above $100m (shared thresholds).
  3. Queensland: deduction = share × ($1.3m × days − (W − $1.3m × days) ÷ 7), not below nil; tax at 4.75% (4.95% if W is above $6.5m; 1% less for regional employers); mental health levy 0.25% above $10m and 0.5% above $100m (shared thresholds).
  4. Western Australia: nil if W ≤ $1m × days; 5.5% of all WA wages if W ≥ $7.5m × days; otherwise deductable amount = ($1m × days − (W − $1m × days) × 2/13) × share, tax = (WA wages − deductable amount) × 5.5%.
  5. South Australia: nil if W ≤ $1.5m × days; deduction = $600,000 × share × days; rate 4.95%, or (annualised W − $1.5m) ÷ $200,000 × 4.95% when annualised W is $1.7m or less.
  6. Tasmania: nil if W ≤ $1.25m × days; 4% on wages above the lower threshold ($1.25m × share × days); above $2m × days, 4% on the band between the two thresholds and 6.1% above the upper one.
  7. Australian Capital Territory: nil if W ≤ $1.75m × days; tax = (ACT wages − $1.75m × share × days) × rate, where the rate is 6.75%, 6.85%, 7.35%, 7.85% or 8.75% according to W.
  8. Northern Territory: nil if W ≤ $2.5m × days; deduction = $2.5m × days − half of the excess, not below nil; tax = (NT wages − deduction × share) × 5.5% (6.5% if W is $100m or more).

Where W is total Australian wages (yours or your group's), the share is the state's wages ÷ W and days is the days you paid wages ÷ 365. Method and sources.

What changed on 1 July 2026

  • ACT: threshold down from $2,000,000 to $1,750,000; five rates from 6.75% to 8.75%, chosen by total wages and charged on all ACT wages above the threshold.[8]
  • Northern Territory: a 6.5% rate for employers and groups with Australian wages of $100 million or more; 5.5% and the $2.5m threshold unchanged for everyone else.[1],[9]
  • Payday Super: from 1 July 2026 employers must get super into the fund within 7 business days of each payday.[10] What it means.
  • NSW, Victoria, Queensland, WA, SA and Tasmania kept their 2025-26 thresholds and rates.[1]

Paying wages in more than one state?

You do not get a full threshold in every state. Each state gives you its threshold multiplied by the share of your Australian wages paid there. With $900,000 of NSW wages out of $3,000,000, your NSW threshold is $1,200,000 × 900,000 ÷ 3,000,000 = $360,000.[2] Victoria, Queensland, Western Australia and the Northern Territory also shrink the threshold as total wages grow, so the order in which you add wages matters less than the total.

Common questions

Questions people in Australia search for, answered from the official sources.

What are the payroll tax rates by state in 2026-27?

New South Wales 5.45%; Victoria 4.85% (1.2125% for regional employers); Queensland 4.75%, or 4.95% when Australian wages are above $6.5 million; Western Australia 5.5%; South Australia 0% to 4.95% between $1.5 million and $1.7 million of Australian wages and 4.95% above; Tasmania 4% on wages from $1,250,001 to $2 million and 6.1% above; ACT 6.75% to 8.75% depending on total Australian wages; Northern Territory 5.5%, or 6.5% when Australian wages are $100 million or more.[1]

What is the payroll tax threshold in each state for 2026-27?

Annual thresholds for 2026-27: NSW $1,200,000; Victoria $1,000,000; Queensland $1,300,000; Western Australia $1,000,000; South Australia $1,500,000; Tasmania $1,250,000; ACT $1,750,000; Northern Territory $2,500,000. The thresholds are tested against your total Australian wages (or your group's), not just the wages paid in one state.[1]

Is payroll tax a state or federal tax?

Payroll tax is a state and territory tax. Each of the eight revenue offices sets its own rates and thresholds, and you register with each state or territory where you pay taxable wages once your Australia-wide wages exceed the threshold.[1],[11]

Which state has the lowest payroll tax?

It depends on your payroll. The Northern Territory has the highest tax-free threshold ($2.5 million) and Victoria and Western Australia the lowest ($1 million). The lowest rates are Victoria's regional rate of 1.2125% and Tasmania's 4% band on wages between $1.25 million and $2 million; the highest are the ACT's 6.75% to 8.75%. Because each threshold is shared by your wage share in that state, the calculator gives the real comparison for your numbers.[1]

Does payroll tax include superannuation?

Yes. Employer superannuation contributions are part of taxable wages for payroll tax, together with salaries, fringe benefits, termination payments, some contractor payments, allowances, bonuses, directors' fees and shares and options (Revenue NSW list; the other states follow harmonised rules).[12]
