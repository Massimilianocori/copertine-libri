How VIC payroll tax is worked out

Victoria charges 4.85% on Victorian taxable wages above your threshold, or 1.2125% for regional employers.[1],[2] The full threshold is $1,000,000 a year ($83,333 a month).

The State Revenue Office works the threshold out in four steps:[3] (1) multiply $1,000,000 by your Victorian share of Australian wages and the share of the year you employed; (2) multiply $3 million by the same two shares; (3) subtract step 2 from your Victorian wages and multiply by the phase-out rate, 50% from 2025-26 onwards; (4) subtract step 3 from step 1. Schedule 1 of the Payroll Tax Act 2007 takes the lesser of step 4 and step 1, and treats a negative step 4 as nil.[4]

Because the $3 million is scaled by the days you employed, the phase-out starts at $3 million × days ÷ 365 of Australian wages and the threshold reaches nil at $5 million × days ÷ 365. For a full year that is $3 million and $5 million; for an employer that paid wages on only 183 days, $1.5 million and $2.5 million: with $2.9 million of Victorian wages over 183 days there is no threshold and the tax is $140,650.[4]

Employers and groups with Australian wages above $10 million also pay two surcharges on their Victorian wages above the shared thresholds (see below).[5]

D1        = $1,000,000 × share × days ÷ 365
phase-out = D1 − (VIC wages − $3,000,000 × share × days ÷ 365) × 50%, not below 0
threshold = the lesser of D1 and the phase-out amount
tax       = (VIC wages − threshold) × 4.85%   (1.2125% regional)

Formulas used
  1. Victoria: threshold = the lesser of $1,000,000 × share × days and that amount minus 50% of (Victorian wages − $3m × share × days), never below nil (so it shrinks from $3m × days and is nil from $5m × days); tax at 4.85% (1.2125% regional); surcharges 1% above $10m and another 1% above $100m (shared thresholds).

Where W is total Australian wages (yours or your group's), the share is the state's wages ÷ W and days is the days you paid wages ÷ 365. Method and sources.

Regional employers: 1.2125%

You pay 1.2125% instead of 4.85% if at least 85% of your Victorian taxable wages go to regional employees, who must do more than half of their work for you in regional Victoria (48 listed councils and six alpine resorts). For the annual reconciliation the 85% test applies to the whole year. In a group, each business is tested on its own.[6] See regional rates.

Surcharges above $10 million

The mental health and wellbeing surcharge and the COVID-19 debt temporary surcharge (until 30 June 2033) are each 0.5% when Australian wages are over $10 million (1% together) and each another 0.5% over $100 million (2% together). They apply only to Victorian taxable wages above the thresholds, which are shared by your Victorian share of wages and by days employed: for example, $80m in Victoria and $70m in NSW gives a first threshold of $5,333,333.33.[5] The calculator adds them as a separate line.

Monthly threshold and registration

The monthly threshold is $83,333.[1] You must register if you pay wages in Victoria and your taxable Australian wages exceed the threshold.[7]

Due dates

Monthly returns and payments are due by the 7th of the next month (or the next business day).[8] The annual reconciliation is due by 21 July.[9]

All states: see payroll tax due dates.

What changed in 2026-27

Nothing new on 1 July 2026. The threshold rose to $1,000,000 and the phase-out rate to 50% from 1 July 2025, and both apply to 2026-27.[1],[3]

Victorian payroll tax: common questions

Questions people in Australia search for, answered from the official sources.

What is the Victorian payroll tax threshold for 2026-27?

$1,000,000 a year ($83,333 a month). With Australian wages above $3 million the threshold is phased out at 50%, and at $5 million there is none left (for a part year both limits are scaled by the days you employed).[1],[3],[4]

What is the regional payroll tax rate in Victoria?

1.2125%, instead of 4.85%, if at least 85% of your Victorian taxable wages are paid to regional employees, that is employees who do more than half of their work for you in regional Victoria.[6]

What is the Victorian mental health and wellbeing surcharge?

It is one of two payroll tax surcharges (the other is the COVID-19 debt temporary surcharge, until 30 June 2033). With Australian wages over $10 million each is 0.5% (1% in total); over $100 million each adds another 0.5% (2% in total). They apply only to Victorian taxable wages above the thresholds, which are shared by the Victorian share of wages and by days employed.[5]

How is payroll tax calculated in Victoria?

Take your Victorian taxable wages and subtract your threshold: $1,000,000 times your Victorian share of Australian wages and the share of the year you employed, reduced by 50% of the Victorian wages above $3 million (scaled the same way), never below nil. Then multiply by 4.85%, or 1.2125% for regional employers.[1],[3],[4]
