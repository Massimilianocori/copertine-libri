Interstate wages

Payroll tax and interstate wages: how the threshold is shared

If you pay wages in more than one state, each state gives you only a share of its threshold. Here is the rule, every official example, and a four-state worked case.

Interstate wages are wages that are taxable under the payroll tax law of another state or territory.[1] Every state counts them, because every threshold is tested against your total Australian wages and then shared by the part of those wages paid in that state. Interstate wages must be declared in each state's returns even when you are under that state's threshold.[1]

The rule in every state

Your share of a state's threshold = the state's threshold × wages paid in that state ÷ total Australian wages, and × days you paid wages ÷ days in the year for a part year.[2],[3] On top of that, some states reduce the threshold itself as total wages grow, before sharing it:

  • Victoria: phased out at 50% from $3m, nil from $5m (both scaled by days for a part year).[4],[5]
  • Queensland: $1 less for every $7 above $1.3m, nil at $10.4m.[6]
  • Western Australia: $2 less for every $13 above $1m, nil at $7.5m.[7]
  • Northern Territory: $1 less for every $2 above $2.5m, nil at $7.5m.[8]

Tasmania shares both of its thresholds ($1.25m and $2m),[9] and South Australia shares its $600,000 deduction while its rate depends on total wages.[10]

Which state taxes an employee who works in several states?

The harmonised nexus rules decide where each month's wages are taxable. If the employee works only in one state, it is that state. Otherwise the tests apply in order: where the employee is based (principal place of residence); if not based in Australia, where the employer is based; then where the wages are paid; and finally where the services are mainly performed.[13],[14] RevenueSA has a page on the same rules.[15]

Common questions

Questions people in Australia search for, answered from the official sources.

What are interstate wages for payroll tax?

Wages that are taxable under the payroll tax law of another state or territory. You declare them in each state's returns so that the state can work out your share of its threshold.[1]

Which state taxes an employee who works in more than one state?

The nexus rules decide it, month by month: first where the employee is based (principal place of residence), then where the employer is based, then where the wages are paid, then where the services are mainly performed.[13],[14]
