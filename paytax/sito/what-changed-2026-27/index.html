What changed · 1 July 2026

Payroll tax changes for 2026-27

New ACT threshold and brackets, a 6.5% NT rate for large payrolls, and what stayed the same in the other six states.

Two jurisdictions changed their payroll tax on 1 July 2026: the ACT and the Northern Territory. The joint portal of the revenue offices, last updated on 28 October 2026, shows the other six unchanged.[1] Payday Super, a federal change for every employer, also started on 1 July 2026.[2]

ACT: lower threshold, five rates

The 2026 determination lowered the threshold from $2,000,000 to $1,750,000 (monthly $145,833.33) and folded the surcharges for larger employers into a single general rate set by total wages: 6.75% up to $20m, 6.85% up to $50m, 7.35% up to $100m, 7.85% up to $150m and 8.75% above.[3],[4] In 2025-26 the rate was 6.85%, plus 0.5% above $50m and 1% above $100m, with 8.75% from 1 January 2026 for employers above $150m.[5],[6]

Northern Territory: 6.5% for payrolls of $100 million or more

From 1 July 2026 the Payroll Tax Act 2009 charges 6.5% instead of 5.5% when the Australian wages of the employer, or of its group, are $100 million or more for the year. The $2.5 million threshold, the deduction and the 5.5% rate are unchanged for everyone else.[1],[7]

The other states

  • NSW: $1,200,000 and 5.45%, as every year since 2022-23.[8]
  • Victoria: $1,000,000 and the 50% phase-out, both from 2025-26.[9]
  • Queensland: unchanged rates; regional discount to 30 June 2030 with an amnesty until 31 December 2026 for certain incorrect claims; an Act assented on 22 October 2026 confirms the Commissioner may exclude a set of persons from a group.[10],[11]
  • Western Australia: $1,000,000, 5.5%, diminishing to $7.5m.[12]
  • South Australia: $1,500,000 and up to 4.95%.[13]
  • Tasmania: $1.25m and $2m, 4% and 6.1%.[14]

Common questions

Questions people in Australia search for, answered from the official sources.

What changed in payroll tax on 1 July 2026?

The ACT lowered its threshold to $1.75 million and replaced its flat rate and surcharges with five rates from 6.75% to 8.75%; the Northern Territory added a 6.5% rate for employers with Australian wages of $100 million or more. NSW, Victoria, Queensland, Western Australia, South Australia and Tasmania kept their 2025-26 thresholds and rates.[1],[3],[7]
