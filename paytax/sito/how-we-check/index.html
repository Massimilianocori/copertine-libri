How we check

How we check every figure

Our sources, what we could not read, the tests the calculator passes before each update, and how to report an error.

Every figure on this site comes from an official page or a piece of legislation. On 11 November 2026 we read 66 official sources and checked 181 exact phrases and figures in them (for example “$1,200,000 x (184 ÷ 365) = $604,931.51” on the Revenue NSW page). If a phrase disappears from its page, our checks fail and the figure is reviewed before it stays on the site.

Where the figures come from

  • The joint portal of the eight revenue offices, payrolltax.gov.au, for the current rates and thresholds of every state.[1]
  • The pages of Revenue NSW, the State Revenue Offices of Victoria and Tasmania, the Queensland Revenue Office, RevenueWA and RevenueSA.[2],[3],[4],[5],[6],[7]
  • The legislation itself where the formula matters: the payroll tax Acts of Victoria, Queensland, Western Australia, South Australia, Tasmania, the ACT and the Northern Territory, and the ACT 2026 determination.[8],[9],[10],[11],[12],[13],[14],[15]
  • The Federal Register of Legislation for Payday Super.[16]

What we could not read

Three websites answer our checking server with a 403 (bot protection): the ATO, the ACT Revenue Office and the NT Treasury.[17],[18],[19] We do not try to get round these blocks. ACT and NT figures come from the joint portal and from the legislation; for Payday Super we rely on the Act and point you to the ATO page.

How the calculator is tested

The calculator in your browser is a copy of a reference implementation that we run before every update. The checks:

  • recalculate 28 worked examples published by the revenue offices (listed below), each within a dollar or two of the official figure where the office rounds its steps;
  • recalculate cases worked out by hand from the formula in an Act where no office publishes an example (for instance Victoria for a part year);
  • compare every row of RevenueSA's indicative rate table with the formula in the South Australian Act;
  • check the ACT brackets and the NT rules at their limits;
  • run the browser calculator and the reference implementation on thousands of random payrolls and require identical results;
  • check that the official calculators and pages we link still answer.

The formulas are listed on the calculator page.

The one case we do not calculate

For the ACT, an employer that paid wages for only part of the year and whose total Australian wages are $1.75 million or less, but above its reduced threshold, is liable. The 2026 determination's rate table starts above $1.75 million, so it sets no rate for that level. Rather than guess, the calculator shows “not calculated” and sends you to the ACT Revenue Office.[13],[14]

When we update

Payroll tax rates and thresholds change on 1 July, and state budgets in May and June announce them. Each year the site moves to the new financial year only after every figure has been re-read on the official pages; between updates, the checks above run again and flag any page that has changed. A page whose “last updated” date moves after our check is re-read by hand.
