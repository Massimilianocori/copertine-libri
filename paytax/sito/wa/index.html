How WA payroll tax is worked out

Western Australia charges 5.5% on WA taxable wages above your deductable amount.[1] The annual threshold is $1,000,000 and the upper threshold $7,500,000; between them the threshold diminishes by the tapering value 2/13, that is $2 for every $13 of wages over $1 million.[1],[2]

If you also pay wages interstate, the deductable amount is multiplied by WA wages ÷ Australian wages. For a part year, the Pay-roll Tax Assessment Act 2002 apportions both the annual and the upper threshold by days before the taper is applied.[2],[3]

AT = $1,000,000 × days paid ÷ 365;  UT = $7,500,000 × days paid ÷ 365
if W ≤ AT: no tax
if W ≥ UT: tax = WA wages × 5.5%
otherwise: deductable = (AT − (W − AT) × 2/13) × (WA wages ÷ W);  tax = (WA wages − deductable) × 5.5%

Formulas used
  1. Western Australia: nil if W ≤ $1m × days; 5.5% of all WA wages if W ≥ $7.5m × days; otherwise deductable amount = ($1m × days − (W − $1m × days) × 2/13) × share, tax = (WA wages − deductable amount) × 5.5%.

Where W is total Australian wages (yours or your group's), the share is the state's wages ÷ W and days is the days you paid wages ÷ 365. Method and sources.

Monthly threshold and registration

The monthly threshold is $83,333. Register within seven days after the end of the month in which your total Australian taxable wages exceed it.[1],[5]

Due dates

Returns are due on the 7th day of the month after the return period (April by 7 May). The annual reconciliation, with the June return, is due on or before 21 July.[6]

All states: see payroll tax due dates.

What changed in 2026-27

Unchanged for 2026-27. The extra rates on very large payrolls (6% on wages from $100m to $1.5bn and 6.5% above) applied only up to 30 June 2023.[1]

WA payroll tax: common questions

Questions people in Australia search for, answered from the official sources.

What is the WA payroll tax threshold for 2026-27?

$1,000,000 a year ($83,333 a month). It is a diminishing threshold: above $1 million it falls by $2 for every $13 of wages and disappears at $7.5 million of Australian taxable wages.[1],[2]

What is the payroll tax rate in Western Australia?

5.5% of taxable wages above your deductable amount. For example, with WA wages of $1,200,000 the deductable amount is $969,231 and the tax is $12,692.30.[1],[4]

When is WA payroll tax due?

Monthly returns are due on the 7th day of the following month; the annual reconciliation, together with the June return, is due on or before 21 July.[6]
